Verdict

A high-return, net-cash business on a fair multiple, but customer concentration and related-party history argue against chasing it. Hold, high risk. FY26 revenue was ₹457 cr (+64%), EBITDA margin 23%, PAT ₹84 cr and ROE about 32–39%, with ₹79 cr of net cash before the IPO money. The order book was ₹982 cr at 31 Jul 2026, about 2.1x FY26 revenued. Management guides to about 25% revenue growth in FY27 at a 23–25% EBITDA margin and an 18–20% PAT margin. That implies PAT of about ₹103–114 cr and a P/E of 19–21xd on guidance, against 26.6x trailing.

Against that: the top three clients were 57% of FY26 revenue and the top ten 86%. They include the US window-wall buyers Winpro International and Reflection Window + Wall. The international supply business (45% of revenue) depends on US construction and tariffs. The June quarter was soft: revenue +36% but PAT −8%, with international revenue −12% on timing. The other half of the anchor shares unlocks in early December. And in August 2025 the company bought Yes Systems, the promoter family's fenestration business, with ₹155 cr of new shares. Its revenue fell from ₹34 cr to ₹27 cr in FY26.

Business

Nature of business

Glass Wall has three verticals. Domestic façade EPC (49% of FY26 revenue) designs, engineers, makes and installs curtain walls for commercial real-estate developers such as Embassy, Bagmane, Prestige, K Raheja and Lodha, almost all for non-residential projects. International product supply (45%) makes ready-to-install unitised window-wall and curtain-wall systems for US and Australian façade contractors, with no installation risk and higher margins. Premium fenestration (6%, Yes Systems) makes high-end aluminium windows. It manufactures at one owned site at Vile Bhagad, Maharashtra. The ₹50 cr from the fresh issue goes to an in-house glass processing unit there (₹82 cr total), due in about August 2027. The company describes itself as India's second-largest façade provider and largest façade exporter, based on the KEN industry report it commissioned for the RHP.

Moat

Moderate but narrow. It has 8–12-year relationships with major developers, engineering credentials for unitised systems accepted by US contractors, and a low-cost export base. Against that, façade EPC is won project by project through competitive bids, and a handful of customers make up most of revenue.

The investor presentation splits the order book by vertical roughly as domestic façade about ₹626 cr, international about ₹186 cr and fenestration about ₹169 cr. These are read from the slide layout, so treat them as approximate. The two 15 Oct LOIs add an undisclosed amount, each under ₹50 cr.

Risks

  • Customer concentration. Top 3 clients were 57% and top 10 were 86% of FY26 revenue. Winpro and Reflection Window + Wall (US) are among them. Losing one would cut revenue sharply.
  • US exposure. 45% of revenue is product supply to North America and Australia. US tariffs or a slowdown in US construction would hit the highest-margin vertical first.
  • Supply overhang. About 35 lakh anchor shares unlock around 9–11 Dec. Pre-IPO PE holders (IBEF II 9.41%, First Bridge, Baring) can sell after the six-month lock-in on pre-issue capital, around March 2027.
  • Related parties. There is the Yes Systems purchase from the promoter family and a lead manager affiliated with the main seller. Watch for further related-party deals.
  • Lumpy quarters. Revenue is weighted to the second half and recognised by project. A short listed history means little track record of meeting guidance.
  • Single plant at Vile Bhagad, and a reported indirect-tax dispute (Maharashtra VAT) listed in the RHP's litigation section. This note did not quantify it.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: an H1 FY27 at or above ₹230 cr of revenue with EBITDA margin of 22% or more and operating cash flow in line with PAT, showing that the second-half-weighted guidance is on track. Or a price near ₹200 (about 16–17x guided FY27 earnings) once the December anchor unlock has passed.
  • Downgrade to Sell: FY27 guidance cut, loss of a top-three customer, a fall in international revenue in H2, or any further acquisition of a promoter-family business paid for in shares.
