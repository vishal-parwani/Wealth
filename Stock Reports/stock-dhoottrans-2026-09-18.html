Verdict

The IPO call was wrong on the price. We keep it, because the reasons for it have got stronger. The 10 August note said ₹871 already paid for three years of growth. Anyone who got shares is up 83%. The issue was subscribed 74 times, listed at ₹1,200 on 17 August, and has since traded up to ₹1,729. That is a real miss and should be said plainly.

The business did well in its first quarter as a listed company. Revenue was ₹1,446 cr, up 50%. EV harness revenue was up 79% and is now 27% of sales. Profit was ₹133 cr, up 38%. But the problem the IPO note flagged is still there. EBITDA margin fell to 15.1% from 17.5% a year earlier, so profit grew slower than sales again. Management guides 25–30% revenue growth for FY27 and a 15–16% margin. At the IPO the margin was 17%.

The price now asks for far more than it did in August: 73x trailing earnings, against 45x at the issue price. On our own rough FY27 estimate built from the guidance, it is about 56xd. Bain Capital still owns about 43% and will sell over time. Anchor investors' lock-ins are ending: half after 30 days (mid-September) and the rest after 90 days (mid-November). A 24%-return business with a strong EV position is worth a premium, but not this one. Stays Sell, which here means take the listing gain.

Business

What it does

Wiring harnesses make up 77% of revenue. The rest is battery packs, sensors, controllers, switches, connectors and chargers. By vehicle, 67% is two-wheelers and 11% three-wheelers. By powertrain, 63% is petrol and diesel and 27% electric. 92% of sales are in India. The company has about 38–41% of India's two- and three-wheeler harness market and about 70% of the electric two- and three-wheeler harness market. Its overseas plants (UK, Slovakia, Thailand, Korea, Japan) came through acquisitions. New plants at Jhajjar and Hosur will add 15–20% capacity.

Moat

Real. A harness is designed for a specific vehicle platform, so switching supplier during a model's life is costly. And 38–41% share in a concentrated market is hard to attack in the short term. The EV lead matters more: an electric scooter carries more and higher-spec electrical content. The weakness is the customer base. The top three customers are 63% of revenue, and a customer that large has a lot of say over price, which is one likely reason margin keeps slipping.

Order book

No order book is disclosed. As with any auto-component maker, revenue follows the customers' production plans. The forward commitments are the 25–30% FY27 growth guidance and the medium-term ₹5,000 cr revenue target from the IPO documents. On current growth, FY27 alone would get close to it.

Risks · to the Sell call

The Sell could keep being wrong. New listings with strong growth and a hot sector story often stay expensive for longer than seems reasonable. If EV adoption speeds up and margin holds at 15–16%, profit growth close to 30% could carry the stock. The rating is a view on price, not on the quality of the company.
  • For holders: margin compression continues (17.5% → 15.1% year on year), more shares come to market as lock-ins end in September, November and February, and Bain eventually sells its roughly ₹14,000 cr staked.
  • Customer concentration: Bajaj is 31% of revenue and the top three are 63%.
  • Up to ₹350 cr of IPO money set aside for acquisitions not yet named, from a company whose earlier acquisitions coincided with the margin decline.
  • Short history. One quarter as a listed company, two quarters missing from the record, no broker coverage yet.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: margin stabilises at or above 16% for two quarters while growth stays above 25%. Or the price falls back to about 45x trailing earnings (roughly ₹1,000 on current profit).
  • Upgrade to Buy: the same margin recovery, plus a price closer to the IPO range.
