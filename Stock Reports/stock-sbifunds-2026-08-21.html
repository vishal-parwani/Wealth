Verdict

Buy — unchanged, and it is cheaper than when the last note was written. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹585.30 on 3 Aug 2026, shortly after the 21 July listing. The stock is ₹549.55, down 6.1%, and sits within ₹2 of its ₹548 low. It listed from a ₹545–574 band, so the entire listing gain has gone and the stock now trades essentially at its IPO price.

The franchise is doing what it was bought for. FY26 revenue ₹4,966 cr, up 17.3%, with PAT ₹3,067 cr, up 20.7%. Quarterly AUM reached ₹12.61 lakh crore in Q1 FY27 at a 15.12% market share — still the largest fund house in India. Operating margin is around 80%, ROE 43%, ROCE 56.5%, and there is no debt at all. Very few businesses of this size have these economics.

The valuation argument that justified the IPO still holds. At 36.1x trailing it remains the cheapest of the listed AMCs on the comparison the last note used — HDFC AMC ~42x, ICICI Pru AMC ~49x, Nippon ~51x. Those peer multiples are carried forward from July and have not been re-verified here, so treat the gap as indicative rather than precise.

Two things to hold in mind, and they explain the price. Q1 FY27 PAT grew only 3.7% while revenue grew 15.2% — though operating profit was up 17%, so the shortfall is below the operating line rather than in the business. And the free float is tiny: promoters still hold 88.19%. Meeting the 25% minimum public shareholding rule means a great deal more stock has to be sold. That overhang, not the earnings, is the most likely reason a company 50 funds just bought is trading at its low.

This is the most important number in the note: 88.19%. Non-promoter holding is 11.81%. Indian listing rules require public shareholding to reach 25% within three years of listing. Getting there means roughly 13 more percentage points of stock coming to market — on today's market cap, in the order of ₹14,800 cr, or nearly seven times everything the 50 mutual funds bought in July.

That supply is known, scheduled and unavoidable. It does not make the business worse, and a patient holder may well be handed better prices because of it. But it is the reason to expect the stock to stay heavy, and it is why a Buy here should be sized with a multi-year horizon rather than an expectation of a near-term re-rating.

On the positive side, the register is institutional and broad already — 24.8 lakh shareholders at the first disclosure, 50 fund schemes, and a promoter pair (State Bank of India and Amundi) whose selling is regulatory housekeeping rather than a signal about value.

Business

Nature. SBI Funds Management runs SBI Mutual Fund, India's largest asset manager, with ₹12.61 lakh crore of quarterly average AUM and 15.12% market share. It earns a management fee on assets, so revenue is essentially a percentage of the market value of what it looks after. Total assets under management including EPFO and ETF mandates are far larger than the mutual fund book alone.

Why the economics are so good. Fee income scales with assets while costs scale with headcount and distribution, so incremental AUM is close to pure margin. That produces an 80% operating margin, 43% ROE and no need for debt. The SBI branch network is the distribution moat — it is the reason this AMC is number one and would be very hard for a competitor to replicate.

The structural headwind. Regulators have pushed AMC fee rates down steadily for a decade and there is no reason to expect that to stop. Growth therefore has to come from AUM rising faster than fees fall. It has, comfortably, for years — but it is the reason AMCs trade at a discount to the growth rates they post.

What you are actually buying. A geared, high-quality claim on the growth of Indian household savings moving into mutual funds. When markets fall, AUM and fees fall with them, and the earnings are more cyclical than the 80% margin makes them look.

Risks

  • Minimum public shareholding overhang. About 13 points of stock, roughly ₹14,800 cr, must reach the market within three years. The largest single risk to the price.
  • Earnings follow markets. AUM is the revenue base. A sustained equity market fall cuts fees, profit and the multiple at the same time.
  • Fee compression. A decade-long regulatory grind on expense ratios that shows no sign of reversing.
  • Reported profit is noisy. Q4 FY26 turned ₹918 cr of operating profit into ₹634 cr of PAT. Below-the-line investment gains and losses move the headline around.
  • Q1 FY27 PAT grew only 3.7%. Operating profit grew 17%, so the business is fine — but anyone screening on headline profit growth will see a weak number.
  • One month of listed history. No track record as a public company, no broker coverage, and the price series starts in July.
  • Peer multiples unverified. The claim that it is cheaper than HDFC, ICICI Pru and Nippon AMC is carried forward from the July note and has not been re-checked.

What would change the rating

  • Stays a Buy — largest AMC in the country, no debt, 43% ROE, at the cheapest multiple in its peer group, with the price back at the IPO level.
  • Raise conviction — a clear, announced plan for the minimum-public-shareholding sell-down. Certainty about the size and timing of that supply would remove the main overhang.
  • Downgrade to Hold — operating margin slipping below 75%, AUM market share falling under 14%, or a sustained equity market downturn.
  • Downgrade to Sell — a large block sale at a steep discount to market, or fee regulation materially worse than the current gradual compression.
