Verdict

A genuine transformation, bought rather than built, at a price that assumes it works. Hold, high risk. Fineotex made textile auxiliaries and cleaning chemicals — a steady ₹533–569 cr business earning 24–26% operating margins. In FY26 it spent about US$26 mn buying CrudeChem Technologies, a US oilfield-chemicals group in Texas, and the company is now something else entirely: oil and gas was roughly 65% of the June quarter's revenue. Quarterly revenue has gone ₹120 cr → ₹137 cr → ₹138 cr → ₹184 cr → ₹314 cr → ₹377 cr in six quarters.

The growth is real and so is the cost of it. Operating margin fell from 18–23% to 15.7%, because oilfield chemicals is a service-led business with people and delivery costs the textile business never carried. Return on capital fell from 39% (FY24) to 18%. Most importantly, FY26 operating cash flow was −₹24 cr against ₹135 cr of operating profit, and free cash flow −₹49 cr. Receivables went from ₹116 cr to ₹290 cr and inventory from ₹64 cr to ₹155 cr. The cash cycle stretched from 64 days to 133.

At ₹56 the stock is up 191% from its 52-week low on 53x trailing earnings. The only house that covers it, Deven Choksey, has a target of ₹39.50 — 30% below the market, after three raises in nine months (₹28 → ₹36 → ₹45.80 → ₹48), each one passed by the price within weeks. Management guides the oilfield platform to US$100 mn of revenue in FY27 and US$200 mn in FY28, which if delivered makes 53x look different. Nothing in the filings yet shows the cash arriving. Hold: the September-quarter cash flow statement is the thing that decides this, not the next announcement.

On the tweet that prompted this report. On 24 September the company incorporated Fineotex America Inc. in Delaware, a wholly owned step-down subsidiary, to target water-treatment solutions for data centres and semiconductor manufacturing. The filing states it has no turnover and no acquisition cost. It is a registered shell with an intention attached — the second Delaware entity in a month, after Fineotex Holdings Inc. on 8 September. The underlying idea is reasonable (cooling and process water chemistry is adjacent to what it already does), but "data centre" and "semiconductor" in a headline should not move a valuation until there is a customer, a contract or a capex number. There is none of that today.

Business

What it does

Two businesses now. The original one, founded 1979 and run from Mumbai with plants at Ambernath and in Malaysia, makes textile auxiliaries (chemicals used in dyeing and finishing cloth), cleaning and hygiene chemicals, and water-treatment chemicals. The new one, CrudeChem Technologies in Texas, makes specialty chemicals for oil and gas drilling and production and sells them as a service, with field support and last-mile delivery. That second business is now about two-thirds of revenue and all of the growth. A separate Delaware entity was registered on 24 September to chase water treatment for data centres and semiconductor plants; it has no operations yet.

Moat

Modest, and now in two different places. In textile auxiliaries it has long-standing customers and formulation know-how in a competitive Indian market that management itself calls intense. In oilfield chemicals the moat is service: being near the well, delivering on time and solving production problems. That is a real barrier in a local market, but it is also why the margin is lower — you are paying people, not just selling litres.

Order book

None disclosed, and not typical for the industry. The forward commitments are the oilfield revenue targets (US$100 mn FY27, US$200 mn FY28) and the Texas capacity now in place at 63% utilisation. Management explicitly links the oil and gas guidance to customer demand and visibility rather than to crude prices.

Risks

Profit that hasn't become cash, at 53x. FY26 turned ₹135 cr of operating profit into −₹24 cr of operating cash. For an acquisition-led scale-up, the first thing to check is whether the acquired revenue collects. The September-quarter statement is the test.
  • The multiple against the only published view: 53x trailing, with the sole covering broker at ₹39.50, 30% below.
  • Margin reset: 26% operating margin in FY24 to 16% now, with no margin guidance from management.
  • US oil and gas exposure: drilling activity is cyclical and tied to American shale spending, a very different risk from Indian textile chemicals.
  • Announcement flow: two Delaware shells in a month, both announced, neither with revenue. Watch whether announcements keep outpacing results.
  • Thin institutional ownership: 1.6% held by mutual funds, DII holding falling, 1.8 lakh retail holders and ₹79 cr traded a day.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 operating cash flow at or above 60% of operating profit with receivable days back under 100 — proof the acquired revenue collects. A price near ₹40 (about 38x trailing, and level with the broker target) would do it on valuation alone.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a second year of negative operating cash flow, EBITDA margin below 14%, the FY27 US$100 mn oilfield target being walked back, or a further acquisition funded with new shares.
