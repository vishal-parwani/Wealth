Verdict

At 27x FY28 consensus earnings, with profit compounding 40%-plus and a clean US FDA audit in September, the price now pays for growth already being delivered. Buy, sized for high risk. FY26 revenue rose 59% to ₹633 cr and net profit 110% to ₹122 cr. The operating margin went from 22.6% to 26.5%, and Q1 FY27 reached 30.0%. The approved ANDA (US generic drug approval) count nearly doubled in a year, from 30 to 58, and only 23 are commercialised so far. That leaves launches still to come. Five analysts' consensus is ₹1,614, which is 22% above spot. Their EPS estimates are ₹35.2 for FY27 and ₹49.6 for FY28, or 37x and 27x.

The counter-argument is cash. Free cash flow was −₹203 cr in both FY25 and FY26. Operating cash flow only turned positive in FY26, at ₹62 cr. Borrowings are ₹342 cr and still rising, and the IPO cash pile has been run down from ₹386 cr to ₹160 cr. Debtor days were 187 in FY26. ROCE is 15% and ROE 13.4%, held down by IPO equity that is still being put to work. Other income was ₹47 cr in FY26, close to 30% of profit before tax. This is a growth story funded by the IPO and by debt, and it has not yet funded itself.

Business

Nature of business

A complex-generics maker for regulated markets. It owns ANDAs — 58 approved at June 2026, 23 commercialised — and manufactures for US partners as a CDMO/CMO, alongside an emerging-markets formulations business. In Q1 FY27, regulated markets were ₹128 cr (71% of revenue), emerging markets ₹38 cr, and the API and other businesses made up the rest. The newly inspected Apnar plant in Vadodara adds scale for launches and contract manufacturing. Subsidiaries are being set up in Mexico and Canada.

Moat

Moderate, and built on approvals rather than products. Each ANDA takes years and US FDA-inspected capacity to obtain, and a portfolio of niche, limited-competition molecules is harder to copy than a commodity generics book. Against that, US generics prices only fall over time, and every molecule faces new entrants once it proves profitable. The moat is the pipeline's pace, not any single product.

Risks

Funding. −₹203 cr of free cash flow in each of the last two years, net debt of ₹182 cr and rising, and IPO cash down to ₹160 cr. If FY27 operating cash flow does not cover capex, the next round of growth needs debt or dilution. At 0.37x debt-to-equity, there is room, but not unlimited room.
  • US regulatory and pricing. One warning letter at a key site would halt launches, and generic price erosion steepens as competitors file. US tariffs on imported generics were raised on the Q1 call as a live risk.
  • Growth is slowing at the top line. Q1 revenue growth was 30%, against 54–66% in the prior three quarters. Consensus FY27 EPS of ₹35.2 needs growth to reaccelerate from here.
  • Earnings quality. Other income was close to 30% of FY26 pre-tax profit, and receivables run at 187 days.
  • Management turnover. The CFO was replaced in August 2026.
  • Fund selling. HSBC, the largest fund-house holder, cut all four of its positions in August.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: H1 FY27 operating cash flow above 70% of EBITDA with free cash flow near breakeven, or Q2 revenue growth back above 40%. Either would mean the quality concern is being fixed at the same pace as the growth.
  • Downgrade to Hold: the price runs through about ₹1,600, roughly 32x FY28 consensus, without estimates rising. Or H1 FY27 PAT trends below a ₹30 FY27 EPS run-rate.
  • Downgrade to Sell: an FDA warning letter or import alert at any manufacturing site, or an equity raise to fund capex in FY27.
