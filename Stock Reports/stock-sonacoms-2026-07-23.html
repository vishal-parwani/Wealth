Verdict

The highest-quality EV-driveline compounder in India, and now with a genuine catalyst — but the price already reflects the good news. Sona Comstar is a global leader in differential gears (6–7% of the world PV differential market) that has successfully pivoted into EV traction motors and driveline. FY26 was strong: revenue +26% to ₹4,475 cr, EBITDA margin a fat 24.7%, with a ₹23,700 cr net order book (~5.3x revenue, ~70% EV across 67 programmes / 35 customers). The fresh news the market is digesting: on 22 Jul 2026 the board approved two JVs with Japan's DENSO to co-develop electric/hybrid powertrains (details below) — a structurally positive deal that imports DENSO's high-voltage electrification tech and shortens Sona's R&D runway. The sell-side responded constructively: UBS initiated Buy at ₹830, Citi and Jefferies at ₹700. But at ~70x earnings with FY26 adjusted PAT up only 11% (vs revenue +26%), even a strengthened moat leaves little margin of safety at the ~₹727 CMP — the targets bracket, rather than clear, the current price. Own the franchise; accumulate on weakness rather than chase the JV pop.

New: the two DENSO JVs (approved 22 Jul 2026)

Sona signed definitive agreements with DENSO Corporation (Japan — one of the world's largest auto-component suppliers) to form two India-based JVs for electric and hybrid powertrains:

  • 4W / CV / larger vehicles — DENSO 51% (management control), Sona 49%: high-voltage liquid-cooled traction inverters, traction motors and generators. Sona's existing EV business is transferred into "Sona Comstar eDrive," valued at ~₹893 cr (₹8,932 mn).
  • 2W / 3W — Sona 51%, DENSO 49%: air-cooled traction inverters, motors, generators and e-Axles. DENSO acquires its 49% at an enterprise value of ~₹1,750 cr (₹17,500 mn).
Why it matters for valuation: the deal hands Sona DENSO's high-voltage electrification IP without the multi-year R&D gestation, letting it offer localized high-voltage powertrains to Indian and global OEMs almost immediately — a structural add to the long-term margin profile and product pipeline. It broadens Sona from components toward systems. Caveats: in the larger-vehicle JV Sona is the minority, non-controlling partner (49%), so 4W economics flow through associate/JV accounting rather than consolidated revenue; and accretion is a multi-year, execution-dependent story, not an FY27 earnings event.

Business

Gurugram-based global auto-tech supplier with plants in India, US, Mexico, China and Serbia. Core: differential gears & assemblies (world top-3) and starter motors, increasingly EV traction motors and driveline (BEV now 39% of revenue). Diversification adds optionality: NOVELIC (Serbia mmWave radar/sensing + mobility software), the acquired Escorts Kubota railway equipment division (~₹913 cr FY25 revenue, ₹188 cr PBT), and an emerging robotics push — a NEURA Robotics humanoid/industrial-robot MoU (Oct 2025) plus a ₹62.6 cr robotics-components capex approved Jun 2026. Powertrain-agnostic by design: it sells into ICE, hybrid and BEV platforms alike.

Bull case

  • Order book of ₹23,700 cr (~5.3 years of revenue), 70% EV — visibility few auto-component names anywhere can match.
  • DENSO JVs deepen the EV moat across 2W/3W/4W and import world-class high-voltage tech; robotics/NEURA adds a genuinely new long-dated optionality leg.
  • 24.7% EBITDA margin is best-in-class for auto components; diversification into radar/software, railway and robotics lowers auto-cycle dependence.
  • Post-JV sell-side skews positive (UBS ₹830, ~20% rev / 25% EPS CAGR thesis).

Risks

~70x for 11% PAT growth. The stock has run +63% in six months to a 52-week high; the good news — order book, DENSO JVs, robotics — is largely in the price. Citi/Jefferies at ₹700 imply mild downside, so multiple compression on any stumble is the base-case risk.
  • Margin/return-ratio squeeze: FY26 adjusted PAT grew less than half as fast as revenue — new-program ramp costs and mix are pressuring returns; the market prices no disappointment.
  • JV execution & control: Sona is the minority partner in the larger 4W/CV JV; realized economics depend on DENSO-led execution and the accounting treatment, not headline TAM.
  • Customer/geo concentration & US exposure: large US footprint carries tariff/policy and OEM-EV-slowdown risk (a past BYD program cancellation showed order-book fragility).
  • China EV competition compressing global supplier pricing; multiple acquisitions/JVs add integration complexity outside the core.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a correction toward ₹560–620 (~50–55x) with the order book intact, return ratios re-expanding, or early evidence the DENSO JVs are winning incremental OEM platforms.
  • Downgrade to Hold or Sell: order-book cancellations, US EV-demand slump, JV delays, or another year of PAT growth lagging revenue by half.
