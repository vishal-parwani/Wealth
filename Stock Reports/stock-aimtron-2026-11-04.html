Verdict

Why it jumped · 4 Nov 2026. The stock closed at ₹1,920.50, up ₹279.15 or 17.0%, after ₹1,641.35 on 3 November. Volume was 1.77 lakh shares, about 3.8 times the six-month average. The cause is confirmed: the board approved H1 FY27 results during market hours. The outcome was filed at 2:52 pm and the press release at 2:57 pm. Consolidated revenue was ₹224.7 cr (+83% YoY), PAT was ₹34.8 cr (+71%) and the order book was ₹733 cr, up from ₹604 cr three months earlier. Peers rose much less the same day: Avalon +2.5%, Apollo Micro +8.4%, Syrma +1.4%, Kaynes +2.8%. So this was a stock-specific move, not a sector move.

Rating unchanged: Hold, high risk. The 27 October report set an explicit upgrade test for this result. It asked for positive operating cash flow, receivables at or below ₹135 cr, and revenue growth above 50%. Two legs pass clearly. Revenue grew 83%, and reported trade receivables fell to ₹71 cr from ₹135 cr in March. The third leg, cash flow, cannot be shown to pass. The half-year cash-flow statement could not be opened (below). The balance sheet also shows "loans & advances" and "other assets" up ₹107 cr over the same six months. Net of payables, working capital rose about ₹48 crd, which implies operating cash flow of roughly −₹6 crd, around break-even. That is far better than −₹40 cr for FY26, but it is not the clean positive result the trigger required. The price also moved first. At ₹1,920 the stock is 15% above the ₹1,674 preferential price, 6% below Nuvama's ₹2,036 target, and on 67x trailing earnings.

What the half says. H1 is usually the weaker half: H2 was 1.5–1.8x H1 in both FY25 and FY26. Even so, H1 FY27 revenue was 26% above H2 FY26, against 21% a year earlier (H1 FY26 on H2 FY25), and the operating margin recovered to 22% from 20%. The mix matters. Standalone revenue was flat half-on-half at ₹162 cr (+69% YoY), so all the sequential growth came from subsidiaries, mainly the US arm AIC acquired in February. Subsidiaries contributed about ₹63 cr of revenued, against ₹17 cr in H2 FY26. Other income rose to ₹6 cr from ₹1 cr, about 13% of PBT. The order book implies about ₹270 cr of Q2 inflowd, roughly 1.9x Q2 revenue, so visibility improved as well as revenue.

For a buy/hold decision: hold if you own it; this is not the entry point. The results reduce the bear case. Debtor days on the half fell to about 58d from 164, and the ₹205 cr raise, once allotted, leaves the balance sheet in net cash. A pullback towards the preferential price, or a full-year cash-flow statement showing receivables have not simply moved into another line, would make this a Buy. The H1 FY27 earnings call is on 6 November 2026, and the reclassification question should be answered there.

The receivables drop is real on the face of the balance sheet, but it is not yet cash. Trade receivables fell ₹64 cr while "loans & advances" and "other asset items" rose ₹107 cr. That pattern fits several readings. It could be a reclassification of billed receivables into unbilled revenue or contract assets. It could be supplier advances for the larger order book, or receivables sold or discounted with recourse. Screener's line items cannot separate these, and the notes to the results could not be opened. The half-year operating cash flow above is derived: PAT plus depreciation, less the ₹48 cr working-capital build. A cash walk gives the same answer: cash +₹22 cr, capex about ₹16 cr, borrowings +₹3 cr, and about ₹44 cr of equity added beyond profit. About ₹21 crd of that equity is warrant conversions (4.1 lakh shares at the ₹510 balance due). The remaining ~₹23 cr is unreconciled and may be translation reserve on the US subsidiary. Either way, H1 operating cash flow was near zero, not −₹40 cr, and not clearly positive.

Business

Nature of business

Aimtron is a Vadodara-based ESDM/ODM company, incorporated in 2011. It started in PCB design and assembly and is moving into box-build, system integration and product design. End markets are industrial electronics, power and energy, ruggedised/defence-adjacent electronics, IoT, battery storage and medical. The February 2026 acquisition of ICS (Decatur, US; ~US$17 mn revenue; customers include Caterpillar and John Deere) operates as Aimtron International Controls (AIC). H1 FY27 is the first half in which it shows up at scale: about 28%d of consolidated revenue came from outside the Indian entity.

Peers

The listed EMS set (Kaynes, Syrma, Avalon, Dixon) and defence-electronics names such as Apollo Micro are much larger, report quarterly, and have broad broker coverage and MF ownership. Aimtron has one broker and no MF holder. A ratio table would compare unaudited half-yearly SME numbers against audited quarterly large caps, so it is omitted. The price table shows the useful comparison. On 4 Nov Aimtron rose 17% while the peer set rose 1–8%.

Moat

None yet. Contract electronics is a scale-and-execution business, and customers dual-source. If the design/ODM capability and the US customer relationships work, they would create stickiness. The AIC bookings converting to revenue in H1 is early evidence that the US channel is real.

Order book & guidance

₹733 cr consolidated at H1 FY27 (press release, 4 Nov 2026), up from ₹604 cr at Q1 (27 Jul). That is about 1.8x TTM revenue. With ~₹141 crd billed in Q2, gross Q2 inflow was about ₹270 crd. The order book is a company disclosure, unaudited, with no conversion schedule. Guidance: the standing FY26-results guidance is a consolidated EBITDA margin of 20–22% and PAT margin around 15%. H1 delivered 22% and 15.5%d, at the top of the range. No revenue guidance for FY27 was found (n/a). Expect an update on the 6 Nov call.

Risks

Working capital has moved line, not necessarily away. Receivables halved, but ₹107 cr appeared in advances and other current assets, and short-term borrowings rose to ₹22 cr. Until a cash-flow statement shows positive operating cash, the three-year pattern of profit without cash is improved but not broken.
  • Valuation after the move. 67x trailing earnings and 13x book (about 8.4x post-issued). The price is 6% below the only broker target, which itself assumes roughly 55% EPS growth into FY28.
  • Growth now leans on the US subsidiary. Standalone revenue was flat half-on-half. A slip in AIC's bookings conversion would show up straight in the consolidated growth rate.
  • Earnings quality at the margin. Other income was ₹6 cr, 13% of PBT, against ₹1 cr in each prior half. Interest cost tripled to ₹3 cr.
  • No MF holder, one broker. The outside validation is AIFs buying in at ₹1,674 and a single initiation.
  • Half-yearly reporting. The next full set of numbers is May 2027.
  • Liquidity. About 0.5 lakh shares a day. A 17% day on 1.8 lakh shares can reverse just as quickly.
  • Acquisition-led growth. Use of proceeds includes "mergers and JVs across globe". More US deals add integration and goodwill risk.
