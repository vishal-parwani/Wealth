Verdict

The business grew fast. The price rose faster. The big question is still open. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹271 cr, up 135%, and profit was ₹12.8 cr, up 129%. But most of that growth came from Kuiper, the UAE drilling business bought in August 2025, and Kuiper earns thin margins. Group operating margin fell to 8%, from 10% a year ago and 14% in March. The India business on its own did better: revenue up 29%, margin 10.9%.

The Oilmax merger is still the main issue. Oilmax is the promoter's holding company. It is being folded into the listed company at 117 AESL shares for every 10 Oilmax shares, valued at about ₹2,582 cr. That means roughly 46% dilution for today's shareholders. The court (NCLT) heard the case and reserved its order on 7 September. Completion is expected in September or October.

When we last looked on 5 August the stock was ₹392. It is ₹492 now. So the market has priced in more good news, but the dilution hasn't happened yet and nobody outside the company has published post-merger numbers. At 37x trailing earnings, it is still Hold. Check the combined share count and earnings once the merger is done.

One thing is clearer now. Management says Oilmax already produces about 2,500 barrels of oil a day and is aiming for 10,000 by FY29–FY30. If that holds, Oilmax is more than a shell holding AESL shares, and the 53x price tag is easier to defend. It is still the company's own figure, not an audited one.

Business

What it does

AESL started as a seismic survey and oilfield services contractor. It maps underground rock for oil, gas and mineral explorers and runs oilfields for their owners. It now does three things. It provides services in India across oil and gas and minerals and coal (about ₹149 cr of Q1 revenue). It does offshore drilling in the UAE through Kuiper, 100% owned since August 2025. And it owns a small amount of production: a 50% stake in the Indrora block in Gujarat, where management expects revenue to start building in FY27. After the merger it also takes on Oilmax's producing fields, including Mewad, which is targeted at about 1,000 barrels a day.

Moat

Thin. Seismic and field services are won by tender, often from state-owned companies, so price matters a lot. What AESL does have is a record of delivering, plus the pre-qualifications that let it bid at all. The merger and Indrora are attempts to move into owning production, which earns much more. Right now that is a plan, not a proven business.

Order book

₹1,754 cr standalone at 30 June 2026, according to the company. That is about 2.2 years of FY26 revenue and excludes Kuiper. The split is about 60% oil and gas services and 40% minerals and other energy. It includes the ₹187.6 cr GSECL Ukai order. Management says bids worth ₹3,000–4,000 cr are in progress. The order book has barely changed since March (about ₹1,750 cr), so new wins are roughly replacing work done.

Risks

The merger price. Minority holders are paying about ₹2,582 cr for the promoter's holding company, roughly 53x Oilmax's FY25 earnings, and taking about 46% dilution. The stock is now 25% higher than when we last looked, so less of that is priced in. The production numbers that would justify it (2,500 barrels a day now, 10,000 targeted) come from management and haven't been independently checked.
  • Falling margin. 14% in FY24 and FY25, 12% in FY26, 11% over the last twelve months, and 8% in the latest quarter. Growth is coming from lower-margin work.
  • Slow collections. Customers take 160 days to pay, receivables have risen 2.6x in two years, and free cash flow is still negative.
  • Middle East exposure. Kuiper was disrupted by the regional situation until June. It is now 45% of revenue.
  • Management changes: three senior managers resigned in late August, just as the company is about to double in complexity.
  • No institutional owners, no broker coverage. Nobody outside is checking the numbers, and a 42% public float can move the price sharply both ways. The stock has doubled from its 52-week low.
  • Oil price. Once it owns production, revenue moves with crude prices, not just with customers' spending.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the merger closes and the published combined numbers leave the stock at or below about 30x post-merger earnings. Also needed: Oilmax production is confirmed in a filing (not just on an earnings call) and heading toward the 10,000 barrel target, and group margin gets back above 12% as Kuiper recovers.
  • Downgrade to Sell: the combined company turns out more expensive than AESL alone, margin stays at or below 8% through the September and December quarters, or more senior people leave.
