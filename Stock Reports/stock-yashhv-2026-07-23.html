Verdict

One of the better niche businesses in this library, with a capability upgrade that genuinely deepens the moat — and a price ~29% above what informed money just paid. Yash makes transformer bushings, the high-voltage insulation components without which a power transformer cannot operate. FY26 was excellent and is now audited: revenue ₹235.2 cr (+57%), EBITDA ₹60.4 cr (+75%) at a 25.7% margin (+260 bps), PAT ₹37.3 cr (+75%), with an order book around ₹400 cr (~1.7x revenue). Margins of that order in electrical equipment tell you this is a specification-driven product, not a commodity.

The strategic development is the ₹151 cr preferential issue approved 15 July (12,62,131 shares plus 8,32,177 warrants to non-promoters, 99.99% shareholder approval). It funds an extension of RIP bushing manufacture from the planned 245 kV range to 550 kV — a genuinely harder segment with very few global players — plus testing infrastructure, with trial production targeted in Q2/H1 FY27. That is the clearest upgrade catalyst in the name.

What holds it at Watch is arithmetic. At ₹932 the stock trades around 70x+ trailing earnings, and no broker formally covers it — so there is no analyst anchor at all. What there is, is better: incoming institutional investors negotiated in at ₹721 a few weeks ago. Paying a 29% premium to the most recent informed price, ahead of a plant that has not yet produced, is not the risk-reward this business deserves. ₹721 is your reference.

Business & the 550 kV step-up

Transformer bushings are the insulated conductors that carry current through a transformer's earthed tank wall — a small share of transformer cost but a total point of failure, which is why utilities specify tightly and switch suppliers reluctantly. Yash is India's leading domestic player with a growing export presence. The technology axis is RIP (resin-impregnated paper) bushings, the modern dry-type standard replacing oil-impregnated designs.

Why 550 kV matters. Moving from a planned 245 kV range up to 550 kV is not incremental capacity — it is entry into extra-high-voltage, where the qualification bar, failure consequences and pricing are all materially higher, and the global supplier list is short. It aligns directly with India's inter-state transmission build-out (the same grid capex driving Transrail and Laser Power in this library). Trial production is targeted for Q2/H1 FY27 — that timeline, and the yield achieved on first production, is the thing to watch.

Bull case

  • Specification moat with margin proof: 25.7% EBITDA margin, expanding 260 bps, on 57% revenue growth — pricing power, not volume-chasing.
  • 550 kV capability opens a higher-value segment with few credible suppliers, funded and underway rather than aspirational.
  • Grid-capex tailwind: every new transmission line and transformer needs bushings; India's build-out is multi-year and policy-backed.
  • Institutional validation: non-promoter investors committed ₹151 cr at ₹721 with 99.99% shareholder approval — real money, recent, at a struck price.
  • Order book ~1.7x revenue gives visibility unusual for a company this size.

Risks

~70x with no analyst anchor, 29% above the last informed price. There is no published broker target to sanity-check the valuation, and the one hard reference point available — the ₹721 preferential price — sits well below the market. That gap is the entire argument for waiting.
  • Execution risk on 550 kV: extra-high-voltage qualification is difficult; trial production slipping, or poor first-pass yields, would undercut the core upgrade thesis.
  • Dilution: ~20.9 lakh new securities (shares + warrants) issued below market; warrant conversion adds further supply over time.
  • Customer concentration among transformer OEMs and utilities; order timing is lumpy and tender-driven.
  • Commodity and import exposure: resin, paper and copper input costs, plus competition from established global bushing makers in the EHV segment it is entering.
  • No coverage, thin scrutiny: price discovery is retail-driven; figures come from company releases and aggregators.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: price into the ₹720–800 zone (i.e. at or near where institutions bought), or confirmed successful 550 kV trial production with initial EHV orders — that would justify the premium rather than merely hoping for it.
  • Downgrade to Watch-only/Avoid: trial production slipping materially beyond H1 FY27, margin compression below ~22%, or order book stalling.

Near-term calendar

Board met 3 Aug 2026 to consider a final dividend for FY26. Q1 FY27 results not yet published; the trial-production milestone is the more important event.
