A niche leader in transformer bushings — an unglamorous but critical component riding India's power-transmission and transformer capex super-cycle. Real, high-quality growth (revenue ₹108 → ₹235 Cr in two years, PAT tripling) and strong returns, but the market has already paid up handsomely: ~71x earnings and ~15x book on a ₹2,761 Cr cap after a ~6.6x run from its Dec-2024 IPO. The question isn't whether the business is good — it's whether anything short of perfection justifies the price.

02Business & Qualitative

Model. Yash manufactures and distributes high-voltage transformer bushings — the insulated feed-throughs that carry current into/out of a transformer — plus related insulation materials. Product range spans OIP (oil-impregnated-paper) condenser bushings, RIP/RIS (resin-impregnated) condenser bushings, high-voltage & high-current bushings, OIP wall and oil-to-oil bushings, and FRP/fibre-glass cylinders & guard rings. Single manufacturing base at Savli, Dist. Vadodara, Gujarat, with installed capacity of ~2,500 OIP, ~500 high-current, ~1,500 RIP bushings and ~1,200 FRP cylinders/guard rings p.a.

Moat. Narrow but real. Bushings are a highly engineered, reliability-critical part where failure is catastrophic for a transformer, so OEM/utility qualification, long approval cycles and a track record create genuine switching friction and import-substitution pull (India historically imported high-end bushings). It's a small, specialised niche — the flip side of the moat is a small addressable market and dependence on transformer-OEM and utility capex cycles.

05Valuation

At ₹962 the stock trades at ~71x trailing earnings and ~14.8x book with a negligible yield — a rich, priced-for-continued-hypergrowth multiple. From a Dec-2024 IPO around ₹146 the stock has compounded ~6.6x in ~18 months, so the market has already extrapolated the growth aggressively. Even crediting ~40%+ earnings growth, a 71x multiple demands that pace persist for years without a capex-cycle pause or margin give-back. The business quality is high; the price carries essentially no margin of safety, and small industrials on 70x+ can de-rate sharply when growth merely normalises to "good."
