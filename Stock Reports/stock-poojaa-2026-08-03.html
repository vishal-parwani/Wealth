Verdict

A genuinely good business that was priced cheaply at issue — and by the time you can buy it, probably won't be. Poojaa (formerly Pooja Castings, incorporated 1992) makes aluminium die-castings and precision-machined components across gravity, low-pressure and high-pressure die casting, with 600+ SKUs going into engines, drivetrains, EV powertrains and electrical systems. FY26 was strong and clean: revenue ₹295.2 cr (+32%), PAT ₹30.9 cr (+29%), RoNW 23.2%. At the ₹301 issue price that was just 19.4x earnings and 3.2x book — genuinely inexpensive for that growth and return profile, and materially cheaper than OBSC Perfection (~56x) elsewhere in this library.

The problem is arithmetic. The IPO was subscribed 278.8x and the grey market was quoting a ₹205 premium (+68%) on 3 Aug, implying a listing around ₹500. At that level the multiple is roughly 33x and the market cap ~₹1,000 cr — no longer a bargain, merely reasonable, on a BSE SME line with all the liquidity constraints that carries. The smart-money signal is real and worth respecting: Abakkus Venture Opportunities Fund took 31.02% of the ₹45.14 cr anchor book, the single largest allocation, with Motilal Oswal Finvest at 12.19%. But anchors bought at ₹301, not ₹500. Let the listing-day froth clear and judge it on where it settles.

Business

Precision engineering across two linked capabilities: aluminium die-casting (gravity, low-pressure and high-pressure) and precision machining, plus assembly and engineering services — a full "concept to component" offering rather than job-shop machining alone. End applications span engines, drivetrains, EV powertrains and electrical systems, across 600+ SKUs.

  • Customers: OEMs and Tier-1 suppliers, including international accounts in Germany, the US, Italy and Switzerland. Count grew from 39 (FY24) to 58 (FY26), with 25 repeat customers.
  • New vertical: recently entered aerospace via approved-vendor partnerships — early, but the qualification barrier there is high and durable if it converts.
  • Mix: ~72% of revenue is automotive.
  • Promoters: the Kulkarni family (Anil, Jayshree, Sanket), the Agrawal family (Dakshendra, Vaishali, Bhavya), Rahul Ranka and Bhavya Financial Services.
The Abakkus link. Abakkus Venture Opportunities Fund took 31.02% of the ₹45.14 cr anchor book — the largest single allocation. Sunil Singhania's house is already the anchor investor behind six names in this library (Dynamatic, SPR Auto, Carysil, Technocraft, Ion Exchange, Mrs. Bectors). Abakkus backing a small die-caster at 19x is consistent with its bottom-up small-cap style, and is the strongest third-party validation available here given there is no analyst coverage. Bull case

  • Cheap at issue for the quality: 19.4x with 23.2% RoNW and 32% revenue growth is a rare combination — most comparable SME precision names in this library trade at 55x+.
  • EV-relevant, not EV-dependent: aluminium die-casting benefits structurally from vehicle lightweighting and EV battery/powertrain housings, while the ICE book keeps paying today. Powertrain-agnostic in the same way SPR Auto is.
  • Export and customer diversification improving: 39 → 58 customers, with European and US accounts that impose higher quality bars and better pricing than domestic tier-2 work.
  • Aerospace optionality — approved-vendor status is slow to earn and sticky once held.
  • Capex-funded growth: ₹106 cr of the fresh issue goes to capacity, so the money is being raised to expand rather than to cash out promoters (it is a fresh issue, not an OFS).

Risks

Customer concentration is the headline risk. The top five customers were 74.7% of FY26 revenue (₹219.6 cr of ₹295.2 cr). It is improving — 84.4% in FY24 — but three-quarters of revenue in five relationships means losing one is an earnings event, not a wobble.
  • Auto-cycle exposure: ~72% automotive revenue ties the P&L to Indian vehicle production; a slowdown, chip shortage or policy shift hits orders directly.
  • Aluminium price risk: die-casting margins are sensitive to input costs and the lag in passing them through — note margins held flat rather than expanding in FY26.
  • Legal proceedings: the RHP discloses matters involving the company, directors and promoters. Read that section before sizing — adverse outcomes are a stated risk.
  • BSE SME platform: lot-size trading, thin liquidity, no analyst coverage, and exits in a drawdown are genuinely hard.
  • 278x subscription is froth, not a fundamental. Extreme SME oversubscription reliably produces a listing pop and just as reliably produces a subsequent fade; the two are the same phenomenon.
  • IP/know-how: the RHP flags counterfeit-product and technical know-how leakage risks.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: the stock settling toward ₹350–420 post-listing (roughly 23–27x FY26) with the order profile intact — that keeps a real discount to SME precision peers. Also: top-5 concentration falling below ~65%, or aerospace converting to disclosed revenue.
  • Stay away / Avoid: chasing a listing above ~₹520 (33x+ on a 29%-PAT-growth SME), any major customer loss, or margin compression as aluminium moves.
