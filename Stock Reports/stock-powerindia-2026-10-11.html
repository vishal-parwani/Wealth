Verdict

The business is everything the bull case says, and the price already pays for it. Hold. Revenue has grown from ₹5,237 cr in FY24 to ₹9,162 cr on a trailing basis. Operating margin has gone from 7% to 16%. Net profit has gone from ₹164 cr to ₹1,150 cr. The order backlog reached a record ₹32,222 cr in June 2026, about 3.5 times trailing revenue. The balance sheet carries about ₹4,600 cr of net cash. None of this is in dispute.

What is in dispute is the multiple. At ₹30,605 the stock trades at 115x trailing earnings and 88x consensus FY27 earnings. Even FY28 earnings, which assume another 48% growth on top of FY27's 57%, leave it at 59x. Its two closest listed rivals, GE Vernova T&D and Siemens Energy India, trade at 80x and 72x. They earn 24–25% operating margins against Hitachi's 15%. Hitachi is the most expensive of the three and the least profitable per rupee of sales.

Where the name came from. It came from a Jefferies "India Industrial Revolution" call reported by NDTV Profit in September 2026, which rated it a Buy as a beneficiary of data-centre and transmission capex. The demand half of that claim checks out. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 69% and profit 123%. Order intake excluding HVDC rose 26%, and the backlog is at a record. The data-centre half cannot be tested: the company does not report data-centre revenue or orders separately. Its Q1 order commentary names HVDC, grid connection, power quality and transformers. The reported Jefferies target of ₹43,145 implies 41% upside. The 20-analyst consensus is ₹37,240, which implies 22%. Prabhudas Lilladhar is at ₹30,768, which is the current price. The stock has fallen 21% from its ₹38,800 high, so the tweet is right that this is a dip. It is a dip from a level the earnings had not yet caught up to.

Counter-argument. If consensus FY28 EPS of ₹516 is delivered and the stock holds a 70x multiple, it is worth about ₹36,000. That is 18% higher over roughly 18 months, not enough to compensate for the execution risk on HVDC timing. HVDC orders also convert slowly: most HVDC sales in the sector land only from FY29. A Buy needs either a lower price, about ₹25,000 or 50x FY28, or margin closing towards the peer level, which would lift the earnings base.

Hitachi Energy Ltd (Switzerland)
Hitachi Energy is the Hitachi group's grid business, formerly ABB Power Grids, which Hitachi took over in stages from 2020. The Indian listed company was carved out of ABB India in 2019. The parent supplies technology, global HVDC know-how and export channels. A parent at 71% means minority holders ride on its capital-allocation choices, including technology and royalty flows between parent and subsidiary.
Managing Director & CEO
N Venu
Has led the India business since its ABB Power Grids days and is also the group's regional head. Since June 2025 he has additionally been Managing Director of Hitachi India, the group's wider Indian holding. That is a vote of confidence from the parent, and a wider span of attention.

  • Control: a 71% foreign parent with a single, stable stake. There have been no promoter sales, no pledge and no change in holding across the five quarters shown.
  • Notable: related-party transactions with the Hitachi Energy group, covering imported components, technology licences and export orders routed through group companies, are the governance item to watch at an MNC subsidiary. They were not quantified for this note.

Business

Nature of business

The company makes and installs transmission equipment for utilities, industry and railways. Its range covers power and traction transformers, HVDC converter stations, switchgear and GIS substations, power-quality equipment such as STATCOMs and capacitors, grid automation and, from this year, battery storage. Customers include Power Grid and state transmission companies, renewable-energy developers who need grid connection, railways and metros, data-centre operators, and export customers through the parent group. Exports were 34% of non-HVDC orders in Q1 FY27. HVDC links, the long-distance high-voltage lines that carry renewable power from Rajasthan and Gujarat to load centres, bring the largest and lumpiest orders.

Among the three pure-play transmission MNCs, Hitachi has the highest P/E and the lowest margin and returns. Its returns are diluted by the large cash pile, which flatters the other two by comparison. Even so, the gap on operating margin, 15% against 24–25%, is a business gap, not an accounting one. The bull case is that Hitachi's margin catches up as HVDC and export work scales. The bear case is that the market is already paying for that catch-up.

Moat

Real, and among the strongest in Indian capital goods. HVDC converter technology is held by a handful of companies worldwide, of which Hitachi Energy is the largest. Qualification with Power Grid and the transmission companies is slow and track-record driven. The installed base generates service and replacement work. The limits are customer pricing power, since utility contracts are tendered, and a shared supply chain for transformer components that every competitor is now expanding into.

Order book

₹32,222 cr unexecuted at 30 Jun 2026, ≈ 3.5x trailing revenue: a record, and a confirmed backlog rather than a pipeline. A large part is HVDC, which executes over four to five years, so it gives visibility more than near-term revenue. Q1 FY27 intake was ₹5,097 cr. The company has committed about ₹2,000 cr to a greenfield large-power and HVDC converter-transformer plant at Karjan, Vadodara, targeted for Q4 CY28, and indicates a further ₹4,000 cr from FY30.

Risks

De-rating, not disappointment, is the main risk. The company can deliver consensus and the stock can still fall. At 59x FY28 earnings, a move to the 72–80x trailing multiple of its better-margined peers is already assumed one year out. If the market instead paid 50x FY28, the stock would be about ₹25,800, 16% below today.
  • Margin has stalled at 15%. Two quarters below the December peak while revenue accelerates. If execution-heavy HVDC work dilutes margin, earnings growth trails revenue growth.
  • HVDC lumpiness. Order intake fell 55% year on year in Q1 because last year had a large HVDC award. Headline inflow will swing, and the market reads inflow quarterly.
  • Capex cycle ahead. About ₹2,000 cr committed to Karjan, plus about ₹4,000 cr indicated from FY30. The cash pile funds it, but returns on equity will stay diluted until the plants fill.
  • Treasury income in the earnings. About 15% of trailing PBT is other income on QIP cash. It shrinks as the cash is spent on capex.
  • Parent-subsidiary flows. Technology fees and group sourcing are a standing minority-shareholder risk at a 71%-owned MNC subsidiary.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the price falls to about ₹25,000, 50x FY28 consensus, with the backlog intact; or two consecutive quarters of operating margin at 18% or more, which would put earnings ahead of consensus and justify the premium.
  • Downgrade to Sell: backlog falls for two quarters running, or operating margin drops back to 12% or below, while the multiple stays above 80x FY27 earnings.
