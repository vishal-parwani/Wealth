Verdict

Everything about the business is working and the share price has run past every published estimate of its value. Fourteen analysts average ₹619 against a market price of ₹818 — the stock trades 32% above consensus, and even the most bullish target on the street, ₹720, is 12% below where it is now. Nine of those fourteen still rate it Buy. That combination means one thing: the targets are stale relative to a stock that doubled from ₹402 in a year, and nobody has yet published a number that justifies today's price.

The operating case is genuinely strong. Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹1,310 cr was up 54%, battery-EV revenue nearly doubled to ₹436 cr and is now 44% of automotive sales, and the net order book stands at ₹24,000 cr with 64% of it electric. The company has also opened a robotics and physical-AI vertical with an ₹800 cr order book of its own — small, but it is the kind of optionality that supports a 70x multiple in a bull market.

The crack in it is margin. Operating margin has gone 28% → 27% → 24% over three years and 23% in the June quarter, down 144 basis points year on year. Revenue grew 54%; operating profit did not grow sequentially at all — ₹296 cr in March, ₹293 cr in June. Growth is being bought with mix and with price. At 70x trailing earnings and 8.5x book, you are paying a premium multiple for a business whose margin is going the wrong way.

Balance sheet is pristine — ₹94 cr net debt on ₹5,983 cr of equity — and promoters have not sold a share. This is not a governance problem or a leverage problem. It is a price problem.

Promoter holding has not moved in five quarters — 28.02% to 28.01%, which is rounding, not selling. That is a real positive and it separates this name from most of its peer group this year. The rotation is foreign to domestic: FIIs handed over 6.14 points in the September 2025 quarter and have drifted lower since; domestic funds took all of it and have kept adding. Retail is shrinking — 4.05 lakh shareholders down to 3.64 lakh — so the float is consolidating into institutional hands.

Sona Comstar is controlled by Singapore VII Topco III Pte (Blackstone-origin holding) alongside the Sona group; the promoter block at 28.01% is below what most Indian mid caps carry, and the register is genuinely institution-led. With 35% of the company in mutual funds and a further 24% with FIIs, this is a professionally owned company rather than a family business — which cuts both ways: better disclosure, but no anchor holder with a decade-long horizon.

Business

What it does

Sona makes differential assemblies, differential gears, conventional and micro-hybrid starter motors, and — the part that matters — traction motors and driveline systems for battery electric vehicles. It supplies global OEMs across North America, Europe, India and China. The company's distinguishing feature among Indian auto suppliers is that electrification is an opportunity rather than a threat: 44% of automotive sales are already battery-EV, and 64% of the order book is.

Moat

Genuine, and unusually well-placed. Driveline components are designed into a vehicle platform years ahead of production and validated by the OEM; once nominated, the supplier holds that programme for its life. Sona's EV order book is that moat expressed as a number — ₹24,000 cr of nominated business that competitors cannot bid for again. The risk is not displacement, it is pricing: OEMs know these are multi-year captive relationships and negotiate accordingly, which is visible in the margin line.

Order book

₹24,000 cr net order book as at Jul 2026, of which 64% is battery-electric — about 4.9x trailing annual revenue. A further ₹800 cr sits in the new robotics vertical. These are nominated programmes rather than firm purchase orders, so the timing depends on OEM volumes, but the visibility is real and it is the strongest such book in the Indian component space.

Risks

The share price is 32% above consensus and margin is falling. Fourteen analysts, top target ₹720, market ₹818. Meanwhile operating margin has gone 28% → 23% and operating profit has been flat for three quarters on rising revenue. If the market re-rates to even the most bullish published target, that is a 12% fall from here.
  • Growth is not reaching the profit line. Revenue +54% year on year; operating profit flat sequentially. Mix shift to EV appears to come at a lower margin.
  • Customer and geography concentration. North American and European EV programmes drive the order book; a slowdown in Western EV adoption, or a tariff regime, hits directly.
  • Everyone already owns it. 230 mutual fund schemes hold 35% of the company and FIIs a further 24%. Widely held mid caps fall fastest when the crowd turns.
  • The robotics vertical is a narrative, not yet a business — ₹800 cr of order book against ₹24,000 cr, launched this quarter.
  • No promoter anchor. 28% promoter holding in a financial-sponsor structure means no family with a twenty-year view underneath the stock.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: operating margin stabilising at 23–24% for two consecutive quarters with revenue still compounding — proving the EV mix is not structurally dilutive — or a pullback towards ₹620, where the price meets consensus.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin through 22%, a cut to the ₹24,000 cr order book, or evidence that Western EV programme volumes are being deferred.
