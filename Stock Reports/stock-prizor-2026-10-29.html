Verdict

Hold. Don't buy at ₹1,335, and don't buy the day before the 30 Oct half-year print. The growth is real and has a policy cause. Since April 2025, CCTV cameras sold in India need STQC-tested, BIS-ER registered designs. That shut out much of the Chinese supply and handed volume to compliant Indian brands. Prizor's revenue went from ₹71 cr in FY25 to ₹148 cr in FY26 (+108%). H2 FY26 alone was ₹105.9 cr, up 164%, with EBITDA margin at 22.3%. On 24 Oct it raised ₹119.45 cr at ₹876 from investors including Ashish Kacholia, Motilal Oswal Financial Services and Alchemy. That clears its ₹41.7 cr of debt and funds working capital. The price is the problem. Even if you annualise H2 FY26, the best half on record, the stock sits at 55x earnings, or 59x on a fully diluted basisd. Cash conversion is close to zero, and governance has several soft spots.

Why not Buy. (1) Valuation. Trailing P/E is 78x on the 1.216 cr shares now in issue, which is in line with or above much larger and more established names (table below). The stock has risen 3.9x in 12 months (₹345 → ₹1,335) and sits 52% above the price paid by the October investors. (2) Cash. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹0.3 cr against EBITDA of ₹33.2 cr. FY25 was −₹14.1 cr. Inventory plus receivables of ₹88.6 cr equals 60% of FY26 salesd. Profits are being reinvested in working capital as fast as they are booked. (3) What it makes. As recently as FY25, ₹62.8 cr of purchases were finished goods bought in for resale. By the company's own description it was an "import-and-assemble" business. In FY26 it moved to an in-house SMT and assembly line. Chips, sensors and most components are still bought in, and the TVs and monitors are made by third parties. (4) Governance. Promoters took 9.55 lakh warrants at ₹291 in Dec 2025, when the stock was at its 52-week low; they are now worth 4.6x that. A sitting independent director also subscribed, and was later redesignated non-independent. The subsidiary was diluted from 51% to 5% through an issue to undisclosed parties. The CFO is the CMD's husband. Details below. (5) Price action. Much of the rally came in chains of consecutive limit-up days on ₹2 cr of daily turnover. There is no mutual fund holder. The stock fell 15% in one session on 21 Oct with no filing to explain it.

On "drones" and "government tenders". Nothing in the filings, the FY26 annual report or the April 2026 investor deck mentions drones. This is a CCTV camera, NVR/DVR and display business. Government demand is a stated aim: the company registered as a GeM OEM for CCTV cameras on 22 Apr 2026. But sales go through about 11,000 dealers and distributors, and the company has disclosed no order book and no tender wins. Treat it as a channel-driven consumer and commercial electronics brand, not a defence or homeland-security contractor.

For a buy decision. Wait for H1 FY27 tomorrow. Buy only if two things hold. First, H1 revenue is at least ₹120 cr (more than 2.8x H1 FY26's ₹42 cr, and above H2 FY26), with PAT margin of at least 13% and positive half-year operating cash flow. Second, the price is at or below about ₹900, which is about 37x annualised H2 FY26 EPSd and close to the ₹876 the October investors paid. Size it as a high-risk position. A holder can stay in until the print. The tailwind is real, but there is little margin of safety.

Business

Nature of business

Prizor sells analog and IP CCTV cameras, 4G and solar standalone cameras, PTZ cameras and NVRs/DVRs under the Prizor brand. It also sells LED TVs, monitors and touch panels made by third parties. Sales go through about 11,000 dealers and installers in 21 states, strongest in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, J&K and the Andamans. What it actually makes: the Gandhinagar plant has an SMT line (50 lakh PCBs a year), three assembly lines (24 lakh cameras a year since Jul 2026) and housing moulds. The company itself describes its history as "import and assemble". In FY25, 92% of purchases by value were finished goods for resaled (₹62.8 cr of ₹68.4 cr). The SoC (the vision chip) is still bought in; a Dec 2025 MoU with IndieSemic aims to co-develop an Indian one, but nothing has been delivered yet. So the honest description is: PCB assembly and final assembly of cameras built from bought-in chips, sensors and lenses, with BIS-ER/STQC compliance as the key credential. Compliance is a real barrier now that the rules exclude many Chinese designs.

One possible reason is that FY26 rode a scarcity premium while compliant supply was short.

Moat

Thin but real for now. BIS-ER/STQC registration is model by model and takes months, and Prizor has 12+ registered models plus GeM OEM status. That matters while compliant capacity is scarce. Against that: CP Plus, Hikvision's Indian-assembled lines, Sparsh, Matrix, Godrej and others are all certifying too. The product is a commodity once supply normalises, and Prizor does not own the chip.

Order book

None disclosed. This is a channel-sales business with no order-book disclosure and no announced government or defence tender wins. GeM OEM registration (22 Apr 2026) makes public procurement possible but has produced no disclosed orders. Revenue visibility rests on dealer sell-through.

Risks

  • Results tomorrow. H1 FY27 is due on 30 Oct. At 78x trailing earnings, a half that merely matches H2 FY26's ₹106 cr would disappoint.
  • The tailwind fades. The growth spike coincides with the 2025 STQC rules. As CP Plus and others add compliant capacity, the scarcity premium in pricing and margin can reverse.
  • Working capital and cash. CFO was negative or flat in three straight years, with 204-day cash conversion. Growth is equity-funded.
  • Governance. Promoter warrants priced at the low, an independent director in the warrant round, an ex-subsidiary diluted to undisclosed parties, an undisclosed ₹10 cr advance, and a related CFO.
  • Supply. 13.6 lakh October shares sit 52% in profit at ₹876. Non-promoter preferential shares unlock about six months after trading approval (≈ Apr–May 2027d). The ₹291 warrants (4.6x in profit) convert by about Jun 2027.
  • Thin market. SME board, ₹2.2 cr of daily turnover, no mutual fund holder, circuit-driven price moves, and a 15% one-day fall on no news.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 revenue of at least ₹120 cr with PAT margin of at least 13% and positive half-year operating cash flow, and a price at or below about ₹900 (≈37x annualised H2 FY26 EPSd). Also wanted: disclosure of who now owns Prizor Aitech and who received the ₹10 cr advance, with nothing adverse found.
  • Downgrade to Sell: H1 FY27 revenue below ₹85 cr (a 20% fall from H2 FY26, implying the STQC spike was one-off), or EBITDA margin below 17%. Also: debtor days above 120, any promoter selling or pledge, or a related-party transaction surfacing with the ex-subsidiary or Om Security Solutions.
