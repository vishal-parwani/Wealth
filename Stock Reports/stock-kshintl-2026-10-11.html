Verdict

The business is a real beneficiary of the transformer cycle, but at 52x trailing and 33x Ventura's FY27 estimate the price already pays for that growth, while the cash flow says it is not yet a high-quality compounder. Hold.

The evidence for. FY26 revenue was ₹3,107 cr, up 61%, and profit was ₹110 cr, up 62%. Q1 FY27 sales volume was 7,969 tonnes, up 30%. EBITDA per tonne rose from ₹65,885 to ₹93,325 as continuously transposed conductor (CTC, the specialised wire used in large power transformers) took a larger share. Specialised wire is now about 75% of revenue. Exports grew 76%. The company has signed a five-year framework agreement with a global transformer maker. Ventura's note names it as Hitachi Energy; the company's own press release does not. Capacity goes from 43,445 tonnes to 59,045 tonnes by March 2027, and the company commissioned its own copper-rod (upcast) line on 4 August. ROCE is 21%.

The evidence against. This is a copper converter earning a 6% EBITDA margin. The 108% revenue jump in Q1 is mostly copper prices passed through to customers. Volume grew 30%. Operating cash flow was negative in FY24, FY25 and FY26: −₹17 cr, −₹9 cr and −₹65 cr. Free cash flow was −₹228 cr in FY26. Debt went from ₹321 cr in March to ₹481 cr in June, almost all of it working-capital loans. Management guides EBITDA per tonne back to about ₹75,000 for FY27, so Q1's ₹93,325 is not the run-rate. Mutual funds were net sellers in August by 12.7 lakh shares. Kotak's multi-asset fund sold its whole position, and Kotak's infra and manufacturing funds each cut a third or more.

Testing the tweet. On 20 Sep 2026 @TheAlpha10X put KSH on a 63-stock list of "High Growth + High Quality" names to buy on a dip. The user saved it to the knowledge base. High growth holds up: revenue rose 61% in FY26 and volume 30% in Q1. High quality only half holds up. ROCE of 21% and ROE of 20% are respectable, but three straight years of negative operating cash flow, a 6% margin and rising working-capital debt are not what quality usually means. The dip is real but small. The stock is ₹1,001.50, 16% below its ₹1,198 high and 4% below ₹1,046.90 on the last close before the tweet (18 Sep). It is still 2.6x the ₹384 issue price.

Business

Nature of business

KSH makes magnet winding wire, the insulated copper and aluminium conductor wound into transformers, motors and generators. Specialised wire is about 75% of revenue. That includes CTC for power transformers, paper-insulated and fibreglass-covered conductors. Standard enamelled wire for motors and appliances makes up the rest, along with other operating income. Exports were about 27% of revenue in Q1 FY27. KSH is India's third-largest maker by capacity and the largest exporter. Plants are in Chakan and Supa in Maharashtra. Copper is passed through to customers, so the company earns a conversion margin per tonne rather than a percentage of the metal.

Moat

Narrow. Large transformer makers take a long time to qualify a CTC supplier, and those approvals are sticky, as the five-year global framework shows. That is a real switching cost at the specialised end. Standard enamelled wire is close to a commodity. Customers hold pricing power, and a 6% margin leaves little room for error if copper swings or a big buyer delays.

Order book

No order-book figure is disclosed. Sales are made against customer schedules and framework agreements, so capacity and utilisation (73% in Q1) are the better forward indicators.

Risks

Working capital outrunning profit. Growth is being financed with debt: borrowings rose ₹160 cr in one quarter to ₹481 cr, and operating cash flow has been negative three years running. If copper keeps rising or a large OEM pays late, more debt is needed before profit arrives. At 52x, the market will not forgive a quarter of negative surprise.
  • Copper. It inflates and deflates reported revenue. A sharp fall would make headline growth look weak even if volumes hold.
  • Mix normalisation. Management itself guides EBITDA per tonne back to about ₹75,000 from Q1's ₹93,325.
  • Customer concentration and OEM bottlenecks. Management flagged delays at some transformer customers' own capacity expansions.
  • Fund selling. Net −12.7 lakh shares in August, as post-IPO holders exit.
  • Short public record. Ten months listed, and only three shareholding disclosures.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a full half-year of positive operating cash flow with debt flat or falling while volume growth stays above 25%. Alternatively, a price below about ₹750, roughly 25x FY27E.
  • Downgrade to Sell: EBITDA per tonne below ₹70,000 for two quarters, debt-to-equity above 0.8x, or Supa Phase 2 slipping past March 2027.
