Verdict

Sell is maintained, still high risk. Q2 met the revenue and margin parts of the upgrade trigger, but not the order-book part, and the quality of the quarter is weaker than the headline. Consolidated Q2 FY27 revenue was ₹75.3 cr, up 56% from ₹48.4 cr a year ago and up 31% from ₹57.6 cr in Q1. EBITDA margin was 26.8%d and PAT was ₹15.3 cr, up 72%. That answers the 29 Oct worry that revenue had stalled. The trigger, however, required all three conditions, and the third, a disclosed order book above ₹200 cr, is still missing: the results filing gives no backlog and no presentation has been filed yet. Two new facts also argue against reading this as a clean beat. Trade receivables went from ₹34.7 cr in March to ₹88.9 cr in September, about 122 days of H1 salesd, and standalone operating cash flow for H1 was −₹5.2 cr, against +₹86 cr for FY26. Purchases of traded goods also rose to ₹12.2 cr in the quarter, 16% of revenue, from ₹1.9 cr a year ago.

What changed since 29 Oct 2026. The rating is unchanged at Sell. The price basis moves from ₹1,151.20 (intraday 29 Oct) to ₹1,150.30, the 29 Oct close. Results came out after the market closed. On 30 Oct the stock opened at ₹1,190, reached ₹1,282.80 (+11.5%) and was at ₹1,229 (+6.8%) at about 10:00 IST. About 4.7 lakh shares traded in the first 45 minutes, nearly three times the 6-month daily average. New facts: (1) Q2 and H1 FY27 results, summarised above; (2) an interim dividend of ₹0.75 per share, up from ₹0.50 last year, with a record date of 5 Nov; (3) a new company secretary, Chandni Shah, filling the post left vacant on 14 Aug, and a new independent director, Mitanshu Shah, a former CFO of Arvind SmartSpaces, both from 1 Nov; (4) no order-book update; (5) the earnings call is scheduled for 3:00 pm today, after this note's data cut, so no transcript or summary exists yet. TTM EPS rises from ₹28.99 to ₹33.29, so the multiple at the close falls from 39.7x to 34.6x.

For a buy/hold decision: don't buy into today's jump; holders who stayed can wait for this afternoon's call before trimming. This is closer to Hold than it was yesterday. Revenue growth is back, margin held at a record, and the board appointments fix a governance gap. The case for still rating it Sell has three parts. First, the order book: Q2 revenue of ₹75 cr was executed from a disclosed backlog of ₹117 cr at June plus a ₹20 cr MoD order. Unless the company booked orders it did not file, the September backlog is smaller than June's, not larger. Second, cash: the growth was billed but not collected, and the company drew ₹3.8 cr of short-term debt in H1. Third, the promoters sold at ₹1,203. Today's intraday price of ₹1,229 is above that and equals about 37x TTM EPSd.

The call decides the next move. If management discloses a September order book of ₹200 cr or more and explains the receivables as timing (for example, shipyard milestone billing at quarter-end), the conditions for Hold are met. If the backlog is near ₹100–130 cr, the Q2 surge looks like execution of existing orders pulled forward, and Sell stands.

Business

Nature of business

The core business is special-alloy steel sections used as hull stiffeners in naval warships, along with ballast bricks, welding consumables, armoured-steel profiles and high-altitude heaters. Customers include the Indian Navy, Army, DRDO and the shipyards: Mazagon, Garden Reach, Cochin, Goa, Hindustan Shipyard and L&T. A second division makes dairy equipment at Kalol. RF-over-fibre, composite doors and hatches, AUVs and smart ammunition are early, pre-revenue bets. Defence is the only reportable segment.

Moat

Narrow but real. Naval special steels need metallurgical qualification and long trial cycles. Once a section is qualified on a ship class, the yard rarely switches suppliers. The limit is scale. The addressable market is the Navy's hull programme, and orders arrive in ₹20–50 cr lumps. The rise in traded-goods purchases to 16% of Q2 revenue deserves a question on the call: traded goods carry a thinner moat than qualified steel.

Risks

Revenue grew faster than cash or the disclosed backlog. Receivables of ₹88.9 cr equal about 122 days of H1 salesd, up from 52 debtor days in FY26, and H1 operating cash flow was negative. A record quarter executed from a backlog that was ₹117 cr in June leaves the next two quarters dependent on new orders that have not been filed. At 35–37x TTM, the price assumes those orders arrive.
  • Collections. If receivables stay near ₹90 cr into December, FY27 cash flow will be weak and the ₹2 cr ECL provision may grow.
  • Further promoter selling. The stock traded above ₹1,203, the promoters' June selling price, within an hour of today's open.
  • Revenue mix. Traded goods were ₹12.2 cr in Q2, up from ₹1.9 cr a year ago. If they keep growing, margin will be harder to hold.
  • Customer concentration. The MoD and a handful of shipyards account for most of the revenue, and award timing is lumpy.
  • Retail-driven price. Shareholders rose from 4,656 to 28,320 in a year. The stock has had two 10%+ intraday swings in October.
  • Upside risk to this call. A September order book of ₹200 cr or more disclosed on today's call, with receivables explained as quarter-end milestone billing, would make Hold the right rating. Antique's ₹1,725 target is 50% above the close.
