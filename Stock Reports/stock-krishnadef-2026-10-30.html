Verdict

Sell is maintained, still high risk. The earnings call and the Q2 presentation settled the order-book question the wrong way: the unexecuted order book at 30 September was ₹109 cr, below June's ₹117 cr and well short of the ₹200 cr the Hold trigger needed. Consolidated Q2 FY27 revenue was ₹75.3 cr, up 56% from ₹48.4 cr a year ago and up 31% from ₹57.6 cr in Q1. EBITDA margin was 26.8%d and PAT was ₹15.3 cr, up 72%. That answers the 29 Oct worry that revenue had stalled. The trigger, however, required all three conditions, and the third, an order book above ₹200 cr, failed. The backlog is about 0.42x TTM revenued, roughly five months of sales, and management expects to execute most of it in Q3 with some spill-over into Q4. Its 30% growth target therefore depends on converting a ₹305 cr tender pipeline, where it puts its win rate at about 50%, within three to six months. Two new facts also argue against reading this as a clean beat. Trade receivables went from ₹34.7 cr in March to ₹88.9 cr in September, about 122 days of H1 salesd, and standalone operating cash flow for H1 was −₹5.2 cr, against +₹86 cr for FY26. Purchases of traded goods also rose to ₹12.2 cr in the quarter, 16% of revenue, from ₹1.9 cr a year ago.

What changed since 29 Oct 2026. The rating is unchanged at Sell. The price basis moves from ₹1,151.20 (intraday 29 Oct) to ₹1,150.30, the 29 Oct close. Results came out after the market closed. On 30 Oct the stock opened at ₹1,190, reached ₹1,282.80 (+11.5%) and was at ₹1,229 (+6.8%) at about 10:00 IST. About 4.7 lakh shares traded in the first 45 minutes, nearly three times the 6-month daily average. New facts: (1) Q2 and H1 FY27 results, summarised above; (2) an interim dividend of ₹0.75 per share, up from ₹0.50 last year, with a record date of 5 Nov; (3) a new company secretary, Chandni Shah, filling the post left vacant on 14 Aug, and a new independent director, Mitanshu Shah, a former CFO of Arvind SmartSpaces, both from 1 Nov; (4) TTM EPS rises from ₹28.99 to ₹33.29, so the multiple at the close falls from 39.7x to 34.6x.

For a buy/hold decision: don't buy at ₹1,214; holders can use the post-results strength to trim. The business is executing well. Revenue growth is back, margin held at a record, and the board appointments fix a governance gap. The case for Sell still has three parts. First, the order book. The call confirmed what the arithmetic suggested: Q2 was executed largely from the existing backlog. The book fell to ₹109 cr, implying about ₹67 crd of Q2 order inflow (₹109 cr − ₹117 cr + ₹75 cr revenue), against ₹75 cr billed. Management argues that orders now arrive "on demand" in smaller pieces with shorter delivery cycles, so a thin book is normal. That may be true, but it also means visibility beyond Q3 is low. Reaching 30% growth on FY26's ₹245 cr implies FY27 revenue of about ₹318 crd and an H2 of about ₹186 crd, 40% more than H1, from a ₹109 cr book plus pipeline wins not yet awarded. Second, cash. The receivables explanation is plausible, but it is unverified until the December balance sheet, and no collected amount was given. Third, the promoters sold at ₹1,203, and the 30 Oct close of ₹1,213.60 is above that, at about 36x TTM EPSd. The call did not address the sale.

What the call resolved. The 29 Oct note set the test: a September order book of ₹200 cr or more, with receivables explained as timing, would justify Hold; a backlog near ₹100–130 cr would mean the Q2 surge was existing orders pulled forward, and Sell would stand. The book came in at ₹109 cr, so Sell stands. Management gave the timing explanation for receivables, but the order-book condition failed on its own.

Business

Nature of business

The core business is special-alloy steel sections used as hull stiffeners in naval warships, along with ballast bricks, welding consumables, armoured-steel profiles and high-altitude heaters. Customers include the Indian Navy, Army, DRDO and the shipyards: Mazagon, Garden Reach, Cochin, Goa, Hindustan Shipyard and L&T. A second division makes dairy equipment at Kalol. RF-over-fibre, composite doors and hatches, AUVs and smart ammunition are early, pre-revenue bets. Defence is the only reportable segment.

Moat

Narrow but real. Naval special steels need metallurgical qualification and long trial cycles. Once a section is qualified on a ship class, the yard rarely switches suppliers. The limit is scale. The addressable market is the Navy's hull programme, and orders arrive in ₹20–50 cr lumps. The rise in traded-goods purchases to 16% of Q2 revenue was not raised on the call, and traded goods carry a thinner moat than qualified steel. On the call, management said bulb bars are 10–15% of a ship's weight and that the company expects at least a 50% share of the new naval programmes. It also said it is the only approved domestic source for commercial-ship bulb bars, where imports are barred except under export-linked advance licences. These are management's claims and have not been checked.

Order book

₹109 cr unexecuted as at 30 Sep 26 (Q2 presentation, confirmed twice on the call), against ₹117 cr at 30 June. The tender pipeline is ₹305 cr (₹289 cr in June) of bids already submitted. Management puts its win rate at about 50% and hopes for awards in three to six months. This replaces the 29 Oct estimate of ₹60–70 cr, which was derived. The actual book is higher because orders below the filing threshold arrived: implied Q2 inflow was about ₹67 crd (₹109 cr − ₹117 cr + ₹75.3 cr revenue), of which only the ₹20 cr MoD order of 1 Aug was filed. The book equals about 0.42x TTM revenued, roughly five months of sales. Management expects most of it to be executed in Q3 and the rest in Q4. It disputed that this is a decline ("last quarter was about 110", although the Q1 presentation said ₹117 cr). Its explanation is that customers now place smaller, on-demand orders with shorter delivery cycles, rather than ₹80–100 cr blocks delivered over 12–18 months, because Krishna has added capacity. One analyst noted that book-to-bill has fallen from about 1.5–2x to about 0.5x over four quarters. An earlier presentation gave ₹196 cr for March 2026 against the annual report's ₹103.4 cr; the annual report figure is used.

Risks

Revenue grew faster than cash or the backlog. Receivables of ₹88.9 cr equal about 122 days of H1 salesd, up from 52 debtor days in FY26, and H1 operating cash flow was negative. The order book shrank to ₹109 cr, about five months of sales, and management plans to execute most of it in Q3. H2 growth and FY28 therefore depend on pipeline awards that have not yet been made. At about 35–36x TTM, the price assumes those orders arrive.
  • Collections. Management calls the receivables a cut-off effect (45–60-day terms on late-quarter billing). If they are still near ₹90 cr in December, that explanation fails, FY27 cash flow will be weak and the ₹2 cr ECL provision may grow.
  • Order visibility. The book is at about 0.42x revenue, and orders now arrive in small pieces. A delay in pipeline awards, which management hopes for in three to six months, would show up as a revenue gap in Q4 FY27 or Q1 FY28.
  • Further promoter selling. The stock closed 30 Oct at ₹1,213.60, above the promoters' June selling price of ₹1,203, and the call gave no comment on the sale.
  • Revenue mix. Traded goods were ₹12.2 cr in Q2, up from ₹1.9 cr a year ago. If they keep growing, margin will be harder to hold.
  • Customer concentration. The MoD and a handful of shipyards account for most of the revenue, and award timing is lumpy.
  • Retail-driven price. Shareholders rose from 4,656 to 28,320 in a year. The stock has had two 10%+ intraday swings in October.
  • Upside risk to this call. At a 50% win rate on the ₹305 cr pipeline, awards of about ₹150 crd in the next three to six months would rebuild the book above ₹200 cr. The Vabo hatches JV and commercial-shipbuilding demand add optionality. Antique's ₹1,725 target is 50% above the 29 Oct close.
