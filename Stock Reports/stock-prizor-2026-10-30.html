Verdict

Hold. The rating has not moved. H1 FY27 kept the higher level of business but did not add to it, and cash conversion got worse. Revenue was ₹106.50 cr, up 153% on H1 FY26 (₹42.01 cr) but only 0.5% above H2 FY26 (₹105.93 cr). PAT was ₹15.34 cr, up 159% on the year and 3.3% on the half. EBITDA margin rose to 23.6%d from 22.3%. Gross margin also improved: bought-in finished goods fell to ₹2.1 cr from ₹27.8 cr in H2 FY26, so a real share of the product now comes off the company's own line. That is the good part. The bad part is cash. The half used ₹4.6 cr in operations, as receivables (+₹12.4 cr), inventory (+₹8.7 cr) and short-term loans and advances (+₹7.5 cr) absorbed all ₹25.8 cr of operating profit. Borrowings rose to ₹58.3 cr. Cash at 30 Sep was ₹0.17 cr. The ₹119.45 cr preferential money arrived on 24 Oct, after the half closed, so none of it is in these numbers.

Against the 29 Oct triggers. The Buy test needed four things: revenue of at least ₹120 cr (missed, at ₹106.5 cr), PAT margin of at least 13% (met, at 14.4%), positive half-year operating cash flow (missed, at −₹4.6 cr) and a price at or below about ₹900 (missed, at ₹1,323). The Sell test was not hit either. Revenue held well above ₹85 cr, EBITDA margin stayed well above 17%, and debtor days were about 87d, under the 120 limit. So the result is Hold, with valuation lower than before: 53x trailing and about 52x annualised H1, down from 78x trailing, because a ₹15 cr half has replaced a ₹6 cr one in the trailing figure. Risk stays high.

Why not Buy. (1) Growth has paused. It is one flat half, but this is the first since the STQC rules took effect. At 53x, the stock needs H2 FY27 to show sequential growth again. (2) Cash. Operating cash flow was −₹14.1 cr in FY25, ₹0.3 cr in FY26 and −₹4.6 cr in H1 FY27. Loans and advances to undisclosed parties now total ₹21.9 cr (₹13.3 cr long-term, ₹8.7 cr short-term), double the ₹11.2 cr at March. (3) Tax. The cash-flow statement shows ₹0.06 cr of income tax paid in the half, against a ₹5.10 cr current-tax charge. Short-term provisions rose from ₹7.05 cr, which equals FY26's whole current-tax charge, to ₹12.53 cr. Read at face value, FY26 and H1 FY27 tax were largely unpaid at 30 Sep unreconciled. (4) Disclosure. The results say nothing about the ₹10 cr advance (now ₹13.3 cr), the buyers of the Prizor Aitech stake, or related parties. (5) Price. The stock is still 51% above the ₹876 the October investors paid, with no broker or fund support.

For a buy decision. The next print is H2 FY27, due around May 2027. Buy only if H2 FY27 revenue is at least ₹125 cr (a return to sequential growth) with positive operating cash flow now that the company is funded, and the price is at or below about ₹900, which is about 36x annualised H1 FY27 EPSd and close to the issue price. Until then a holder can stay; a new buyer is paying a full price for a growth rate that has just stalled.

Business

Nature of business

Prizor sells analog and IP CCTV cameras, 4G and solar standalone cameras, PTZ cameras and NVRs/DVRs under the Prizor brand. It also sells LED TVs, monitors and touch panels made by third parties. Sales go through about 11,000 dealers and installers in 21 states, strongest in Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, J&K and the Andamans. What it actually makes: the Gandhinagar plant has an SMT line (50 lakh PCBs a year), three assembly lines (24 lakh cameras a year since Jul 2026) and housing moulds. The company itself describes its history as "import and assemble". In FY25, 92% of purchases by value were finished goods for resaled (₹62.8 cr of ₹68.4 cr). H1 FY27 shows the mix moving: finished goods bought for resale were ₹2.1 cr against ₹72.3 cr of materials consumed, and gross margin improved to 31.7%d. The SoC (the vision chip) is still bought in; a Dec 2025 MoU with IndieSemic aims to co-develop an Indian one, but nothing has been delivered yet. So the honest description is: PCB assembly and final assembly of cameras built from bought-in chips, sensors and lenses, with BIS-ER/STQC compliance as the key credential. Compliance is a real barrier now that the rules exclude many Chinese designs.

Moat

Thin but real for now. BIS-ER/STQC registration is model by model and takes months, and Prizor has 12+ registered models plus GeM OEM status. That matters while compliant capacity is scarce. Against that: CP Plus, Hikvision's Indian-assembled lines, Sparsh, Matrix, Godrej and others are all certifying too. The product is a commodity once supply normalises, and Prizor does not own the chip. Revenue flattening in H1 FY27, with 24 lakh-camera capacity in place since July, is the first sign the easy share gains may be over.

Order book

Risks

  • Growth stall. H1 FY27 revenue was flat on H2 FY26. At 53x trailing, a second flat half, or a fall, would re-rate the stock sharply.
  • The tailwind fades. The growth spike coincides with the 2025 STQC rules. As CP Plus and others add compliant capacity, the scarcity premium in pricing and margin can reverse.
  • Working capital and cash. Operating cash flow was negative again in H1 FY27 (−₹4.6 cr). Cash was ₹0.17 cr at 30 Sep, and ₹21.9 cr sits in loans and advances to undisclosed parties. Growth is funded by equity and debt.
  • Tax provisions. ₹0.06 cr of tax was paid against ₹5.10 cr charged in H1, and short-term provisions reached ₹12.5 cr. If the tax is genuinely unpaid, interest under s.234B/C follows. The provisions breakdown is not filed.
  • Governance. Promoter warrants priced at the low, an independent director in the warrant round, an ex-subsidiary diluted to undisclosed parties, growing undisclosed advances, and a related CFO.
  • Supply. 13.6 lakh October shares sit 51% in profit at ₹876. Non-promoter preferential shares unlock about six months after trading approval (≈ Apr–May 2027d). The ₹291 warrants (4.5x in profit) convert by about Jun 2027.
  • Thin market. SME board, ₹2.1 cr of daily turnover, no mutual fund holder, circuit-driven price moves, a 15% one-day fall on no news, and a 13% intraday range on results day.
