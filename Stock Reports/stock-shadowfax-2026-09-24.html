Verdict

A business that has genuinely turned, at a price that assumes the turn continues for years. Shadowfax delivers parcels for e-commerce sites and does hyperlocal delivery for quick-commerce and food apps. Two years ago it barely made money: FY24 revenue ₹1,885 cr at a 1% operating margin and a ₹12 cr loss. The last twelve months: revenue ₹4,737 cr, operating margin 6%, profit ₹169 cr. Q1 FY27 was the best yet — revenue ₹1,358 cr (+65%), 24.7 crore orders (+83%), profit ₹65 cr against ₹8 cr. Management raised FY27 revenue growth guidance from 27–30% to 38–40%. Lost and damaged shipments fell from 7.9% of revenue to 5.5%, and they're targeting 3.5–4%. That single line is much of the margin improvement, and it's within the company's control.

Now the price. The IPO in January was priced at ₹124 and listed 9% below that. The stock is ₹286, up 185% from its low, at a 52-week high. Reported earnings carry almost no tax because of past losses, so profit is close to pre-tax profit. When tax normalises, roughly a quarter of reported profit goes. On FY27 guidance of about ₹5,850 cr revenue at Q1's pre-tax margin of 4.8%, taxed at 25%, this is about 80xd FY27 earnings — and about 64x if margin keeps climbing to 6%. Lower than the headline 99x, but still a growth-company price.

What you're underwriting: 28–30% share of a fast-growing e-commerce delivery market (up from 8% in FY22), a hyperlocal business at a ₹1,000 cr annual run rate, and an extra layer of dark stores (47 live, 100+ targeted). Against that: a gig workforce whose costs can be changed by state minimum-wage rules, customers large enough to push prices down, and Delhivery and Ecom Express competing for the same parcels. Hold, high risk. The business deserves following; the entry doesn't. Buy nearer ₹210, roughly 25% lower, which is about 60x FY27 taxed earnings at Q1 margins.

Business

What it does

Founded in 2016 in Bengaluru. It runs a delivery network built on gig workers rather than employed drivers, and on partner-run touchpoints rather than owned hubs, which is why it can add pin codes quickly without heavy capital. Express carries e-commerce parcels for online marketplaces, now 16,372 pin codes and roughly 28–30% share of third-party e-commerce delivery. Hyperlocal delivers for quick-commerce and food-delivery apps within cities. Prime Large handles bulky goods such as appliances and furniture. Dark stores and the Shadowfax 360 seller platform (1,200+ sellers in 90 days) are newer additions that hold client inventory and handle direct-to-consumer brands.

The older asset-heavy carriers (Blue Dart, TCI) earn higher margins on much slower growth and trade at a third of the multiple. The market is paying for growth and for the belief that e-commerce delivery ends up as a two- or three-player market.

Moat

Scale and density, not technology. Once a network carries enough parcels per pin code, each extra parcel is cheaper to deliver, which lets it price lower and win more volume. Going from 8% to 28–30% share in four years suggests that is working. The weakness is the customer side: the big marketplaces and quick-commerce apps are few, large, and can move volume between carriers or build their own. The ₹50 take rate leaves little room if they push.

Order book

Not applicable. Volumes come from contracts with marketplaces and quick-commerce apps. The forward commitment is management's FY27 guidance of 38–40% revenue growth, which needs 31–34% growth over the remaining three quarters.

Risks

Tax normalising and price falling at the same time. Reported profit is untaxed today. When past losses run out, about a quarter of it goes. If realisation per order keeps falling as volumes grow, both halves of the story weaken at once, and the stock is priced for neither happening.
  • Gig-worker rules: state minimum-wage changes already cost ₹2–2.5 cr a month. Wider regulation of gig work is an active policy topic in India.
  • Customer concentration: a handful of marketplaces and quick-commerce apps drive volumes and negotiate hard. unreconciled The published Q1 material doesn't give customer shares.
  • Lock-in expiries: venture investors hold large blocks from before the IPO. unreconciled Exact expiry dates weren't found.
  • Competition: Delhivery, Ecom Express and the marketplaces' own delivery arms.
  • Fixed costs: 53 lakh square feet of space and growing. Operating leverage works in reverse if volumes stall.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a price near ₹210 (about 60x FY27 earnings at a normal tax rate, or nearer 47x if margin reaches 6%). Or the September and December quarters holding 7%+ operating margin while revenue grows above 35%, with lost-shipment costs reaching the 4% target, which would make the margin gain look permanent.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating margin falling back below 5%, revenue growth below 25% against guidance of 38–40%, or a large block sale by pre-IPO investors at a discount.
