Verdict

Buy — reaffirmed, and the thesis has strengthened. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹2,525 on 13 Aug 2026. Q1 FY27 was the strongest quarter in the company's history: revenue ₹3,168 cr up 54%, EBITDA ₹285 cr up 99% with margin expanding 205 bps to 9%, PAT ₹205 cr up 129%. Two full turns of operating leverage came through at once.

The FMEG breakeven matters more than the growth. The fans, lighting and appliances business has been the drag on this equity for years — 9% of revenue, consistently loss-making, and the reason the whole company traded at a discount to Polycab and Havells on margin. It reached operational breakeven this quarter. If that holds, consolidated margin has a structural step up ahead of it rather than a cyclical one.

The core wires and cables business grew 57% with segment margin up 232 bps. ROCE is 28.1% and borrowings are ₹337 cr against ₹2,518 cr of reserves — this is growth funded from operations, not leverage.

What restrains the enthusiasm is price. At 50.9x trailing the stock has already re-rated hard — up 136% from the 52-week low of ₹1,165. Much of the FMEG turnaround is now in the multiple. This stays a Buy on the strength of the operating trajectory, not on valuation support, and a buyer here is paying for execution that has to continue.

Business

Nature. Two segments. Wires and cables — house wire, industrial cable, speciality cable — is 91% of revenue and effectively all of the profit. FMEG (fans, lighting, switches, appliances) is 9%, sold under the Luminous and RR brands, and has been the loss centre.

Moat. Distribution. In Indian wires and cables the electrician and dealer network is the barrier, not the product — cable is close to a commodity and brand preference is set at the point of installation. R R Kabel has built that network over decades. Exports add a second leg with different competitive dynamics.

Peers. Polycab (larger, higher margin, more expensive), Havells (FMEG-led), KEI Industries, Finolex Cables. R R Kabel has historically traded below Polycab on margin; the FMEG breakeven is the event that could close part of that gap.

Demand backdrop. Housing completion, electrification and data-centre build all feed cable volume. The 57% segment growth is running well ahead of the industry, so share gain is happening alongside the cycle.

Risks

  • Copper. The dominant input. Cable pricing passes through with a lag, so a sharp copper move compresses margin for a quarter or two regardless of demand.
  • Valuation. 50.9x trailing after a 136% move off the low. Any quarter that breaks the margin trajectory would be punished from this level.
  • FMEG durability. Operational breakeven in one quarter is a milestone, not proof. It has to hold through a seasonally weaker period before it can be treated as structural.
  • Competitive intensity. Polycab and Havells are both larger and both investing in the same distribution channel; the growth rate invites a response.
  • Working capital. Inventory days rose from 59 to 81 in FY26 — worth watching if revenue growth slows while stock stays high.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively — FMEG holding breakeven or better for two more quarters, with consolidated EBITDA margin sustained above 9%.
  • Downgrade to Hold — EBITDA margin falling back under 7%, FMEG returning to losses, or the multiple exceeding ~60x without matching delivery.
  • Downgrade to Sell — a copper-driven margin squeeze coinciding with volume deceleration, or a debt-funded acquisition that changes the balance-sheet character.
