Hold

Verdict

Outstanding growth, bought with the balance sheet, priced at 51x. Craftsman is executing extremely well: FY26 revenue ₹8,069 cr (+41.8%) with PAT +91% to ₹384 cr, and Q1 FY27 carried on — revenue ₹2,432 cr (+36%), EBITDA ₹408 cr (+51%), PAT ₹151 cr (+116%), EPS ₹62.12 against ₹36.89. All three segments grew. ROE improved to 13% from 9%. There is nothing wrong with the operating performance.

The reservations are structural. Net margin is only ~6% — this is a high-revenue, thin-margin business, so profit is volatile in both directions. Leverage has risen sharply: debt-to-equity 0.72 → 1.02, net debt/EBITDA at 2.55x, and a ₹1,500 cr capex programme still ahead. Management wants net debt/EBITDA back to 1.0–1.5x, partly via land sales — which tells you the deleveraging plan is not purely operating cash. And the stock trades at ~51x trailing, a 54% premium to the ~34x auto-components sector average. Growth this good deserves a premium; whether it deserves that one, on 6% margins and rising debt into a big capex cycle, is the debate. Hold.

A correction to something I wrote in the Sansera note. I described Craftsman as pivoting to aerospace and defence alongside Sansera and NRB. On the evidence available, that is wrong for Craftsman: it reports three segments — Powertrain, Aluminium Products, and Industrial & Engineering, and no distinct aerospace/defence segment surfaced. Craftsman's growth story is aluminium and EV-adjacent components (largely the Dr Axion acquisition), not aerospace. Judge it on that.

Aluminium Products is now the company — 61% of revenue, and largely the acquired Dr Axion business. That is the source of both the growth and the thin blended margin. The original Powertrain franchise, historically the higher-quality earnings stream, is now barely a quarter of the top line. Anyone valuing Craftsman on its old identity is valuing the wrong company.

Bull case

  • Broad-based growth — all three segments contributing, not a single-line story.
  • EBITDA growing faster than revenue (+51% vs +36%), so operating leverage is real.
  • PAT +116% with EPS up ~68% — genuine per-share progress, not just consolidation optics.
  • ROE improving 9% → 13% despite the enlarged asset base.
  • Aluminium exposure is structurally right — lightweighting and EV platforms both increase aluminium content per vehicle.
  • Management has a stated deleveraging target (1.0–1.5x net debt/EBITDA).

Risks

Thin margins plus rising leverage plus a large capex plan is a demanding combination at 51x. At ~6% net margin, a 200 bps move in operating costs swings profit by a third. Net debt/EBITDA at 2.55x with ₹1,500 cr of capex still to spend means the balance sheet has limited room if demand pauses mid-programme. The deleveraging plan leaning partly on land sales is worth noting — asset sales are a one-time lever, not recurring cash generation.
  • 51x trailing, a 54% premium to sector. The premium assumes this growth rate persists; auto-component growth rarely does.
  • Acquisition-driven growth — the FY26 revenue jump is substantially Dr Axion consolidating, which is not organic and does not repeat.
  • Aluminium price pass-through risk on a 61%-of-revenue segment.
  • Cyclicality — commercial vehicles and off-highway are the most cyclical end-markets in the sector.
  • Capex execution — ₹1,500 cr deployed into a slowing cycle would be the worst outcome.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: net debt/EBITDA falling toward 1.5x on operating cash flow rather than asset sales, with net margin sustained above ~7% and organic growth clearly separated from acquired growth in disclosure.
  • Downgrade to Sell: net margin slipping below ~5%, leverage rising past ~3x net debt/EBITDA, or capex being funded by fresh borrowing rather than accruals.
