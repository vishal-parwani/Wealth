Verdict

The business is in the best shape it has ever been in. The share price has moved further than the business has. Welspun Corp makes large-diameter steel pipes for oil, gas and water, in India, the United States and Saudi Arabia, plus ductile iron pipes, steel billets and rebar, and the Sintex water-tank and plastics business. Q1 FY27 was a record: EBITDA ₹756 cr, up 35%, operating margin 17% against 15%, and an order book of about ₹25,750 cr ($2.7 bn), the highest ever, with US bookings running through FY28 and into FY29. Return on capital is above 20% and the company says it is net cash of ₹2,336 cr with net debt under 1x EBITDA.

Two adjustments before judging the price. First, the headline Q1 profit of ₹1,048 cr (up 199%) includes a ₹548 cr one-off gain from selling its stake in East Pipes Integrated Company in Saudi Arabia. Strip that out and the quarter is good, not spectacular. Strip it from the trailing year and the stock is on about 40xd, not the 30.8x on screens. Second, this is still a cyclical business: pipe demand follows oil and gas capital spending, and Indian water-pipe demand is currently weak because Jal Jeevan Mission funding has slowed.

The strategic shift is real and in the right direction: the US is now the growth engine (75% export-linked, 25% data centres, EBITDA per tonne above the $300 target), two Saudi plants commission by Q3 FY27, and India has been redirected toward exports and pig iron while domestic water pipes are soft. But the stock is up 273% in a year, and the brokers' average target of ₹2,575 is below the price — the same houses whose ₹875–1,150 targets from December 2025 the stock passed months ago. Hold. Worth owning on a pullback toward ₹1,900, about 28x earnings excluding the one-off.

Business

What it does

One of the world's largest makers of large-diameter welded steel pipes — the pipes that carry oil, gas and water over long distances — with plants in India, the US (Little Rock, Arkansas) and Saudi Arabia. Around that core it has added ductile iron pipes and steel billets and rebar in India, stainless pipes and tubes, and Sintex, the water-tank and plastics brand bought out of insolvency. US demand comes from LNG export pipelines and, increasingly, from data-centre construction. Indian demand is mostly government water projects, which is the weak part right now.

Moat

Real but cyclical. Large-diameter pipe for oil and gas needs mill approvals from each customer, and delivery to US projects effectively requires US plants — which Welspun has and most Indian competitors don't. That is why it earns $300+ per tonne in the US against thin margins in Indian water pipes. What it can't control is when oil, gas and water projects get sanctioned. Sintex adds a consumer-brand business, but it is small and currently weak.

Order book

₹25,750 cr (about $2.7 bn) at 30 June 2026, the highest in the company's history and about 1.5 years of revenue. US bookings run through FY28 with visibility into FY29. This is the strongest single fact in the report and the reason the rating isn't lower.

Risks

A cyclical business at a growth multiple. About 40x earnings excluding the one-off gain, after a 273% rise in a year, for a company whose revenue fell 19% as recently as FY25. Pipe demand depends on energy capital spending, which turns quickly.
  • One-off gains flatter the numbers: ₹548 cr from the EPIC stake in Q1, and a large other-income year in FY25. Judge it on operating profit.
  • India water pipes are weak while Jal Jeevan Mission funding is constrained, and Sintex is under margin pressure.
  • US concentration risk: the growth case rests on LNG and data-centre pipelines, plus trade policy. Tariffs cut both ways for a company with US plants and Indian exports.
  • Steel prices and 166-day inventory: a sharp fall in steel prices hits both margin and stock value.
  • Consensus is below the price, so the market is ahead of the analysts who model it.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a pullback toward ₹1,900 (about 28x earnings excluding one-offs). Or two quarters at 16%+ operating margin with the order book above ₹25,000 cr and the Saudi plants commissioned on time, which would make the higher margin structural rather than mix-driven.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating margin back below 12%, the order book shrinking materially, US bookings for FY29 not building, or debt rising to fund new capex after management said none is planned.
