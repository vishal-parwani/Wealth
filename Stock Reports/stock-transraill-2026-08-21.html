Verdict

Downgrade to Hold. This is a rating change. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹508.15 on 3 Aug 2026, arguing "a growth EPC at a value multiple, mid-correction". The stock is ₹460, down 9.5%, and now sits ₹10 above its 52-week low, 44% below its ₹823 high. The valuation argument is intact. The growth half of that thesis is the part that has weakened.

Q1 FY27 is the problem. Revenue ₹1,736 cr, up just 5%. PAT ₹108 cr, up 3%. Management is guiding to 20–22% revenue growth for FY27. EPC revenue is genuinely back-ended and the June quarter is seasonally the weakest, so one soft quarter does not kill the year — but it opens a wide gap that the remaining three quarters have to close, and the note can no longer assume it closes.

The order book has stopped growing. ₹16,035 cr unexecuted at 30 June 2026, against ₹16,361 cr at the previous note. Still 2.3x revenue and still excellent visibility — but flat-to-slightly-down is a change of direction from a book that had been compounding.

What tips it to Hold is the institutional exit. DII holding has fallen from 14.08% to 6.51% in five quarters. Worse, as the fund section below shows, active mutual funds have now left entirely — what remains is index money that holds the stock because a benchmark tells it to. When the professionals who studied this company most closely have all sold, a Buy rating needs better evidence than a low multiple.

Why not a Sell. Nothing is actually deteriorating: profit still grew, margin held at 12%, ROCE is 29.2%, the balance sheet is sound, and the order book is 2.3x revenue. At 14.8x you are not paying for optimism. This is a Hold while the FY27 guidance is tested — not a stock to exit.

Business

Nature. Transrail is an EPC contractor in power transmission and distribution — it builds transmission lines and substations — and it also manufactures its own towers and conductors, which is unusual and gives it more control over cost and schedule than a pure contractor. Smaller businesses in poles, lighting and railway work sit alongside.

The cycle it rides. Indian and overseas grid capex. The order book splits roughly 61% domestic and 39% international. Integration into towers and conductors is the real differentiator versus a pure EPC bidder, and it shows up as a 29.2% ROCE, which is high for contracting.

Why EPC deserves a lower multiple. Revenue is recognised on execution, so it is lumpy and back-ended; margins move on project mix; and working capital absorbs cash as the book grows. 14.8x is not a mistake by the market — it is what contracting earns. The question is whether this contractor deserves the top or bottom of that range.

Peers. KEC International and Kalpataru Projects, both larger and both on richer multiples. Transrail's discount to them was the core of the original Buy case and it still exists.

Risks

  • Guidance risk. 5% growth in Q1 against 20–22% for the year. Three quarters of roughly 25% growth are now needed. If the year lands at 10%, the "growth EPC" description stops being true.
  • Order book has plateaued. ₹16,361 cr to ₹16,035 cr. Intake of ₹1,034 cr in Q1 against a ₹10,000–11,000 cr annual plan is well behind pace.
  • Active funds have all sold. Only index trackers remain, and DII holding has more than halved. No discretionary institutional support under the price.
  • EPC lumpiness. Quarterly revenue and margin swing on execution mix. Five flat quarters show how little operating leverage there is when volumes pause.
  • Working capital. Total assets grew from ₹4,621 cr to ₹7,354 cr over two years while borrowings stayed near ₹670 cr — funded so far, but a growing book consumes cash.
  • Falling knife. The stock is 44% off its high and ₹10 from its low. Cheap has not yet been a floor.

What would change the rating

  • Back to Buy — H1 FY27 revenue growth recovering above 15%, or order intake getting back on track towards the ₹10,000 cr plan. Either would show the Q1 softness was timing. The first active fund rebuilding a position would also count.
  • Stays a Hold — while 14.8x and a ₹16,035 cr book offset a year that is running behind guidance.
  • Downgrade to Sell — FY27 guidance cut, operating margin falling below 10%, or the order book shrinking for a second consecutive quarter.
