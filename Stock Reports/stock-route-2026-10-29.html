Verdict

Q2 FY27 results are out. The board approved them on 27 Oct 2026 and they were filed on 28 Oct. Revenue was ₹1,179.7 cr, up 5.4% YoY and 2.4% QoQ. Gross margin recovered to 22.4% from 20.9% in Q1, against 22.1% a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA was ₹118.4 cr at 10.0%, up 8.7% QoQ but down 11.1% YoY. PAT was ₹82.6 cr, against a ₹18.8 cr loss in Q2 FY26, which carried ₹135.9 cr of vendor-advance write-offs. On an adjusted basis PAT fell 17.8% YoY. Basic EPS was ₹12.63. New-product revenue (WhatsApp, RCS, IP messaging) was ₹128 cr, up 37% YoY, but Heltar has been included since 29 July. Cash was ₹1,356 cr. A second interim dividend of ₹4 has a 2 Nov record date. The auditor, MSKA & Associates, issued an unmodified limited review. The stock rose 6.3% the next session and stands at ₹456.10, up 10% from its 8 Oct closing low of ₹414.80.

Buy, high risk, as a small position. The market prices the company as if its ₹1,317 cr of net cash were stuck and its earnings were in permanent decline. Neither is established. At ₹2,870 cr, enterprise value is about ₹1,550 crd. That is roughly 3.1x LTM adjusted EBITDA of ₹505 crd and about 4.6x ex-cash earningsd. Operating cash flow was ₹602 cr in FY25 and ₹581 cr in FY26. Part of that came from receivables falling from ₹1,072 cr to ₹909 cr. Even so, normalised free cash flow near PAT (about ₹350 cr) is a 12% yield on the market capd. The stock trades at book value for a business earning a 12–13% ROE on a balance sheet that is half cash. Tanla, the closest Indian peer, trades at 15x earnings and about 9x EV/EBITDAd.

Why it is this cheap, and why the rating carries high risk. (1) No growth. Revenue was ₹4,023 cr in FY24 and ₹4,569 cr TTM. EBITDA has been flat at ₹500–540 cr for three years, because international A2P SMS (ILD) volumes keep shrinking. (2) Cash quality. In FY26 the company wrote off ₹136 cr of advances paid to messaging vendors under minimum-guarantee deals. In H1 FY27 it paid another ₹117 cr advance to a vendor. That cut reported operating cash flow to ₹33 cr, or 15% of EBITDA, so the same pattern is recurring. (3) Controller. Proximus sits at the 75% public-float ceiling, so it cannot buy more without a delisting. Its own Global segment's EBITDA fell 35% YoY in Q2 2026. (4) Institutions leaving. DIIs held 8.1% in Mar 25 and 2.2% now. FIIs went from 4.5% to 1.7%. SBI Innovative Opportunities cut from 20.5 L to 5 L shares in six months. Retail now holds 21%.

Counter-argument. This could be a value trap. A controller can leave cash idle for years, or spend it on group-friendly M&A. Management said in May that acquisition is the "first choice" for the cash. The ₹16.5 dividend uses only about ₹104 cr of about ₹500 cr of yearly cash. The rating depends on three things: no new vendor-advance losses, H2 cash flow recovering, and a capital-allocation decision that returns more cash. See the triggers below.

The Proximus situation, and what it means for minorities

  • Who owns it. 74.85% is held by Proximus Global SA/NV (formerly Proximus Opal). The ultimate parent is Belgium's Proximus SA, whose majority owner is the Belgian state. Proximus bought the founders' 57.56% at ₹1,626.40 in 2023-24 and ran the mandatory open offer at the same price. The founders reinvested about €299 m into the Proximus holding vehicle (Clear Bridge Ventures LLP). The founders' economics now sit mainly above Route, at the Proximus Global level, not alongside Route's minority holders. Rajdip Gupta is still MD. Sandip Gupta is now a director at the UK subsidiary.
  • No open offer, delisting or merger is pending as of 29 Oct 2026. Nothing appears in exchange filings, Proximus's Q2 2026 release or press searches. The December 2024 creation of Proximus Global (BICS + Telesign + Route Mobile, valued at €3.1 bn equity) was framed as a path to "future value crystallisation", which is the only hint of an eventual listing or sale.
  • Mechanics. At 74.85%, Proximus cannot creep up through market purchases. Consolidating further takes a formal SEBI delisting offer, which succeeds only when the acquirer reaches 90% and is priced by reverse book-building or a fixed-price premium over a floor set mainly by recent trading prices. The 2024 ₹1,626 price gives today's holders no protection; a delisting would be priced off the current ₹400s. Even so, any offer would almost certainly come at a premium to today's price. Retail's 21% is hard to buy out cheaply, so the delisting optionality is positive at this level.
  • The real minority risks are slower and quieter than a lowball offer. One is cash left idle or used for group-aligned acquisitions; the May call said the board, not Proximus, decides. Another is related-party traffic and pricing with BICS and Telesign, which appear as transacting fellow subsidiaries in FY26 related-party notes; the amounts were not extracted for this note. A third is management churn. The CEO has changed twice in a year (Gautam Badalia until Jul 25, Rajdip Gupta interim, Tushar Agnihotri from Feb 26), and the chair moved from Guillaume Boutin to Mark Reid to Seckin Arikan, CEO of Proximus Global, in Jan 26.

Business

Nature of business

Route Mobile is a CPaaS aggregator. Enterprises and OTT apps send OTPs, alerts and marketing through its APIs, and Route delivers them over SMS, WhatsApp, RCS, voice and email through about 280 direct operator connections and access to 900+ operators. It also sells SMS firewalls to operators. It has 2,400+ billable clients, but the top 10 are about 44% of revenue and the top 50 about 75%. By customer headquarters, revenue is about 37% Americas, 33% Asia and 18% Europe; by message termination, about 35% India. Volume was 93.4 bn billable transactions in H1, up 11%, at a falling price per message. Recurring revenue is 90–93% and net revenue retention 105% (LTM Sep 26). Headcount is 786.

Structural pressures. International A2P SMS (ILD), long the main revenue source, is shrinking. OTP traffic is moving to WhatsApp authentication, app-based push and silent network authentication. Interactive use cases are shifting to WhatsApp and RCS, which pay aggregators a thinner layer; management admits competitors' WhatsApp/RCS base is about 3x its own and that it is avoiding "aggression in the marketplace on pricing". Domestic SMS is regulated by TRAI's DLT, header and template rules, and the 2024 URL whitelisting and traceability mandates disrupted volumes. The Truecaller business-messaging partnership is a test only. A gross margin around 22% says this is a routing business with modest pricing power, not software.

Moat

The moat is narrow. Direct operator connections, the SMS firewall relationships with operators and a 2,400-client base give some scale and switching friction. Proximus Global adds BICS's carrier network and Telesign's identity cross-sell. But messages are a commodity, the biggest customers are concentrated and price-sensitive, and the channel is moving toward platforms (Meta, Google RCS) that set the price. The moat is not durable enough to justify a premium multiple. At 3x EBITDA, none is being paid.

Risks

The cash is the thesis, and it is not under minority control. ₹1,356 cr is about ₹215 per shared, or 47% of the price. If it goes into a large acquisition at a full price, especially one sourced from or aligned with the Proximus group, or into more prepaid vendor minimum-guarantee deals like the ₹136 cr written off in FY26, the valuation floor disappears. The ₹117 cr H1 FY27 advance is the live version of this risk.
  • Structural SMS decline. If ILD keeps shrinking faster than WhatsApp/RCS and new products grow, revenue stays flat and margins drift toward 9–10%, and the stock remains a value trap. Adjusted EBITDA fell 11% YoY in Q2 despite revenue growth.
  • Customer concentration. The top 10 are 44% of revenue. A traffic pause at a single large high-margin domestic customer drove most of the 2.4-point gross-margin drop in Q1 FY27. Losing one large OTT account would be material.
  • Controller strategy. Proximus Global's EBITDA is falling. The parent could restructure, merge or sell Proximus Global on terms set at the parent level, and Route's minority would have little say until a delisting or open offer is triggered.
  • Guidance credibility. The FY27 guide was mid-to-high single-digit growth and ~12% adjusted EBITDA margin. H1 was +7.4% growth (in line) at a 9.8% margin (behind). Management "will come back" on the margin.
  • Security and regulation. The Masivian cyber investigation is still open. TRAI or DoT changes to commercial-communication rules can change domestic volumes overnight.
  • Technical overhang. Institutions are still exiting (DII 2.2%, FII 1.65%), so price support comes mainly from retail.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: a buyback, a special dividend, or a payout policy above 50% of FCF. Alternatively, H2 FY27 operating cash flow above ₹250 cr with the ₹117 cr advance visibly used up, plus gross margin back at 23% or more for two quarters.
  • Downgrade to Hold: any further write-off of vendor advances, H2 operating cash flow below 50% of adjusted EBITDA, or adjusted EBITDA margin stuck below 10% through Q4 FY27.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a large acquisition (over ₹500 cr) at more than about 10x EBITDA, or any transfer of cash or business to the Proximus group on terms not clearly arm's-length. Also an exit of the founder-MD alongside a further fall in revenue.
