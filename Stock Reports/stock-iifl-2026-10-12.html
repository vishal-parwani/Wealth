Verdict

Buy. A lender earning 19.5% ROE on an annualised basis costs 1.9x book and under 10x next year's consensus earnings. Every broker covering it sits above the current price, with a consensus target of ₹730, 20% upside.

What changed since 24 Sep 2026. The price fell from ₹617.55 to ₹606.65 (−1.8%), after trading as low as about ₹591 in early October. The rating stays Buy. Three new facts. First, the analyst target is now verified. Trendlyne's consensus from 6 analysts is ₹730 (median ₹750, range ₹610–₹770). It replaces the unverified "~₹625" carried in the earlier notes, so the margin of safety is wider than the September note assumed. Second, Q2 FY27 results are due on 15 Oct, three days after this note, so any rating change should follow the print rather than lead it. Third, funds sold in the August disclosure. Franklin India Small Cap exited its 22.6-lakh-share position and the net change across all schemes was −17 lakh shares, although 45 schemes bought against 9 sellers. Nothing new has appeared on the departures of the head of legal and the LAP business head on 11 September. Crisil reaffirmed its ratings on 25 August.

The evidence. Quarterly PAT has risen four quarters running, from ₹418 cr to ₹501 cr, ₹623 cr and ₹713 cr. Over the same quarters the financing margin widened from 18% to 25% and GNPA fell from 2.14% to 1.55%. Q1 FY27 annualises to EPS of ₹63.5d, almost exactly the FY27 consensus of ₹63.2, so the street is not assuming any further improvement. Management's FY27 targets are ROE of about 25%, ROA of 3.1–3.3%, credit costs of 1.5–1.7% and AUM growth of 16–20%. If they are met, 1.9x book is cheap. Muthoot Finance, the gold-loan benchmark, trades at 2.6x book.

The counter-argument. Gold loans are about half of AUM (₹58,406 cr, 51%). The RBI barred this exact business in March 2024. Gold-loan growth of 114% also rode a record gold price, and a sharp fall in gold would tighten loan-to-value cushions and push auctions up. GNPA ticked up in Q1, from 1.46% to 1.55%. FIIs cut 4.3 points in the June quarter, and the September-quarter register is not out yet. These risks are why it trades at 1.9x book, and the price already reflects them. For a holder: keep holding, and add after Q2 if GNPA is back under 1.5% and AUM growth is within guidance.

2 · Asset quality and the gold book

  • Mix. Gold is 51% of AUM, up from about a third before the embargo. The rest is home loans, LAP, microfinance and MSME. About 90% of the book is secured and about 35% sits off balance sheet through co-lending and assignment.
  • The gold book itself is clean. Gold-loan GNPA is 0.61% at 70% LTV. The stress pockets are microfinance and micro-LAP, which management is still cleaning up.
  • Rules are now uniform. The RBI gold-loan directions took effect on 1 Apr 2026 for banks and NBFCs alike: LTV caps of 85% (loans up to ₹2.5 lakh), 80% (₹2.5–5 lakh) and 75% (above ₹5 lakh); bullet loans repaid within 12 months; interest cleared before any renewal. That removes regulatory uncertainty but forces periodic interest collection, which raises delinquency risk.
  • Track record. The RBI stopped new gold loans from March to September 2024. The 11 Sep exits of the head of legal and the LAP business head are not control roles, but worth watching at a lender with this history.

Business

Nature of business

A diversified retail NBFC with ₹1,15,523 cr of AUM. It lends against gold (51% of AUM, ₹58,406 cr), and also writes home loans (IIFL Home Finance), microfinance (IIFL Samasta), loans against property and digital/MSME loans. About 35% of the book is off balance sheet through co-lending and assignment, which lifts ROE above what on-book leverage alone would give. Funding has been diversified offshore with $300 mn of 2030 senior secured notes.

That gap is the opportunity, and it narrows only if IIFL proves it can stay out of regulatory trouble.

Moat

Narrow. The gold-loan branch network and its appraisal and vaulting infrastructure take years and regulatory trust to build, which is a real barrier. Muthoot and Manappuram are larger in gold, though, and banks compete hard for secured gold credit. The other businesses are commoditised retail lending. The edge is execution and cost of funds, not a structural moat.

Risks

A regulatory repeat on gold loans would break the thesis. Half the book is the product the RBI halted in 2024. A second action now would hit about ₹58,000 cr of AUM and most of the earnings recovery, and the stock would de-rate below book.
  • Gold price. Record gold prices have inflated ticket sizes and collateral values. A sharp fall would compress LTV headroom and raise auctions and credit cost.
  • Asset quality turned in Q1. GNPA rose from 1.46% to 1.55% and NNPA from 0.73% to 0.82% while AUM grew 38%. It was the first deterioration of the recovery.
  • Growth normalising. FY27 AUM guidance is 16–20% against 38% delivered. The base effect is over.
  • Leverage. Borrowings are about 5x net worth, normal for an NBFC, which is why a 1.5–1.7% credit cost matters so much to ROE.
  • Institutional thinning. FIIs cut 4.3 points in Jun-26, and funds were net sellers of 17 lakh shares in August.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: Q2 FY27 (15 Oct) shows GNPA back under 1.5%, quarterly ROE at or above 20%, and AUM growth inside the 16–20% guide, together with a stabilising FII line in the September shareholding.
  • Downgrade to Hold: GNPA above 2%, annualised ROE below 15%, or the price reaching the ₹730 consensus without earnings upgrades.
  • Downgrade to Sell: any fresh RBI supervisory action on the gold-loan book, or credit cost well beyond the guided 1.7%.
