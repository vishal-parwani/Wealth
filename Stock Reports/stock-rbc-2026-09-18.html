Verdict

Upgrade to Buy. The price came to our range while the business kept getting better. In July the problem was price, not quality: about 65x trailing earnings at $591. The note said buy on weakness at $450–500. It's $496 now. Meanwhile the fiscal first quarter (June) was the best in the company's history. Sales were $519.5 m, up 19%, with aerospace and defense up 37%. Gross margin was a record 47.7%. Net income rose 48% to $101.5 m. Free cash flow was $147 m in the quarter and $385 m over twelve months. The order backlog is about $2.3 bn, more than double a year ago.

The fall came from next quarter's guidance: sales growth slowing to 11–13% and gross margin back to about 45.6%. Then the market cut back richly valued industrial stocks generally. The stock fell 26% from its $668 high. Trailing GAAP P/E went from 65x to 49x and forward P/E is 33x. That still isn't cheap. But it's a fair price for a sole-source aerospace and defense supplier with a doubled backlog, record margins and cash flow above net income. It's also the level the July note named.

The robotics angle (Morgan Stanley's precision-bearing thesis) remains a long-term option, not part of the price. Buy. Consensus is $631 from 10 analysts, 27% above today.

Corporate governance

A US-style widely held company, not founder-controlled like the Indian names in this library. Michael Hartnett has been Chairman and CEO since 1992 and built the company through a long series of acquisitions (Sargent, Dodge, VACCO). His long tenure is the main governance point. It has been good for shareholders, but it combines the chair and CEO roles, and who follows him is the obvious question. The 5.00% mandatory convertible preferred shares issued for the Dodge deal have converted, which removed a class of preferred dividends. There's no promoter pledge or related-party structure to watch.

Business

What it does

Highly engineered precision bearings and components: ball, spherical, thin-section and needle-roller bearings, and rod ends. It also makes gearing and power transmission products (Dodge) and, since VACCO, valves and manifolds for space and naval defense. Aerospace & Defense is about $849 m of trailing sales, Industrial about $1,106 m. In aerospace the company is often the only approved supplier on aircraft and weapons programmes that run for decades, which gives it pricing power most bearing makers don't have.

Moat

Strong. Bearings approved for aircraft and defense programmes can't be swapped out without requalification, which takes years. That gives lasting single-supplier positions and pricing power. The industrial side (Dodge) is more ordinary but benefits from a large installed base and distributor relationships.

Order book

Backlog about $2.3 bn at June 2026, more than double a year earlier, driven by aerospace and defense (commercial aircraft production rising, defense and naval programmes). That's about 1.2 years of revenue, unusually high visibility for a component maker.

Risks

The price can fall further even if the business is fine. 33x forward earnings is below where it was but still high. The 52-week low was $364.50, 27% below today, and that was only a year ago. If industrial stocks fall further, the Buy could lose money for a year even as earnings grow.
  • Aircraft production rates (Boeing and Airbus supply chains) and defense budgets drive the growth segment.
  • Industrial cycle: about 57% of sales. The Q2 guidance hints at slowing.
  • Acquisition risk: the balance sheet is mostly goodwill. A poorly priced deal would hurt.
  • Key person: a long-serving founder-era CEO with no public successor.
