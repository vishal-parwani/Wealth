Verdict

Buy, unchanged. The fall since 11 Oct came from the sector, not the company, and it has made the stock cheaper on unchanged estimates. ENRIN has not reported anything since the 6 Aug results. Q4 FY26 (Jul–Sep 2026) is not yet declared, and no board-meeting date had been filed by 30 Oct. Last year the board approved the September-quarter results on 24 Nov, so expect them in the second half of November. Over three quarters, year-on-year PAT growth went from +35% to +52% to +68%. The June quarter delivered ₹441 cr on ₹2,486 cr of revenue with a 23.6% operating margin. The order backlog is ₹19,331 cr, about 2.05xd trailing revenue and up 16% in a year.

Why the price fell. The stock dropped from ₹3,101 to a ₹2,653 close on 23 Oct (−14%) and has since recovered to ₹2,812 (−9.3%). The move tracked a global de-rating of grid and power-equipment stocks, in two legs:

  • 14–16 Oct. Siemens Energy AG fell 5.4% on 14 Oct and ENRIN fell 6.5% the next day on about 1.8x its normal volume. No company-specific cause was reported.
  • 22–23 Oct. Barclays questioned how long equipment makers can keep raising prices and margins, amid wider doubts about the payback on AI data centres. Siemens Energy AG fell 9.4% on 22 Oct, to its lowest in six months.

Over the same period, from 9 Oct, Hitachi Energy India fell 8.1%, Siemens Ltd 6.4%, ABB India 4.3%, GE Vernova T&D 3.2% and CG Power 3.0%, while the Nifty rose 1.8%. A ₹44 cr Russian court award against ENRIN (9 Oct) is a one-off and was not the driver.

Valuation now. At ₹2,812 the stock trades at 48.5xd Motilal Oswal's FY27 (September 2027) EPS, down from 53.5x on 11 Oct, against 75–103x trailing for GE Vernova T&D, ABB, CG Power and Hitachi Energy. No broker has cut its target. Trendlyne's 15-analyst consensus is ₹3,818, 36% above spot. The 23 Oct close was below the ₹2,700 "add more aggressively" level set on 11 Oct, which shows the call was right about where value lies.

What changed since 11 Oct.

  • Price: ₹3,101 → ₹2,812, a sector move.
  • Forward P/E: 53.5x → 48.5xd.
  • Consensus target: ₹3,816 → ₹3,818. There have been no new broker notes since 21 Sep.
  • Shareholding (September quarter): FII down 0.12 points to 7.60%, DII flat at 6.43%, 9,857 more shareholders.
  • Mutual funds (September disclosure): 176 schemes, up from 162, with 83 buyers and 53 sellers. Net selling was a small 1.56 lakh shares, and five thematic funds exited.
  • Ownership: the parent confirmed its stake has risen to 16% (see governance).
  • Corrections to the 11 Oct report: the Q3 results date is 6 Aug, not 7 Aug.

Counter-argument: the Barclays point applies directly to ENRIN. Its margins have risen from 18% to 24% in four quarters, partly on export pricing through the parent. If transformer pricing plateaus, as Siemens Energy's own CEO described it in August, growth has to come from volume, and that depends on capacity arriving on time. Brokers have already trimmed estimates for slower commissioning.

India's near-term HVDC ordering is also skewed toward LCC technology, where ENRIN's positioning is weaker than in VSC, and no HVDC award has been disclosed in 2026. Q4 also faces a heavy base: ₹2,646 cr of revenue in September 2025. The reported history is still only one full financial year.

Promoter & management

Promoter group (75.00%)
Siemens AG and Siemens Energy AG

The promoter block is split between the two German companies. ENRIN listed with Siemens AG holding 69% and Siemens Energy AG 6%. About 5% moved within the group in December 2025. On its 5 Aug 2026 call, Siemens Energy said the second and final swap was complete and its own stake is now 16%, which leaves Siemens AG with about 59%d unreconciled.

Siemens Energy has said it intends to take control eventually, subject to approvals. Until then, the largest holder is Siemens AG, which does not own the business's technology. Any parent-level sale, for example an OFS, is a possible supply overhang.

ENRIN depends on Siemens Energy AG for technology licences and export allocations, a related-party relationship to monitor.

Business

Nature of business

ENRIN has two segments.

  • Power Transmission (56% of 9M FY26 revenue): power transformers (Kalwa), high-voltage switchgear (Sambhajinagar), substations, HVDC and STATCOM/FACTS. Customers are utilities, transmission developers, renewable parks and, increasingly, data centres, plus exports through the parent.
  • Power Generation (44%): industrial steam turbines up to 250 MW, refurbishment of large steam turbines, and gas-turbine services and long-term service agreements. It does not manufacture gas turbines in India.

A Gurgaon R&D centre with about 1,500 engineers also serves the parent's global projects.

Moat

Strong. It rests on technology and qualification. HV transformers, GIS and HVDC need parent technology, long utility qualification and type-testing. In India the market is effectively shared among Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy and GE Vernova, and transformer capacity is sold out industry-wide.

The open question raised by the October sell-off is pricing, not share. The parent's CEO described transformer pricing in August as "intact… plateauing on a high level". That supports today's margins but not further expansion. The moat's main dependency is still the parent, which owns the technology and decides which global projects come to India.

Order book & capacity

Backlog of ₹19,331 cr at 30 Jun 26, about 2.05xd trailing revenue and up 16.4% from ₹16,601 cr a year earlier. Q3 inflow was about ₹3,294 cr, led by transmission orders including 420 kV GIS for a large solar park and a ±300 MVAr STATCOM in southern India. No new order has been announced to the exchanges since. The September-quarter backlog will come with the Q4 results.

  • Transformers: the Kalwa expansion and the switchgear expansion at Sambhajinagar total ₹740 cr over FY26–28, of which ₹460 cr is Kalwa. A ₹2,060 cr greenfield plant will take transformer capacity to 60,000 MVA by FY30–32, funded from internal accruals.
  • HVDC: ENRIN is one of three players in India with HVDC capability. Prabhudas Lilladher and Business Standard note that most upcoming Indian HVDC tenders are LCC-based and that near-term VSC opportunities are thin. No HVDC award has been disclosed in 2026.
  • Grid / STATCOM: ENRIN is the dominant STATCOM supplier, and STATCOM ordering revived from Q2 FY26.

Motilal Oswal's 2 Sep note cites an "INR135b" backlog unreconciled. That is probably the transmission book alone, but it could not be confirmed.

Risks

Sector de-rating that feeds on itself. The October sell-off was a global question about whether grid-equipment pricing has peaked. ENRIN's margin expansion came partly from export pricing through the parent. If pricing flattens, the multiple, now 48x forwardd, depends entirely on volume growth. The 52-week range of ₹2,105–₹3,968 shows how far it can swing.
  • Q4 base and order lumpiness. September 2025 was a heavy ₹2,646 cr quarter. Transmission inflow depends on TBCB tender timing and large single orders, and a quarter with book-to-bill below one would test the multiple.
  • HVDC mix. Near-term Indian HVDC ordering is LCC-heavy, where ENRIN is weaker than in VSC.
  • Capacity, not demand, sets the pace. New capacity phases in over FY26–28 and the greenfield over FY30–32. Brokers are already trimming estimates for commissioning delays.
  • Two-parent ownership. Siemens AG holds about 59%d, and technology, royalties and export allocations run through Siemens Energy AG. A parent-level OFS, or a change in royalty or export terms, would hit minority holders.
  • Inherited litigation. The ₹44 cr Russian award shows that demerger-era liabilities can surface.
  • Short and messy reported history. The September 2026 annual report is the first clean comparison.
  • Supply catch-up. Transformer capacity additions by Hitachi, GE Vernova, CG Power and domestic makers will eventually ease pricing power.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively:
    • Price below about ₹2,700, roughly 46x Motilal Oswal FY27E EPSd. This was briefly met on 23 Oct.
    • Or the Q4 FY26 print showing PAT at or above about ₹485 crd (the Motilal Oswal run-rate), inflow above ₹4,000 cr and margin at or above 22%. That would show the sector scare is not in ENRIN's numbers.
  • Downgrade to Hold:
    • Two consecutive quarters of book-to-bill below 1, with the backlog falling below about ₹17,500 cr.
    • Operating margin back below 19%, which would confirm the pricing peak in ENRIN's own numbers.
    • Brokers cutting the consensus target below ₹3,300.
  • Downgrade to Sell:
    • A stall in the transmission ordering cycle (a TBCB pause) with the backlog shrinking while the multiple stays above 60x.
    • Adverse changes to parent royalty or export terms.
    • A large promoter OFS priced well below market.
