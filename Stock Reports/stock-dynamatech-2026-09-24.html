Verdict

Update · 24 Sep 2026 — Hold reaffirmed, and the multiple has got harder to defend. Up 8.3% since 23 August, ₹11,588 to ₹12,551, taking trailing P/E from 134x to 146x on earnings the August note showed were driven by the tax rate rather than operations — profit multiplied sevenfold across four quarters while revenue was flat and operating profit rose only ₹9 cr. Return on equity is 6.6% and net debt about ₹570 cr. No new results; Q2 is due late October. Nothing here has improved to justify the higher price.

The quarterly recovery is genuine and the valuation has already priced several more years of it. Net profit has gone ₹3 cr → ₹6 cr → ₹13 cr → ₹21 cr across the last four quarters, with June up 93% year on year and EBITDA margin up 278 basis points to 12.97%. After two dreadful years — profit fell from ₹122 cr in FY24 to ₹32 cr in FY26 — the company is visibly mending.

What you pay for that is 134x trailing earnings and 9.3x book, on a business earning 6.6% on equity and 10.0% on capital. Annualise the best quarter it has had in three years and the multiple is still about 94x. Return on capital below 10% and a price-to-book above 9 is a combination that only works if profits multiply several times over, quickly.

There is a real thesis underneath. Dynamatic is one of very few Indian companies with flying hardware on Airbus and Boeing programmes as well as HAL work, and the aerospace segment grew 17% to ₹202 cr in the June quarter — it is now the largest of the three segments and the highest quality. ICICI Direct carries a Buy with a ₹12,700 target, set when the stock was ₹10,590 and now 10% above the market.

Against that: ₹570 cr of net debt on ₹794 cr of equity, ₹15 cr of interest every quarter against ₹55 cr of operating profit, and free cash flow of ₹30 cr in FY26 on a ₹7,870 cr market capitalisation. The business is improving from a low base with a levered balance sheet, and the shares are priced as though the improvement is finished. Hold, and treat it as high risk.

Business

What it does

Founded in 1973, Dynamatic runs three businesses. Aerospace — now half of revenue — makes flying hardware: airframe structures and assemblies for Airbus and Boeing programmes, and work for HAL on Indian defence platforms. Hydraulics makes gear pumps, where the company is a long-standing supplier to tractor and construction-equipment makers. Metallurgy makes automotive turbochargers and precision castings. It has plants in India and Europe and sells across six continents.

Moat

Narrow but genuine where it exists. Flying hardware requires airframer qualification — Airbus and Boeing audit and certify a supplier's processes for a specific part, and requalifying elsewhere is expensive and slow, so nominated work tends to stay. That applies to roughly half the company. Hydraulics and metallurgy have no such protection: they compete on cost against larger specialists, which is visible in an 11–13% operating margin that has not moved in three years.

Risks

134x earnings for a business earning 6.6% on equity, with ₹570 cr of net debt. Even annualising the best quarter in three years leaves the multiple near 94x. There is no valuation support at all if the recovery stalls.
  • The profit recovery is partly a tax artefact. The effective rate went from 72% to 21% across four quarters while operating profit rose only ₹9 cr. That is not repeatable growth.
  • Interest consumes the business. ₹15 cr a quarter against ₹55 cr of operating profit, on debt that has not fallen in three years.
  • Free cash flow of ₹30 cr against a ₹7,870 cr market capitalisation — a 0.4% yield on the cash the business actually generates.
  • Very thin liquidity. 0.23 lakh shares a day. On a stock this volatile, exiting a position of any size will move the price against you.
  • Index-driven ownership. Much of the fund holding is defence-index product. If the stock leaves an index, those holders are forced sellers regardless of the fundamentals.
  • Order book unverified. The number supporting the bull case is a broker expectation, not a company disclosure.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: two more quarters of ₹20 cr-plus net profit driven by operating profit rather than the tax line, with net debt falling below ₹450 cr — or a company-published order book that stands up to the ₹11,000 cr claim.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating margin back below 11%, net debt through ₹650 cr, or a quarter where the tax rate normalises and profit falls back towards ₹10 cr, which would expose the multiple.
