Verdict

The valuation discount that made this interesting at listing has largely closed — before the company has published a single quarter as a listed entity. Laser Power makes power cables, conductors and specialised components for the T&D industry, and also runs an EPC arm. At the ₹214 IPO it came at ~15x FY26 earnings, a wide discount to Polycab/KEI at 40x+. Three weeks later the stock is ₹299 (+40% over issue, market cap ₹4,202 cr), which is ~28x FY26 earnings. Still below the large-cap peers — but the gap is no longer the compelling part of the case.

What remains unresolved is the thing the discount was pricing: FY26 revenue fell ~9% to ₹2,348 cr while PAT jumped 42% to ₹151.6 cr. That is a margin-driven profit spike on shrinking sales, and IPO analysts (SP Tulsian, Value Research) flagged it as possibly unsustainable. The ₹3,243 cr order book (+40% YoY, ~1.4x revenue) argues revenue re-accelerates in FY27, and a new TS Conductor partnership for HTLS conductors adds a genuinely premium product line. But with no Q1 FY27 disclosure yet, no listed track record and anchor lock-ins ahead, there is nothing here that requires acting before the first print. Wait for it.

Business

Incorporated 1988, Kolkata-based. Two segments: manufacturing — LT/HT power cables, conductors (including specialised/high-efficiency types) and components for transmission & distribution — and EPC, executing T&D projects. Customers are utilities, EPC contractors and industrial buyers. Fresh-issue proceeds (₹542 cr) are earmarked for capacity expansion and working capital. Competes against far larger listed peers: Polycab, KEI Industries, Apar Industries, Universal Cables, Dynamic Cables — and now, in your library, against RR Kabel, which is compounding faster from a much bigger base.

New: the TS Conductor partnership. Laser Power received its first Letter of Intent from HPSEBL — ₹4.15 cr for an HTLS conductor project under its TS Conductor tie-up. The order is tiny, but the strategic content is not: HTLS (high-temperature, low-sag) conductors are the premium product used to re-conductor existing lines for higher throughput without new towers — exactly what India's grid-upgrade cycle needs, and a higher-margin niche than commodity conductor. Treat this as an option on mix improvement, and watch whether follow-on orders scale.

Bull case

  • Same structural driver as Transrail: India's grid build-out and renewable evacuation need conductors and cables in volumes incumbents can't fully serve — one layer down the supply chain from the EPC names.
  • Order book cover: ₹3,243 cr, up 40% YoY, ~1.4 years of revenue — points to FY27 revenue recovery after the FY26 decline.
  • Still a peer discount: ~28x vs Polycab ~50x and RR Kabel ~44x leaves re-rating room if execution proves out.
  • HTLS optionality via TS Conductor — a credible route into premium product mix rather than commodity volume.
  • Fresh capital in hand (₹542 cr) to fund the growth the order book implies.

Risks

FY26 may be peak earnings, and you're now paying ~28x for it. PAT +42% on revenue −9% means margin did all the work. If commodity (copper/aluminium) tailwinds and mix reverse, the earnings base falls and the multiple re-rates against you simultaneously. The discount that cushioned this at ₹214 is much thinner at ₹299.
  • No listed track record. Three weeks old; the first quarterly print will set the tone violently in either direction, and it has not arrived.
  • Lock-in supply: anchor and pre-IPO lock-in expiries over the next 1–6 months create overhang into a stock that has already run 40%.
  • Scale disadvantage versus Polycab/KEI/RR Kabel in distribution, brand and working-capital access; cables is a thin-margin volume game where scale compounds.
  • EPC working capital: the EPC segment brings utility receivable cycles that strain cash exactly when growth accelerates.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a first print showing revenue growth genuinely returning with EBITDA margin holding — that would confirm FY26 wasn't peak — or a fade back toward ₹230–250 with the order book intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: Q1 confirming margin normalisation on flat revenue, or order-book conversion slipping.
