Verdict

What changed since 12 Oct 2026: upgraded from Hold to Buy. The price has fallen 31.7%, from ₹2,721.50 to ₹1,858. That breaches the last note's own "Upgrade to Buy" trigger: a pullback to about ₹2,250 with the margin trend intact. The margin trend held. Q2 FY27 (declared 28 Oct) beat the 13 Oct consensus on revenue (₹3,284 cr vs ₹3,055 cr), EBITDA and profit. The W&C EBIT margin was 9.4% (H1 9.6%), against a 9.5% FY27 guide, and management kept both the 9.5% FY27 and 10.5% FY28 targets. The damage is to volume: W&C volume grew only about 8% in Q2, cables slowed to single digits, and FY27 volume guidance was cut from 16–18% to 14–15%. Consensus EPS did not move (FY28 ₹80.06, up 1% in the past week); only the multiple did. Two Sell tripwires were touched on a literal reading: FMEG lost ₹12 cr in Q2, and the price closed below ₹2,000 after the print. Both are weighed in the trigger test below. Neither changes the call.

At ₹1,858 the stock pays for less growth than the company is delivering. It trades at 28.6xd FY27 and 23.2xd FY28 consensus EPS, below Polycab (33.0x / 29.3x) and KEI (32.5x / 27.6x). Five weeks ago it was the most expensive cable maker on every line. Earnings expectations are intact: FY27 EPS is forecast to grow 49% and FY28 23%. ROCE is 28%. Management's 14–15% volume guide is still above Polycab's implied 12–13% and KEI's "over 10%". The fear the last note centred on also eased in Q2: in the first month of Ultravolt, RR Kabel's wires grew in double digits.

The fall is not irrational. It priced the first real cracks in the growth story. Cables were meant to lead, with 80% of capex behind them, yet they grew slower than wires. Gross margin fell 100 bps year on year to 17.9%. Interest cost doubled, borrowings rose ₹241 cr in six months, and consensus now expects negative FY27 free cash flow (−₹124 cr), down from +₹165 cr in October. The promoter group's stake also fell 2.05 points in the September quarter, and no sale disclosure was found. But earnings per share have not been cut, the company is still guiding the margin up, and the price sits inside the "tail" range the 13 Oct deep dive drew for a price war plus a copper reversal, neither of which has happened. For a holder this is a Buy: the risk-reward has swung from about +9%/−20% in October to about +12–29% in the base case against −6% to −14% in the bear case. Add in tranches rather than all at once, because the Q3 print (due by 14 Feb 2027) still has to show cables re-accelerating.

2 · Competitive threat and business quality

  • Ultravolt: no visible damage yet, one month in. On the Q1 call, management put domestic wire margins at 11–12%, against 6–7% for domestic cables, 11–12% for export cables and about 5% for export wires. Domestic house wire is therefore RR Kabel's best business and exactly what UltraTech launched first: ₹1,800 cr at Jhagadia, 10.98 lakh km a year of capacity, more than 1 lakh retailers at launch and richer channel incentives. In Q2, wires grew in double digits overall. Domestic W&C revenue grew 41% (PL), and exports grew faster. Asked about the new competition on 29 Oct, management said only that "competition was always there". No RR Kabel price action or Ultravolt pricing update since launch was found. One month is not a verdict, so the 15–75 bps margin-cost estimates (Nuvama, Kotak, others; unreconciled) still stand as the main bear input.
  • Cables are the new question. Cables grew in single digits while wires grew in double digits, and Umang Mehta (Kotak) got management to confirm that domestic cable growth was lower still ("not declined"). Peers reported similar softness. Polycab's Q2 underlying volume was mid-to-high single digit, KEI guided "over 10%" for FY27 and trimmed its margin band to about 11%, and a Sundaram fund manager on the call noted that "all the peers in the industry are reporting kind of low volumes". This looks like an industry volume slowdown with copper inflating revenue, not an RR Kabel share loss. It still undercuts the mix-shift story behind the premium the stock used to carry.
  • Other entrants. Adani's Praneetha Ecocables JV (Mar 2025) still has no launch found; no JSW wires entry was found. Industry capacity is rising in cables, and the Sundaram question on the call was about exactly that. Management said it sees no slowdown in growth drivers.
  • Channel. More than 1.5 lakh retail touch points, adding 20,000–25,000 a year (Q1 call). North and West are about 65% of domestic sales. B2C vs B2B is not disclosed. No update on Q2.
  • FMEG: a seasonal loss, the full-year target kept. Q2 revenue was ₹274 cr (+42%, led by appliances and new products) with a ₹12.4 cr EBIT loss, against breakeven in Q1. Management had warned on 27 Jul that Q2 breakeven "may not be possible": the range is fan-heavy and Q2 is the low season. The FY27 segment-breakeven target was reaffirmed "in spite of the cumulative losses of around INR 17 crores", a figure that does not match the deck's ₹12 cr H1 loss and is unreconciled. The Roorkee FMEG plant is being relocated to leased premises, with more capacity and no planned production disruption.
  • Capex is growing, not peaking. FY27 capex stays at ₹550–600 cr; ₹280 cr was spent in H1. Two new items sit on top. The board approved ₹200 cr for land in Rajasthan (about ₹100 cr in H2, the rest in H1 FY28) as the first step of a new, still-undisclosed W&C capex plan, due "after one or two quarters". The UMCL optical-fibre slump sale (₹77 cr cash, Usha Martin subsidiary) completed on 31 Oct. Management gave no revenue guidance for it this year and said the plant runs at low utilisation; one analyst put its capacity at 80,000 fibre-km a year. FY28 capex is pencilled at about ₹300 cr. The deep dive's hope that cash flow improves "as capex peaks in FY27" is weaker: consensus FY27 FCF is now −₹124 cr (from +₹165 cr) and FY28 +₹370 cr (from +₹444 cr).
  • Balance sheet: still comfortable on the headline, tighter underneath. Borrowings were ₹473 cr in September (₹243 cr long-term plus ₹230 cr short-term), against ₹232 cr in March. Cash and liquid investments were ₹46 cr, giving net debt of ₹427 crd before leases. Finance cost doubled year on year to ₹33 cr a quarter. LC-backed metal payables of about ₹1,290 cr (management, 29 Oct) sit inside trade payables. Adding them gives adjusted net debt of roughly ₹1,720 crd, or about 1.7xd TTM EBITDA. That is above India Ratings' 1.5x downgrade guide, although India Ratings put FY26 at 1.78x and still upgraded the company to IND AA on 16 Sep. The methods differ and the comparison is unreconciled. Management called its leverage "around 0.55", which matches debt plus acceptances over equity (0.58xd), and said debt will rise further in value terms.
  • Holders: a 2-point promoter-group reduction in the September quarter. Promoter holding fell from 61.38% to 59.33%. Screener's holder detail puts it in four promoter-group lines: the Esses Shares Family Private Trust (trustee Mahendrakumar Kabra) from 1.46% to 0.01%, Hemant Kabra from 5.83% to 5.47%, Sarita Jhawar from 3.88% to 3.70% and Sumeet Kabra from 1.46% to 1.38%. The Hemlata Home Solutions company-to-LLP change is internal and nets to zero. That is about 23 lakh shares, roughly ₹550–700 crd at the quarter's prices. No SAST disclosure, bulk deal or block deal for it was found (Trendlyne lists none after Nov 2025), so whether these were market sales or transfers out of the group is unreconciled. It is the first meaningful promoter reduction since listing and came near the top. FIIs rose to 12.51% and DIIs fell to 10.25% (mutual funds 9.70%).

Business

Nature of business

The company has two segments. Wires & cables (house wire, industrial and power cable, and now optical fibre through UMCL) is 92% of Q2 revenue and all of the profit, and 32% of revenue is exported, chiefly to Europe and the Middle East. FMEG (fans, lighting, switches and appliances, including the range bought from Luminous in 2022) is 8% of revenue. It grew 42% in Q2 but lost ₹12 cr in its seasonally weak quarter, and is targeting full-year breakeven in FY27. Q2 broke the recent pattern: wires grew in double digits and cables in single digits. Revenue growth of 52% on about 8% volume was mostly copper price and export mix.

Moat

Distribution. In Indian house wire, the electrician and dealer choose the brand at the point of installation, so the network is the barrier and the product is close to a commodity. Ultravolt tests this moat directly with UltraTech's building-materials network and balance sheet. Q2 is the first data point, and it was favourable: wires grew in double digits in Ultravolt's first month. One month proves little. The moat is stronger in cables (specification-driven, institutional and export customers) and exports (3–4 new international approvals a year), but that is where Q2 growth disappointed. The new Rajasthan plan and the optical-fibre buy extend the cable bet.

Order book

Not a meaningful concept for this business. Wires and cables are sold through distribution and short-cycle institutional orders, with no disclosed backlog. UMCL's optical-fibre business has no disclosed order book.

Risks

The volume story could keep slipping while the cash story gets worse. The FY27 guide implies H2 volume growth in the high teens, on a base that grew about 30% in Q3 FY26. That is what PL's analyst pressed on the call, and a second cut would hit a stock that has just shown how it reacts. Meanwhile capex is rising (₹550–600 cr, plus Rajasthan land and UMCL), FY27 FCF is expected to be negative and debt is rising. If W&C margin also stalls at about 9.4% instead of reaching 10.5%, FY28 EPS falls toward the bear case's ₹72. At 22x, that gives about ₹1,600d, 14% below today.
  • Ultravolt (unchanged, untested). One month of data. Analysts' 15–75 bps estimates of the cost of defending share are the largest single swing factor in FY28 EPS, and RR Kabel's exposure to domestic house wire is among the highest of the listed names.
  • Copper. Revenue growth is mostly copper (52% revenue growth on about 8% volume). Gross margin is already 100 bps lower on "very high raw material base prices". A reversal would cut reported growth and could bring an inventory loss.
  • Funding and leverage. Borrowings doubled in six months, and about ₹1,290 cr of LC-backed payables sits on top. Interest doubled year on year. Adjusted leverage of about 1.7xd is above India Ratings' 1.5x monitorable on a rough basis (unreconciled with the agency's method).
  • Promoter-group selling. A 2.05-point reduction in the September quarter, with no disclosure found. More of it would weigh on the stock even if the business performs.
  • FMEG durability. Back to a loss in Q2. The full-year breakeven now depends on H2 fan season.
  • Export concentration. 32% of revenue, chiefly Europe and the Middle East. Export growth (+80%) carried Q2, and management expects the share to stay at 30–35%.
