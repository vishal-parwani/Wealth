Verdict

Do not own this. The listed entity itself reported nil revenue from operations in FY26 and in Q1 FY27, and a standalone loss of ₹1.09 crore. Every rupee of the ₹22,426 crore consolidated revenue and ₹450 crore consolidated profit comes from a foreign subsidiary in the Seychelles that the Indian statutory auditor did not audit. That auditor has since resigned, its FY26 opinion was qualified, and its Q1 FY27 review recorded that the company "has neither paid nor provided for GST during the review period."

The arithmetic does not survive contact. A group claiming ₹22,426 crore of FY26 revenue — which would place it among India's hundred largest companies by turnover — reported total employee benefits expense of ₹39 lakh for the year, depreciation of ₹1.09 crore, fixed assets of ₹5 crore, and zero tax on ₹450 crore of pre-tax profit. In Q1 FY27 it booked ₹7,385 crore of revenue against a staff cost of ₹3.71 lakh. No business trades ₹22,000 crore of ferrous and non-ferrous scrap on ₹39 lakh of payroll.

The market is not fooled — it is simply not present. At ₹602 crore the stock trades at 1.04x claimed earnings and 0.35x claimed book. A P/E of one is not a valuation; it is the market declining to credit the numbers. What is happening instead is a price ladder: the stock has closed at a limit price with no intraday range in 83 of the last 124 sessions, including several with literally zero shares traded, and has run twelve consecutive upper circuits from ₹84.14 on 12 August to ₹150.64 today. On 25 August it rose 4.99% on 1,319 shares — roughly ₹1.8 lakh of turnover repricing ₹602 crore of market capitalisation. With 92.82% held by the promoter, the entire free float is ₹43 crore.

There is also a mechanical overhang that is not a matter of opinion. The company exited CIRP in December 2024 and is required to bring promoter holding down — the auditor notes the post-CIRP requirement to get below 90%, and minimum-public-shareholding rules require 25% public holding within three years. Promoter stake was still 92.82% at 30 June 2026. A very large promoter sell-down into a ₹43 crore float is not a risk; it is a scheduled obligation.

This note makes no allegation of wrongdoing. It reports what the company's own audited and reviewed filings say, and concludes that the reported figures cannot be relied on as a basis for valuation.

Business

Nature of business

Incorporated in 1994 as Nova-era garment maker Frontline Business Solutions Limited, which later became a staffing and BPO operator, went through insolvency, and was renamed Maxgrow India after the resolution plan took effect in December 2024. It now describes itself as a trader of ferrous and non-ferrous scrap metals with interests in recycling, value-added metal processing and mining infrastructure. The listed entity conducts none of this: its standalone revenue from operations is nil. The activity sits in a Seychelles subsidiary. Note 6 to both the FY26 and Q1 FY27 results states the company "has derived its revenue from rendering of services", which contradicts both the stated scrap-trading business and the "cost of sales" line in its own profit and loss account.

Moat

None. Commodity scrap trading at a reported 2% operating margin has no barrier to entry, no pricing power and no switching cost. Nothing in the filings identifies a customer, a supplier, a yard, a weighbridge, a licence or a contract.

Risks

The reported financials may not represent real economic activity. Nil standalone revenue, an unaudited offshore subsidiary in a secrecy jurisdiction generating 100% of group turnover, zero tax, no GST paid or provided, ₹39 lakh of annual payroll against ₹22,426 crore of sales, and an accounting system without an audit trail. If the subsidiary's numbers are ever restated, there is nothing underneath them.

  • Auditor resignation. The statutory auditor left the day it signed the interim review, having just issued a qualified FY26 opinion. Successor auditors routinely reopen prior-period judgements.
  • Unquantified GST exposure. Scrap metal supply in India is taxable. A quarter with ₹7,385 crore of sales and no GST paid or provided implies either an enormous unrecorded statutory liability or that the "sales" are not taxable supplies. Neither reading is good.
  • Forced promoter sell-down. Minimum public shareholding must reach 25% within three years of the resolution plan. Getting from 7.19% to 25% means selling roughly 71 lakh shares into a market that trades 1,358 a day.
  • No exit. 83 of the last 124 sessions closed with no intraday range at all, several on zero volume. A holder cannot sell into a limit-down sequence; the price simply steps down 5% a day with no trades.
  • Regulatory action. The company has already been penalised by the exchange for listing non-compliances and has breached Regulation 33 filing timelines. A price rise of this shape in a 7% float is the standard trigger for exchange surveillance escalation, which typically freezes liquidity further.
  • Delisting / suspension. Persistent non-compliance following a resolution plan carries suspension risk, which would strand the position entirely.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: a full-scope audit of the Seychelles subsidiary by a firm of standing, with the GST position quantified and settled; a clean successor-auditor opinion on FY27; named customers and counterparties; and daily turnover sustained above ₹5 crore with the promoter below 75%. Any one of these on its own is not enough.
  • Confirmation of Sell: the new auditor qualifies or resigns; the GST liability is quantified; FY26 or FY27 figures are restated; the exchange escalates surveillance or suspends the scrip; or the promoter sell-down begins into this float.
