Verdict

Hold. At 22x trailing earnings the stock is priced like its listed copper peers, but most of its growth comes from the copper price and it makes 2.2% operating margin. That is a fair price for the business. It is not a cheap one, and the price is now 3.8x where it stood a year ago.

What follows is the case on the business.

The evidence for the business is real. Standalone revenue went from ₹1,397 cr to ₹2,765 cr in FY26, and profit rose from ₹14 cr to ₹40 cr. Operating margin went from 1.7% to 2.2%. Management puts that down to value-added products: tinned copper (ATC) wire and busbars, which it started making in April and September 2025. EBITDA per tonne rose from about ₹14k to ₹23k. ROE is 36% on a standalone basis. In Q1 FY27 consolidated revenue was ₹784 cr, up 75%.

The counter-argument is the price-volume split. In Q1 FY27 copper revenue rose 72.9%, but tonnes sold rose only 15.4%. Management said on the April call that FY26 revenue roughly doubled on about 50% more volume, with the rest coming from copper prices. The company passes copper through at a thin spread, so a fall in copper shrinks revenue just as quickly. The margin gain is the part worth paying for, and that is 50 basis points so far. Other worries: there are only 901 shareholders, ₹1.65 cr trades a day, and the aluminium arm was bought from the promoter family.

Business

Nature of business

Sunlite buys copper scrap from more than 10 countries and the domestic market, then melts and refines it at Kheda, Gujarat into FRHC and oxygen-free copper rods, wires, strips and conductors. It is moving downstream into tinned copper (ATC) wire for solar, and into busbars. About 80% of sales go to cable makers, 5–10% to transformer makers and the rest to enamel-wire producers. Customers are entirely B2B, and the top 20 account for about half of revenue. Aluminium rods come through SAPL, which has 12,000 t of capacity running at 56% in FY26. Management says it physically hedges copper, matching purchases to sales, which is how it keeps a stable spread on a volatile metal.

Moat

Thin. The edge is sourcing: knowing which scrap grades to buy at what price. Customer quality approvals at cable makers also slow new entrants down. Neither stops a well-capitalised competitor. Margins of 2% leave little room for error, and pricing power belongs to the large cable buyers. The shift to ATC and busbars is the only lever that has moved the spread so far.

Order book

An order book is not a meaningful measure here. Sales are run-rate contracts priced off the copper rate. Capacity utilisation is the better forward indicator, and the copper rod line is at 93.6%. Growth beyond FY27 therefore depends on the 20,000 t expansion due in Q1 FY28.

Risks

The copper price. Revenue grew 73% in Q1 on 15% more tonnes. If copper falls 20%, revenue falls with it even if tonnes keep growing. If the physical hedge slips, inventory losses would wipe out a 2% margin quickly. Inventory was ₹87 cr standalone at March 2026, about two years of profit.
  • Liquidity. About ₹1.65 cr trades a day, among roughly 900 holders, on the SME platform. Moves of 25% in two days happen in both directions, and an exit at size is not guaranteed.
  • Related parties. The aluminium arm was bought from the family, the board is all family, and short-term loans and advances rose from ₹6 cr to ₹41 cr standalone in FY26. That last item could be funding for the subsidiary. Look for the breakdown in the annual report.
  • Cash conversion. FY26 operating cash flow was roughly nil and free cash flow was −₹9 cr. Management attributes this to inventory build to support higher volume.
  • Half-yearly disclosure. The last full profit figure is to March 2026. Q1 FY27 revenue was disclosed with no margin.
  • Capex execution. The ₹37 cr expansion and a planned copper anode plant are both slated for FY27–28. A delay would cap volume, because the existing rod line is close to full.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 (due November) holding EBITDA margin at or above 2.3% with volume growth above 15%. That would show the value-added mix is lifting the spread, not just the copper price. A price back near ₹500 (about 15x FY27 arithmetic EPS) would also do it.
  • Downgrade to Sell: EBITDA margin back below 1.9% in H1 FY27, a further related-party transaction with the promoter group, or the expansion slipping past Q1 FY28.
