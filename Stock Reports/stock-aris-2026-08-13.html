Verdict

Real earnings inflection, genuinely cheap headline multiple, and a working-capital model that quietly eats the profit it reports. The optics are excellent: FY26 revenue ₹1,067 cr (+39%) with PAT up more than tenfold to ₹60.3 cr, then Q1 FY27 revenue +37% and PAT ~4x to ₹20 cr with EBITDA margin widening to 10.5% from 8.6%. Annualise Q1 and the stock is on roughly 13x at ₹136.40. For 37% growth that looks like an obvious bargain.

The reason it is not obvious: ArisInfra gives its customers five months of credit and receives one month from its suppliers. Debtor days ran from 102 in FY22 to 137 by FY24. That is not a rounding detail in a business earning ~10% EBITDA on construction materials — it means every rupee of growth pre-funds a developer's balance sheet, and the faster it grows the more cash it absorbs. The company is net cash today, but it got there by raising IPO money and repaying debt, not by generating it. Mukul Agrawal's fresh 1.59% is a real signal and the margin trend is legitimately improving. Own it small, and watch receivables more closely than earnings.

Business

A technology-enabled B2B platform for bulk construction materials — steel, cement, ready-mix concrete and aggregates — connecting real-estate and infrastructure developers with suppliers, plus material financing. Three legs: core materials supply, contract manufacturing, and a Developer-as-a-Service vertical. Mix by revenue: aggregates ~44%, ready-mix concrete ~19–20%. Classified under Industrials / Building Products; the MCA classification is bluntly honest about what it is — "other wholesale n.e.c."

Call the model what it is. The "tech-enabled platform" framing is accurate but does not change the economics: this is distribution. Gross margins are wholesale margins, the moat is procurement scale and developer relationships rather than software, and it competes with much larger, better-capitalised players in Infra.Market and OfBusiness. Judge it on inventory turns, credit losses and working capital — the metrics you would apply to a distributor — not on the multiples you would pay for a marketplace.

Bull case

  • The margin trend is real — 8.6% → 10.5% EBITDA YoY, helped by buying direct from manufacturers rather than through intermediaries.
  • Operating leverage is showing up — revenue +37% translating into PAT +292% means fixed costs are being covered.
  • Undemanding multiple — ~13x annualised Q1 earnings for a business compounding revenue near 40%.
  • Net cash balance sheet post-IPO, removing near-term solvency risk.
  • Mukul Agrawal's 1.59% — a specialist small-cap investor taking a fresh position after the stock had already fallen 45% from IPO, i.e. buying the business rather than the listing.
  • Structural tailwind — Indian construction-materials procurement is fragmented and genuinely inefficient; organising it is a real opportunity.

Risks

The working-capital asymmetry is the thesis-breaker. Five months of credit extended, one month received. Debtor days 137 and rising. On ~10% EBITDA margins, a business like this can report growing profits for years while consuming cash the entire time — and the cash it is currently living on is IPO proceeds, which are finite. If receivable days do not come down as revenue scales, reported PAT will keep diverging from cash flow and the low P/E will stay low for a good reason. Check the cash-flow statement before the P&L on this one.
  • Customers are real-estate developers — a counterparty class with a long history of stretching payables and, in downturns, not paying at all. ₹20 cr of debtors were credit-impaired over FY22–9MFY25 on ₹2,440 cr of cumulative revenue (~0.8% booked as bad). That rate is tolerable now; it is not stable through a property downcycle.
  • Structurally low RoE — the long receivables cycle mechanically caps returns; commentary at IPO flagged low-single-digit RoE as the natural state of this model.
  • Thin margins, large competitors — Infra.Market and OfBusiness are far better capitalised and can outspend on price.
  • Poor listing record — IPO'd at ₹222 in June 2025, listed at a 5.8% discount and fell ~45% within a year. Something about the original pricing or disclosure did not survive contact with public markets.
  • Short public history — barely one year of listed reporting, and the profit base is small enough that a couple of large bad debts would erase a quarter.
  • Cyclicality — directly geared to Indian construction activity.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: two consecutive halves with debtor days falling and positive operating cash flow while revenue still grows 25%+. That combination would prove the model funds itself.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: receivable days pushing past ~150, a step-up in credit impairments, or operating cash flow staying negative through FY27.
