Verdict

Cheap on earnings, expensive on debt and working capital. Hold, high risk. At ₹261.75 the stock trades at about 24x FY26 profit on the post-IPO share count, against 45x for KEI and 27x for Universal Cables. The operating trend is good. FY26 EBITDA rose 20% to ₹301 cr while revenue fell 10%, because management chose higher-margin manufacturing over low-margin EPC turnover. The June quarter's margin was 12.6%, against 11.5% a year earlier. The order book was ₹2,788 cr at June, about 1.2x revenue, and the company has added ₹117 cr of orders sinced.

The problem is everything beneath EBITDA. Interest was ₹36 cr in the June quarter against ₹66 cr of EBITDA, and ₹133 cr against ₹301 cr in FY26. Borrowings rose from ₹504 cr to ₹871 cr in FY26 as receivables climbed to ₹1,375 cr (216 days). Operating cash flow was −₹119 cr. FY26 profit also leaned on ₹55 cr of other income, 28% of pre-tax profit. The ₹542 cr fresh issue in July should cut the debt, but the company has not yet reported a balance sheet that shows it.

So the low multiple is earned. Until a listed quarter shows interest falling and receivables turning, this is a cheap stock with an expensive balance sheet. Stays Hold, with the high-risk tag.

What changed since 24 Sep 2026. The price is down 4.6%, from ₹274.50 to ₹261.75, its lowest close since the 16 July listing. It is still 22% above the ₹214 IPO price. Rating unchanged: Hold, high risk. New facts are mostly positive. The company won a ₹72.76 cr POWERGRID order (5 Oct), its first commercial order for HTLS conductors, made under licence from TS Conductor (USA). That's a technology credential, not just volume. It also won a ₹44 cr CESC EPC order (29 Sep), its first 220 kV underground-cable job. And it received a provisional allotment of about 40 acres in a West Bengal industrial park for expansion (24 Sep). On governance, the board approved reclassifying two promoter-group companies that hold no shares as public (23 Sep), which is housekeeping, and the company secretary's resignation was reported the same day. Against that, the August fund disclosure shows four schemes selling and none buying; Bandhan Small Cap and Motilal Oswal Large Cap roughly halved their positions. No new results or shareholding yet. Q2 FY27 and the first September shareholding pattern are both due in the coming weeks.

Deep dive · added 13 Oct 2026, price ₹254

Keep holding; don't add before the September-quarter print. The low headline P/E flatters the stock. FY26 profit included a one-off gain of about ₹32 cr, so on underlying earnings the stock trades at about 30x. That is in line with Universal Cables, the closest listed match, not at a discount to it. The IPO really has repaired the balance sheet, which supports the bull case. But receivables, cash conversion and a thin June-quarter order inflow are still unproven. The last anchor shares unlocked on 12 Oct, and the stock is breaking below its listing low. A base case of about +8% against −40% in a bad case doesn't justify adding.

The order book is the number to watch. The FY26 investor deck puts the March book at ₹3,243 cr. The June book was ₹2,788 cr after ₹522 cr of revenue, which implies only about ₹70 cr of new orders in the quarterd. By segment, manufacturing took about ₹146 cr against a FY26 average of ₹531 cr a quarter. EPC is negative by about ₹80 cr, which points to de-scoping or a change in basis. The EPC book includes GST and the cable sourced from the company's own plants, so the arithmetic is approximate. The deck also gives the June book as both ₹27,844 mn and ₹27,884 mn. On the interest line, management guides about ₹40 cr a year of savings (9% on ₹490 cr). Repayment happened during Q2, so only part of the saving shows this quarter. Q3 should run at about ₹26 cr a quarterd. Q1's year-on-year profit gap is now reconciled. On the call, the CFO said consolidated FY26 included a one-time gain of about ₹32 cr, which sits in the June-2025 quarter. Standalone PAT rose from ₹16.4 cr to ₹21.1 cr (+28.8%).

2 · Balance sheet and quality of earnings

  • Debt after the IPO. On the call, the CFO said ₹490 cr was repaid, leaving gross debt of about ₹360 cr against ₹240 cr of fixed deposits, which he called "negligible" net debt. Those deposits are margin money held against bank guarantees and letters of credit, so they aren't free cash. Gross debt to post-IPO equity is about 0.28xd, against 1.2x at March. Sources differ on the March figure: Screener shows ₹871 cr of borrowings, while the prospectus and Chittorgarh show ₹828 cr. The difference is probably lease and other items.
  • Interest coverage. EBITDA covered interest 2.3x in FY26 and 1.8x in Q1, and EBIT covered it about 1.6xd. At the guided saving, coverage is about 2.5xd. That is better, but still thin next to KEI or Polycab, which have almost no debt.
  • Earnings quality. Management told analysts to treat FY26 as about ₹120 cr, not ₹152 cr, because of the ₹32 cr one-off; the standalone deck shows ₹119 cr. Other income was ₹55 cr, 28% of pre-tax profit. Tax is a timing benefit: carried-forward losses from the FY23 Bhuvee Stenovate unit (acquired through the NCLT) save about ₹125–130 cr of cash tax, used up in FY27. The P&L already books normal tax, but cash tax starts in FY28.
  • Cash conversion is the weak link. Over FY22–26, operating cash flow totalled ₹196 cr against ₹936 cr of EBITDA (21%d), and free cash flow was about −₹7 crd over five years. Receivable days were 216 at March (Screener). The company's own working-capital days were 77, 86 and 129 for FY24–26, a narrower measure that nets off payables. Both are rising. Management says ₹800 cr of EPC projects started in Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 tied up inventory, and that this should unwind as billing catches up.
  • Related parties: small. The prospectus shows purchases from promoter-linked entities (G.M. Dalui & Sons, UIC Udyog, Ceebuild and others) of at most about 1.4% of revenue a year. Rent paid to a related party was ₹4.2 cr in FY26, three times FY25. Promoters have given personal guarantees on bank loans, and no shares are pledged. Contingent liabilities at March were ₹26.8 cr, including a ₹10.1 cr performance guarantee.
  • The IPO. It ran 9–13 July 2026 at ₹214 (band ₹203–214) for ₹742 cr: a ₹542 cr fresh issue and a ₹200 cr promoter sale. It was subscribed 28.97x and listed on 16 July. Anchors bought ₹222.6 cr (1.04 cr shares) on 8 July. Half unlocked on 12–13 Aug and the rest on 11–12 Oct (sources differ by a day), which is about 0.52 cr shares, or two days of average volumed. Promoter shares above the 18-month minimum unlock about 14 Jan 2027d, six months after allotment. Promoters hold 75.30%, slightly above the 75% public-float ceiling, so a small sell-down is due within three years.
  • Governance flags. Company secretary: the resignation rests on one source (SAHI, Sep 2026). Neither the exchange filing list nor a web search turned up the Regulation 30 letter, and Debendra Banthiya still signed filings on 29 Sep. So it is unverified, not confirmed. Missing quarters: the Sep and Dec 2025 quarters were never published because the company was unlisted. That isn't a breach, but it means no listed year-on-year base until Q4. Auditor: V. Singhi & Associates, a Kolkata firm, not a large network. That isn't a flag in itself, but any qualification or emphasis-of-matter on receivables would be. Subsidiary: Akshat Builders, an SPV with no revenue, is the gap between consolidated and standalone figures.

Business

Nature of business

Two linked businesses. Manufacturing makes power cables, conductors and, now, HTLS conductors under licence for transmission and distribution. EPC builds substations, transmission lines and underground cable projects, mostly for state utilities, POWERGRID, NTPC and private distributors such as CESC. The order book is split roughly evenly between the two (₹1,433 cr and ₹1,356 cr at June). The FY26 shift towards manufacturing explains why revenue fell while EBITDA rose.

Moat

Limited, though improving. Utility vendor approvals and type-testing create some barriers to entry, and the HTLS licence plus the first POWERGRID HTLS order is a real step up from commodity conductors. But most of the product range is close to a commodity, and EPC work is won on competitive bids. The advantage lies in execution and relationships.

Order book

₹2,788 cr confirmed at 30 Jun 2026 (₹1,433 cr manufacturing, ₹1,356 cr EPC), about 1.2x FY26 revenue. Since then the company has announced ₹117 cr of new orders (CESC ₹44 cr, POWERGRID ₹72.8 cr), plus a small HPSEBL reconductoring job. The CESC award is a letter of intent rather than a signed contract.

Risks

Receivables funded by debt. Receivables are ₹1,375 cr (216 days), borrowings were ₹871 cr, and operating cash flow was −₹119 cr in FY26. Interest takes more than half of EBITDA in the latest quarter. If state-utility payments slow further, the IPO cash goes into working capital rather than paying down debt, and earnings stall.
  • Earnings quality. Other income was 28% of FY26 pre-tax profit, and the June-2025 base is still disputed.
  • Supply overhang. Anchor lock-ups are expiring and funds were net sellers in August, on a float of only 12% held by the general public.
  • Short listed record. One reported quarter and one shareholding disclosure as a listed company.
  • EPC execution. About half the order book is project work, where cost overruns and delays hit margins directly.
  • Governance change. The company secretary is leaving within three months of listing. It's not a red flag on its own, but worth watching.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the September-half balance sheet shows IPO proceeds cutting net debt below about ₹400 cr, quarterly interest falls under ₹25 cr, and margin holds at 12% or more. Or receivable days fall back below 180.
  • Downgrade to Sell: borrowings rising again alongside receivables, the order book falling below ₹2,000 cr, or promoters selling below 75% outside an offer-for-sale.
