Verdict

The business is doing what the bulls said. It's the price and the cash that keep this at Hold. The June quarter was the best yet. Revenue was ₹43.7 cr, up 50%. Operating margin was 18%. Profit was ₹5.6 cr, up 104%. The company says its order book rose ₹60 cr to ₹660 cr, over 3.5x trailing revenue. Return on capital is 27%. For a ₹726 cr company, those are strong numbers.

Two things hold it back. First, profit is not turning into cash. FY26 operating profit was ₹24 cr but operating cash flow was only ₹2 cr. Free cash flow was −₹12 cr. Customers owed ₹43 cr at March, 93 days of sales. For a software company, that looks more like project services than product licensing. Second, the price. The July note said wait for the stock to settle after Vijay Kedia's buying. It didn't. At ₹721 it is 18% higher than then and 68% above Kedia's ₹429 purchase. About ₹1.7 cr of stock trades a day, so a small amount of selling can move the price a lot.

Stays Hold, high risk. What changed is that the growth now looks real. What hasn't changed is that the entry price is poor and the cash hasn't followed.

Business

What it does

Exato provides "customer experience as a service" and AI tools to companies with large customer-contact operations: diallers, call-compliance, speech analytics and AI agents. Its own platforms are ExatoIQ, Prompt Base Dialer, UAM and CompliCall. It also resells and integrates telephony and hardware, which is why March-quarter revenue jumps while margin falls. Exports were 25% of FY26 revenue, with a stated target of 60% in two to three years. It has subsidiaries in the US, Singapore and (since March 2026) Australia.

Moat

Narrow. Once a dialler or compliance platform is running in a client's contact centre, switching is annoying, so there is some stickiness. But this is a crowded market with global software vendors and many Indian integrators. The 15–18% margin looks like a services business, not software. A real moat would show up as rising margin and faster cash collection. So far only the margin is rising.

Order book

₹660 cr, company-stated (up from ₹600 cr at the FY26 results), against ₹183 cr of trailing revenue. How it is counted (multi-year contract values, renewals, telephony pass-through) and when it will turn into revenue are not disclosed. Orders announced since then (₹22.4 cr in July–August, US$479k on 1 July) are much smaller than the headline suggests. Treat the figure as unverified.

Risks

Being unable to sell. ₹1.7 cr trades a day, SME lots are large, and the price has doubled since the IPO on a well-known investor's buying. If sentiment turns, SME stocks can hit lower circuits several days in a row, and you can't sell at any price.
  • Profit not turning into cash: ₹1 cr of operating cash against ₹46 cr of operating profit over three years.
  • The order book can't be checked. The company announces it but gives no breakdown, and announced wins are small next to it.
  • Relying on one investor's reputation. Much of the rerating came from Kedia's buying. If he reduces his stake, it would hit the price hard.
  • Limited disclosure: SME reporting, no broker coverage, no mutual funds, 469 shareholders.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 operating cash flow of at least half of operating profit, with growth above 30%. Or a pullback to ₹480–520, about 25x trailing earnings.
  • Downgrade to Sell: receivables keep growing faster than revenue, the Kedia group or promoters sell, or two quarters of growth below 15%.
