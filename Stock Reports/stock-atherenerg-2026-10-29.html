Verdict

Ather is executing, but the price has already moved ahead of it. Revenue grew 63% in Q2 FY27 to ₹1,464 cr on 93,211 scooters (+42%). Operating EBITDA turned positive, at ₹11.5 cr (0.8%) before other income. Adjusted gross margin was 24%. Pre-orders stand at 1.8 lakh, and Hosur ran at 98% utilisation, so supply is the constraint, not demand. The balance sheet was rebuilt in July–August with about ₹1,500 cr of new equity (a ₹1,300 cr QIP and a ₹200 cr India-Japan Fund placement), plus Hero MotoCorp's ₹960 cr warrant commitment, of which 25% has been paid.

Why not Buy. The ₹1.26 cr net profit is a rounding error that rests on ₹62.7 cr of other income, which is mostly treasury yield on the IPO and QIP cash. PM E-DRIVE support is worth up to about ₹47 cr a quarterd (₹5,000 × 93,211 units), four times operating EBITDA. On a no-subsidy basis the vehicle business is therefore not yet self-funding. At ₹66,054 cr the market values Ather at 13.7x TTM sales and about 10x FY27E salesd, against 1–3x for the profitable incumbents. Its Vahan share has also slipped, from about 18–19% (Oct 2025–Mar 2026) to 15.0% in September, as TVS and Bajaj take half the market. The stock sits 4% below its 52-week high after a 135% one-year run. The Trendlyne consensus target of ₹1,776 leaves only 7% upside.

What would change it. Two things together: AURIC (Bidkin) Factory 3.0 coming up on schedule from December 2026 without a margin dip, and Konarc pulling Vahan share back above 17%. Either would justify the post-results targets of ₹1,970–2,250. A pullback toward ₹1,300 would also make the risk-reward work on current numbers.

Path to breakeven and cash

  • Operating breakeven has been reached, just. Operating EBITDA went −20.8% → −14.8% → −7.5% → −5.9% → −2.7% → +0.8% over six quarters. That is roughly 400 bps a quarter of operating leverage on a nearly flat employee-plus-other cost base of about ₹290 cr a quarter. Gross margin is the swing variable: Q2's 24% adjusted GM was up 160 bps despite higher commodity costs.
  • Cash is not the constraint. Sep 2026 cash and equivalents were ₹1,893 crd, investments ₹1,133 cr and borrowings ₹961 cr. FY27 inflows already received: ₹1,300 cr QIP at ₹1,202 (20 Jul), ₹200 cr India-Japan Fund equity, and about ₹250 crd of upfront warrant money (25% of ₹1,000 cr). About ₹750 crd more is due when the warrants convert, within 18 months of Aug 2026. Reserves rose from ₹2,534 cr to ₹4,247 cr in H1 FY27, which ties out. Q2's ₹62.7 cr other income implies ₹2,500–3,000 cr of treasury at about 8–9%.
  • Cash use now is capex, not losses. The IPO earmarked ₹927 cr for Factory 3.0 phase 1, ₹750 cr for R&D, ₹300 cr for marketing and ₹40 cr for debt repayment, deployed FY26–FY28. The 26 Oct board added ₹122 cr: Hosur 4.2 → 4.95 lakh units by Dec 2026, AURIC 5 → 6.5 lakh by Q2 FY28, and ₹53 cr for a new battery line by Q3 FY28. Half-year cash flow is not on Screener, so H1 FY27 FCF is n/a.

Business

Nature of business

Ather designs and assembles electric scooters at Hosur, Tamil Nadu: the 450 series (performance), Rizta (family, now the volume model) and, from Sep 2026, Konarc (mass market, from ₹99,999, on the new EL platform). It makes its own battery packs, vehicle software (AtherStack), the Ather Grid fast-charging network and accessories. Non-vehicle revenue was 15% of Q2 FY27. Sales are domestic, historically South-led, and Rizta and the network expansion have taken it national. Cumulative sales passed 8 lakh units in Sep 2026. Capacity is 4.2 lakh a year at Hosur, going to 4.95 lakh, plus Factory 3.0 at AURIC Bidkin, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (Aurangabad), on 95-year leased land. Phase 1 there is 5 lakh units a year, with phased production from Dec 2026 and expansion to 6.5 lakh by Q2 FY28, for a combined total of about 11.4 lakh.

Moat

The moat is narrow but real, and it rests on vertical software and product integration plus brand, not scale. Ather builds its own battery packs, BMS and AtherStack. 94% of Q2 buyers chose AtherStack Pro, and that recurring, high-margin software attach is what lifts non-vehicle revenue to 15%. It also runs its own fast-charging grid and leads on product quality and resale. Those are switching costs and reputation, not barriers. TVS and Bajaj have 10x the dealer reach, cheaper capital and ICE cash flows to subsidise EV pricing, and together they now take 50% of the market. Ather's edge is staying ahead on product while it gets Bidkin's scale. Konarc is the test of whether that edge carries down-market.

Order book

Not a meaningful concept for a B2C vehicle maker. The nearest proxies are 1.8 lakh pre-orders (Q2 FY27, more than double a year ago) and about 10 lakh enquiries (+109%). These are not firm orders. Ather also flagged about 20,000 units a month of unmet demand.

Risks

Valuation leaves no room for a stumble. At 13.7x TTM sales the price assumes Ather scales to about 11 lakh units and reaches incumbent-like margins. Today it earns 0.8% before other income. A slip in the Bidkin ramp, a share loss to TVS/Bajaj, or a gross-margin hit from cells or magnets would each compress the multiple. A move from 10x to 6x FY27E sales (Emkay's own multiple on Sep-28E) would put the stock near ₹960d at FY27E revenue.
  • Subsidy dependence. PM E-DRIVE (₹5,000/vehicle cap) runs to Mar 2028 and is worth up to about ₹47 cr a quarterd, versus ₹11.5 cr of operating EBITDA. Ather has not shown it can pass that cost through when the subsidy ends. Its PLI-Auto status and any PLI income are n/a: no 2026 disclosure was found. Treat PLI as upside not in the numbers.
  • Rare-earth magnets. China's 2025 export curbs cost Ather about a week of dealer supply, an 8–10% production trim and ₹26.25 cr of deferred subsidy claims. It has since type-approved a heavy-rare-earth-free (HREF) motor. Its 2026 supply status and HREF mix are n/a. A renewed curb would hit just as Bidkin needs parts.
  • Profit quality. ₹62.7 cr of other income turned a ₹61 cr operating shortfall into a ₹1.3 cr profit. Treasury income falls as the cash is spent on capex.
  • Execution at Bidkin. Phase 1 (5 lakh units) is to start phased production in Dec 2026. Sources variously cite Oct 2026, end-CY26 and Mar 2027. New-plant start-up costs could reverse the margin trend for one or two quarters.
  • Competition, including from its own promoter. TVS (26.5%) and Bajaj (23.8%) are gaining, and Hero's Vida doubled its share in a year. Konarc at ₹99,999 means lower revenue per unit and possibly lower gross margin.
  • Supply and sentiment. The promoter minimum-contribution lock-in likely ends in early Nov 2026 (unconfirmed). Retail holders have doubled in a quarter, and FIIs keep selling. The stock moved 14% on results day.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: operating EBITDA, before other income, of at least 4% in Q3 or Q4 FY27 while AURIC phase 1 ramps, and Vahan share back above 17% for two consecutive months once Bidkin supply arrives. Alternatively, a pullback to about ₹1,300 (about 8x FY27E salesd) with the operating trend intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating EBITDA turning negative again in Q3 or Q4 FY27 on Bidkin start-up costs or Konarc mix; Vahan share below 13% for three consecutive months; a renewed rare-earth disruption that cuts output; or the subsidy being withdrawn early without price pass-through.
