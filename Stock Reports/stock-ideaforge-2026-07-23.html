India's listed drone pioneer — a high-beta turnaround story. FY26 was loss-making at full-year level, but a blockbuster Q4 (revenue +594% YoY, return to profit) plus record order inflows suggest defence procurement is finally flowing. The thesis is leverage to India's drone push; the risk is that lumpy government orders make any single quarter a poor guide.

02Business & Qualitative

Model. Designs and manufactures unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for security, surveillance and mapping — products span NETRA, SWITCH, Q6 and the long-range YETI logistics drone, plus payloads, ground-control software (BlueFire) and AI image-intelligence. Customers are dominated by Indian defence, paramilitary and government.

Moat. First-mover and brand in indigenous VTOL UAVs (since 2009); a leading position in ISR (intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance) drones with home-grown autopilot and AI stack — relevant as India pushes import substitution and electronic-warfare-resilient designs. Moat is real but contested: drone tech moves fast and competition (Indian and global) is rising.

Management & signals. Founder-led (Ankit Mehta & co-founders); Chairman Srikanth Velamakanni. FY26 milestones: first US order, global expansion, CRISIL/India Ratings upgrade to BBB / Stable (from Negative), and a board-approved ₹500 Cr fundraise via securities.

09Risks
  • Lumpy, loss-making base. FY26 ended in the red despite a strong Q4 — one good quarter is not a trend yet.
  • Order-timing dependence. Near-total reliance on government/defence procurement cycles; revenue can swing violently quarter to quarter.
  • Dilution. ₹500 Cr fundraise + low promoter holding (~29%).
  • Competition & tech risk in a fast-moving drone market; supply-chain / working-capital strain.
  • Valuation on hope — P/B ~6x with no earnings cushion; high beta.
