Verdict

The plan is on track, and the numbers show what it costs. Q1 FY27 was the first full quarter with the three Antolin India interior businesses. Total income was ₹1,499 cr, up 51%. EBITDA was ₹283 cr, up 27%. Profit was only ₹148 cr, up 9%. EBITDA margin fell from 22.5% to 18.9% because interiors earn lower margins, integration costs are running, and commodity costs are passed on to customers a quarter late. Interest went from ₹9 cr to ₹34 cr and depreciation from ₹32 cr to ₹53 cr, both from buying Antolin with debt. More than 35% of revenue now doesn't depend on engine type, and about 60% of the business is protected from the shift to electric vehicles.

The ₹1,000 cr QIP (share sale to institutions) closed on 20 August. 23.4 lakh new shares were sold at ₹4,280, 3.6% below the ₹4,438 floor price. That cut the promoter stake from 43.75% to 41.55% and took domestic institutions from 12.2% to 16.0%. The money roughly clears the ₹932 cr of net debt from March, which fixes the balance sheet.

But the stock is ₹4,577, above the QIP price and 36x trailing earnings. Profit grew 9% in FY26 and 9% in Q1. The July note wanted either a pullback to ₹3,400–3,600 or evidence that Antolin margins were rising (toward 13–14%). Neither has happened, and interiors margins aren't disclosed separately. The only published broker target, Emkay's ₹4,850, is 6% above today. Hold.

Business

What it does

Formerly Shriram Pistons & Rings, renamed in April 2026. The core business is India's leading maker of pistons, piston rings, pins and engine valves, for cars, two- and three-wheelers, commercial vehicles and tractors, plus exports. Since FY26 it also makes vehicle interiors (door trims, headliners) through Antolin India's three entities, with long-term access to Grupo Antolin's technology, and interior lighting. More than 35% of revenue doesn't depend on engine type. Management expects EVs to reach only 15–17% of Indian vehicles by 2030, which gives the engine business a long, profitable tail to pay for the shift.

Among Indian auto-component makers of similar size, SPR is one of the cheaper stocks with some of the best returns: about half Craftsman's or Sansera's P/E with far higher ROE. The market has priced in the drag from its engine exposure. That's the argument against a Sell. The argument against a Buy is that 36x is already far above its own history (it was about 20x two years ago).

Moat

Strong in the core: pistons, rings and valves are a concentrated market with long OEM approval cycles and a big replacement-parts business. The moat in interiors hasn't been shown yet. It's a more competitive, lower-margin product that depends on Antolin's technology licence.

Order book

No order book is disclosed. Sales follow OEM production schedules and the replacement market. The forward commitment is management's target of doubling Antolin's margin from about 9–10% to 20%+ within three years. That's the test for the re-rating, and it isn't yet reported separately.

Risks

The multiple already assumes the change will succeed. The stock has gone from ₹2,593 to ₹4,747 in a year on profit growth of 9%. If Antolin margins don't rise, this becomes a slower-growing, more complicated company at a growth company's price.
  • Integration: interiors margins of 9–10% vs 20%+ in the core business. Integration costs are hitting now.
  • More shares after the QIP (about 5%) hold back EPS growth this year.
  • Long-term decline of combustion engines: slow, but it never goes away.
  • Commodity timing: cost increases are recovered a quarter late, so margins swing from quarter to quarter.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a pullback to ₹3,800–4,000 (about 30x, close to the QIP price). Or consolidated EBITDA margin back above 20% by Q3 FY27, showing the Antolin drag is temporary.
  • Downgrade to Sell: core-business profit stalls while acquisitions push margin below 17%, or another large debt-funded acquisition.
