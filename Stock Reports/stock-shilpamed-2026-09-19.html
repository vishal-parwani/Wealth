Verdict

A good turnaround, priced for more than it has delivered. Shilpa is moving from low-margin bulk drug ingredients (APIs) toward finished medicines, biosimilars and contract manufacturing (CDMO), and the numbers show it. Q1 FY27 revenue was a record ₹466 cr, up 43%. Finished medicines (formulations) more than doubled to ₹198 cr, and biologics grew 42%. Gross margin was 71% and EBITDA margin 30%, up from 21% two years ago. Net debt has fallen from ₹905 cr to ₹615 cr, and debt is now 1.3x EBITDA, down from 6.7x. Management says the big capital spending is done. Credit rating was upgraded to AA−.

The problem is how much of that the price already assumes, and how clean the profit is. The stock is up about 280% in twelve months and made a new high yesterday. Reported Q1 profit of ₹101 cr (+115%) came with a negative tax rate, from a one-off deferred-tax reversal. Profit was higher than pre-tax profit. FY26's March quarter included about ₹45 cr of one-off other income, probably linked to the ₹50 cr sale of its Sravathi stake agreed in October 2025. Remove both and apply the 25% tax rate management says to expect: trailing profit is about ₹228 cr, not ₹297 cr, and the stock is on about 88xd, not 70x. The only aggregated broker target is ₹881, 14% below the price. Management gives no revenue or margin guidance.

The cash side hasn't caught up either. Free cash flow was negative in each of the last three years. Inventory sits for 291 days and customers take 123 days to pay. ₹884 cr of plant is still unfinished (capital work in progress), a quarter of total assets, earning nothing yet and adding depreciation when it switches on. Return on capital is 11%. This could become a very good business: biosimilar launches, three CDMO drugs going commercial in FY28, a new antibody-drug-conjugate plant. At ₹1,021 you pay for all of that now. Hold, high risk. Buy nearer ₹750, about 50x Q1's profit rate at normal tax.

Business

What it does

Founded in 1987 in Raichur, Karnataka, as a maker of niche APIs, mainly for cancer drugs. It is still the largest division (₹260 cr in Q1). The growth is in formulations: 16 injectable and 19 oral dosage forms, plus oral dissolving films and skin patches, made at Jadcherla (Unit IV) and elsewhere. In biologics (Shilpa Biologicals, Dharwad) it has an aflibercept biosimilar due to launch in India in FY27, a nivolumab biosimilar with Orion for Europe, albumin, and a new antibody-drug-conjugate plant. CDMO (contract development and manufacturing) has more than 20 customers and 25+ new-drug programmes, three of them expected to reach commercial supply in FY28. Peptide capacity is due by the end of FY27.

Moat

Building, not built. Hard-to-make cancer-drug APIs, complex dosage forms (oral films, skin patches, injectables) and biosimilars all need technical skill and regulatory approvals that most competitors lack. That explains the 71% gross margin. But the higher-value businesses are only now reaching commercial scale, and in biosimilars and CDMO Shilpa competes with much bigger companies. The moat will be proven by approvals turning into revenue, starting with aflibercept in India and the FY28 CDMO drugs.

Order book

No order book is disclosed. The forward indicators are the pipeline: aflibercept (India, FY27), nivolumab (Europe, with Orion), rivaroxaban oral films (Europe), rotigotine patch (US filing), three CDMO drugs going commercial in FY28, and Abraxane, enzalutamide and abiraterone filings for FY28.

Risks

The valuation after a near-fourfold rise. At about 88x profit at normal tax, with the price above the average broker target and momentum funds doing the buying, the stock needs every pipeline item to land on time. A quarter at 25% tax will show much slower profit growth than the headlines suggest, even if the business does exactly as expected.
  • Profit quality: the last two quarters were lifted by one-off other income and a deferred-tax reversal.
  • Cash: three years of negative free cash flow and a 301-day cash cycle. ₹884 cr of plant still unfinished.
  • US regulators: Jadcherla (Unit IV) received eight inspection observations in November 2025 and had a warning letter in 2020. A US recall in August 2026. The outcome of the 2025 inspection hasn't been disclosed.
  • People: the biologics CEO changed in July–August, and the Bhutada–Inani promoter families have put separation terms in place.
  • No guidance and thin broker coverage, so it's hard to know what the market is expecting.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the price back near ₹750, about 50x Q1's profit rate at 25% tax. Or two quarters at a normal tax rate with profit still growing 40%+ and positive free cash flow, showing the change is producing cash.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a warning letter or import alert at Jadcherla, profit at normal tax growing less than 20%, or the promoter-family separation leading to large share sales.
