Verdict

A real, improving engineering business priced as though the defence story has already been delivered. Sell. After demerging Lifestyle (September 2024) and Realty (July 2025), what remains of Raymond is auto components, tools and hardware, and a growing aerospace and defence arm. That business is doing fine: Q1 FY27 income ₹628 cr, up 13%, EBITDA ₹100 cr at a 15.9% margin, and aerospace revenue up 40% to ₹123 cr. Operating margin across the company has climbed from 1.6% in FY24 to 11% trailing. Nothing here is broken.

The price is the problem, and so is who is buying it. The stock is up about 89% in September alone, 212% in six months, and sits at its 52-week high. The trigger was an aerospace order worth about ₹33 cr a year — roughly 1.4% of annual revenue — announced into a defence-sector rally. On trailing profit of ₹64 cr the stock is on about 125xd. Even annualising the much better June quarter it's about 65x, and 35x EV/EBITDA. Aerospace is still only a fifth of revenue.

Two things make this worse than an ordinary expensive stock. First, the register: foreign investors have cut from 15.64% to 7.89% over five quarters and domestic institutions from 6.84% to 3.81%, while the public holding rose from 28.6% to 39.4%. Professionals have been selling all the way up; only one mutual fund of size still holds it. Second, the company is issuing 33.28 lakh convertible warrants to Minerva Ventures Fund at ₹645, raising ₹214.7 cr — a 46% discount to today's price, about 5% dilutiond, with the shareholder vote on 2 October. A board that priced new capital at ₹645 a few weeks ago is telling you something about ₹1,204.

Business

What it does

What's left inside Raymond Limited after two demergers is the engineering group: auto components (ring gears, water-pump bearings, shafts) sold to Indian and global carmakers, tools and hardware (files, drills, power-tool accessories) sold under the Raymond and JK brands, and aerospace and defence — precision machining, castings and structural assemblies for aircraft programmes. Aerospace is about ₹123 cr of a ₹628 cr quarter, roughly a fifth, and is growing 40%. The consumer-facing Raymond you know — suiting, retail, apparel — is now Raymond Lifestyle, a separately listed company. Real estate is Raymond Realty, also separately listed.

Moat

Modest and mostly in the old businesses. Auto components and tools are competitive markets where Raymond has long customer relationships and a known brand. The aerospace arm needs customer qualification, which takes years and is a genuine barrier — but the company is early in building it, and the orders announced so far are small.

Order book

No total order book is disclosed. The only disclosed forward number is the ₹33 cr a year of aerospace orders announced in September, running through 2026 and 2027. Set against ₹2,293 cr of trailing revenue, this is not yet a material contract — which is the heart of the disagreement between the share price and the filings.

Risks · to the Sell call

Defence re-ratings can run for a long time. If the aerospace arm wins a genuinely large programme, or the group announces a bigger defence strategy, the stock can keep moving regardless of current earnings. Selling a momentum stock early is uncomfortable and often looks wrong for months.
  • For holders: 125x trailing earnings, 65x even on the best quarter annualised, with aerospace a fifth of revenue.
  • Dilution at ₹645, about 5%, voted on 2 October.
  • Institutions leaving: FIIs halved, one real mutual fund holder left.
  • Weak cash conversion: ₹42 cr of operating cash on ₹232 cr of operating profit in FY26.
  • Accounting complexity: three years of demerger gains make trailing comparisons unreliable, and the "debt-free" claim doesn't match the consolidated balance sheet.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: a price back near ₹700 (about 38x the June quarter annualised, in line with better-quality defence suppliers). Or aerospace revenue passing 35% of the total with margin above 16%, showing this is genuinely becoming a defence company.
  • Upgrade to Buy: the above plus a disclosed multi-year order book of meaningful size (₹1,000 cr or more) and operating cash flow back above 70% of operating profit.
