Verdict

The operating recovery is real and the balance sheet is the reason to wait. Q1 FY27 was excellent — revenue ₹2,432 cr, up 36%, EBITDA up 51%, net profit up 116% to ₹151 cr. Margin has recovered from 15% to 16% and the aluminium-casting and industrial segments are carrying it. None of that is in dispute.

What is in dispute is what you are paying for it, and what has been borrowed to build it. Net debt is ₹3,444 cr against ₹3,264 cr of equity — 1.11x, up from ₹1,672 cr two years ago. Free cash flow has been negative three years running: −₹115 cr, −₹706 cr, −₹647 cr. Operating cash flow of ₹522 cr in FY26 was swallowed whole by ₹1,302 cr of investing outflow, and management has just outlined a further ₹1,500 cr capex plan. The company is growing by borrowing and spending, which works while demand holds and hurts badly when it doesn't.

Then the register. Promoter holding fell from 48.70% to 42.41% in the June quarter — 6.29 points in three months, after four quarters of not moving at all. Chairman Srinivasan Ravi alone sold 5.25 lakh shares on 23 June for about ₹486 cr, taking his own stake from 40.15% to 38.14%. Domestic funds bought the lot; DII holding went from 28.29% to 32.90%.

Goldman Sachs initiated with a Buy and ₹11,600 in July, when the stock was ₹9,105. It is ₹10,644 now, so most of that call has already been collected. At 59x trailing earnings, with leverage rising and the founder selling, Hold is the honest position.

Business

What it does

Founded in Coimbatore in 1986, Craftsman is a precision manufacturer across three legs: powertrain and other machined components for commercial vehicles and tractors; aluminium die-cast products for two-wheelers, passenger vehicles and increasingly for industrial and data-centre power equipment; and an industrial and engineering segment covering storage solutions, gears and material handling. The aluminium and industrial legs are what have driven the last two years of growth and are where the incremental capital is going.

Moat

Modest and mostly about capital, not technology. Large aluminium die-casting and precision machining lines are expensive, slow to commission and require OEM qualification, so incumbency counts. It is not a pricing moat: the customers are commercial-vehicle and two-wheeler OEMs who negotiate annually, and the industrial segment competes on cost. The genuinely interesting angle — castings for data-centre backup power — is new enough that it is a thesis rather than a track record.

Order book

Craftsman does not report a confirmed order book in the way a project or defence business would; revenue runs on OEM schedules and annual contracts. The ₹1,500 cr capex plan is the forward commitment worth watching in its place, since it is a firm call on cash and the returns on it are not yet visible.

Risks

Leverage into a cyclical. Net debt ₹3,444 cr, 1.11x equity, against a commercial-vehicle and two-wheeler end market that turns. Interest is already ₹86 cr a quarter. FY25 showed exactly what a bad year looks like with this structure: revenue up 28%, profit down 40%.
  • Free cash flow has been negative for three years and another ₹1,500 cr of capex is planned. Growth is being funded by debt, not by the business.
  • Promoter selling. 6.29 points in one quarter after four static ones, including a disclosed ₹486 cr sale by the chairman.
  • The valuation assumes the recovery continues. 59x trailing earnings on a business whose ROCE is 14% and whose margin is 16%.
  • Data-centre demand is a promise, not a segment. It underpins the one published Buy target and is not yet separately disclosed in the results.
  • Thin liquidity for the market cap — 0.93 lakh shares a day on a ₹27,840 cr company. Exiting size is a real cost.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a year of positive free cash flow with net debt/equity back below 0.8x, or the data-centre and industrial revenue disclosed separately and growing — evidence the capex is earning its cost of capital.
  • Downgrade to Sell: further promoter selling, net debt through ₹4,500 cr without a matching step-up in revenue, or a repeat of FY25 — a quarter where revenue grows and net profit falls because interest and depreciation outrun it.
