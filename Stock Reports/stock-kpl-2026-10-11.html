Verdict

The growth is real; the cash is not following it yet, so Hold. FY26 revenue rose 36% to ₹503 cr and profit 68% to ₹67 cr. The June quarter was better again: revenue ₹162 cr (+46%), operating margin 25.3% (from 21.6%), profit ₹26 cr (+117%). Management has raised FY27 guidance to more than ₹700 cr of revenue and at least ₹109 cr of profit. ROE is 22.6%. On the guided profit, the stock trades at about 34x forward earningsd — not expensive for that growth.

The balance sheet is where it goes wrong. Trade receivables went from ₹154 cr to ₹280 cr in FY26 — up 82% while sales rose 36%. Debtor days stretched from 152 to 203. Operating cash flow fell to ₹17 cr against ₹118 cr of operating profit, which means only 32% of operating profit turned into cash. Free cash flow was −₹29 cr. Borrowings rose to ₹130 cr. Half of revenue is exports to emerging markets, where long credit terms are normal. Even so, profit that turns up as receivables has not been banked yet.

What it would take: September's half-year balance sheet, due with Q2 results in November, showing receivables growing no faster than sales. That would make this a Buy at today's price. Receivables growing faster again would make it a Sell. The register is promoter plus retail, with no fund support. Liquidity has only just improved with the NSE listing.

Business

Nature of business

An Amritsar-based formulations maker, incorporated in 1983. It makes general, beta-lactam, cephalosporin and oncology dosage forms, which need segregated plants. Four of its facilities hold EU-GMP approval: the Amritsar general and beta-lactam units, re-audited in November 2025, and the Himachal Pradesh cephalosporin and oncology units, approved in 2023. Saudi SFDA approval came in January 2025. Exports were ₹250 cr in FY26, about half of revenue, sold across roughly 80 countries. Most of these are emerging markets in Africa, Latin America, CIS and Asia, where it supplies distributors and tender buyers.

Moat

Narrow. Segregated EU-GMP plants for cephalosporins and oncology are slow and costly to build and qualify, so they keep out casual entrants into those categories. But the end markets are price-led tender and distributor channels, where KPL has no pricing power and its customers set the credit terms, as the receivables show. Regulatory approvals are a barrier to entry, not a moat on margins.

Risks

Receivables. ₹280 cr owed at March 2026 is 56% of a year's revenue, and 203 debtor days in emerging-market exports leaves exposure to currency controls and distributor defaults. A ₹50 cr write-off would equal three-quarters of FY26's ₹67 cr profit.
  • The re-rating has already happened. The stock is up 4.3x in a year and moved from BSE-only trading to NSE. Part of the multiple is a liquidity premium that can reverse.
  • The guidance needs more margin. Guided FY27 EBITDA implies 27–28% against 25.3% now. Missing the margin target would miss the profit target too.
  • No institutional floor. No active mutual fund holds the stock, and 41% of shares are with retail. A 20% upper circuit after results works just as easily in the other direction.
  • Governance hygiene. A family-executive board, an auditor change in 2024 and an independent director's resignation in 2026. The successor auditor's name was not confirmed for this note.
  • Debt is creeping up. Borrowings rose from ₹98 cr to ₹130 cr in two years to fund working capital, not capacity.
