Verdict

Nothing has changed except that the price has fallen further. Gensol was admitted to insolvency on 13 June 2025 over a ₹510 cr default to IREDA. SEBI barred the founders, Anmol and Puneet Singh Jaggi, after finding they had diverted money from about ₹978 cr of IREDA and PFC loans. On 14 July 2026, IREDA formally classified the loans as fraud. In the June quarter, CRISIL reported that the company is not cooperating with its monitoring. No quarterly results have been filed since December 2024.

In insolvency, shareholders are paid last, after secured lenders, other creditors and employees. Borrowings at the last filing were ₹1,372 cr. Listed Indian companies that go through insolvency very often end with the old shares cancelled or reduced to almost nothing. The ₹66 cr market cap is a bet on that not happening. The stock has fallen from ₹19 at the last note to ₹17. It only traded on 64 of the last 180 days, because shares in insolvent companies trade only in limited periodic sessions.

Sell. A low P/E or P/BV here means nothing, because the earnings and assets behind those numbers are what the investigation is looking into.

Business

What it was

Two linked businesses. It built solar power projects for clients and gave related advice and maintenance. It also bought electric vehicles with IREDA and PFC loans and leased them, mainly to BluSmart, another Jaggi company. SEBI found that the leasing loans were partly sent to related parties and spent personally, including on luxury property. Today the company is run by the resolution professional, with frozen bank accounts, lessors taking back vehicles and no reporting management.

Peers

No useful comparison. Solvent solar builders (Sterling & Wilson, Waaree, Premier Energies) are valued on earnings and order books. Gensol is valued, if at all, on what might be left for shareholders after insolvency. The relevant comparison is other listed Indian insolvency cases, where old shareholders usually ended up with nothing or close to it.

Moat · Order book

None that matters. Whatever solar contracts were in progress are being handled by the resolution professional for the creditors, not for shareholders.

Risks

Losing everything. In insolvency, secured lenders owed about ₹1,372 cr are paid ahead of shareholders. Most resolutions cancel or heavily dilute existing shares. The fraud classification and missing accounts make a bidder less likely to pay more than the debt.
