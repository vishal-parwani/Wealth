Verdict

The business is doing everything it said it would; the price has already paid for it. At ₹3,877 the stock trades at 64.9x trailing and 53x consensus FY27 earnings, against a consensus target of ₹3,836 — one percent below the current price. Twelve analysts raised targets after Q1 FY27 and the stock still outran them.

The operating story is genuinely good. Q1 FY27 was the first ₹1,000 cr quarter, revenue up 33% year on year, and margin has climbed four quarters running from 17% to 19%. The engine is the non-auto segment — aerospace, defence and semiconductors — where revenue more than tripled year on year to ₹145 cr and the order book jumped from ₹4,440 cr to ₹5,750 cr on a single new semiconductor-equipment order worth roughly $75m a year at run rate.

The counter-argument is arithmetic, not scepticism. FY26 net profit was ₹327 cr; the market is paying ₹24,193 cr for it. Consensus has FY27 EPS at ₹72.60 against ₹51.99 delivered — a 40% jump that assumes the semiconductor ramp lands on schedule, and management has said the meaningful revenue starts in calendar 2027 and peaks in 2029. You are underwriting three years of execution at a price that already reflects it.

What would change this: a pullback towards ₹3,000 (roughly 41x FY27E), or evidence the semiconductor order is converting to revenue faster than guided. Neither is here yet.

Business

What it does

Sansera makes complex, close-tolerance forged and machined components. The historic base is two-wheeler and passenger-vehicle engine and transmission parts — connecting rods, rocker arms, crankshafts, gear shifter forks. The growth engine is everything that isn't that: aerospace structural and engine components, defence, off-road, agriculture, and now precision parts for semiconductor capital equipment. Non-auto is where the margin and the multiple live.

Moat

Real but narrow. Aerospace and semiconductor components need customer qualification that takes years — Nadcap accreditation, part-level approvals, audited process control — and once a part is qualified, switching supplier means requalifying. That is a genuine barrier and it is why the ADS order book has multi-year visibility. It does not extend to the auto business, which is competitive, price-negotiated annually, and roughly two-thirds of revenue.

Order book

₹5,750 cr ADS order book as at 13 Aug 2026, up from ₹4,440 cr — about 1.5x trailing group revenue, and this is the confirmed aerospace/defence/semiconductor book only, not the whole company. Management guides the large semiconductor order to start contributing in calendar 2027 and peak in 2029.

Risks

You are paying 53x FY27 consensus for a 40% earnings jump that has not happened. Consensus FY27 EPS is ₹72.60 against ₹51.99 delivered in FY26. If the ramp slips a year — entirely possible given management's own calendar-2027-to-2029 language — the multiple on delivered earnings stays above 60x and the stock has no support from its own numbers.
  • Working capital is absorbing the profit growth. Inventory days went 126 → 147 → 169 while operating cash flow stayed flat at ₹374–387 cr across three years in which net profit went from ₹188 cr to ₹327 cr. Growth that does not convert to cash is growth financed by the balance sheet.
  • Domestic funds and promoters both sold in June. DIIs down 4.34 points, promoters below 30% for the first time, retail count up 35%. The people closest to the business reduced into strength.
  • Capex is open-ended. ₹134 cr in one quarter, no updated guidance, depreciation already up 24% year on year. Each new facility carries fixed cost before it carries revenue.
  • Auto is still two-thirds of the business and is exposed to two-wheeler and PV cycles, annual price negotiation with OEMs, and the powertrain shift away from internal combustion.
  • Customer concentration in the new segment. The order-book jump came from one semiconductor-equipment customer. That is a single relationship carrying a large part of the growth case.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: price back near ₹3,000 (about 41x FY27E) with the ADS order book intact — or two consecutive quarters where non-auto revenue and operating cash flow both grow, showing the ramp is arriving early and converting to cash.
  • Downgrade to Sell: inventory days past 180 with operating cash flow still flat, a slip in the semiconductor timeline beyond calendar 2027, or promoter holding falling materially further.
