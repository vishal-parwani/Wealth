Verdict

Update · 24 Sep 2026 — Hold reaffirmed. Up 8.8% since 23 August to ₹529, a new high, with trailing P/E moving from 31.5x to 34.3x. No new results or announcements; Q1 FY27 stands and Q2 is due late October. The August balance — a well-run bearings business with 18.4% return on capital, against a multiple that already reflects it — is unchanged, just slightly less favourable.

A good business at a full price, with the family that built it taking money off the table. NRB has made needle roller bearings since 1965 and by its own account is fitted to more than 90% of vehicles on Indian roads. Return on capital is 18.4%, the dividend yields 1.64%, debt is negligible, and free cash flow turned strongly positive in FY26 at ₹136 cr. This is a quality small cap, not a story stock.

The problem is the entry price. The stock has gone from ₹213 to ₹486 in twelve months and trades within 4% of its high, at 31.5x earnings that only just recovered — FY26 net profit of ₹146 cr is still below FY24's ₹242 cr, and the comparison flatters neither year, because FY24 carried ₹205 cr of other income and FY25 carried a negative ₹27 cr. Strip both and the clean run rate is roughly ₹150 cr, which is what the TTM shows.

Into that move, the promoter group went from 51.20% to 44.73% in a single quarter. Trilochan Singh Sahney Trust 1 sold 43.7 lakh shares — 4.51% of the company — in one block on 8 June for about ₹192 cr, and a further ~1.5% went in a separate transaction. Foreign and domestic institutions bought it. The family also released 45.68 lakh pledged shares in late June, cutting promoter encumbrance from 11.42% to 6.70%, which is a genuine improvement.

Operations are fine and there is a real new hook: a General Motors Corvette production order via the US facility takes lifetime nominated business to ₹1,100 cr. At 31.5x, with the sellers being the people who know it best, Hold.

Business

What it does

NRB makes needle roller bearings and related precision products — the small, high-load bearings used in gearboxes, transmissions, engines and two-wheeler applications. It supplies essentially every Indian vehicle OEM, exports to European and American programmes, and runs a US facility that has now won a General Motors Corvette production order. Aftermarket and industrial applications add a second, higher-margin leg.

Moat

Real, and specific. Needle bearings are designed into a gearbox at the development stage and requalifying a supplier means re-validating the assembly, so incumbency on a platform lasts the life of that platform — often a decade. Sixty years of OEM relationships and the claim to be fitted on 90% of Indian vehicles is the accumulated result. What it does not confer is pricing power: OEMs negotiate annually and the aftermarket is competitive.

Order book

₹1,100 cr of lifetime nominated business as at Aug 2026, after the GM Corvette win. Note the term: lifetime nominated means revenue expected across the whole programme life, not a confirmed order book to be delivered in a defined period. Against ₹1,395 cr of trailing annual revenue it is meaningful but not transformative, and it will arrive spread over years.

Risks

The price already reflects the recovery. 31.5x earnings that are only now back to a clean ₹150 cr run rate, at 4% below the all-time high, after a 125% twelve-month move. There is no valuation cushion if the auto cycle turns.
  • Promoters sold 6.47 points in one quarter. Explained and orderly, but they sold below where the stock trades now.
  • Ownership concentration in one fund. HDFC Small Cap holds 9% of the company against a 7 lakh share daily volume. Any decision by that one holder moves this stock hard.
  • Inventory at 302 days ties up capital and depresses return on capital versus every listed peer. Improving, but from an extreme.
  • Reported profit is noisy. Other income swung from +₹205 cr to −₹27 cr to +₹27 cr in three years. Headline EPS comparisons are close to meaningless here.
  • Auto cyclicality and OEM price pressure — two-wheeler and commercial-vehicle volumes drive the business, and contracts reprice annually.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: inventory days below 250 with free cash flow held above ₹150 cr — the working-capital release is worth more than the next order — or a pullback towards ₹380 (about 25x clean earnings) with the GM programme intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: further promoter selling below 40%, a return to negative free cash flow, or operating margin slipping back below 15% as OEM pricing bites.
