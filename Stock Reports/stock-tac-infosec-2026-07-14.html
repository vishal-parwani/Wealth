Verdict

The best fundamentals of Kedia's three cyber bets, at the most reasonable moment to look. TAC is a genuine product company: its ESOF platform does AI-driven vulnerability management and risk quantification, and FY26 numbers are remarkable — revenue +88% to ₹57.3 cr, EBITDA margin 53.8%, PAT margin 46.1%. Those margins put it in the top tier of global cybersecurity peers, most of whom burn cash at this stage. Unlike Exato (up 68% in a week), TAC is down ~51% from its 52-week high of ₹899 — you're buying after a de-rating, not into a spike. It remains a ~₹919 cr SME stock trading at ~35x earnings on a ₹57 cr revenue base, so size it as speculation, not as a core holding.

Business

Founded by Trishneet Arora, TAC Security sells ESOF (Enterprise Security in One Framework) — a SaaS platform for vulnerability management, penetration testing, and cyber-risk scoring. Kedia was an early pre-IPO backer (Pre-Series A), so his stake predates the listing hype — this is his longest-held and deepest-diligence name of the three. The company is pushing into AI-powered compliance, targeting what it describes as a $4 bn market.

Bull case

  • Rare profitable cyber-SaaS: 53.8% EBITDA / 46.1% PAT margins with 88% revenue growth — a Rule-of-40 score above 130 where most global peers are below 40.
  • Sticky, recurring compliance-driven demand (regulatory pentesting mandates for BFSI/fintech).
  • Entry after a 51% drawdown rather than at euphoria; P/E has compressed from triple digits at the 2025 peak.
  • Founder-led with a marquee early investor still holding ~7.85%.

Risks

Small revenue base, big expectations. ₹57 cr of revenue supporting a ₹919 cr valuation means growth must stay extreme; any deceleration re-rates it violently (the 51% fall from ₹899 is exactly that happening once already).
  • Margin credibility: 46% PAT margins are extraordinary for the industry — verify revenue recognition and receivables in the annual report; press-release figures are not independently audited by me.
  • Client concentration typical at this scale; a couple of large BFSI logos likely dominate.
  • SME platform mechanics: lot-size trading, thinner liquidity, and Yahoo symbol quirks (trades as TAC-SM.NS).
  • Key-man risk around the founder-CEO.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: FY27 quarters holding >50% growth with margins intact, or migration from SME to mainboard improving liquidity/institutional access.
  • Downgrade to Sell: growth dropping below ~30%, receivables ballooning, or Kedia exiting.
