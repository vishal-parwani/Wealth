Hold

Verdict

The volumes are doubling and the margins are falling faster than the volumes are rising. That is the whole story, and it is not a good one. Websol's Q1 FY27 (reported 10 Aug) shows revenue up 70% to ₹372.6 cr — and PAT up only 16% to ₹77.8 cr. EBITDA margin fell to 34% from 47% a year earlier; PAT margin to 21% from 30%. Trace the sequence: 43.9% (FY25) → 40.8% (FY26) → 34.0% (Q1 FY27). The decline is not just present, it is accelerating.

Those 40%+ margins were never a sign of a great business. They were the margin a protected domestic solar-cell maker earned while ALMM rules and import duties kept Chinese cells out and domestic cell supply was scarce. That window is closing: Indian cell capacity has gone from 3 GW to 24.6 GW, module capacity from 8 GW to 68.4 GW, and industry forecasts put annual module production at 60–65 GW against 45–50 GW of installations. Websol operates 1.2 GW of cell capacity in a market where Waaree is building toward 10 GW and Premier Energies toward 10.6 GW. Sub-scale players do not set price in an oversupplied commodity market. The balance sheet is clean and 12x earnings looks cheap — but it is cheap against a peak-margin earnings number. Watch.

Why the low P/E is a trap here, in one calculation. At ₹3,808 cr market cap and ~₹311 cr of annualised Q1 profit, the stock is on ~12x. But Q1 already carries 34% EBITDA margins versus 47% a year ago. If margins normalise toward 20–25% — still healthy for commodity manufacturing — profit on the same revenue base falls toward ~₹200 cr and the multiple becomes ~19x without the share price moving at all. You are not buying 12x; you are buying 12x on earnings that the industry structure is actively compressing.

Business

Founded 1990, manufacturing at Falta SEZ, West Bengal. Makes mono-crystalline solar PV cells and modules under the Webel Solar brand — M10 and G12 bifacial Mono-PERC cells, and bifacial/monofacial modules in the 525–550 Wp and 640–660 Wp ranges — supplying utility-scale, rooftop and C&I installations. Cell capacity was recently doubled to 1.2 GW (Phase 1 of a larger capex plan).

What Q1 got right

  • Execution on volume is excellent — cell output 259 MW from 126 MW at 92% utilisation; module output 103 MW from 50 MW with utilisation up to 81% from 39%. The new capacity is being absorbed, not idled.
  • Debt cleared. Prepaid the entire ₹110 cr IREDA term loan on 4 Aug 2026 out of internal accruals. That is a genuinely strong signal about cash generation and removes refinancing risk.
  • Order book ₹1,278 cr at 30 June — roughly 3.4x the quarterly revenue run-rate, so near-term revenue visibility is solid.
  • Mono-PERC cells sit on the right side of the ALMM List-II mandate, which from June 2026 requires domestically manufactured cells for government projects.
  • Vijay Kedia adding (1.02% → 1.10%) — a long-standing holder incrementally increasing.

Risks

Scale is destiny in commodity manufacturing, and Websol does not have it. 1.2 GW of cell capacity against Waaree's planned 10 GW and Premier Energies' 10.6 GW by September 2026. Industry commentary is explicit that "integrated and scale players are expected to be relatively better positioned to withstand pricing pressures" while "non-integrated and smaller module manufacturers" bear the brunt of the coming consolidation. Websol is on the wrong side of that sentence.
  • Structural oversupply is forecast, not hypothetical — 60–65 GW of annual module production against 45–50 GW of installations. Cell capacity has grown 3 GW → 24.6 GW; modules 8 GW → 68.4 GW.
  • Margins are policy rents. 40%+ EBITDA in cell manufacturing exists because of ALMM and import duties. Any softening of protection, or simply enough domestic capacity arriving, removes it — and the second is already happening.
  • Chinese TOPCon cell prices are falling on weak demand and oversupply, which sets the global reference price and pressures the landed-cost gap that protection creates.
  • Technology risk — Websol is a Mono-PERC producer while the industry is migrating to TOPCon and HJT. Staying current requires continuous capex that a 1.2 GW player funds less easily than a 10 GW one.
  • Single-site concentration at Falta SEZ.
  • Sector-wide financial risk — analysts warn the cell-module mismatch plus leverage across the industry could produce price crashes, cash-flow stress and rising NPAs. Websol's own balance sheet is clean, but a price war does not spare the solvent.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: EBITDA margin stabilising around 30%+ for two consecutive quarters (proving a floor exists), a credible funded path to 3 GW+ of cell capacity, or a move into TOPCon with committed offtake.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin dropping below ~25%, utilisation falling as capacity floods in, or the order book shrinking materially from ₹1,278 cr.
  • Where it gets interesting: a de-rating toward ₹60–70 would price in normalised margins rather than peak ones, and at that point the clean balance sheet and 92% utilisation become a genuinely attractive setup.
