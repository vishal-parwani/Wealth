Verdict

The market doubled the multiple in a day, and the first quarter as a listed company did not earn it. Sell. The IPO priced Tempsens at about ₹2,517 crd, or 35x FY26 profit of ₹71 cr. On the listing day the stock opened 111% higher, and at ₹576 it is 68x the same profit. FY26 revenue grew 17% and profit 13%. The first quarterly result after listing showed revenue up 33%, helped by acquisitions in Germany and India. Operating margin fell to 20.2%, from 23.6% a year earlier and 21.8% in March. Consolidated profit rose 16%, attributable profit 14%, and standalone profit under 3%.

The business is good; the price assumes it is extraordinary. Tempsens is the largest Indian maker of contact and non-contact temperature sensors, with a claimed 10.5% share. It has three segments — sensing (45% of FY26 revenue), electrical heating (21%) and specialised cables (35%) — and exports are a third of product revenue, growing 78% in Q1. ROCE is a respectable 18%, but it has fallen every year from 29% in FY23. Inventory days rose from 107 to 170 over the same period. Management guides to about 27% revenue growth and says profit should catch up by year-end. Even if both happen, FY27 EPS is about ₹10.7d and the stock is at 54x.

The tweet, tested. @TheAlpha10X listed Tempsens among "my recent 9 stock bets across emerging themes" (27 Sep 2026). The theme — industrial electrification, process heat and the instrumentation that measures it — is real, and Tempsens is a genuine pure play on it. But the numbers do not show an emerging-theme growth rate: 17% revenue and 13% profit growth in FY26, then 16% profit growth in Q1 with margins falling. The bet was made at about 1.9x the IPO price. The theme is right, but the entry price does not leave much return.

Selling shareholders took ₹555 cr off the table at ₹300 through the offer for sale. The remaining 50% of the anchor book, about 32 lakh shares, comes out of lock-in around 23 November. Mutual funds hold 5.35% of equity, all of it bought at or near the issue.

Business

Nature of business

A thermal-engineering manufacturer serving process industries, for both new projects and OEMs and for maintenance, repair and operations. It has three verticals. Temperature sensing (44.6% of FY26) covers thermocouples, RTDs, pyrometers, thermal imagers and fibre-optic sensors, and Tempsens says it is the only Indian maker of non-contact sensors. Electrical heating (20.7%) covers industrial heaters, and is the fastest-growing at +149% in Q1, partly from acquisitions. Specialised cables (34.7%) covers mineral-insulated and compensating cables. Exports are about a third of product revenue.

Moat

Real but narrow. Sensors that sit in a steel furnace or a glass tank are qualified once and then replaced on a schedule, so MRO demand recurs and customers are slow to switch suppliers. Breadth across contact, non-contact and fibre-optic sensing is hard to replicate domestically. Against that, the sensor market is small, global players (Watlow, WIKA, Endress+Hauser) compete at the high end, and cables are closer to a commodity.

Risks

Valuation reset. A return to the IPO multiple of 35x FY26 earnings is ₹300. Even 45x FY27 on the optimistic +27% case is about ₹480, 17% below today. The price relies on listing enthusiasm holding up, and the anchor unlock around 23 November is the first test of it.
  • Margin trend. Four periods of falling operating margin, and materials and staff costs rising faster than revenue.
  • Growth from acquisitions. Standalone Q1 PAT grew under 3%. Consolidated growth relies on newly acquired subsidiaries whose economics are not separately disclosed.
  • Working capital. Inventory days went from 107 to 170 in three years, and the cash conversion cycle reached 210 days.
  • Supply overhang. About 32 lakh anchor shares unlock around 23 November, and float turnover is very high, with about 32 lakh shares a day after the listing frenzy.
  • Short public record. Six weeks of trading, one listed quarter, and no five-quarter shareholding history.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: Q2 FY27 margin back above 22% with consolidated PAT growth above 25%. Or the price falls to about ₹430, roughly 40x FY27 on the +27% case.
  • Upgrade to Buy: two quarters of 25%-plus organic profit growth at 23%-plus margins, at a price near 35x forward earnings, about ₹375 on today's arithmetic.
