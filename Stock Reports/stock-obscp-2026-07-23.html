Update · 13 Aug 2026 — re-review after Q1 FY27 (12 Aug). The business inflected; the rating does not move, and the reason is only price. Revenue ₹78.7 cr, +84.7% YoY, PAT +88.3%, with the order book now above ₹1,200 cr. That order book is roughly 5.5x FY26 revenue — exceptional visibility for a company this size. Growth also accelerated: FY26 was +53.8% revenue and +61.2% PAT, so Q1 is a step up, not a continuation. FY26 itself was strong — revenue ₹219.5 cr, PAT ₹27.0 cr, EBITDA ₹43.6 cr (+57%) at a 19.5% margin, with exports +50% and non-automotive revenue +150%. The diversification into defence, aerospace and robotics has stopped being a slide and started being revenue.

Two new things to weigh. First, the Supa build-out: 11+ acres bought for ~₹17–18 cr, which management believes could generate ₹700–800 cr of revenue at full capacity from that plant alone, against group revenue of ₹219.5 cr today. Second, a preferential issue of 14,39,600 shares at ₹601 raising up to ₹86.52 cr, with an EGM on 2 Sep 2026 also seeking an increase in authorised capital. Note the issue price is below the market — roughly 4–5% dilution, priced at a discount, to fund the expansion.

Rating held (Hold · High risk). This is now a much closer call than on 3 Aug, and the hesitation is entirely valuation. On FY26 earnings the stock is around 80x; even annualising the much stronger Q1 it is roughly 55x. Work the Supa case forward — group revenue reaching ₹800–1,000 cr in three years at a low-teens net margin — and you get to roughly ₹100–120 cr of profit, which at a sane 30x is only about 30–60% above today's market cap. That is a real return, but it is three years out, it assumes the expansion lands on schedule, and it comes with SME-scale liquidity, pending dilution and a plant that has not yet produced anything. Strong execution already in the price.

Verdict

Genuinely accelerating precision-machining business, priced as though the acceleration is permanent. OBSC makes precision metal components for tier-1 suppliers into India's major automakers. The numbers are improving, not just growing: FY26 total income ₹223.5 cr (+53.9%) with PAT ₹27.0 cr (+61.2%) — profit outpacing revenue, i.e. margin expansion — and the exit quarter was stronger still, with Q4 FY26 sales ₹71.5 cr (+77.4%) and PAT ₹8.7 cr (+70.1%). Order flow supports it: a ₹23 cr auto-components award with a seven-year programme life and a ₹10.6 cr order from a French customer, the latter useful evidence that the quality clears export scrutiny rather than only domestic tier-2 standards.

The catch is what you pay: ~56x on a ₹27 cr profit, at an all-time high set on 2 July, in an NSE SME line. Auto components is a cyclical, customer-dictated business — tier-1s squeeze suppliers when volumes soften, and a seven-year programme is only as good as the platform it sits on. The long-programme visibility and export validation are real positives that distinguish this from pure momentum, but they don't justify chasing the high. Hold if owned; add on weakness, not strength.

Business

Manufactures a range of precision metal components — machined parts supplied to tier-1 vendors who in turn serve India's leading automotive OEMs. This is a second-tier position in the auto supply chain: the customer is the tier-1, not the carmaker, which means less pricing power but also lower direct exposure to any single OEM's fortunes. The recent ₹23 cr award carries an estimated seven-year programme life cycle — in auto components that is the valuable kind of order, because tooling and qualification make supplier switching expensive once a programme starts. The French order signals a nascent export/quality credential.

Bull case

  • Margin is expanding, not just revenue — PAT growth outpaced income growth in both FY26 and Q4, which is the hallmark of operating leverage in a machining business as utilisation rises.
  • Programme-life visibility: a seven-year award converts to predictable, annuity-like volume; qualification costs deter competitive displacement.
  • Export validation: the French order suggests the quality system clears international audit — the route to better margins than domestic tier-2 work.
  • Q4 acceleration (+77% sales) suggests FY27 opened from a higher base than the full-year average implies.

Risks

~56x, at an all-time high, on an SME line. Precision machining is a good business but not a 56x business through a cycle — the multiple assumes the current growth rate and margin expansion both persist. Any auto-volume softness compresses earnings and multiple together.
  • Tier-2 position: selling to tier-1s means margins are dictated from above; cost-down demands are routine in auto supply chains.
  • Cyclicality: Indian auto volumes have been strong; a downturn hits utilisation, and machining is fixed-cost heavy so margins fall faster than revenue.
  • Customer concentration at this scale; order sizes (₹10–23 cr) are meaningful relative to a ₹223 cr revenue base, so both wins and losses move the needle.
  • Capital intensity: CNC capacity expansion consumes cash; watch whether growth is funded from operations or borrowings.
  • SME platform: lot-size trading, thin liquidity, negligible analyst coverage; Q1 FY27 was not even reliably locatable in public sources.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a pullback toward ~₹480–540 (~40x) with growth intact, or Q1/H1 FY27 confirming margin expansion is durable and export orders scaling beyond one-off wins.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin reversal as auto volumes normalise, a lost programme, or working capital ballooning to fund growth.
