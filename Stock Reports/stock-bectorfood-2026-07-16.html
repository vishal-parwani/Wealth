Watch / Smart-Money Entry

Verdict

A quality franchise in an earnings lull, marked to the bottom by a smart-money entry. The trigger: on 15 Jul 2026, Sunil Singhania's Abakkus bought ~29.4 lakh shares (0.96%) at ₹168.97 — almost exactly the 52-week low of ₹168.60 — and the stock popped 15% the next day to a ₹195 intraday high. The fundamentals explain why it was there: FY26 revenue grew 9.1% to ₹2,044 cr but PAT actually fell 1.6% to ₹140.9 cr as margin pressure ate the growth; the stock had fallen ~45% from its ₹318 high. At ~₹190 you're paying ~41x flat earnings — not cheap on numbers, cheap only against its own history and brand quality (Cremica biscuits, English Oven premium bakery, buns supplier to McDonald's/Burger King India). Anand Rathi sees 48% upside. This is a "follow the entry, not the pop" situation: attractive nearer Abakkus's ₹169 cost, ordinary at ₹190+ until margins visibly turn.

Update · 23 Jul 2026: Up to ₹208 (22 Jul) — now ~23% above Abakkus's ₹169 entry, i.e. further from the smart-money cost basis. Reinforces the wait-for-a-pullback stance; rating unchanged (Watch / Smart-Money Entry).

Business

Two engines: biscuits under Mrs. Bector's Cremica (strong in North India, plus exports to 60+ countries) and bakery under English Oven — one of India's largest premium bread/bun makers and a key QSR supplier (McDonald's, Burger King, KFC ecosystem). Capacity expansion (new Dhar/Khopoli-type greenfield plants) has been the recent capex story — the cause of near-term margin drag and the source of the volume runway.

Bull case

  • Abakkus's bottom-up small/mid-cap discipline buying the exact low is a strong quality signal — this is now a top-followed position from the KB-captured portfolio (₹50 cr initial).
  • QSR-linked bakery volumes grow structurally with India's fast-food expansion, largely independent of biscuit-category competition.
  • FY26 was a capex/margin trough; operating leverage from new plants can restart PAT growth in FY27–28.
  • 45% drawdown has reset expectations; Q4 already showed margin stabilising (12.7% vs 12.5% YoY).

Risks

41x for zero profit growth. If FY27 PAT stays flat, there's no valuation support until the low ₹30s P/E — i.e., meaningfully lower prices. The 15% pop on the Abakkus news can fade as fast as it came.
  • Biscuits face brutal competition (Britannia, Parle, ITC) with limited pricing power; input costs (wheat, palm oil, sugar) swing margins.
  • QSR client concentration in bakery; slower QSR store-adds directly caps growth.
  • North-India skew in biscuits limits national brand leverage.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: price back near ₹170–175, or two quarters of double-digit PAT growth confirming the margin turn.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: another year of flat/declining PAT, or QSR contract losses.
