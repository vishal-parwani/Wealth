Verdict

A well-run small manufacturer making a bet several times its own size. Worth following, not worth chasing at the high. Kalyani Cast-Tech makes shipping containers (including double-stack "dwarf" containers for electrified rail routes), steel and iron castings, and is now building railway wagons at a new 144-acre campus in Kutch, Gujarat. Customers include CONCOR, Indian Railways and DP World. The existing business is good: 27% return on capital, effectively no debt, customers paying in 80 days and stock turning in 17 days, which is unusually tight for this kind of engineering.

Two things to be clear about. First, last year was not a growth year. FY26 revenue was ₹152 cr, up 8%, and the second half actually shrank (₹58 cr against ₹69 cr a year earlier) on weak steel prices, even though margin recovered to 18%. Profit still rose 20% to ₹17.1 cr. Second, the price already reflects the future: ₹1,403, up 220% in a year, at its 52-week high, on 67x trailing earnings and 12x book.

The growth case is real but not yet earning anything. The company is spending ₹450–500 cr over four to five years, and the wagon plant starts commercial production in the second half of FY27 with a first phase of about 2,500 wagons a year, and stage-1 RDSO approval in hand. Third-party analysis puts FY27 revenue near ₹262 cr and FY28 near ₹393 cr. If FY28 lands with a 12% net margin, this is about 22xd those earnings, which is reasonable. That is two years away and depends on a first-time wagon builder ramping to schedule, funded largely from its own cash. Hold, high risk. Only about ₹1.2 cr of stock trades a day, so this is a position you cannot get out of quickly.

Two name traps. This is not part of the Kalyani Group (Bharat Forge, Kalyani Steels). It's a separate company founded in 2012, run by Naresh Kumar, a former CONCOR and Pipavav Railway technical head. And on the NSE the symbol "KALYANI" belongs to a different company trading near ₹145. The right series for this stock is BSE 544023 / KALYANI.BO.

Business

What it does

Three things. Containers: ISO 20- and 40-foot boxes, heavy and coil containers, and dwarf containers that can be stacked two-high under electrified railway lines, a niche created by Indian Railways' own network. Capacity is being expanded from about 6,000 to 16,000 units a year. Castings: steel and iron parts such as brake blocks, couplers and bearing housings, mostly for railways. Wagons: the new business, at the Kutch campus, targeting about 2,500 units a year in phase 1 and 7,500–8,000 over four to five years. Customers are CONCOR, Indian Railways, DP World and private logistics operators.

Moat

Modest but real in one place. Wagon supply to Indian Railways needs RDSO approval, which takes years and keeps out newcomers. Dwarf containers are a niche with few Indian makers. Against that, castings are competitive, container making has a big Chinese cost advantage, and the customer list is dominated by government-linked buyers who set the terms.

Order book

No order book is disclosed. The forward commitments are the capex plan (₹450–500 cr over four to five years), the wagon capacity targets, and the new container depot approval. For a company this size, the absence of a disclosed order book is the main information gap: there's no way to check the FY27–28 revenue path from filings.

Risks

A ₹1,000 cr company spending ₹450–500 cr on a business it has never run. Wagon building is competitive, and the buyer is largely Indian Railways. If the ramp slips a year, FY28 earnings don't arrive and 67x trailing is all you own.
  • You can't get out quickly. About ₹1.2 cr trades a day. A modest sell order moves the price.
  • Funding: ₹5 cr of cash and ₹18 cr a year of operating cash against a ₹450–500 cr plan. Debt or new shares will be needed.
  • Steel prices swing both revenue and margin, as the second half of FY26 showed.
  • Government customers: slow payments and lumpy tender timing.
  • Thin disclosure: half-yearly reporting, no order book, no broker coverage, no fund ownership, and an unexplained promoter-stake drop.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: first wagon orders disclosed with values, or the September 2026 half showing revenue above ₹120 cr (on track for the ₹262 cr FY27 figure) with margin at or above 15%. A pullback to about ₹1,000 (roughly 16x the FY28 target profit) would also do it.
  • Downgrade to Sell: the wagon plant slipping past FY27, a large equity raise at a discount, another half-year of falling revenue, or an explanation of the promoter-stake drop that turns out to be selling.
