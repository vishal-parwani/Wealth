Verdict

Update · 24 Sep 2026 — Hold reaffirmed, high risk. The QIP is approved but still unpriced. Up 13.5% since 24 August to ₹1,692, taking trailing P/E from 105x to 120x. Shareholders approved the ₹750 cr QIP at the 28 August AGM, but it has not been placed, so the August condition — the QIP placed with named long-only institutions at a price near the market — is still open, and so is the dilution.

Three smaller developments since: the company bought 850 shares of Dheya Engineering for ₹3 cr on 7 September, aimed at aerospace gas-turbine technology; CARE assigned an A (stable) issuer rating on 15 September; and the stock is being added to the Nifty India Defence and Equal Weight indices this month, which brings passive buying unrelated to earnings. Q1 FY27 (revenue ₹107.6 cr, +71%; PAT ₹27.9 cr, +46%) remains the latest quarter.

The upgrade test is unchanged: two more quarters above ₹100 cr of revenue with operating margin held over 30%, proving the weak December quarter was the anomaly. At 120x, index inclusion is not a reason to pay up.

A high-quality niche, a lumpy P&L, and a valuation that assumes neither of those things matters. Unimech makes aero engine and airframe tooling — the ground support equipment, jigs and fixtures used to assemble and maintain aircraft — plus precision components. Gross margins run near 65% and the company guides to 34–35% EBITDA margin. That is a real, defensible business.

The record is more uneven than the multiple implies. Revenue was ₹209 cr in FY24, ₹243 cr in FY25 and ₹240 cr in FY26 — flat for a year — and net profit went ₹58 cr, ₹83 cr, ₹63 cr. The December 2025 quarter is the one to look at: revenue ₹34 cr against ₹62 cr the quarter before, operating margin 4.6%, net profit ₹2 cr. Tooling revenue is recognised on delivery of large one-off items, so quarters swing violently.

It is now recovering hard. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹108 cr, up 71% year on year, net profit ₹27.9 cr, and the order book grew from ₹215 cr to ₹280 cr in a quarter. A long-term supply agreement with Austria's FACC, a US$10m infusion into the Saudi joint venture and a plan to double gross block by FY27 all point the same way.

Against that: 105x trailing earnings, 10.3x book, and an 8% return on equity — because ₹603 cr of the balance sheet is cash and investments from the IPO earning treasury income rather than manufacturing returns. Other income is ₹7–15 cr a quarter, a meaningful chunk of profit. And the board has just approved a QIP of up to ₹750 cr, which will add another 10% to the share count at a price yet to be set. Hold, and the risk tag is new this refresh: quarterly earnings that can fall 90% sequentially do not belong in an ordinary-risk bucket.

Business

What it does

Founded in 2016 in Bengaluru, Unimech builds the tooling used to assemble and maintain aircraft and aero engines: ground support equipment, engine build-up stands, jigs, fixtures and precision components, made to airframer and engine-maker specifications. It is an export business — the customers are global aerospace OEMs and their tier-one suppliers — and it has been extending into flying parts, where the FACC agreement is the first significant win. A Saudi joint venture gives it a Middle East manufacturing base.

Moat

Real, and it is qualification-based. Aerospace tooling is built to an OEM's drawing and validated for a specific engine or airframe programme; once approved, the supplier holds that tooling package for the programme's life, and a competitor cannot bid for it again. High gross margin — around 65% — is the evidence that this is not commodity machining. The limit is scale: the addressable market for any one tooling package is small, so growth means winning new programmes continually rather than compounding on an installed base.

Risks

105x earnings on a P&L that produced ₹2 cr of net profit two quarters ago. Operating margin has ranged from 4.6% to 43% across four consecutive quarters. If FY27 turns out like FY26 — revenue flat, margin down six points — there is no earnings floor under this price.
  • The ₹750 cr QIP is unpriced. Roughly 10% dilution at a price the board has not yet set, into a stock 4% below its all-time high.
  • Treasury income flatters profit. ₹7–15 cr a quarter of other income on IPO cash; in the December quarter it was five times operating profit.
  • No active institutional owner. Two passive ETF/index positions totalling 4,345 shares. Nobody with a research team has underwritten these numbers.
  • 14% free float on a ₹7,592 cr company, 1.54 lakh shares a day. Price discovery is thin in both directions.
  • Order book of one year's revenue — shorter visibility than the multiple implies, and lumpy delivery timing.
  • Execution risk on the expansion: doubling gross block, a Saudi joint venture and a move into flying parts, all at once, from a ₹285 cr revenue base.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: two more quarters at ₹100 cr-plus revenue with operating margin held above 30% — proving the December quarter was an anomaly rather than the norm — or the QIP placed with named long-only institutions at a price near the market, which would both validate the story and fix the float.
  • Downgrade to Sell: another quarter like December 2025, the order book falling back below ₹200 cr, or the QIP being placed at a material discount.
