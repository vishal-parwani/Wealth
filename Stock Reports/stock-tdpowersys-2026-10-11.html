Verdict

The quality is real; the dip is not. Hold. TD Power makes AC generators and motors for steam, gas and hydro turbines, diesel gensets and railways, and almost all of its new orders now come from exports. Revenue has gone from ₹872 cr in FY23 to ₹2,124 cr trailing, a 29% three-year compound rate. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 72% and management raised FY27 guidance to ₹2,600 cr, 40% above FY26. ROCE has climbed every year from 16% to 34%, operating margin is steady at 17–19%, and the company has net cash.

What it costs: 90.6x trailing earnings and 67x the two-analyst FY27 estimate. At 53x FY28, the multiple already assumes guidance is met and beaten. The consensus target of ₹888 is 12% above spot.

Where the name came from. It was on @TheAlpha10X's 20 September 2026 list of 63 "high growth + high quality" stocks for buying the dip. Growth: confirmed. Sales compounded at 29% for three years and are accelerating, profit grew 37% in FY26 and 72% in Q1. Quality: mostly confirmed, with one exception that matters. ROCE is 34%, there is net cash and margin is stable. But the business turns profit into cash poorly. Operating cash flow was 39% of operating profit in FY25 and 59% in FY26, and free cash flow was ₹19 cr in FY26 against ₹239 cr of profit. Receivable days have risen from 112 to 146 in two years. The dip: refuted. At ₹793 the stock is 7% below its ₹856 high and 2.7 times its 52-week low. There was no dip to buy on 20 September, and there is none now.

The fundraise is the open question. On 20 September the two promoters were allotted 12.5 lakh shares at ₹600, 24% below today's price. A QIP of up to ₹600 cr opened on 7 October at a floor of ₹775. The company has ₹199 cr of cash, ₹18 cr of debt and only ₹50 cr of FY27 capex planned. The stated reasons are larger generators above 100 MW and a possible expansion to ₹4,000 cr+ of revenue capacity by FY29–30. Those are ambitions, not commitments yet. Dilution is about 2.5%: small, but it is being taken near the high.

Business

Nature of business

The company makes AC generators for steam, gas and hydro turbines and for gas and diesel engines. It also makes large electric motors, traction motors and components for railways, plus a small engineering and services segment. Customers are global turbine and engine makers, for whom TD Power is an outsourced generator supplier. Exports and deemed exports were 93% of Q1 FY27 inflow. Demand comes from new gas-fired capacity in the US, much of it for data-centre power, from distributed power and from Indian Railways. The ₹192 cr US order and the planned move above 100 MW are the data-centre exposure in concrete form.

There is no listed Indian pure-play generator maker. Triveni Turbine, which makes the steam turbines that drive generators, earns the same ROCE at half the multiple. CG Power, the market's favourite for motors, is pricier still. TD Power's multiple sits with the momentum names, not the value ones.

Moat

Moderate. Generators are engineered to order for each turbine, and qualification with a global OEM takes years. Once designed in, a supplier tends to stay for the life of a turbine platform. That stickiness is real. The limit is concentration: a handful of global OEMs are the customers and hold the pricing power, and TD Power competes for their outsourced volume against in-house capacity and Chinese and European suppliers.

Order book

₹2,207 cr consolidated at 30 Jun 2026, ≈ 1.04x trailing revenue. This is a confirmed book. Q1 inflow was ₹734 cr, and the ₹192 cr US order came after the quarter. Sources disagree on the export share of the book, 69.5% against 57% excluding railways unreconciled. A book of about one year's revenue is normal for a short-cycle generator maker and is consistent with the ₹2,600 cr guidance.

Risks

The multiple prices in the US gas-turbine cycle continuing. At 90x trailing and 53x FY28, a slowdown in US gas-plant orders, or in data-centre power spending, would hit growth and the multiple at the same time. About 93% of new orders are exports, so this is not an India story.
  • Cash conversion. ₹43 cr of free cash flow against ₹532 cr of profit over three years, and receivable days are rising.
  • Customer concentration. A small number of global OEMs, each of which could in-source or switch suppliers.
  • Dilution near the high. The ₹600 cr QIP and the ₹75 cr preferential issue come before a capex plan has been firmly committed.
  • Thin coverage. Two analysts. Estimates move in large steps and price reactions to results can be sharp.
  • Currency and tariffs. A dollar-revenue business exposed to US trade policy on imported electrical equipment.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a real dip, to about ₹600 or 40x FY28 consensus; or H1 FY27 tracking ahead of the ₹2,600 cr guidance with operating cash flow above 75% of operating profit, which would close the quality gap.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating margin below 16% for two quarters, quarterly order inflow below ₹500 cr for two quarters, or the QIP proceeds parked without a committed capacity plan by FY27 end.
