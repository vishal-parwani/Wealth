Hold
Do not confuse this with NRB Industrial Bearings (NRBIB). Different listed company, ~₹88 cr market cap, loss-making — it posted a ₹6.59 cr consolidated net loss in Q1 FY27 on ₹18.9 cr of revenue. This report is about NRB Bearings, the ~₹3,900 cr needle-roller bearings maker. The names are close enough that screeners and news feeds mix them up regularly.

Verdict

The same aerospace pivot as Sansera, at roughly a third of the multiple — because it is at roughly a tenth of the progress. NRB is India's needle-roller bearings specialist. FY26 looked spectacular: PAT +77% to ₹146 cr on revenue up only 11% to ₹1,335 cr, with EBITDA +19% to ₹267 cr. That gap is operating leverage off a weak FY25 base, not a new growth rate — and Q1 FY27 says so plainly: revenue +19.2% to ₹370 cr but PAT only +15% to ₹38 cr. Profit is now growing slower than sales.

The aerospace story is real but embryonic. NRB acquired Mahant Tool Room (MTRPL), which has since been awarded AS9100D certification — the entry ticket to aerospace supply. Management talks about addressing a $14.5–16.5 bn aerospace market and doubling group revenue in five years. Note what is missing: any disclosed aerospace revenue. Compare Sansera, whose ADS division already bills ₹315 cr a year with ₹3,870 cr of lifetime orders and Boeing Tier-1 status. At ~27x for ~15% profit growth, NRB is priced sensibly for what it is today and cheaply for what it might become. That is a Hold, not a bargain.

The business

India's leading maker of needle roller bearings and friction-reduction components, supplying automotive (2W, PV, CV), EV and industrial customers. Management is repositioning the company from a bearings vendor to a "system partner" in advanced friction solutions, leaning on proprietary simulation and computational analysis to move up the value chain. FY26 saw ROE, ROCE and ROIC all improve.

The aerospace angle — honestly sized

  • Mahant Tool Room (MTRPL) acquired, giving NRB an entry into aerospace and defence manufacturing.
  • AS9100D certification awarded to MTRPL — the aerospace quality standard, and a genuine prerequisite rather than a marketing badge.
  • Management cites a $14.5–16.5 bn addressable aerospace market and a goal of doubling group revenue within five years.
  • No aerospace revenue has been disclosed. This is a certification and an acquisition, not yet a P&L line. Treat it as an option with a long expiry, not a driver of the next two years.

Bull case

  • ~27x is a reasonable price for a category leader with improving returns — a third of Sansera's 74x for a similar strategic direction.
  • EBITDA margin around 20% is healthy for an auto ancillary.
  • Structural moat: needle-roller bearings are engineered-in per platform, with high switching costs.
  • Aerospace optionality is free at this multiple — nothing in the price appears to assume it works.
  • EV and industrial diversification reduces pure ICE dependence.

Risks

FY26's +77% PAT is a base effect, not a trend. Revenue grew 11% while profit grew 77% — arithmetic that only works off a depressed prior year (FY25 included a notably weak Q4). Q1 FY27 is the cleaner read: PAT +15% against revenue +19.2%, i.e. profit growth has already fallen below revenue growth. Anyone extrapolating the FY26 number will be badly wrong.
  • Aerospace is years from mattering — certification is step one of a multi-year qualification cycle.
  • Auto cyclicality across two-wheelers, passenger and commercial vehicles.
  • Competitive intensity from global bearing majors (SKF, Schaeffler, Timken, NSK) with deeper R&D budgets.
  • Ticker confusion with NRB Industrial Bearings can distort screener data and news sentiment.
  • No visible analyst coverage surfaced — no consensus target to anchor against.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: first disclosed aerospace/defence revenue with an order book attached, or two quarters of PAT growth reaccelerating above revenue growth with margins holding ~20%.
  • Downgrade to Sell: EBITDA margin slipping below ~17%, or the MTRPL acquisition proving to be a cost centre with no qualified programme wins by FY28.
