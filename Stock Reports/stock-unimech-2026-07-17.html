Verdict

A genuinely rare franchise, bought today at ~90x earnings that just fell 24%. Unimech is one of the very few Indian companies making complex "build-to-print" aero-engine and airframe tooling — engine lifting beams, calibration tooling, ground support equipment — plus precision components, exported to marquee aerospace customers across 7 countries. The moat is real: AS9100-grade process certification, single-digit global competitors in its niches, and an export-first model with historically fat margins. But FY26 told a more sober story: revenue grew 18% to ₹287 cr while net profit fell ~24% to ₹63.3 cr (margin 22% vs 34% in FY25) as costs and mix normalised from the IPO-year peak. The stock nevertheless sprinted 9% to a 52-week high of ₹1,308 on 16 Jul when Motilal Oswal initiated with a Buy and ₹1,530 target — a target that itself needs 50x FY28E earnings to work. Everything now rests on the FY27–28 scale-up materialising on schedule. Great business, wrong entry: at ~90x trailing with declining profits, one soft quarter can cost 30%. Watch, and let a post-euphoria dip provide the entry.

Business

Bengaluru-based maker of high-precision aerospace tooling and components on build-to-print (customer designs) and build-to-specification (Unimech engineers) models. Product families: aero-engine tooling (lifting beams, balancing/calibration kits), airframe tooling and platforms, ground support equipment, and precision machined/sheet-metal parts. Also serves defence, energy and semiconductor capital-equipment customers. FY22–24 shipped 2,356 tooling SKUs and 624 precision parts to 26 customers across 7 countries — an export-dominant, engine-MRO-linked demand base (GE/airline MRO ecosystem).

Bull case

  • Structural niche: global engine-MRO volumes are exploding (LEAP/GTF shop visits ramp through 2030) and tooling qualifies slowly — incumbents keep the business for a program's life.
  • China+1 / friend-shoring pushes aerospace supply chains toward certified Indian vendors; Unimech is among the few at scale.
  • IPO proceeds (₹500 cr, Dec 2024) fund capacity that should convert to FY27–28 revenue acceleration — the thesis MOSL is underwriting at 25%+ CAGR.
  • Revenue +18% in FY26 shows demand intact even as margins reset.

Risks

Earnings went down while the price went up. Profit −24%, stock at all-time/52-week highs, ~90x trailing: the multiple assumes the FY26 margin reset is temporary and the capacity ramp lands on time. If FY27 margins stay ~22%, even MOSL's 50x FY28E math stops working.
  • Small revenue base (₹287 cr) with lumpy, order-driven deliveries — quarterly volatility is structural, and the market currently has no tolerance for a miss.
  • Customer concentration typical of aero tooling (top clients dominate); a program pause hits hard.
  • Broker-initiation spikes often mark short-term tops; anchor/early-investor supply can cap rallies.
  • Margin normalisation may be the new normal: FY25's 34% net margin looks like an outlier, not the baseline.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: price back toward ₹950–1,050 (~55–60x with the growth intact), or quarterly results showing margins recovering toward ~28%+ with 25%+ revenue growth — evidence the FY26 dip was transition, not trend.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: a second year of profit decline, or export order softness as MRO cycles digest.
