Speculative / Watch

Verdict

Solid services business, but the cheapest label of the three hides the weakest moat. TechD (formerly TechDefence Labs) is a CERT-In empanelled cybersecurity services firm — audits, VAPT, compliance, training — growing fast (FY26 revenue +79% to ₹53.2 cr, PAT +68% to ₹14.0 cr) and trading at ~27x earnings, the lowest multiple among Kedia's cyber trio. But services businesses scale with headcount, not code: margins (~26% PAT) are good yet structurally below TAC's product margins, and revenue durability depends on client renewals rather than platform lock-in. At ₹573, the stock has nearly tripled over its ₹193 IPO price (Sep 2025) and sits mid-way in its ₹360–844 range. Watch for a couple more quarters of execution or a better price.

Update · 23 Jul 2026: Up ~15% to ₹660 (16 Jul). Won 5 orders worth ~₹1.57 cr (domestic + UAE) on 14 Jul; TECHD ONE roadshow running across 10 cities (Jul–Sep). Momentum positive but valuation richer after the run; rating unchanged (Speculative / Watch).

Business

CERT-In empanelled, ISO 27001-certified provider of end-to-end cybersecurity services: security audits, penetration testing, compliance (incl. RBI/SEBI-driven mandates), managed security, and training. Clients span BFSI, NBFCs, manufacturing, healthcare, aviation, IT/ITES, education and government. International share of revenue rose to 21% in H1 FY26 from 15% in FY25 — the export push is working.

Bull case

  • Regulatory tailwind: Indian compliance mandates (CERT-In empanelment is a real gate) create recurring, non-discretionary demand.
  • 79% revenue growth with ~26% PAT margins and minimal capital intensity.
  • Cheapest of the Kedia cyber trio on P/E while growing at a similar rate.
  • Rising export mix diversifies away from India-only pricing pressure.

Risks

Services, not software. No proprietary platform means people-cost inflation, attrition risk, and price competition from Big-4 and larger IT firms as the segment matures. The growth rate will be harder to defend than a SaaS peer's.
  • Tiny base: ₹53 cr revenue; one or two lost anchor clients would show up immediately.
  • Post-IPO volatility: listed <1 year, already traded ₹360–844; SME liquidity is thin and lot-size bound.
  • Kedia's smallest position of the three (~5.26%) — conviction signal is weakest here.
  • Data caveat: figures from press releases and aggregator sites; no audited-filing review.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: price into the low ₹400s, or evidence of productised/recurring revenue (managed-services contracts, IP-led offerings) lifting margin durability.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: growth halving, receivable stretch, or senior-team churn.
