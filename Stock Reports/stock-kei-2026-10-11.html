Verdict

The operating story is improving, but the cash story is not there yet. At 37xd FY27 earnings and a 20% ROCE, KEI costs about the same as Polycab, which earns 33% and generates free cash. Hold. The P&L is good. Revenue grew 21% in FY26 and 23% in Q1 FY27, PAT rose 40% in Q1, and the operating margin has risen four quarters in a row from 9.9% to 12.4%. The order book rose to ₹4,292 cr from ₹3,585 cr in March, and Sanand Phase I began production in December 2025.

The cash flow statement is the problem. Free cash flow was −₹726 cr in FY25 and −₹412 cr in FY26, and the cash conversion cycle is 102 days against Polycab's 39. Capex is guided at ₹600–700 cr a year for three to four years, and a further ₹700 cr greenfield plant at Tijara was approved in August. The ₹2,000 cr QIP of late 2024 is why the balance sheet still shows ₹1,260 cr of net cash. ROCE has fallen from 27% to 20% while that capital waits to earn.

Testing the tweet (Jefferies "India Industrial Revolution" via NDTV Profit, 10/14 Sep 2026: KEI a Buy as a data-centre beneficiary).

Confirmed: Jefferies named KEI among its five preferred industrial stocks on 2 Sep 2026, Buy with a ₹6,920 target. That was 30% upside then and is 51% now, after the stock fell 17% in a month. Its industrial-revolution note of 9 Sep sizes data centres at 2 → 10 GW.

Partly supported: the data-centre link runs through EHV and HT power cables to substations. EHV sales were ₹186 cr in Q1, up 48% but under 6% of revenue, and KEI does not disclose a data-centre figure. It is a real but small contributor. The tweet's direction is fine. The rating here turns on cash conversion, not on the theme.

Counter-argument: KEI is the cable maker best placed against the Ultravolt threat. It is institutional, EHV and export-weighted, with little retail-wire exposure, yet it fell as hard as wire-heavy RR Kabel (−9% intraday on 4 Sep). If Sanand lifts utilisation and the margin reaches 13%+, FY27 consensus profit growth of 29% looks achievable. At ₹4,585 the stock is 23% below its high, with consensus 25% above spot. The upgrade case is close. It needs one year of positive free cash flow.

Business

Nature of business

KEI makes power cables across voltages: LT, HT and EHV (220 kV and above), plus control and instrumentation cables, house wires and stainless-steel wire. It also runs an EPC arm for cabling projects. Its customers are weighted towards utilities, infrastructure, industrial and real-estate projects, with a growing retail house-wire channel and exports (~16% of FY26 revenued). Capacity is anchored at Pathredi, Chopanki and Bhiwadi (Rajasthan), Silvassa, and now the Sanand (Gujarat) greenfield.

Moat

A moderate moat, strongest at the top of the voltage range. EHV cable needs utility approvals, long type-testing and specialised plant, and only a handful of Indian makers qualify. That part of the business is protected. The LT and house-wire end is exposed to the same entrants as everyone else.

Order book

₹4,292 cr as at 30 Jun 26, ≈ 0.35xd trailing revenue, up from ₹3,585 cr at 31 Mar 26. Split: domestic cables ₹2,400 cr, exports ₹822 cr, EHV ₹793 cr, EPC ₹271 cr. These are confirmed pending orders as reported on the Q1 call. Most revenue is still channel and spot business outside the book.

Risks

The capex has to earn its keep. ₹2,688 cr of fixed assets and CWIP, up from ₹891 cr in two years, plus ₹600–700 cr a year of guided capex and ₹700 cr for Tijara. If Sanand utilisation lags, ROCE keeps falling from 20%, and the 37x forward multipled has no support.
  • Working capital. A 102-day cash conversion cycle with inventory days at 100. Institutional and EPC customers pay slowly, and growth absorbs cash.
  • Income-tax search outcome unresolved, with an emphasis of matter in the audit report.
  • New entrants. Ultravolt and Adani start in wires, but scale players tend to move up into LT and HT cables over time.
  • Copper and aluminium price swings flow through inventory and receivables.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: positive free cash flow for FY27 (or H1 FY27 operating cash flow above capex) with the margin held at 12%+. Or the price falls below about ₹4,000 (roughly 32x FY27 consensus EPSd) with the business intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a tax demand material to net worth from the May 2026 search, operating margin back below 10% as entrants reach cables, or net cash turning to net debt before Sanand Phase II.
