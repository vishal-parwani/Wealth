Verdict

A real, high-quality business, priced for growth that is not yet showing in the profit line. Hold, high risk. Azad makes turbine blades (airfoils) and other flight- and life-critical rotating parts for gas, steam and aero engines. It sells to GE Vernova, Mitsubishi Heavy, Siemens Energy, Baker Hughes and Honeywell, and has builds under way for Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney Canada. FY26 consolidated revenue rose 32% to ₹603 cr at a 37% operating margin. The order book was about ₹6,500 cr at 31 March 2026, roughly 10.8x FY26 revenued, which is unusually long visibility for a company this size.

The problem is the price and the cash. At ₹2,741 the stock trades at 127x trailing earnings and 11.6x book, and about 104x the consensus FY27 estimated. Trendlyne's 7-analyst consensus target is ₹2,618, 4.5% below today's price. Profit growth slowed to about 20% in the June quarter (from 46–57% in the three before), and consolidated pre-tax profit actually fell 7% year on year (₹42 cr to ₹39 cr) as depreciation and interest from the new plants came through. Only a 10% tax rate held net profit up. FY26 operating cash flow was −₹119 cr, with inventory up from ₹189 cr to ₹329 cr. Management says the eight new customer-dedicated plants ramp from H2 FY27 and could add ₹1,200–1,440 cr of revenue at full use. If that shows up in the December and March quarters, today's multiple starts to make sense. Until then the stock is ahead of the numbers.

Where this name came from. A tweet by @vsvicky_ on 14 Oct 2026, saved in the knowledge base, described an unnamed "Hyderabad-based precision engineering company, quietly selling to GE, Mitsubishi and Siemens, now entering Rolls-Royce & Pratt & Whitney aero engines. Order book ₹6,500 cr. EBITDA margins 37%. PAT growth YoY 49%. 93% revenue from exports."

Identification: Azad Engineering. The evidence: Hyderabad HQ and plants (Tunikibollaram); customer-dedicated facilities for Mitsubishi Heavy (Mar 2025), GE Vernova (Apr 2025, plus two more Sep 2026) and Siemens Energy (Sep 2025); signed agreements with Rolls-Royce plc (civil aero engine parts) and Pratt & Whitney Canada (Nov 2025 long-term agreement). The obvious alternative, MTAR Technologies (also Hyderabad, also exports to aerospace and energy), fails on three of the numbers: its Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin was 23.6%, exports 81%, and order book ₹5,143 cr. No other Hyderabad component maker carries a ₹6,500 cr order book with a 37% margin.

Testing the numbers:

  • Order book ₹6,500 cr — confirmed, but dated. About ₹6,500 cr at 31 Mar 2026, per the FY26 results release. The Q1 FY27 deck and call gave no updated figure.
  • EBITDA margin 37% — confirmed. FY26 consolidated 37.4%; Q1 FY27 37.3% consolidated, 37.6% standalone.
  • PAT growth 49% — true for the year, not the latest quarter. FY26 standalone PAT rose 49.3% (consolidated: +54%). The March quarter was +48%d. But the latest quarter, June 2026, was +19.5% consolidated (+21.2% standalone). Read as a current run rate, the tweet overstates growth by more than double.
  • 93% exports — confirmed for FY26, slipping. The company's deck says ~93% for FY26. In Q1 FY27 it was 88%, against 92% a year earlier, as domestic BHEL and DRDO work grew.
  • "Entering Rolls-Royce & Pratt & Whitney" — fair, but early. Both agreements are signed. The first Rolls-Royce qualification batch was due to ship in the September quarter. No revenue or order value has been disclosed for either.
  • "Quietly" — not really. 82 mutual fund schemes hold 8.9% of the company, it joined the Nifty Smallcap 250 index in September, and seven analysts cover it.

Business

Nature of business

Azad machines turbine airfoils (rotating and stationary blades), rotor and engine parts, and increasingly sub-assemblies, for gas, steam and nuclear turbines and for aero engines. It has about 1,700 qualified parts, sells in 12+ countries, and earned ~93% of FY26 revenue abroad. Energy and oil & gas is about 81% of revenue and aerospace & defence about 17% (Q1 FY27, standalone). The model is customer-dedicated plants: a building and line set up for one OEM under a long-term contract, now eight in Hyderabad (Mitsubishi, GE Vernova steam and gas, Siemens Energy, Baker Hughes and others), each meant to earn ₹150–180 cr a year at full use. The next step up is "hot-section" parts (combustion-side nozzle vanes, under an eight-year single-source deal with Mitsubishi signed March 2026) and complete small engines for DRDO.

Moat

Strong, and the best part of the case. Turbine airfoils are life-critical; each part is qualified individually with the OEM, which takes years, and Azad has ~1,700 of them with "zero ppm" defect requirements. Customers it says control about 75% of the gas-turbine market have built dedicated plants with it, which ties both sides in. The weakness is concentration: a handful of OEMs buy almost everything, and they set prices.

Order book

About ₹6,500 cr at 31 Mar 2026, delivered over five to six years, per the FY26 results release and trade press; no updated figure was given with Q1 FY27. Disclosed contracts include Mitsubishi (US$73.5 m in Sep 2025; cumulative ~US$156 m), Baker Hughes/Nuovo Pignone (extended to 2030), Arabelle Solutions, GE Vernova, GTRE (a complete gas-generator engine) and BHEL (₹52.7 cr, Oct 2026); the deck's dollar values for the others could not be matched to customers reliably. Rolls-Royce and Pratt & Whitney Canada values are confidential and not counted in revenue yet.

Risks

Valuation ahead of delivery. 127x trailing and 104x forward, above the consensus target, with profit growth just slowed to ~20%. If the H2 FY27 ramp slips by even a quarter, there is a lot of multiple to give back.
  • Cash burn. Operating cash flow was −₹119 cr in FY26 and free cash flow −₹691 cr; net debt went from net cash to ₹289 cr in a year. Interest cost is rising.
  • Plant ramp-up. Eight new facilities need customer qualification part by part before they earn; depreciation is already in the P&L.
  • Customer concentration. A few global OEMs take nearly all output.
  • Currency. ~90% exports; Q4 FY26 other income included ₹11.9 cr of currency gains that did not repeat. Hedging is still being put in place.
  • Holder rotation. FIIs selling for five straight quarters while retail holders grow 12% in a quarter is a fragile base.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q2 or Q3 FY27 revenue growth of 35% or more with EBITDA margin at or above 35%, showing the new plants ramping, plus positive operating cash flow for H1 FY27. Or the same business at about ₹2,100 (80x FY27 consensus EPS).
  • Downgrade to Sell: revenue growth below 20% in Q2 or Q3 FY27, EBITDA margin below 32% (the bottom of guidance), or a further sharp rise in inventory with operating cash flow still negative.
