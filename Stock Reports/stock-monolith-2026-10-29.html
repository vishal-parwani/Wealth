Verdict

Q2 / H1 FY27 results (board meeting 24 Oct 2026, limited review). Consolidated Q2 revenue was ₹53.6 cr, up 88% YoY and 14% on Q1. EBITDA was ₹15.8 cr, a 29.5% margin, against 19.0% a year earlier. PAT was ₹11.2 cr, up 145%, and diluted EPS ₹5.14. H1 revenue was ₹100.8 cr (+76%) and H1 PAT ₹21.2 cr (+141%). Volume handled was about 55,000 t, and the call put realisation at about ₹8,700–9,000/t delivered. Trading made up ₹6.55 cr of Q2 revenue. The like-for-like picture is smaller. The year-ago quarter was standalone only, because MIGPL was bought in November 2025. On a standalone basis, Q2 revenue grew 35% (₹38.5 cr against ₹28.5 cr), EBITDA 87% and PAT 68%. H1 operating cash flow was only ₹9.4 cr against ₹28.9 cr of EBITDA, because receivables rose ₹17 cr and inventory ₹10.5 cr. The stock rose 3.4% on the first session after the print (26 Oct) and 7.0% today after the call transcript was filed.

The business is good, but the price already assumes management's targets. Hold, high risk. Ramming mass is a real consumable. An induction furnace's lining is re-rammed every 40–60 hours, using about 30 kg per tonne of steel. Monolithisch has turned that into a scaled, high-return business, with 35% ROCE, 29% operating margins and a near debt-free balance sheet. It has also grown fast: 54% revenue CAGR over FY22–26 and 576,000 MTPA of capacity after the greenfield plant started on 10 Oct 2026. The Nawada quartzite block is the real upside. Management says it cuts landed stone cost from about ₹2,700–3,200/t to about ₹1,400–1,500/t, against a selling price of about ₹8,800/t. Even if ₹300–400/t is passed on to customers, that is roughly 10 points of margind from FY27 Q4.

The price is the problem. At ₹1,335.60 the stock is 9.3x its ₹143 IPO price 16 months later, at 82x trailing earnings and 19x book. FY27 guidance of ₹250 cr revenue needs ₹149 cr in H2, 48% more than H1d. Even at H1's 21% PAT margin, that gives FY27 EPS of about ₹24 and a 55x forward multipled. Only management's own FY28 target of ₹500 cr+ (with the mine margins) brings the multiple down to the mid-20s. That target needs revenue to double again in a year, from a company whose H1 cash conversion was 32% and whose inventory is 50% above its own year-end target. Organic growth is about 30–35%, not 88%. The closest peer, Raghav Productivity Enhancers, trades even higher (112x, 28x book), so the sector is richly valued, not cheap. The promoter is buying, not selling: Prabhat Tekriwal added about 0.3 points in Q2. Pledges are nil, and there has been no dilution since the IPO. Those points count in the company's favour. A Buy needs either a lower price or proof of the H2 ramp and the mine economics.

Business

Nature of business

Monolithisch makes premixed silica ramming mass. This is crushed and graded quartzite blended with binders and additives, rammed into an induction furnace to form the crucible that holds molten steel at over 1,700°C. The lining is consumed every campaign, so demand tracks tonnes melted. The company sells six grades, and the premium SGB series was about 61% of Q2 revenue. Customers are secondary (induction-furnace) steelmakers and foundries in West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh. The company says it supplies 6 of the top 10 secondary-steel groups, typically 70–80% of each plant's needs, with 73.6% customer retention in Q2. Concentration is moderate: the top 10 customers were 59% of FY25 revenue (69% in FY24, per the RHP). Nepal and Bangladesh are served through Indian trading houses on cash terms. Freight decides who wins, because stone in and mass out are both bulky, so plants sit inside the steel clusters.

Moat

Narrow but real, and widening if the mine performs. Ramming mass is low-value and high-volume, with dozens of unorganised suppliers, and the chemistry is not proprietary. What holds customers is delivered cost per tonne of steel, lining life (heats per campaign) and reliability. Plants sited in the clusters, scale, and soon captive stone are cost advantages competitors can copy only with capital and a block of their own. The SGB premium (₹700–900/t more, for ₹200–300/t more cost) shows that customers pay for performance. Against that, a buyer can switch after a 1–1.5-month trial, and the block lease runs only five years.

Order book

Not an order-book business. Supply is continuous against plant schedules. The deck's claim that "the order book already equals ~130% of monthly supply" is not quantified anywhere else. The best forward indicators are volume (55,000 t in Q2), the SGB mix and utilisation of the greenfield plant.

Risks

Valuation runs ahead of the cash. At 82x trailing and 19x book, after a 3.5x move in a year, the price assumes the guided ₹250 cr / ₹500 cr+ path and the mine margins together. H1 converted only 32% of EBITDA to operating cash, free cash flow was negative, and a ₹157 cr mine commitment now sits on top of ₹45 cr of new plants. Any H2 shortfall against guidance would hit a stock with a 25% float and ₹5 cr of daily turnover.
  • Inorganic growth presented as organic. The like-for-like standalone business grew 23–35% in the last two quarters, not 64–88%. The base effect from MIGPL fades from Q3 FY27, when the year-ago quarter already includes it.
  • Working capital. Inventory is ₹48.8 cr against a ₹30–34 cr target, and receivable days rose to 73. Customers are steelmakers in a cyclical, monsoon-sensitive market.
  • Mine execution. Statutory approvals are still pending, the start date has already moved from Feb/Mar to "mid-January, maybe 15 days more", and contract-mining quality and cost are unproven. Management itself says the margin gain is "2–3% plus or minus" uncertain.
  • Competition. Raghav is moving east with a mining JV. If several organised players secure stone, the cost advantage narrows, and management plans to pass part of it to customers anyway.
  • Related-party and governance depth (above): a family-controlled board, a new and junior CFO, small auditors, and promoter-group steel companies whose trading with the company is unverified.
  • SME mechanics. Lot-based SME trading, no MF ownership, no broker coverage, and a further 26.8% promoter lock-in release in June 2027.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H2 FY27 revenue of at least ₹140 cr with operating cash flow above 70% of EBITDA and inventory cut towards ₹35 cr; plus Nawada producing by March 2027 with a visible margin step-up in Q4 FY27 or Q1 FY28. Or a price near ₹950–1,000 (about 40x FY27 guidance-implied EPS of ₹24d) with the H1 trajectory intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: FY27 revenue guidance cut below ₹220 cr; receivable days past 90 or another half of negative free cash flow; a further slip in mining approvals beyond Q1 FY28; any promoter sale after the June 2027 lock-in release; or new related-party purchases from promoter entities that are not clearly accretive.
