Verdict

The turnaround is real, but at ₹760 the price assumes today's shortage-level fibre pricing will hold. Hold, high risk. Q2 FY27 revenue was ₹2,529 cr, up 145% YoY. Operational EBITDA margin was 27.1%, against 17.7% a year earlier. The open order book reached ₹35,274 cr, about 4.9x TTM revenued, helped by two multi-year hyperscaler agreements of about US$1.1bn (May) and US$1.2bn (Oct). The ₹1,500 cr QIP in July cut gross debt from ₹1,942 cr (Mar 26) to ₹989 cr (Sep 26). CRISIL upgraded the company to AA/Stable on 1 Sep. The industry backdrop is strong too. TrendForce expects 2026 fibre spot prices of 2.8–5x 2025 levels and a shortage lasting through 2028, and STL makes its own preform, which is the input in short supply.

The counter-case is cyclical. The two big hyperscaler contracts are valued at prevailing selling prices, and purchase orders are released periodically. If fibre pricing normalises, both the rupee value of the order book and the margin shrink. Corning, Prysmian and the Chinese majors are all adding capacity into the same window that STL's FY29 roadmap depends on. That roadmap targets ₹20,000 cr revenue at over 30% EBITDA margin, backed by ₹3,000 cr of capex. The Q2 profit of ₹517 cr includes a net ₹161 cr one-off from US IEEPA tariff refunds. Without it, PAT is closer to ₹390 crd, and the TTM P/E of about 52x is above HFCL's 38x. The stock is already 28% off its October peak, a reminder that ASM Stage 4 names move in 5% steps both ways.

The brokers' consensus target is ₹1,473 (+94%), and every covering broker rates it Buy. Those targets are built on FY28–29 estimates that already assume the roadmap is delivered. Q3 delivers only ₹3,021 cr of the book. The H2 test is whether operational margins hold above 25% without refunds while US tariffs and the Prysmian appeal remain open.

Business

Nature of business

STL makes optical preform, fibre and optical fibre cable, and increasingly optical connectivity: pre-terminated, high-density assemblies for data centres. It has plants in India, Italy and the US (South Carolina). Optical Networking was ₹2,461 cr of Q2's ₹2,529 cr revenue. The remainder is a small Digital & Technology Solutions arm (₹86 cr, breakeven). By geography, FY26 revenue was 39% US, 39% Europe and 22% India and the rest of the world. By customer, data centres and cloud grew from 1% of FY26 revenue to 21% in Q1 and 33% in H1 FY27, against a 50% target for FY27. The connectivity attach rate was 24% in H1, with a target of over 25%. Telcos (5G/FTTx, BharatNet) remain the base load.

Pricing & capacity. TrendForce puts 2026 global demand at 577 mn fkm, with data centres rising from 18–20% to 30–35% in 2027. It projects spot prices at 2.8–5x 2025 levels, above 5x for bend-insensitive DC fibre, with the shortage lasting through 2028. CRU saw Chinese G.652.D bare fibre up more than 80% between Nov 25 and Jan 26, and flags a shortage of US preform and draw capacity. STL's own capacity utilisation is n/a: it is not disclosed in sources opened. CRISIL says only that utilisation should "improve gradually". Capacity is being raised about 50% by end-FY29, with capex of ₹700–800 cr in FY27 and about ₹1,000 cr a year over FY27–29 (₹3,000 cr in total), including a new pre-terminated-solutions plant.

US tariffs & trade. US tariffs on Indian fibre peaked near 50% in 2025 and cost an estimated 310 bps of margin in Q2 FY26 and 760 bps in Q3 FY26. They fell to about 18% under the US–India arrangement and then to a temporary 10–15% regime (10% from Feb 26 per CRISIL). The IEEPA refund booked in Q2 FY27 (net ₹161 cr) reverses part of the earlier cost. The US plant partly hedges the exposure. No US anti-dumping case on Indian fibre was found. Other markets do levy duties: the EU charges 11.4% on STL's cable (the highest rate, under appeal at the EU General Court), and China extended its 7.4% duty on Indian single-mode fibre to 2031.

Moat

A moderate moat, and stronger now than in a normal cycle. STL is integrated from preform, the scarce input today, through fibre, cable and connectivity. It has 9% of the global ex-China OFC market, the largest Indian capacity, and is qualified with hyperscalers, which is slow to win and sticky once won. Those are real advantages while supply is tight. Through the cycle, though, fibre is a bid-priced commodity. CRISIL notes competitive bidding "limits pricing power", and the FY19–FY24 margin slide from 22% to 12% shows what happens when capacity catches up. The durable part is the connectivity and solutions layer, where attach rate and engineering content grow. The fibre-pricing windfall is not durable.

Order book

₹35,274 cr open order book at 30 Sep 26, about 4.9x TTM revenued. It was ₹7,687 cr at end-FY26 (CRISIL: ₹7,309 cr at 31 Mar 26) and ₹18,618 cr at 30 Jun 26. H1 intake was about ₹32,030 cr. Of the book, ₹3,021 cr is scheduled for Q3 FY27 and ₹32,253 cr for Q4 FY27 onward. It includes the two hyperscaler agreements of about US$1.11bn (FY27–29) and about US$1.2bn (to CY30). Both are valued at prevailing selling prices with POs released periodically, so they are firm frameworks but not fixed-price, fixed-volume backlog. The ₹960 cr domestic-telco order (FY28–29) is also in the book. The customers are not named.

Risks

Fibre price mean-reversion. Current earnings and the rupee value of the book rest on shortage pricing of 2.8–5x 2025 levels. The big contracts re-price at "prevailing" prices. A return of spot prices toward 2025 levels as Corning, Prysmian, YOFC and STL itself add preform capacity into 2028 would cut both revenue per fkm and margin. FY19–24 is the precedent: OPM fell from 22% to 12% as capacity caught up.
  • Valuation and technicals. At about 52x TTM and 8.6x book after a ninefold rise, and with ASM Stage 4 and a 5% band, liquidity can disappear in a sell-off. The stock fell 28% from its peak in three weeks despite record results.
  • Customer concentration. Two unnamed hyperscaler frameworks worth about US$2.3bn dominate the book. A capex pause by one hyperscaler (the industry flags a "medium-term DC spending slowdown" risk) would hit volume directly.
  • Quality of the Q2 beat. ₹161 cr of the profit came from tariff refunds, and further refunds are not guaranteed. US tariff policy remains a live variable on 39% of revenue.
  • Prysmian judgment. US$96.5m (≈₹914 crd), about 2.3%d of market cap, is under appeal. ₹396 cr is already restricted. An adverse appellate outcome is a cash outflow but not an existential one.
  • Capex execution and working capital. ₹3,000 cr of capex into a possibly peaking cycle. Receivables rose from ₹1,066 cr to ₹1,606 cr in H1, and debtor days were 82 in FY26.
  • Governance. The in-the-money promoter warrants (8% dilution on conversion), Vedanta-group association, and CFO turnover in 2025.
  • Trade defence. The EU charges 11.4% anti-dumping duty and China 7.4%. Any US AD/CVD action on Indian fibre would hit the fastest-growing market.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q3 and Q4 FY27 operational EBITDA margin of at least 25% with no refund or other one-offs, plus evidence that hyperscaler POs are being released on schedule (Q3 delivery of at least ₹3,000 cr). Alternatively, a price near ₹600 (about 21x FY27Ed) with the book intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: any two of the following. Operational margin back below 20%. A cut to the order book or the ₹20,000 cr FY29 revenue target. Fibre spot-price indices falling more than 30% from peak. An adverse Prysmian appellate ruling plus fresh US trade action on Indian fibre.
