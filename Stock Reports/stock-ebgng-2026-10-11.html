Verdict

Growth and margins are moving the right way, but a business whose cash is tied up in 180 days of inventory, priced at 55x trailing earnings while its promoters keep selling, is a Hold and not a Buy.

For. FY26 revenue was ₹1,891 cr, up 34%. EBITDA was ₹196 cr, up 68%, and PAT ₹132 cr, up 91%. EBITDA margin has climbed every year, from 6.9% in FY24 to 10.4% in FY26 and 11.9% in Q1 FY27. After Q1, management raised FY27 revenue-growth guidance from 25% to 30%. It is adding national distribution through Ingram Micro and Supertron (February) and Redington (July). Refurbished devices are a real and under-penetrated category in India, and GNG is the scale player in it, with operations in India, the UAE and the US.

Against. The quality of earnings is weak. Operating cash flow was −₹114 cr in FY25 and −₹215 cr in FY26. Inventory days went from 115 to 153 to 180, and the cash conversion cycle is 213 days. Most of the IPO money has gone into working capital. The effective tax rate is about 10%, because much of the profit is booked in the UAE subsidiary. That is legal, but it makes reported profit more flattering than a domestic peer's would be. In August the board sought approval for related-party limits of up to about ₹2,000 cr with step-down subsidiaries, plus personal guarantees of up to ₹200 cr each from the two promoters. And on 30 September promoter Vidhi Khandelwal sold another 34.2 lakh shares (3.0% of equity). Her June sale of 44.9 lakh shares at ₹390 was needed to bring promoter holding down to the 75% minimum-public-shareholding limit. The September sale was not needed for that, because the promoter group was already at 74.78%.

Business

Nature of business

GNG buys used laptops, desktops and other ICT devices in bulk, mostly corporate lease returns sourced overseas. It refurbishes them and resells them under the Electronics Bazaar brand to retail and enterprise buyers in India, the Middle East, Africa and the US. It refurbished about 5.9 lakh devices in FY25. Operating subsidiaries are Electronics Bazaar FZC (UAE), Bright World Technologies (US) and Kay Kay Overseas. The model is trading-heavy. Margin comes from buying well and grading accurately, and inventory is the main asset. That explains both the 10–12% EBITDA margin and the cash it consumes.

Peers

There is no listed pure-play refurbisher in India to compare against. Peer metrics: n/a. The nearest listed comparisons are IT distributors such as Redington, which is GNG's own distribution partner. They trade at much lower multiples on thinner margins, so they are not like-for-like.

Moat

Modest, and it comes from scale. Global sourcing relationships for lease-return devices, a grading and repair operation at volume, warranty and service capability, and now three national distributors give GNG an edge over the unorganised refurbishers that dominate the category. None of this stops a well-funded entrant or an OEM's own refurbished channel.

Order book

Not a meaningful concept. Sales are run-rate retail and enterprise orders.

Risks

Inventory. 180 days of stock in a category where prices fall as technology ages. A slowdown in sell-through, or a fall in used-device prices, would lead to write-downs. Operating cash flow is already −₹215 cr. At 55x earnings, an inventory charge would hit both earnings and the multiple.
  • Promoter selling. Two sales in four months. The second was optional, at about 1.8x the June price.
  • Offshore profit and tax. The low effective tax rate depends on the UAE structure. Q1's 19% suggests it is already rising.
  • Related-party scale. Limits of about ₹2,000 cr between group entities, plus promoter guarantees backing borrowings.
  • Concentrated fund ownership. Two fund houses hold 86% of fund-held shares, so an exit by either would be felt.
  • Import and e-waste rules. Government policy on importing used electronics could change sourcing economics.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: positive operating cash flow for FY27, with inventory days back below 130 and no further promoter sales for two quarters. Alternatively, a price near 30x forward earnings, about ₹450.
  • Downgrade to Sell: another promoter sale, an inventory write-down, or H1 FY27 growth falling below the 30% guidance with margin back under 10%.
