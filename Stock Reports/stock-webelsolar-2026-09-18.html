Verdict

The price has come to meet the argument, but hasn't quite got there. The 13 August note found that Websol's 40%+ margins were a temporary benefit of trade protection, and that they were shrinking: EBITDA margin 49% in FY25, 44% in FY26, 34% in Q1 FY27. On that basis, 12x earnings was 12x peak profit. Since then the stock has fallen from ₹96 to ₹73, and Chinese cell prices kept falling. Nothing new has been reported. Q1 FY27 (10 August) is still the latest quarter.

At ₹73, the numbers look different. Trailing P/E is 10x. On a normalised profit of about ₹185 cr (a 22.5% EBITDA margin on the Q1 revenue rate), it's about 17xd, a fair price for a debt-free, 92%-utilised manufacturer. The company prepaid its ₹110 cr IREDA loan in August from its own cash. The order book was ₹1,278 cr at June. The August note said ₹60–70 would "price in normalised margins rather than peak ones". We're 4% above the top of that range.

Stays Hold, high risk. It isn't a Buy yet because the margin hasn't stopped falling. The next data point is the September quarter (due in November). If EBITDA margin holds at 30% or more, or the price drops into the ₹60s, this becomes a Buy. If margin goes below 25%, the normalised-earnings case gets worse and ₹73 isn't cheap.

Business

What it does

Founded in 1990. Makes mono-PERC solar cells (M10 and G12 bifacial) and modules at a single plant in the Falta SEZ, West Bengal, under the Webel Solar brand, for utility, rooftop and commercial and industrial projects. Cell capacity recently doubled to 1.2 GW. It is one of the few Indian makers that produce their own cells, which qualifies for ALMM List-II and domestic-content projects. Its technology (mono-PERC) is a generation behind the TOPCon and HJT lines the large players are building.

Websol earns the highest margin and return here. That's because it is almost entirely a cell maker, and domestic cells are the scarcest part of the chain today. The market pays Waaree and Premier more than Websol because they have 10x the scale and newer technology. Websol's discount is the market pricing in what happens when the cell shortage ends.

Moat

Temporary. Domestic cell manufacturing is protected today by ALMM List-II and import duties, and national cell capacity (24.6 GW) is still well below module capacity (68.4 GW). Both of those advantages are shrinking as Waaree (10 GW) and Premier (10.6 GW) bring cell lines online. At 1.2 GW on older technology, Websol won't set prices once the shortage ends.

Order book

₹1,278 cr at 30 June 2026 (company), about 3.4x the quarterly revenue rate. The volume is secure. What happens to the price on that volume is the open question.

Risks

Margins falling faster than volumes rise. Every quarter for a year, margin has dropped 2–5 points. Industry forecasts put annual module production at 60–65 GW against 45–50 GW of installations. Chinese TOPCon cell prices set the global benchmark and are still falling.
  • Technology: mono-PERC is being replaced by TOPCon. Staying current means more spending.
  • Everything at one site: all production is at Falta SEZ.
  • Inventory losing value: 142 inventory days in a falling-price market.
  • Heavy retail ownership: 66% public, 1.65 lakh holders, and no active fund owns it.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the price in the ₹60s (about 14–16x normalised profit), or EBITDA margin at or above 30% in the September quarter (two quarters in a row above 30%, showing a floor). A funded plan to 3 GW+ or a TOPCon line with committed buyers would also count.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin below 25%, falling utilisation, or the order book shrinking materially from ₹1,278 cr.
