Verdict

Upgrade to Buy. The question we left open has been answered, and the price came down to our level. The August update worried about the gap between GAAP guidance ($3.75–4.05) and adjusted guidance ($6.05–6.35), and a GAAP operating margin that looked like it had collapsed. The filings explain it. Q2 carried $94 m of impairment and restructuring charges, $88 m of it from agreeing to sell the belts business to Gates Industrial and closing the Springfield, Missouri plant. That's pruning a low-return business, not a sign of trouble.

Underneath, the business improved. Q2 revenue was $1.26 bn, up 7.5% (4.4% organic). Adjusted EPS was $1.83, up 29%. Adjusted EBITDA margin rose 1.9 points to 19.6%. Industrial Motion grew 14.6% at a 23.3% margin. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance was raised. Trailing free cash flow is $386 m, 4.8% of the market cap, and the company is buying back shares.

The July note said Timken was attractive nearer $120. It's $116.27 now, 18.8x the middle of adjusted guidance, with a 1.2% dividend, against a 12-analyst consensus of $148. Buy. The discount to RBC is for lower quality and more exposure to the economic cycle, and at this multiple that's priced in.

Business

What it does

A 127-year-old US bearings company. Engineered Bearings ($807 m in Q2) makes tapered-roller, ball and thrust bearings for rail, cars and trucks, off-highway machinery, wind energy and general industry. Industrial Motion ($454 m) makes gear drives, couplings, chains, automatic lubrication and linear-motion products. Morgan Stanley's bearing note lists it among the major bearing makers. Its thin-section and precision-drive products are what link it to robotics.

Moat

Moderate but lasting. The top six bearing makers hold more than half the roller-bearing market. Timken's brand, engineering history and position as a US manufacturer (useful as supply chains move back to the US) give it steady share. Its end markets are cyclical and price-competitive, so the moat protects share better than it protects margin.

Order book

Timken doesn't report a backlog the way RBC does. It sells mostly through OEM schedules and distributors. The forward indicator is guidance: revenue up about 5.5% in 2026 and adjusted EPS $6.05–6.35.

Risks

The industrial cycle. The 52-week range ($70.57–146.37) shows how far this stock moves on the economy. A US or European industrial slowdown would hit Engineered Bearings volumes and margin quickly. The 18.8x adjusted multiple assumes the recovery continues.
  • Charges keep recurring. If restructuring becomes permanent, the adjusted numbers will overstate real earnings. Check whether the gap narrows in 2027.
  • A new management team still has to prove its plan in the results.
  • Debt of 2.0x EBITDA limits how much it can spend on buybacks or acquisitions in a downturn.
  • Tariffs and trade: global manufacturing and customers exposed to Chinese competition.
