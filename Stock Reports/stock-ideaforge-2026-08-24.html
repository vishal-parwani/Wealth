Verdict

The company has stopped burning and has just been handed ₹500 cr; what it has not done is prove it can earn. Trailing twelve-month net profit is ₹4 cr on ₹282 cr of revenue. FY25 lost ₹62 cr and FY26 lost ₹17 cr. Return on capital is negative. A price-to-earnings ratio is not meaningful here and quoting the 1,028x that appears on screens would be false precision — the honest measure is 5.9x book and ₹4,039 cr of market value for a business that has never sustainably made money.

Q1 FY27 was genuinely encouraging. Revenue ₹68.6 cr, up 436% year on year, EBITDA positive at ₹4.3 cr against a ₹15.1 cr loss a year earlier, and more than 20% of the opening order book delivered in the first quarter. Management is targeting delivery of the whole FY27 opening order book — over ₹300 cr — by the December quarter.

The July QIP changes the shareholder register more than it changes the business. ₹500 cr raised at ₹795 a share on 10 July, near the market price. Domestic institutions went from 2.69% to 10.98% of the company in one month and foreign institutions from 4.89% to 8.27%; fifteen mutual fund schemes bought, eleven of them opening a position from nothing. For the first time this company has professional owners.

Set against that: quarterly operating margin over the last four quarters was −28%, −84%, +44%, +3%. Revenue by quarter was ₹41 cr, ₹32 cr, ₹141 cr, ₹69 cr. Defence orders land when they land. Cash from operations has been negative two years running and pre-QIP cash had fallen from ₹274 cr to ₹39 cr. The QIP refills the tank; it does not answer whether the engine works. Hold, high risk.

Business

What it does

ideaForge designs and manufactures unmanned aircraft systems — surveillance and mapping drones sold principally to the Indian armed forces, central and state police, and paramilitary organisations, with a growing enterprise mapping and survey business. It is the domestic market leader by deployed fleet, and the moat, such as it is, comes from being an approved supplier on Indian defence procurement lists. The new direction is logistics: YETI, a 200 kg-payload middle-mile platform, backed by a government letter of intent worth up to ₹151 cr.

Peers

No fair listed peer table can be built. The other listed Indian drone names are either far smaller, differently positioned, or valued on order books rather than earnings, and ideaForge has no earnings to compare. A table putting a loss-making company beside profitable defence manufacturers on P/E and ROCE would mislead rather than inform, so it is omitted. The relevant comparison is against its own FY24: ₹314 cr of revenue and ₹45 cr of profit is what this business has already proved it can do, and getting back there is the case.

Moat

Modest and policy-dependent. Being on Indian defence procurement lists, having flown a million missions and holding indigenous-content certification are real barriers to a foreign competitor, and drone procurement rules favour domestic manufacture. They are not barriers to another well-funded Indian entrant, and they do not confer pricing power — government tenders are competitive and margins are set by the buyer. The technology lead is genuine today and needs continuous R&D spend to stay that way, which is exactly why capitalised development costs now carry ₹14 cr a quarter of depreciation.

Order book

Over ₹300 cr at the start of FY27, of which more than 20% was delivered in the June quarter, with full delivery targeted by Q3. Against ₹282 cr of trailing revenue that is a little over one year of work. The ₹151 cr government letter of intent for YETI is separate and is an intent to fund development, not a purchase order.

Risks

₹4,039 cr of market value for a company whose trailing profit before tax is still negative. Two of the last three years were losses. The valuation rests entirely on FY27 execution and on the drone market being what everyone hopes it is.
  • Revenue is entirely lumpy. ₹32 cr in one quarter, ₹141 cr in the next. Government acceptance timing, not demand, decides which quarter revenue lands in.
  • Cash burn until the QIP. Operating cash flow was −₹77 cr and −₹63 cr in the last two years and net cash had fallen from ₹348 cr to ₹60 cr. If FY27 does not convert, the ₹500 cr goes the same way.
  • Depreciation exceeds operating profit in a normal quarter — ₹14.2 cr against ₹2.3 cr in June — because development spend is capitalised.
  • Single-customer-type concentration. Indian government and security forces dominate the order book; a procurement pause stops the company.
  • The new institutional holders bought at ₹795 in a placement. They are not long-standing owners and have no cost-base loyalty at these levels.
  • Trailing profit is a tax artefact — profit before tax is −₹7 cr while net profit is +₹4 cr.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: two consecutive quarters of positive profit before tax with positive operating cash flow, or delivery of the full ₹300 cr opening order book by Q3 as guided with the FY27 exit run rate above FY24's ₹314 cr.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a return to operating losses once the order book is delivered, the QIP money being consumed by working capital rather than capacity, or the FY27 order book failing to be replaced with new orders of similar size.
