Verdict

Why it jumped (26–27 Oct): likely the Defence Acquisition Council's approval, not anything ideaForge did. The DAC met on Monday 26 October and approved procurement proposals worth about ₹2.12 lakh cr. Press reports put about 82% of that with Indian industry. The Army's share included surveillance UAVs, an integrated surveillance-and-strike UAV system, long-range kamikaze drones and counter-drone systems. Reports were out by 6:46 pm IST on the 26th, after the market had closed. On the 26th the stock barely moved: +0.7% to ₹651, on 1.9 L shares. On the 27th it opened 2.4% higher at ₹666.8 and traded 2.3 L shares in the first 45 minutes. It hit the 10% upper price band of ₹716.10 by about 11:00 and stayed there to the close, on 4.5 L shares. Exchange filings show nothing company-specific: no order, no results and no fund-raise. The only filing near the move was a routine notice at 5:07 pm on 26 Oct of the 3 November earnings call. The move was sector-wide. Data Patterns rose 9.2%, Astra Microwave 4.7% and Apollo Micro 3.5%, and the press listed ideaForge as the day's top defence gainer. ideaForge is the purest listed surveillance-UAV name and had just fallen 11% in four sessions to ₹646.5, so it moved the most. Caveat: an Acceptance of Necessity is the first step of procurement, not an order. No quantities or vendors were named, and contracts usually take one to three years. This is a sentiment move. It does not change the earnings outlook.

Buy or hold from here? Hold: don't buy into a 10% sentiment candle six days before results. Today's price is about where it stood a month ago, and the business has not moved since then either. Trailing twelve months still show ₹4 cr of net profit on ₹282 cr of revenue, and a pre-tax loss of ₹7 cr. FY25 lost ₹62 cr and FY26 lost ₹17 cr. Return on capital is negative. Screens show a P/E of about 900x, which means nothing here. The useful measures are ₹3,553 cr of market value against about ₹1,100 cr of post-QIP book (≈3.2xd), and two analysts' FY28 estimates of ₹87 cr of profit and ₹19.70 EPS, which puts the stock on ≈36xd FY28 earnings.

The case for owning it is real. ideaForge is India's largest surveillance-drone maker by deployed fleet. Q1 FY27 revenue rose 436% to ₹68.6 cr with positive EBITDA, and over 20% of the ₹300 cr opening order book was delivered in that quarter. The July QIP left about ₹560 crd of net cash. The policy backdrop is about as favourable as it gets: emergency procurement since Operation Sindoor, a stated preference for drones without Chinese components, and now a drone-heavy DAC approval. The two analysts covering the stock are both Strong Buy, with targets of ₹905–₹1,187.

The case against is execution and quality of earnings. Quarterly operating margin over the last four quarters ran −28%, −84%, +44% and +3%. Depreciation of ₹14 cr a quarter, from capitalised development, is larger than operating profit in an ordinary quarter. Operating cash flow has been negative for two years. The Q2 test is simple. To deliver the whole ₹300 cr book by December, Q2 and Q3 together need about ₹230 crd of revenue, or about ₹115 cr a quarter, against ₹68.6 cr in Q1. A Q2 well short of that, with the stock already 10% up on a sector headline, is the setup that hurts. If you don't own it, wait for 2 November. If Q2 is ≥ ₹100 cr with a pre-tax profit, it becomes a Buy candidate even at higher prices. If you own it, hold. Hold, high risk.

Business

What it does

ideaForge designs and manufactures unmanned aircraft systems. Its main products are surveillance and mapping drones sold to the Indian armed forces, central and state police and paramilitary forces, plus a smaller enterprise mapping and survey business. It is the domestic leader by deployed fleet, with more than one million customer missions flown. Its products range from mini and tactical UAVs (SWITCH, ZOLT, hybrid mini UAVs bought through emergency procurement) to YETI, a heavy-lift, long-range logistics platform now funded by a ₹151 cr TDB loan. The planned US manufacturing JV is now off.

Peers

No fair listed peer valuation table can be built. Listed defence-electronics names such as Data Patterns, Astra Microwave, Paras and Zen are profitable and valued on order books and earnings. ideaForge has neither steady earnings nor a disclosed order book of comparable scale. Those names are useful as a sentiment comparison, which is how they appear in the verdict, and all of them moved together on the DAC approval. The valuation benchmark is ideaForge's own FY24: ₹314 cr of revenue and ₹45 cr of profit.

Moat

Modest and dependent on policy. Being on Indian defence procurement lists, a million missions of field history and indigenous-content compliance (no critical Chinese sub-components, now a procurement condition) are real barriers to foreign suppliers. They do not keep out well-funded Indian entrants, and they give no pricing power, because tenders are competitive and the buyer sets margins. Staying ahead in technology takes continuous R&D, which is why capitalised development now carries ₹14 cr a quarter of depreciation.

Order book

Over ₹300 cr at the start of FY27. More than 20% was delivered in the June quarter, and management targets full delivery by Q3. No new order has been filed since. The 26 October DAC approval covers categories ideaForge makes: surveillance UAVs, integrated surveillance-and-strike systems and possibly loitering munitions. An approval is not an order, though. No quantities or vendors are public, and Indian procurement from AoN to contract typically takes one to three years. The ₹151 cr YETI facility is a loan, not a purchase order.

Risks

A ₹3,553 cr company whose trailing profit before tax is still negative has just risen 10% on a headline that names no vendor. If Q2 on 2 November disappoints, it can give that back quickly.

  • Lumpy revenue. Revenue swung from ₹32 cr to ₹141 cr between consecutive quarters. Acceptance timing decides the quarter.
  • Order-book guidance. About ₹230 crd of revenue is needed in Q2 and Q3 together to deliver the ₹300 cr book by December.
  • GST demand of ₹109 cr plus interest. It covers January 2022 to March 2024. The 2025 clarification the company relies on came after that period. If upheld, it is about 3% of market cap and about 20% of net cashd.
  • Cash burn. Operating cash flow was −₹77 cr and −₹63 cr in the last two years. The QIP and TDB money can drain the same way if working capital keeps growing. Inventory days were 536 at Mar-26.
  • Depreciation exceeds operating profit in an ordinary quarter: ₹14.2 cr against ₹2.3 cr in June.
  • Customer concentration. Indian government and security forces dominate the order book, so a pause in procurement stops the company.
  • Promoter pledges are growing, though still small.
  • QIP holders bought at ₹795 and are still under water. That overhang sells into rallies.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q2 FY27 revenue of ₹100 cr or more with positive profit before tax and positive operating cash flow for H1. Also: delivery of the full ₹300 cr opening order book by Q3 as guided, together with a filed new order (DAC-linked or emergency procurement) that refills the book.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a Q2 below the June quarter's ₹68.6 cr with a return to operating losses, a cut to the Q3 delivery guidance, QIP money absorbed by working capital rather than capacity, or the GST demand upheld on appeal with interest.
