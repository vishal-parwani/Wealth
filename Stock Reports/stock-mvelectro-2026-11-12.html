Verdict

Q2 FY27 results were declared on 12 Nov 2026. The board met from 1:00 to 4:00 pm and the outcome was filed at 4:27 pm, after the market closed. Revenue was ₹18.97 cr, up 48.5% QoQ and 52.3% YoY. EBITDA was a loss of ₹6.24 crd (−32.9% margin), against a profit of ₹3.46 cr in Q2 FY26. The net loss was ₹5.19 cr, against a ₹2.79 cr profit a year earlier. For H1 FY27, revenue was ₹31.74 cr and the net loss ₹12.08 cr, about four times H1 FY26's ₹2.94 cr loss. The auditor's limited review carries an emphasis of matter on cash losses: ₹6.18 cr in Q2 and ₹6.67 cr in Q1. No earnings call or investor presentation had been filed by the close of 12 Nov.

Downgrade to Sell. The ramp has not shown up in revenue, and the 3 Nov note's own Sell trigger has fired. The 3 Nov report said Sell if "Q2 or Q3 FY27 revenue [is] under ₹50 cr". Q2 came in at ₹18.97 cr. The ₹6.2 cr increase over Q1 is about four propulsion sets' worthd at the order book's ₹1.63 cr a set. Management guided 10 sets in September alone. Assume legacy cable and switchgear sales ran near FY26's ₹11 cr a quarter. Then propulsion was roughly ₹8 cr, or about 5 sets, in the whole quarterd. The company does not disclose set counts.

₹400 cr for FY27 is now out of reach. H2 needs ₹368 cr, which is 11.6x H1 and about ₹61 cr a monthd. Suppose every month from October hits the guided cadence: about 15 sets in October, 25 in November, around 30 in December and 40–45 a month from January. That still gives only about 190–205 sets in H2. FY27 would then land at about ₹370–390 crd, and that assumes no slippage from a company that delivered about 5 sets last quarter.

The margin problem is new. Gross margin was 15.5% in Q2 and 8.8% in Q1d, against 29.4% for FY26 and 41.7% in H1 FY26. Some of this is early-batch cost and some is mix, but the filing does not separate them. The order book is fixed-price, and the "10%+ PAT at run-rate" guidance needs gross margin well above where it is now.

Cash is the other new fact. H1 operating cash flow was −₹63.7 cr, more than FY26's full-year −₹57.5 cr, on ₹32 cr of revenue. Advances and other current assets rose by ₹26.3 cr, receivables by ₹12.6 cr and inventory by ₹3.8 cr, and payables were cut by ₹9.6 cr. ₹60 cr of the IPO's ₹180 cr working-capital allocation has been spent. Receivables of ₹23 cr are about 110 days of Q2 salesd, not the 15-day collection management described on its first dispatches.

Why not lower conviction, i.e. Hold. The balance sheet is sound for now. Cash is ₹197.6 cr against ₹56.2 cr of borrowings, ₹194 cr of IPO money is unspent, and book value is ₹311 cr. The order book of about ₹922 cr (at 30 Jun) has not been cut. No LD or short-closure notice has been disclosed, and the CLW tender for about 748 sets is still open. But at ₹2,032 cr the stock is valued at about 37x trailing revenued and 26x management's own FY28 run-rate profitd. That price assumes the ramp happens on time and at a 10% margin. Neither shows up in H1. In February 2027 about 47 L pre-IPO public shares and 1.03 cr surplus promoter shares come out of lock-in. On the evidence, it is a Sell until Q3 shows the sets being delivered.

Business

Nature of business

MV designs and makes railway electrical and power-electronics equipment in Faridabad, Haryana. It runs Unit 1 for manufacturing and a DSIR-recognised R&D centre, and had 206 staff at May 2026. There are three lines. (1) IGBT 3-phase propulsion equipment for 6,000 hp WAG-9/WAP-7 locomotives: the traction converter-inverter, auxiliary converter, vehicle control unit and driver display. The design is the company's own, so it pays no royalty. (2) Switchgear and panels for coaches and EMUs. (3) Cable protection: conduits, end fittings and jackets. Customers: Indian Railways directly was 76.7% of FY26 revenue; the top 10 customers were 93.0%. The rest goes to OEM suppliers of Indian Railways, several of them related parties. Imported content (IGBTs, capacitors, processors) is heavy, and the top supplier is 39% of purchases. In development: composite and hotel-load converters, 12-car MEMU propulsion, an overhead-line fault locator with South Korea's PNC Technologies, and traction motors with Hansung Motors.

Capacity, capex and working capital

  • Capacity. Installed propulsion capacity was 114 sets a year in FY26, at 2.6% utilisation. Management says the lines can do about 55 sets a month on two shifts and targets 40–45 a month from January 2027. Cable products are moving to a new Unit 2 to free up Unit 1.
  • Capex. H1 FY27 capex was ₹4.3 cr; fixed assets plus CWIP rose to ₹35.8 cr. The ₹10.5 cr investment property at Mar 26 was reclassified into PPE. There is no capex object in the IPO.
  • Working capital is the business model. The RHP plans for 105 inventory days, 60 receivable days and 45 payable days, needing ₹138 cr of net working capital in FY27 and ₹286 cr in FY28. In H1 FY27, ₹52.5 cr went into working capital on ₹32 cr of revenue. Inventory is ₹71 cr, about 470 days of H1 materials costd. Receivables are about 110 days of Q2 salesd. Payables were paid down.

Moat

A real regulatory and engineering barrier, but a narrow one. Propulsion is safety-critical: type tests, RDSO field trials (about 4 months or 30,000 km) and production-unit vendor approval. MV took about five years and a 50,000 km trial to get there. Owning the design avoids licensees' royalty cost. But tenders are L1 with thin gaps across six or seven bidders, and each new platform (MEMU, Vande Bharat, higher-hp) needs separate approval. The barrier keeps new entrants out; it does not protect against Medha or the MNCs on price. The H1 gross margin of 12.8% is the first hint of what fixed-price L1 contracts may be worth at low volume.

Order book

₹922 cr executable at 30 Jun 2026 (the latest disclosed): 564 locomotive propulsion sets for CLW, BLW and PLW, ex-GST and AMC, about ₹1.63 cr a setd. On top of that are ₹67.7 cr of 3-year AMC and an ₹86.5 cr MEMU letter of acceptance (MCF Raebareli, development category, supplies only after RDSO approval). No Q2 order-book update was filed with the results. Q2 propulsion revenue was perhaps ₹8 crd, so the book at 30 Sep is likely still about ₹910 crd, about 16x TTM revenue. No new order wins were announced between 3 and 12 Nov. Pipeline, not firm: the CLW tender for about 748 locomotive sets, opened from September; management said tenders convert to orders in 45–90 days. No award, win or loss, has been reported. BLW and PLW tenders are expected to follow. Some current sets fall due by March 2027. LDs run at 0.5% a week up to a 10% cap; none has been disclosed.

Risks

The ramp is late, and the margin on it is unknown. Two quarters into FY27 the company has billed ₹32 cr against a ₹400 cr target. Gross margin on that revenue was 12.8%. Sets due by March 2027 carry LDs of up to 10% if late. If the January 40-set run-rate slips by a quarter, FY27 revenue lands nearer ₹250 crd, and the first profitable quarter moves into FY28.
  • Guidance cut. ₹400 cr needs ₹368 cr in H2, about 3.7x FY26's full-year revenue in each quarter. A cut at the Q2 call, when it comes, is the most likely next event.
  • Margin. Contracts are fixed-price while electronics costs have risen. H1 gross margin was 12.8%, against 29.4% in FY26. The 10%+ PAT guidance at run-rate is untested.
  • Cash burn and working capital. H1 operating cash flow was −₹63.7 cr, the auditor flagged cash losses, and receivables are about 110 days. ₹120 cr of IPO working-capital money is left. The RHP's own model needs ₹286 cr of working capital by FY28, so a further raise is plausible if collections stay slow.
  • Lock-in supply. Around 4 Feb 2027 about 47 L pre-IPO public shares (17% of equity, some bought at ₹273.50) and about 1.03 cr surplus promoter shares become free.
  • Single customer, single product. 77% of revenue comes from Indian Railways, and 100% of the order book is locomotive propulsion. The timing of the 748-set CLW award matters directly.
  • Governance. The promoter is MD of another listed company. Related-party trade with group firms continues in both directions, promoter loans were taken and repaid within H1, there is a group-level RoC penalty under appeal, and a FEMA lapse has been disclosed.
  • Liquidity and volatility. The closing range since listing is ₹560–978. Volume has dropped to about 7 L shares a day in November, and retail owns 33%.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: Q3 FY27 (the December quarter) revenue of ₹75 cr or more, which is roughly 40+ sets, with gross margin back above 25% and the operating cash outflow narrowing. Alternatively, a confirmed award in the ≈748-set CLW tender of about 150 sets or more, with the guidance cut (if one comes) landing at ₹250 cr or above rather than lower.
  • Upgrade to Buy: two consecutive quarters at 40+ sets a month with positive EBITDA, inventory days falling, and a share of the CLW, BLW or PLW tender round that replaces delivered volume. All of that needs to happen at or below the current price, or at a lower one after February's lock-in expiry.
  • What would confirm the Sell: FY27 guidance cut below ₹250 cr; any LD or short-closure notice; losing the 748-set CLW tender outright; a fresh equity or large debt raise within a year of listing; promoter selling after the February 2027 lock-in.
