Verdict

A regulatory-crisis recovery that is now most of the way done, still priced like it isn't. The RBI's March 2024 embargo on IIFL's gold-loan business gutted FY25 and left a permanent mark on how the market prices this NBFC. The ban lifted in September 2024, and the rebuild since has been emphatic: FY26 PAT ₹1,817 cr (+214%), AUM ₹1,08,180 cr (+38%), GNPA down to 1.46% from 2.23%. Q1 FY27 (22 Jul) carried it forward — AUM ₹1,15,523 cr (+38% YoY), PAT +14% QoQ, and ROE at 19.5%, against 13.1% for FY26 as a whole. At ₹627.50 the stock trades on roughly 9.7x forward earnings and ~2x book. A ~20% ROE lender at 2x book with 38% AUM growth is not an expensive asset.

Two things keep this a measured Buy rather than a high-conviction one. The headline growth rates are flattered by a broken base — "+214%" measures the depth of the hole, not the height of the franchise. And the stock has already run 34% in a year to sit essentially at the ~₹625 average analyst target, so the easy re-rating has happened. Ashish Dhawan adding to an existing position rather than initiating is the right read on it: a conviction top-up, not a discovery.

What happened, and why the numbers look the way they do

In March 2024 the RBI barred IIFL Finance from sanctioning or disbursing gold loans, citing supervisory concerns around assaying, LTV breaches and cash-handling. Gold loans were the group's highest-return book. The embargo was lifted in September 2024, but the damage ran through FY25 — which is why every FY26 growth rate looks spectacular. Read FY26 as a recovery year, not a growth year. The genuinely informative figures are the current-quarter ones: 19.5% ROE and 38% AUM growth after the base has normalised.

Management is now leaning on capital efficiency, co-lending partnerships (originating with balance-sheet-light economics) and AI-led operations. Co-lending in particular is what lets an NBFC show 38% AUM growth without a matching equity raise, and it is the main reason ROE has moved up faster than the loan book.

Bull case

  • ~9.7x forward earnings for a ~20% ROE lender. That combination is uncommon; the market is still applying a governance discount from 2024.
  • Asset quality is genuinely good — GNPA 1.46% at FY26 against 2.23% a year prior, NNPA 0.73%.
  • Diversified book — gold, home loans, microfinance, digital and MSME lending, so no single regulatory action can repeat the 2024 shock at the same scale.
  • Ashish Dhawan increasing from 1.10% to 1.30%. Dhawan (ex-ChrysCapital) is a patient, governance-attentive investor; adding to a post-scandal NBFC is a considered vote on management remediation.
  • Gold-loan demand is structurally strong with elevated gold prices raising per-gram lending capacity.

Risks

The provisioning disclosure needs a second look. FY26 was reported with provision coverage of 93%. Q1 FY27 discloses a specific provision coverage ratio of 47.30%. Those are almost certainly two different definitions — total PCR including technical write-offs versus specific provisions against gross NPAs — and not a genuine halving of cover. But it is the single most important thing to confirm from the actual filing before sizing a position, because if it is a real reduction in cover, the reported profit is overstated. Verify before buying.
  • Regulatory history is the overhang. The RBI has intervened at this lender once already; the market's discount is not irrational, and a second action would be far more damaging than the first.
  • GNPA ticked up to 1.55% in Q1 FY27 from 1.46% at FY26 close. One quarter, small move, but the direction reversed.
  • Trading at consensus. ₹627.50 versus a ~₹625 average target means the street sees no upside from here; gains require estimate upgrades.
  • Microfinance exposure is the most fragile part of the book in any rural stress or state-level repayment disruption.
  • Gold price dependence cuts both ways — a sharp correction in gold compresses LTV headroom and can trigger auctions.
  • Recovery-base optics — expect published growth rates to decelerate hard from FY27 as the comparison normalises. That deceleration is arithmetic, not deterioration, but it will read badly on screens.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: two more quarters holding ROE near 20% with GNPA stable or falling, plus clarity that the 47.3% figure is a definitional artefact. A pullback toward ₹520–560 (~1.7x book) would also do it.
  • Downgrade to Hold: any fresh RBI observation or restriction, GNPA rising above ~2%, or confirmation that provision cover has genuinely been reduced to flatter earnings.
