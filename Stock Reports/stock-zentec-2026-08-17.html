Verdict

Downgrade to Sell. This is a rating change, and it rests on one fact: the earnings base has shrunk while the price has not. FY26 revenue fell 29% to ₹688 cr and PAT fell 27% to ₹218 cr. Q1 FY27 continued it — revenue ₹141.6 cr, down 10% year on year and down 20% sequentially; EBITDA margin 27.3% against 40.9% a year ago. That is five quarters of deterioration, not one soft print.

Management calls it revenue timing and points to a ₹1,239 cr order book. The order book is real. But at ₹1,864 the stock trades on 92.6x trailing earnings that are themselves 27% below FY25 — so the multiple on normalised earnings is higher still, and the buyer is paying a growth price for two years of contraction. ROCE has halved, from 37% in FY25 to 16% in FY26.

The balance sheet is where it gets uncomfortable. Working capital days ran to 543 in FY26 and inventory days to 415 — over a year of production sitting in stock. Cash conversion cycle is 425 days. A defence business with lumpy government orders will always carry inventory, but this is inventory growing into falling revenue, which is the combination that precedes a write-down rather than a recovery.

The previous note rated this Hold at ₹1,805 on 13 Aug 2026. Since then the price is up 3.3% and Q1 FY27 has confirmed the deterioration rather than arrested it. Holding required believing the decline was one or two quarters of timing; five quarters in, with the multiple unchanged, that belief no longer has support. The order book argues the company survives and probably grows again — it does not argue that 93x is the right price to wait at.

Business

Nature. Zen Technologies designs and manufactures military training simulators — small-arms, driving, tank and air-defence trainers — and anti-drone systems. The customer is overwhelmingly the Indian armed forces and paramilitary, so revenue arrives as lumpy tenders rather than a subscription-like stream. Roughly a quarter of the order book is annual maintenance, which is the steadier part.

Moat. Genuine but narrow. Simulator supply to Indian defence requires qualification and trials measured in years, and Zen has a long installed base to service. That is a real barrier to a new entrant. It is not a barrier to the customer simply ordering less, which is what has happened.

Order book. ₹1,239.02 cr as at 30 Jun 2026 — ₹920.59 cr equipment, ₹318.43 cr AMC — plus a ₹177.5 cr simulator order in July 2026. Gross margin held at 72.9% in Q1, which supports management's claim that pricing has not deteriorated; the problem is volume and conversion, not unit economics.

Risks

  • Inventory. 415 inventory days and a 425-day cash conversion cycle against falling revenue. If orders slip further, a write-down becomes hard to avoid.
  • Multiple. 92.6x trailing leaves no room for another weak quarter. A de-rating to even 50x on FY26 earnings implies roughly ₹1,000 — a 46% fall with no change to the business.
  • Single customer. Government defence procurement can pause for budgetary or process reasons entirely outside the company's control, as it evidently has.
  • Passive flows. Two of the top five holders are defence-index funds. Index money that arrived on the way up leaves on the way down, and it is price-insensitive both ways.
  • Execution promises. Management guided to stronger Q2 and Q3 FY27. Similar framing accompanied earlier quarters that then disappointed.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold — two consecutive quarters of year-on-year revenue growth with EBITDA margin back above 35%, or the price falling far enough that trailing P/E is under ~45x.
  • Upgrade to Buy — the above plus working capital days back below 300 and order-book conversion demonstrated, not promised.
  • Stays a Sell — while inventory days sit above 400 and the multiple above 80x on declining earnings.
