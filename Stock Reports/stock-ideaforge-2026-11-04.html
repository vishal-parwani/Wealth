  • FTP: management sees about a ₹20,000 cr Fast Track Procurement pool. The first RFP, which ideaForge bid for, is "at an advanced stage of evaluation". RFPs for two or three more are out and others are expected next quarter. Some of these are for jet-type long-range one-way attack systems, where ideaForge has no product.
  • DAC approvals of 26 Oct: management said it can bid for most of them. RFPs usually follow within a quarter, and programmes of this kind are typically worth four-digit crores. No orders have come from them yet.
  • New products: the ZOLT hybrid (100 km, 6+ hours), the ZOLT combat variant (launched October) and multirotor loitering munitions are to be ready for field trials in H2 FY27. YETI development runs into FY28.

Business

What it does

ideaForge designs and makes unmanned aircraft systems. Its main business is surveillance and mapping drones for the Indian armed forces, police and paramilitary forces. Defence was 72% of Q2 revenue and 67% of H1. The rest is civil and enterprise mapping and survey, including the FLYGHT software and drone-as-a-service platform. The company is the domestic leader by deployed fleet, with more than 1.05 million customer flights. Its range runs from quadcopters (Q4i, Q6 V2) and the SWITCH hybrid VTOL to the tactical ZOLT and YETI, a 200 kg logistics drone under development. It is now pushing beyond ISR into combat: the ZOLT combat variant, air-launched effects and multirotor loitering munitions, aimed at field trials in H2 FY27. It holds 65 patents granted and 49 pending.

Peers

No fair listed peer valuation table can be built. Listed defence-electronics names such as Data Patterns, Astra Microwave, Paras and Zen are profitable and valued on large order books and earnings. ideaForge's ₹202 cr book is less than one year of revenue. The peers are useful as a sentiment comparison, as in the market-reaction table. The valuation benchmark is still ideaForge's own FY24: ₹314 cr of revenue and ₹45 cr of profit.

Moat

Modest, and dependent on policy. Its real advantages against foreign and new suppliers are its place on Indian defence procurement lists, field history across Siachen, the Thar desert and EW-contested environments, and an indigenous stack: own autopilot, ground control, CRPA anti-jamming and GNSS-denied navigation, at a time when Chinese components are excluded. They give no pricing power, because tenders are competitive. Staying ahead costs ₹80–90 cr a year of development, now amortising at about ₹15 cr a quarter. In combat systems it starts behind specialists and will partner for munitions.

Order book

₹202.2 cr at 30 September, mostly defence. It was ₹314 cr at the start of FY27 and ₹351 cr at its December 2025 peak. Over 50% of the opening book was delivered in H1, and the rest is guided for Q3. New inflows were only about ₹48 crd in H1. The one order reported since Q1, from Survey of India, has no disclosed value. Management expects most conversion in Q3–Q4, from FTP (RFPs out), the 26 Oct DAC approvals (RFPs expected within a quarter, programmes worth four-digit crores) and command-level buying under DFPDS 2026. None of these is a confirmed order yet. The ₹151 cr YETI facility is a loan, not a purchase order.

Risks

The order book is running down faster than it is being refilled. At the H1 pace of delivery the ₹202 cr book lasts about seven months, and management plans to deliver all of it by March. If FTP and DAC contracts slip past March, FY28 starts with an empty book, which is how FY25 began.

  • Order-inflow timing. About ₹48 crd of new orders in H1. No inflow guidance. Procurement timing is outside the company's control.
  • Working capital and cash burn. H1 operating cash flow was −₹78 cr, after −₹77 cr and −₹63 cr in the last two full years. Receivables of ₹161 cr equal a half-year's revenue.
  • Earnings quality. ₹6.3 cr of the ₹7.4 cr Q2 pre-tax profit was interest income. Depreciation took 87% of operating profit. H1 PBT is still negative.
  • GST demand of ₹109 cr plus interest. It is under appeal. If upheld, it would be about 3% of market cap and about 27% of net cashd.
  • Lumpy revenue. Quarterly revenue has ranged from ₹32 cr to ₹141 cr within four quarters.
  • Combat-UAV entry risk. Much of the new FTP and DAC spending is for strike systems. ideaForge's are not yet field-trialled, and it has no product for some of the RFPs.
  • Customer concentration. Indian defence and security forces account for most of the book.
  • Promoter pledges are small but growing. The company secretary leaves on 25 December.
  • QIP overhang. Institutions bought at ₹795 in July and are still about 10% under water.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: filed new orders (FTP, DAC-linked or command-level) that take the order book back above ₹300 cr by the Q4 FY27 results, together with Q3 FY27 revenue of ₹100 cr or more, positive PBT excluding other income, and positive operating cash flow for H2.
  • Downgrade to Sell: any of the following:
