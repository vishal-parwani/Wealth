Verdict

A good business at a price that leaves no room for error. Hold, high risk. The operating record is strong. Revenue grew 31% in FY26 and profit 46%. The June 2026 quarter was the best June the company has had: revenue ₹128 cr (+38%), profit ₹21 cr (+50%). Operating margin has stayed between 23% and 28% for seven quarters. Management guides FY27 revenue of ₹600–650 cr, 26–36% growth, with exports roughly doubling.

Two things stop this being a Buy. The first is cash. Customers took 278 days to pay in FY26, and the full cash cycle is 435 days. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹25 cr against ₹122 cr of operating profit, and free cash flow has been around zero or negative for three years. The net cash on the balance sheet came from a FY25 share placement, not from the business. The second is the price. At 110x trailing earnings and 14x book, Paras is the most expensive of the small listed defence names (Apollo 123x is the only one higher, Zen 80x, Astra 85x). Even if FY27 profit grows 40%, the stock is on about 84x forward earningsd.

The counter-argument is real. The moat in space-grade optics is the best in this group, institutions bought 5 points of the company in the June quarter, and ICRA has just upgraded the credit rating. A business with this niche deserves a premium. But it already has one.

Business

Nature of business

The business has two arms. Defence & space optics covers sights, submarine periscopes (a ₹72 cr DRDO order for two optronic periscopes) and space-grade optics for ISRO, DRDO and satellite makers. This is India's only listed private player in that niche. Defence engineering covers electromagnetic-pulse (EMP) protection, defence electronics, heavy engineering and, increasingly, counter-drone systems through group companies. Most customers are the defence ministry, DRDO labs and public-sector defence companies. Exports were about 10% of FY26 revenue.

Moat

The strongest in this group. Space-grade optics and EMP protection take years to qualify, are designed into long-life programmes and face almost no Indian private competition. Periscopes and satellite optics used to be imported. The new DRDO hyperspectral order shows the company winning integrator roles, not just component supply. The moat protects margin (25%+). It doesn't protect against slow payment by government customers.

Order book

About ₹900–1,000 cr, last stated around September 2025, which is roughly 1.8–2x trailing revenue. unreconciled No updated figure was found for June or September 2026. Orders since then, including the ₹26.6 cr DRDO hyperspectral unit, periscopes and counter-drone systems, are individually small.

Risks

The price. 110x trailing earnings and 14x book, after the stock went from ₹585 to ₹1,527 in a year. Defence small caps trade as a group. A sector pullback, or a weak September quarter, could cut the price by a third without any change in the business. The stock is already 15% off its high.
  • 435-day cash cycle. Profit becomes cash slowly and unevenly, because government payments arrive late.
  • Lumpy orders. Revenue depends on defence procurement timing and a handful of programmes.
  • Related parties. Counter-drone and optics work runs partly through family group companies.
  • Thin coverage. The only tracked analyst target (₹820) is 37% below the price.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a pullback to about ₹950–1,000 (80–85x trailing, in line with peers) while growth stays above 25%. Or receivable days below 200 with operating cash flow at or above 75% of operating profit.
  • Downgrade to Sell: revenue growth below 20% for two quarters at this multiple (Q2 FY27 must beat ₹106 cr by that margin), receivable days rising again, or promoters selling again.
