Verdict

Hold. The fall in the P/E is genuine and comes from profit growing, but the revenue growth the tweet promises does not show up in the filed numbers. At 42x trailing earnings, the price already assumes it will.

Where this name came from. It came from a tweet saved in the knowledge base. On 21 Sep 2026 @xvi_harley described it as an early pick that returned 5–10x in 2023–24, has since consolidated for two years around its 2024 peak, and has seen its P/E fall from 149 to 38. The tweet claimed 30–50% revenue-growth visibility.

The history checks out. The stock went from ₹135 in March 2023 to ₹1,096 in June 2024 on weekly closes, about 8x. At that peak, FY24 EPS of ₹7.16 put it at about 153xd. On 21 Sep it closed at ₹1,037.60 against TTM EPS of ₹28.99, which is 35.8xd, close to the tweet's 38. "Consolidation" is loosely true: the stock broke above the 2024 peak to ₹1,370 in June 2026 and fell back before the tweet. The growth claim is not supported by filed numbers. FY26 revenue grew 29%. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹57.6 cr, down 15% year on year. TTM revenue of ₹234 cr is below FY26's ₹245 cr. The unexecuted order book at 30 June was ₹117 cr, about 0.5x TTM revenue. That backlog cannot by itself deliver 30–50% growth. The only support for the figure is a forecast: Antique initiated on 5 Oct 2026 projecting a 31% revenue CAGR over FY26–29.

The quality of earnings has improved. EBITDA margin went from 16% in FY25 to 22% in FY26 and 26.3% in Q1 FY27, the highest the company has reported. Operating cash flow turned from −₹11 cr to +₹86 cr in FY26 as inventory days fell from 270 to 47. ROCE is 31%, the balance sheet holds net cash, and there is no interest burden. That is why profit grew 33% in Q1 even as sales fell.

The counter-argument is that the margin is doing all the work. A 26% margin on lumpy Ministry of Defence steel orders is unlikely to keep expanding. Without volume growth, earnings growth stalls at the margin ceiling. The stock is up 18.6% since the tweet, mostly on the broker initiation, which drew an NSE price-movement query on 7 Oct.

Business

Nature of business

The core business is special-alloy steel sections used as hull stiffeners in naval warships, along with ballast bricks, welding wire and electrodes, armoured-steel profiles and high-altitude space heaters. Customers include the Indian Navy, Army, DRDO and the shipyards: Mazagon, Garden Reach, Cochin, Goa, Hindustan Shipyard and L&T. A second division makes dairy equipment at Kalol: bulk milk coolers, milking machines and robotic milk-collection units. Newer bets in RF-over-fibre, composite doors and hatches, AUVs and smart ammunition are each in early, pre-revenue stages.

Moat

Narrow but real. Naval special steels require metallurgical qualification and long trial cycles with the shipyards and the Navy. Twelve-plus indigenised items replace imports, and once a section is qualified on a ship class, the yard rarely switches suppliers. The limit is scale. The market is the Navy's hull-building programme, and orders arrive in ₹20–50 cr lumps.

Risks

Revenue has stalled while the multiple prices in growth. At 42x, the market expects years of 25%+ earnings growth. The filed figures show revenue down 15% in the latest quarter and a backlog of half a year. If margin stops rising, earnings growth goes to zero on current orders, and a stock at 42x with flat earnings has a long way to fall.
  • Customer concentration. The Ministry of Defence and a handful of shipyards account for most of the defence revenue. Award timing is political and lumpy.
  • Margin reversion. 26.3% is a record. Product mix or a single order can move it several points.
  • Diversification into early ventures. AUVs, smart ammunition, composites and RF optics may consume capital and management time for years before producing revenue.
  • Retail-driven price. Shareholders went from 4,137 to 18,720 in a year. The stock rose about 20% on one broker initiation and drew an exchange query.
  • Key-person and compliance. The company secretary resigned in August 2026.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the order book above ₹250 cr (about 1x revenue) and Q2 FY27 revenue growth turning positive again, or a pullback to about 30x TTM (about ₹870).
  • Downgrade to Sell: a second consecutive quarter of falling revenue together with EBITDA margin back under 22%, or promoter holding falling below 59%.
