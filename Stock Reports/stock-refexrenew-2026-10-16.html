Verdict

The equity carries all the risk and has no book value behind it. Sell, high risk. The group runs about 128 MW of solar capacity across 89 sites and a 9 TPD compressed-biogas (CBG) operation. On that, it made ₹30 cr of trailing EBITDA against ₹48 cr of interest. That is interest cover of 0.6xd, and it has made a net loss every year since FY20. Net worth was −₹81 cr at March 2026, and the statutory auditor flagged a material uncertainty about going concern for both the company and the group. The business is being carried by its promoter: ₹152 cr of the ₹487 cr debt is owed to Refex Holding and SILRES Energyd at 8–12.5%, much of it repayable on demand, alongside a promoter letter of support.

The June 2026 quarter was the best in two years: revenue up 33% to ₹22.2 cr and operating margin 53%, which cut the loss to ₹3.1 cr. The order pipeline is real: an 80 MW SJVN wind award (25-year PPA at ₹3.88/kWh), a 100 MW NTPC solar EPC order and three Tamil Nadu CBG projects. But all of it has to be funded, and the board meets on 17 Oct 2026 to consider a preferential issue. Promoters already hold 74.87%, at the 75% cap for public shareholding, so the terms of any issue matter a great deal to minority holders. The stock trades on only about one session in four (31 sessions in six months), so pricing is erratic. It is down 53% from its 52-week high.

Business

Nature of business

Refex Renewables started in 2017 as a solar independent power producer (IPP), building and operating plants and selling the power. It runs about 127.5 MW across 89 sites in 11 states, including a 68 MW Indian Railways plant at Bhilai, canal-top and along-the-track projects, and a 1 MW solar plant with 2 MWh of battery storage at Leh. It also does solar EPC and O&M work and, since late 2024, compressed biogas: 9 TPD operating, with plans for about 33 TPD. A new wind arm, Refex Green Power, holds the 80 MW SJVN award.

None to speak of. The 25-year railway and state-utility PPAs give dependable revenue, but the tariffs are fixed and the returns are below the cost of the debt. EPC and CBG are competitive tender businesses.

Risks

Dilution or restructuring. With negative equity and on-demand promoter loans, the likeliest fix is a large equity issue, possibly converting promoter debt into shares at a price set by a stock that trades on one day in four. Minority holders could be heavily diluted.
  • Going concern. The auditor's material-uncertainty paragraph stays until net worth turns positive.
  • Interest exceeds operating profit. Interest cover is 0.6x, and NCDs cost 14.25%.
  • Funding the pipeline. The 80 MW of wind plus 100 MW of solar means several hundred crore of capex, many times the market cap.
  • Illiquidity. 31 trading sessions in six months and ₹7 lakh a day of turnover. The 52-week range of ₹210–765 shows how far the price can swing.
  • CBG execution. The Kolhapur plant dispute has already disrupted operations at the main CBG asset.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: the preferential issue or a debt-to-equity conversion restores positive net worth at a price no lower than market, the going-concern paragraph is withdrawn, and two consecutive quarters show EBITDA covering interest.
  • Upgrade to Buy: all of the above, plus the SJVN wind project funded without further heavy dilution, and EV/EBITDA below 10x on positive earnings.
