Verdict

The cheapest high-return name in Indian solar manufacturing, and cheap for reasons worth knowing. Buy, high risk. Emmvee makes solar modules (10.3 GW of capacity) and TOPCon solar cells (2.94 GW) in Karnataka. Trailing revenue is ₹5,578 cr, trailing profit ₹1,274 cr, and operating margin 35%. The order book is a record 9.9 GW, more than 7 GW of it executable in 18 months. Management has reaffirmed FY27 EBITDA guidance of ₹2,200–2,400 cr. At ₹302, that is about 9x enterprise value to FY27 guided EBITDAd and roughly 13x an illustrative FY27 EPS of ₹23.d

Why the market is not paying more. Two reasons. First, a 35% margin in module manufacturing is not normal. Waaree earns 18% and Premier Energies 29%. It exists because Indian policy protects domestic makers: the ALMM approved-manufacturer lists and domestic-content rules for government-linked projects. Policy can loosen, and capacity is being added across the industry. Second, cash conversion is poor. Operating cash flow was ₹200 cr in FY26 against ₹1,734 cr of operating profit, 23%. Inventory reached 225 days, and free cash flow has been negative three years running as capex continues.

Where the name came from. Jefferies, via NDTV Profit, named Emmvee its top solar pick (Buy) on a "debt-light balance sheet and existing cell capacity". Debt-light: confirmed, with a caveat. Borrowings fell from ₹2,065 cr at March 2025 to ₹360 cr at March 2026, net debt to ₹6 cr, and debt/equity from 3.8x to 0.1x. But ₹1,621 cr of that repayment came from the November 2025 IPO, not from the business. Existing cell capacity: confirmed, but partial. The 2.94 GW of TOPCon cells covers only 29% of module capacity, so about 70% of modules still use bought-in cells. That matters now ALMM List-II, the approved-cell list, has applied since June 2026. Cell utilisation reached 83% in Q1, and the 6 GW integrated expansion, with cells due by March 2027, is what closes the gap. Jefferies' target is ₹440, 46% above spot.

On balance: at 16x trailing earnings the price already assumes margin comes down a long way. If it settles at 25% instead of 35% and volumes grow with the order book, the stock is still not expensive. That asymmetry is the Buy. The cash-conversion problem and policy risk are the high-risk tag.

Business

Nature of business

Emmvee manufactures crystalline solar modules (10.3 GW) and TOPCon solar cells (2.94 GW) in Karnataka. It sells to independent power producers, EPC contractors, commercial and industrial buyers and rooftop programmes, almost entirely in India. Its own cells go into its own modules, and integrated modules qualify for domestic-content and ALMM-protected segments, which carry higher prices. The planned 6 GW integrated TOPCon line would roughly triple cell capacity, to about 9 GW against about 16 GW of modulesd, and lift the integrated share from 29% to over half.

Moat

Policy-made, and therefore conditional. Cell manufacturing is capital-intensive and technically harder than module assembly, and ALMM List-II gives integrated makers a protected segment. Emmvee's early TOPCon capacity is a real head start. But the protection is regulatory, not structural. Industry cell capacity is being built quickly, and a loosening of ALMM or domestic-content rules, or a glut of domestic cells, would take the margin toward Waaree's or lower.

Order book

9.9 GW at 30 Jun 2026, a record. More than 7 GW is executable over 18 months, against Q1 module output of 970 MW, about 3.9 GW annualised. It is stated in gigawatts, not rupees, and pricing is not disclosed, so it cannot be converted to revenue cover without assuming a module price. These are confirmed orders, not a pipeline.

Risks

Margin normalisation. 35% operating margin against Waaree's 18%. If policy protection weakens or domestic cell supply catches up, every 10 points of margin is about ₹560 cr of EBITDA on trailing revenued, roughly 30% of the total. The 16x multiple absorbs some of that, but not all of it.
  • Cash conversion. CFO was 23% of operating profit in FY26, and free cash flow has been negative for three years. A second year like FY26 during the 6 GW build would put debt back on the balance sheet.
  • Execution on the 6 GW plant. The cell line is due March 2027. A slip leaves about 70% of modules dependent on bought-in cells under ALMM List-II.
  • Promoter supply. About five points must be sold by November 2028 to meet minimum public shareholding.
  • Institutional exit. DII holding fell 2.5 points in the June quarter, and the marginal buyer is retail.
  • Module price deflation and policy shifts, including import-duty changes, DCR rules and ALMM scope.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: H1 FY27 operating cash flow above 60% of operating profit, together with the 6 GW cell line commissioning on schedule by March 2027.
  • Downgrade to Hold: operating margin below 28% for two consecutive quarters, inventory days above 250 at FY27 year-end, or the cell line slipping beyond September 2027.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating margin below 20%, which would mean the policy premium has gone; or the order book falling below 6 GW.
