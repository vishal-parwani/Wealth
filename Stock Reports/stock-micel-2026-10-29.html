Verdict

Sell, high risk. Don't buy. The railway story is real but small, and almost everything else about the equity story points the wrong way. The business the market is paying for is MIC's own LED-display and railway passenger-information (PIS) business. In FY26 it produced ₹87 cr of standalone revenue and about ₹25 cr of operating profit. Of that revenue, ₹32.9 cr came from Indian Railways PIS displays. That business is profitable at roughly 28–30% operating margin, but it is being valued at ₹1,099 cr, or about 45xd group EBITDA.

The headline growth is not that business. Consolidated revenue doubled to ₹191 cr in FY26. More than half of it, about ₹104 crd, was "electrical & electronics spare-parts trading" through SOA Electronics Trading LLC, a Dubai subsidiary, at margins below 1%. In the December 2025 quarter that segment booked ₹77.6 cr of revenue for ₹0.46 cr of profit. That trading drove receivables to ₹196 cr, about a full year of revenue. Cumulative operating cash flow over FY21–FY26 is about −₹268 crd.

Three things decide it.

  1. The promoter is a steady seller. RRK Enterprise sold 3.56 cr shares in six tranches between June 2025 and May 2026. That is about 14.8%d of the pre-swap equity, and all of it went through the open market. Promoter holding fell from 66.5% in March 2025 to 51.6% in June 2026, and then to 41.7% after the swap.
  2. The September share swap was large and opaque. MIC issued 5.69 cr new shares, 23.6% dilution, at ₹41.38. That is ₹235 cr of paper for 59% of Neo Semi SG Pte Ltd, a Singapore company described as working in "semiconductor IP, AI-driven energy logistics, IoT smart grid and circular electronics". The shares went to two Mauritius funds administered from the same address (c/o SpearFin, Ebene), plus a UAE consultancy. No revenue or profit for Neo Semi has been disclosed. An independent director had to be redesignated as non-independent because he holds 7.5% of a Neo Semi subsidiary.
  3. The rest of the deal is unfunded. A further ₹122 cr cash leg, payable to Summitbridge Trade & Investments, Singapore, for the remaining 30.65% has no visible source. The company had ₹11 cr of cash in March, and a ₹250 cr QIP and $15 m of FCCBs were approved in December 2025 but have not been raised. That points to further dilution.

MIC does have positives. It holds RDSO approvals, has entered PAPIS for coaches with its first order from RCF Kapurthala, and has prototype approvals for emergency lighting units, with roof-mounted packaged units (RMPU) in development. Railway station and coach digitisation has real tailwinds. But the disclosed orders are ₹1–5 cr letters of acceptance, and no aggregate order book is published. At ₹1,099 cr, the market already prices in a lot of success. A buyer here is betting on the swap and future fund-raises being value-accretive, and the evidence so far does not support that. Sell, high risk.

March 2026 was the exception at ₹50.8 cr, a year-end rush of railway deliveries. Standalone operating margin is steady at 26–35%.

The Q3 FY26 segment filing shows spare-parts trading at ₹77.60 cr of revenue and ₹0.46 cr of segment profit (0.6%), against LED products at ₹12.63 cr and ₹2.58 cr (20%). The "trading arm" column is consolidated minus standalone revenue, so it is derived. It ignores intra-group eliminations, which is why it shows exactly zero in some quarters.

Q2 FY27 will be the first quarter to include Neo Semi, consolidated from 4 September. Expect a jump in reported revenue and assets that says nothing about the railway business.

History: insolvency, resolution and the new promoter

  • 1988–2017: founded in Hyderabad by Dr M V Ramana Rao. It made LED video displays, at one time the domestic leader, with revenue of ₹150–218 cr in FY15–FY17. In 2015–17 it made repeated ₹3-a-share preferential allotments to the founder's family (the Magantis) and Ideamax Consultancy. In 2017 SEBI refused to extend the conversion window on 3 cr warrants.
  • 13 Mar 2018: NCLT Hyderabad admitted it to the insolvency process (CIRP) on the application of SREI Equipment Finance. Revenue collapsed to ₹1–5 cr a year in FY19–FY21.
  • 31 Jul 2019: NCLT approved the resolution plan of the Cosyn Limited consortium. Payments to secured creditors slipped twice and were completed by 28 Apr 2021.
  • Jun 2021: capital was restructured with a record date of 25 Jun 2021. Existing equity was cut to 5.51 cr shares (25%), and 16.52 cr new shares (75%) went to the new promoters, strategic investors and associates. This is how the current promoter group took control: through the resolution plan, not a market purchase.
  • Today: the promoter is RRK Enterprise Pvt Ltd, which held 44.90% before the swap, and the Kakarala family, who held about 6.7%. Siva Lakshmanarao Kakarala is a non-executive director. How RRK relates to Cosyn Ltd is not stated in the documents read. That is a gap.

Business

What it does

MIC Electronics has four reporting segments.

  • LED products is the real business. It makes RDSO-approved railway passenger-information displays, coach-guidance boards, GPS platform clocks, at-a-glance train-formation displays and integrated PIS for station upgrades under the Amrit Bharat scheme. It also makes regular commercial LED displays. In FY26 it earned ₹32.9 cr from Railways PIS and ₹33.3 cr from regular displays.
  • Electrical & electronics spare-parts trading runs through SOA Electronics Trading LLC, Dubai, set up in 2024 with about ₹51 cr of investment. It is the larger segment by revenue and earns almost nothing.
  • Medical appliances and automobiles (EVs) are dormant.

New lines are coming. MIC has prototype approvals for coach emergency lighting units and PAPIS, its first PAPIS order is from RCF Kapurthala, and RMPU (coach air-conditioning) and integrated power supply products are in development. On top of that are Neo Semi (59%, Singapore) and a new defence subsidiary with no orders.

Moat

The moat is narrow. RDSO type approvals and zonal-railway vendor registration take years to get and do keep fly-by-night competitors out. But PIS tenders are small, price-bid contracts, often ₹1–5 cr, with many approved vendors. The trading arm has no moat, and nothing disclosed shows one at Neo Semi.

Order book

Not disclosed. The FY26 annual report gives no order-book figure, and no quarterly release does either. Letters of acceptance announced in 2026 add up to about ₹8–10 crd:

Risks

A ₹1,099 cr valuation on a business that makes about ₹17 cr before tax. The promoter is selling, new equity is going to offshore funds, and the deal's cash leg is unfunded.

  • More dilution. Funding the ₹122 cr Neo Semi cash leg with ₹11 cr in the bank needs the approved ₹250 cr QIP, FCCBs or another preferential issue.
  • Receivables. ₹196 cr is outstanding, about 375 days of sales. A large share sits in a Dubai trading arm, where any write-off would wipe out several years of profit.
  • Lock-in expiry. 5.69 cr swap shares, 19% of equity, unlock on 14 Apr 2027. The allottees received them at ₹41.38 and the stock is at ₹36.91.
  • Promoter exit. The promoter sold 3.56 cr shares in 11 months, and further sales would add supply.
  • Opaque acquisition. Neo Semi's financials are undisclosed and the swap was priced at ₹41.38 against a ₹30–37 market. It may bring goodwill that later gets impaired.
  • Lumpy core business. Railway revenue bunches into Q4, as March 2026 showed.
  • Liquidity. About ₹3.3 cr a day trades on average, and there is no institutional holder base.
  • Insolvency history. CIRP from 2018 to 2021, plus legacy disputes from the founder era.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: audited Neo Semi financials showing real revenue and profit, or the cash leg funded without dilution. Also: receivables back under 150 days with positive operating cash flow for H1 FY27, a disclosed railway order book above ₹100 cr, and promoter selling stopped for two quarters.
  • Upgrade to Buy: all of the above, plus standalone revenue sustainably above ₹25 cr a quarter, through PAPIS, RMPU or ELU volume orders, at a valuation the earnings support.
  • The Sell call is reinforced by any of: a QIP or preferential issue below ₹41, a receivables write-off, further promoter disposals or pledges, or a qualified audit or emphasis of matter.
