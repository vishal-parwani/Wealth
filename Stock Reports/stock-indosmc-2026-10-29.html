Verdict

Hold. Don't buy ahead of the H1 FY27 results. The growth is real and margins have held at about 15%. The trailing P/E is 30x, about double that of the listed meter and switchgear makers. On management's own FY27 guidance the forward P/E is about 19xd. What stops this being a Buy is two problems the results have to clear: profit is not turning into cash, and the IPO money has not been independently accounted for.

The case for it. FY26 revenue was ₹309.7 cr, up 123%. Profit was ₹32.4 cr, up 92%. EBITDA margin was 15.4%. The growth came from moving out of SMC meter boxes into CT-PT units, busducts and metering cubicles, which now make up 72% of sales. Receivable days fell from 124 to 42. The order book was ₹237 cr at 31 March, and management said another ₹125 cr had been added by 22 May. It guides to FY27 revenue of ₹450–500 cr. Utility approvals keep arriving: MSEDCL approved its metering cubicles in January and its isolators on 27 October. Ashish Kacholia has raised his stake every quarter since listing, from 2.46% to 3.25%.

The case against it. Over FY24–FY26 the company reported ₹52 cr of profit, but cumulative operating cash flow was −₹52 cr. Inventory went from ₹17 cr to ₹85 cr in FY26, and finished goods within it from ₹4.4 cr to ₹33.9 cr. CRISIL, the IPO monitoring agency, issued a qualified report for the March 2026 quarter marked "Issuer Not Cooperating". The company's auditor separately certified no deviation, so the two accounts conflict. CT-PT, the segment driving growth, earned a 12.2% segment margin, half the 25% on SMC. Every anchor and institution that came in at the IPO has since left. The stock trades on the SME platform in 1,000-share lots.

Deep dive · added 29 Oct 2026, price ₹420

Hold, leaning Sell. Don't buy before the H1 FY27 print. The primary filings make the case weaker than the headline numbers suggest. Most of FY26's growth came from one private, low-margin busduct order from Lloyd Metals & Energy, which on our reading was worth 47–65% of the year's revenued. A promoter-group company was a top-10 customer in every pre-IPO year, yet the FY26 annual report carries no related-party note. And the IPO money still has no object-by-object account that anyone outside the company has verified. At ₹420 the base case is below the price, so the stock needs management's guidance delivered in full.

  • Pricing power is limited. Prices are set by tender at 11 and 33 kV, ABB and Crompton sit above that voltage class, and price moves pass through only above 5% and only on newer orders. Management keeps orders to one to five weeks so it can reprice.

4 · Governance: what the prospectus shows and the annual report omits

  • Related-party web (RHP, FY23–H1 FY26). Seven group companies. FY25 included a ₹14.20 cr asset purchase from Indocon Infrastructure, 82%d of that year's capex. The Pirana plant is leased from promoter Nitin Patel. The Ghiloth plant is leased from NE Impex, promoter Rachit Jain's company, which also received ₹0.9 cr for capital work and ₹0.88 cr of rent over FY25 and H1 FY26. Relatives draw rent and salary. Directors' unsecured loans were ₹5.94 cr at Sep 2025 and ₹3.25 cr at Mar 2026. Related-party receivables at 30 Sep 2025 were Indo Alluminium ₹6.25 cr, Indocon ₹3.82 cr and others ₹0.56 cr, about ₹10.6 crd in all.
  • The FY26 annual report has no AS-18 note. A search of all 97 pages of extracted text (notes 1–38) found no related-party disclosure. The board report says no transaction needed reporting, so no AOC-2 is attached. Yet the auditor's CARO clause (xiii) says the details "have been disclosed in the financial statements", and in the same clause calls Section 177 "not applicable", although that section applies to every listed company. Director loans and pay appear in notes 7 and 23, so related-party transactions did take place. This is a disclosure gap, and possibly an audit-quality question.
  • Pre-IPO equity. A 6:1 bonus (Aug 2024) and a 2:1 bonus (Dec 2024) capitalised ₹15.5 crd of reserves and the premium from the September placement. That placement was 4,44,450 shares at ₹342, not ₹352. It adjusts to ₹114d a share against the IPO's ₹149. Ashish Kacholia took 1,87,135 of those shares, 5.61 lakh after the bonus, which is exactly his 2.46% in March 2026. His rise to 3.25% since then is open-market buying. A March 2024 placement at ₹200 adjusts to ₹9.5d.
  • The independent director who left. Chintan Umeshbhai Bhatt (DIN 09289074, aged 30) resigned on 6 Apr 2026, citing lack of time. His only other directorship listed in the RHP was Accent Microcell, chaired by promoter Nitin Patel, and he resigned there on 29 Jun 2026. He was legally independent, but not independent of the promoter group in practice. Arpankumar Patel replaced him on 7 Apr and chairs the audit and nomination committees. The resignation letter itself was not opened.
  • Promoters and management. Nitin Patel has 25 years in pharma plant projects (Accent Microcell, Aneta Pharma, Globe Star Valves). Neel Shah, 37, has "3+ years" in SMC, per the RHP. Chaitanya Patel is 28. Riktaben Sonawala has one year in administration. Rachit Jain runs NE Impex and several polymer LLPs. The board's operating record in electrical equipment is short. Neel Shah gave up the CFO role on 4 Sep 2026 to Anand Saraogi, a chartered accountant, which is a welcome separation. No pledge or encumbrance filing was found, and promoter shares are locked in to about January 2029d.

Business

Nature of business

Indo SMC was incorporated in 2021 and has four plants: two at Pirana in Ahmedabad (SMC and FRP), one at Nashik (CT-PT and electrical components) and one at Gheeloth in Rajasthan (SMC). It works in three segments.

  • SMC. Sheet-moulding-compound enclosures for energy meters, plus distribution and junction boxes, sheets and chequered plates. FY26 revenue ₹64.0 cr (21%), segment margin 25.2%.
  • FRP. Gratings, pultruded profiles and cable trays. ₹23.7 cr (8%), margin 12.7%.
  • CT-PT and electrical. 11 and 33 kV current and potential transformers, LT/HT distribution boxes, feeder pillars, busducts and metering cubicles. ₹222.0 cr (72%), margin 12.2%.

Segment margins are segment results before interest, from note 31 of the annual report. Glass fibre and resin are the key inputs. CRGO cores for the transformers are imported from Korea, Japan, China and Taiwan. Sales reach 20 states but are concentrated in Gujarat, Maharashtra and Karnataka. An analyst on the May call cited 60–70% from Gujarat at the time of the DRHP.

Customers, and exposure to RDSS and smart meters

Customers are state discoms and utilities, including MSEDCL and BESCOM, and the EPC contractors and switchgear makers who supply them. Private customers named in filings are Asiatic Composite, Elite Techno Solution and an unnamed metals and mining group, which placed a ₹20.9 cr busduct order in March. On the May call an analyst referred to a ₹100 cr low-margin busduct order from Lloyd. Management confirmed the margin but not the counterparty by name. The smart-meter programme under RDSS is the obvious tailwind for SMC meter boxes, and the company names RDSS in its SWOT. But the exposure is indirect and shrinking as a share of sales. SMC is only 21% of revenue, and an analyst on the May call noted SMC had come in at ₹64 cr against about ₹90 cr in an earlier segment mix. Management blamed resin shortages in March. The growth is coming from distribution hardware (CT-PT, metering cubicles, pillars), where the demand drivers are RDSS loss-reduction work and new connections rather than smart meters themselves. No customer-concentration figure is disclosed: n/a.

Moat

Narrow, and mostly made of approvals. Utility vendor approvals and type tests take months to obtain, and management says few makers hold them at 11 and 33 kV. Those approvals are the barrier, and Indo SMC keeps collecting them. But at 11 and 33 kV the products are standard. Pricing is set by tender and by larger buyers, and ABB, Crompton and dozens of regional makers sit at or above this voltage class. In SMC and FRP the only edges are tooling and the ability to supply quickly.

Order book

Disclosed order book: ₹142.45 cr at 9 Feb 2026 and ₹237 cr at 31 Mar 2026. Management said a further ₹125 cr was added by 22 May. Of that, about ₹120–125 cr is CT-PT and busducts, about ₹80 cr is SMC, and the rest is FRP and tarpaulins. Orders announced since then are ₹23.7 cr (30 Apr, HT busduct and PMCC panels), ₹9.0 cr (18 May, busbar panels) and ₹31.7 cr (5 Oct, FSP/MSP). Most have 1–5 week delivery terms. Management deliberately takes short orders so it can reprice for input inflation. That makes the order book a measure of throughput, not of long-dated visibility. Risks

Profit that does not turn into cash, plus IPO money nobody has independently verified. Over three years the company reported ₹52 cr of profit, while operating cash flow totalled −₹52 cr and free cash flow −₹78 cr. FY26 closed with ₹85 cr of inventory, ₹34 cr of it finished goods, and with CRISIL unable to confirm the use of ₹84.6 cr of IPO proceeds. If H1 FY27 shows inventory still climbing, or another qualified monitoring report, that is how SME stories fail. The thesis would break.
  • Liquidity and lot size. The minimum trade is 1,000 shares (₹4.2 lakh). About ₹3.2 cr trades a day across 1,471 holders, and two market makers provide the quotes. The stock went from ₹149 to ₹528 and back to ₹420 in nine months. Exiting a position at size is not guaranteed.
  • Margin mix. CT-PT is 72% of sales at a 12% segment margin. Management says it wants above 18% on engineering products, but the reported segment numbers do not show it yet. More busduct work like the low-margin order would dilute margins.
  • Inputs. Resin and glass fibre are petroleum-linked, and supply was disrupted in March 2026. CRGO cores are imported. Contracts pass through price moves only above 5%, and only on newer orders.
  • Lumpy half-year disclosure. Only full-year and H2 numbers are filed. H1 FY25 showed a 22.5% margin and H2 FY25 11.3%, so one half-year can mislead.
  • Execution of ambitions. Management targets ₹1,000 cr of revenue within three years, along with railways (Vande Bharat parts awaiting approval), exports to the Gulf, the US and Germany, and automotive. None of these has produced material revenue yet.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27, due by 14 November, needs to show revenue of ₹200 cr or more at an EBITDA margin of at least 15%, positive operating cash flow for the half, and inventory below ₹85 cr. A clean, unqualified CRISIL monitoring report would also be needed. If all of that holds, about 19x guided earnings is reasonable for this growth. A price nearer ₹330 (about 15x FY27 guidanced) would do it on the numbers alone, provided the monitoring report is clean.
  • Downgrade to Sell: any one of these would do it: a second qualified or non-cooperation monitoring report; a deviation in the use of IPO funds; negative H1 operating cash flow with inventory up again; H1 revenue below ₹160 cr; or a promoter selling or pledging shares.
