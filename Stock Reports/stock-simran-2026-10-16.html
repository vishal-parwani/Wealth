Business

Nature of business

Simran Farms is an integrated poultry company based in Indore. It keeps parent flocks and hatcheries and places day-old chicks with contract farmers ("integration"). It supplies feed, medicine and vaccines and pays the farmers a growing charge, then sells the live broilers. Most revenue comes from selling broilers. It has its own feed set-up but buys much of its finished feed from the related party SAPL. Because contract farmers provide most of the sheds, the model needs little fixed capital and a lot of working capital.

Moat

Narrow. The advantages are regional: an established contract-farmer network and hatchery base in Madhya Pradesh. Broilers are a commodity priced daily in local markets, and margin depends on maize and soymeal costs the company can't control.

Not applicable. Simran sells live birds into the spot market and has no disclosed order book.

Risks

Liquidity. About ₹9 lakh of shares trade a day. A ₹10 lakh position is more than a full day's volume, so an exit could take days or weeks and move the price.
  • Related-party pricing. Nearly half of revenue flows through a promoter-linked feed company. The annual report gives no evidence that the prices are set at arm's length.
  • Commodity margin. At a 1% margin, a small move in feed costs or broiler prices can wipe out a year's profit, as it did in FY20.
  • Disease. An avian-flu outbreak in central India would hit both volumes and prices.
  • Cash burn on growth. Operating cash flow was negative in FY25 and FY26, and growth has been funded by debt and the March 2026 warrant money (₹11.8 cr).
  • Promoter-favoured issuance. Nine of the twelve allottees at ₹155 were promoters. Further preferential issues at below-market prices would dilute minority holders again.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: two consecutive quarters with operating margin of 2.5% or more and positive operating cash flow for the half-year, plus disclosure showing SAPL feed prices are benchmarked to market. Or a price near ₹130, about 1.1x book.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a loss-making quarter while borrowings rise above ₹45 cr, the SAPL share of revenue rising further, or another preferential issue to promoters below the market price.
