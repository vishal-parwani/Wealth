Verdict

The growth is real, but the price is ahead of it. Hold, high risk, and judge it on Q2. FY26 revenue rose 41% to ₹251 cr and net profit 67% to ₹30 cr. Q1 FY27 kept the pace: revenue +38% to ₹73 cr, EBITDA +67% to ₹21 cr at a 29% margin, and PAT +120% to ₹10.3 cr. Oncology is the reason. It was ₹33 cr in Q1, 45% of revenue, nearly double a year earlier, and 22 EU/UK/APAC marketing authorisations plus 11 partner site approvals are now in hand. At ₹998, though, the stock trades at 61x trailing earnings and 43x ICICI Direct's FY27 EPS. Management's FY27 guidance is ₹350–375 cr of revenue, which needs about ₹92 cr a quarter from Q2. Q1 was ₹73 cr, and revenue has sat at ₹70–73 cr for three quarters. Q2 is the test, and the board has not yet set a date.

The quality is mid-table. ROCE is 12% and ROE 10%. Inventory days rose from 121 to 207 in two years, and inventory went from ₹28 cr to ₹70 cr while revenue grew 64%. Free cash flow turned positive only in FY25 (₹2 cr) and reached ₹21 cr in FY26. Promoter family members sold about 1.16 points of equity in the June and September quarters, as the stock tripled. Cobra India (Mauritius), an early FII holder, cut its stake from 6.17% to 3.49% over the same year. The registered-shareholder count nearly tripled to 19,539, and nearly all of that new ownership is retail.

The tweet, tested. @TheAlpha10X (14 Oct 2026): "Be bullish… 1) Govt clarified in its affidavit that it wants to put a 30% margin cap on distributors and not on manufacturing companies. 2) Sakar clarified… no impact on its business."

  • Claim 1: confirmed in substance. The Department of Pharmaceuticals filed an affidavit in the Supreme Court in Kishan Chand Jain v. EMRB (heard 12 Oct 2026). It reports an NPPA decision of 8 October to cap trade margins on non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs at 30% of MRP. A trade margin is the gap between the price at the first point of sale and the MRP, which is the distributor and retailer chain, so the manufacturer's selling price is not capped. Three caveats. The line "manufacturers' selling prices and revenues will not be affected" comes from unnamed officials quoted by ET, not from the affidavit text. The drug list is not final: a DGHS committee has 10–15 days, and the next hearing is 27 October. MRPs are expected to fall 20–70%, and the bench is pressing to extend the cap beyond cancer drugs.
  • Claim 2: the filing exists, but its reasoning is contradicted by the company's own call. The 14 Oct NSE letter says there is no impact because "Sakar's business is majorly skewed towards the export of the oncology products." On the 29 Jul call, management said Q1 oncology was ₹33 cr, of which only ₹6.5 cr (about 20%) was exports. FY26 domestic oncology contract manufacturing was about ₹35 cr, plus about ₹27 cr of API, and the FY27 plan still puts exports at only about 30% of oncology. At company level, exports were 60% of FY26 sales (annual report), but most of that is non-oncology. The direct effect is probably small, because Sakar sells to brand owners such as Zydus, Emcure and Glenmark at manufacturer prices. Domestic oncology is still around two-thirds of the oncology book, though, and a 20–70% MRP cut on those partners' brands could come back as pricing pressure on their contract manufacturer.

The same account put SAKAR on its 20 Sep "63-stock high growth + high quality" watchlist. High growth: confirmed. High quality: not on 12% ROCE and 207 inventory days. The stock closed at ₹1,158 on 21 Sep and is 14% lower now. This note rates it on its own numbers, and they say Hold.

Business

Nature of business

The company has two plants near Ahmedabad. Changodar makes oral liquids, tablets, injectables and dry-powder inhalers for CDMO/CMO clients such as Zydus, Emcure, Glenmark, Cipla, Ipca and Abbott, and for its own-brand exports to 60+ countries (heparin and cephalosporins among them). Bavla is an EU-GMP oncology unit with integrated API, oral solid, liquid and lyophilised injectable lines. In FY26, revenue was ₹251 cr: exports ₹151 cr (60%) and domestic ₹100 cr (40%), per the annual report. The FY26 oncology/non-oncology split was 39:61, and 45:55 in Q1 FY27. Oncology sells three ways: domestic CMO for about 15 regional and five large partners (about ₹35 cr in FY26), oncology API (about ₹27 cr), and exports through tech-transfer site variations (Accord-Intas, Torrent, Tillomed) and Sakar's own or licensed-out MA dossiers. That last channel was only about ₹6.5 cr in Q1 and is guided to about ₹60–70 cr in FY27.

Moat

Narrow but real. An EU-GMP cytotoxic facility with integrated API is expensive and slow to replicate. The dossier pipeline (190 submitted, 22 MAs granted) and the partner site variations take years to build, and switching a qualified site is costly for partners like Accord. Against that, the domestic CMO book is price-negotiated by large Indian pharma clients ("the partners are a little bit aware of the market", per management), and European oncology generics are tender-driven and low-priced.

Order book

No firm order book is disclosed. Management cites more than 65 oncology contracts executed, 50+ commercial discussions, and 33 tech-transfer projects underway with Accord-Intas, Torrent, Emcure, Glenmark and Zydus. These are pipeline indicators, not confirmed revenue.

Risks

Guidance needs a step-change. FY27 guidance of ₹350–375 cr means roughly ₹92 cr a quarter for Q2–Q4, against ₹70–73 cr for the last three quarters. Management itself expects the export ramp in Q3–Q4. A Q2 near ₹75 cr with soft commentary would leave a lot to the second half, at 43x earnings.
  • Trade-margin cap, indirect. About two-thirds of oncology revenue is domestic CMO and API. If partners' cancer-drug MRPs fall 20–70%, they may push for lower contract prices, despite the "no impact" filing. The SC is also pushing to extend the cap beyond oncology, and Changodar's domestic CMO is mostly non-oncology.
  • Working capital. 207 inventory days and rising receivables. If export MAs convert slowly, the inventory build becomes a write-down risk.
  • Holder overhang. Promoter-family selling, Cobra India's exit in progress, and a PE AIF at 10.38% that will eventually sell. There is no mutual-fund bid underneath.
  • Regulatory. EU-GMP status at Bavla underpins the whole oncology thesis. One adverse inspection would stop site-variation supply.
  • Concentration and pricing. Large partners (Accord-Intas, Torrent) and tender-priced European generics. ICICI lists oncology pricing pressure and country export risk as the key risks.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q2 FY27 revenue of ₹85 cr or more at a 28%+ EBITDA margin, with oncology exports clearly above ₹10 cr in the quarter, showing the ramp is on schedule. Or the price falls toward ₹820 (about 20x ICICI's FY28 EPS) with guidance intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: Q2 revenue below ₹75 cr, or FY27 guidance cut. Further promoter selling. Or a notified cap, or partner renegotiation, that visibly cuts domestic oncology CMO realisations.
