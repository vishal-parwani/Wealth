Verdict

You are paying roughly ₹690 cr for a business earning ₹66 cr, because most of the market cap is cash. That is the whole idea. TTK Healthcare carries ~₹790 cr of cash and short-term investments against ~₹31 cr of debt — net cash of roughly ₹759 cr inside a ₹1,448 cr company. Add the ₹256 cr arriving from Wipro for the EVA and Good Home brands (closing by 30 Sep 2026) and over 70% of the market cap is net cash. The stub — pharma, medical devices, protective devices and foods — is being valued at something like ₹430 cr against FY26 PAT of ₹65.7 cr. On the headline P/E of 22x it looks ordinary; on what you actually pay for the operating business it is not.

Operationally the turn is already visible: Q4 FY26 PAT +34.7% and Q1 FY27 standalone PAT ₹21.3 cr, +63.9% on revenue +13%, after an FY26 in which profit fell ~20% on Protective Devices weakness. Sunil Singhania (Abakkus) took a fresh 2.07% in June 2026 — ₹30.9 cr across 2,93,030 shares. The obvious catch is that a cash pile is only worth what management does with it, and this is a promoter group not historically in a hurry. Accumulate, but the capital-allocation question is the entire debate.

Business

Part of the TT Krishnamachari (TTK) group — the same house as TTK Prestige, though separately listed and separately managed. Five reported lines: Pharmaceuticals, Consumer Products, Medical Devices, Protective Devices and Foods. The protective-devices business (condoms, the Skore franchise) has been the recent drag — FY26 profit was explicitly hit by PDD headwinds even as revenue grew ~7%.

The Wipro transaction

Approved 23 Jul 2026 · closing by 30 Sep 2026. Wipro Enterprises (Wipro Consumer Care & Lighting) buys the EVA (personal care) and Good Home (home care) brands for ₹256 cr plus GST. The brands did ₹148 cr of FY26 revenue — about 17% of turnover, so the sale is struck at roughly 1.73x revenue.

Read it as a good trade rather than a great price. 1.73x sales is a fair-not-generous multiple for consumer brands, but these were sub-scale assets in categories where TTK was fighting national FMCG players with a fraction of their distribution and advertising budgets — structurally low-margin positions it was never going to win. Converting them into ₹256 cr of cash, and removing the associated competitive drag, improves what remains. Post-deal revenue drops to roughly ₹710 cr, and the mix skews toward the higher-margin pharma and medical-devices lines.

Bull case

  • The valuation is mostly cash. Net cash ~₹759 cr, rising to ~₹1,015 cr once Wipro pays, against a ₹1,448 cr market cap. The implied EV on the operating business is roughly ₹430 cr for ₹66 cr of profit.
  • Earnings have already inflected — Q4 FY26 +34.7%, Q1 FY27 +63.9%. FY26's ~20% profit decline looks like the trough, not the trend.
  • Portfolio is being actively cleaned up, which is not the historic behaviour of this management and may signal a change in posture.
  • Abakkus validation — Singhania's funds do concentrated, research-led mid/small-cap work; a fresh 2.07% is a meaningful signal in a company this size.
  • Fortress balance sheet — debt/equity 3%. Downside is genuinely cushioned.

Risks

Cash you cannot reach is not worth face value. The entire thesis rests on ₹1,000 cr+ of cash being deployed or returned. If the promoter group simply sits on it — which is the base rate for closely held Indian family businesses with strong balance sheets — the stock stays a perpetual "cheap on EV" story and the discount never closes. There has been no announced buyback, special dividend or acquisition plan for the Wipro proceeds. Until there is, treat the cash at a discount, not at par.
  • Low growth in the core — FY26 revenue +7%. This is a steady, not a compounding, business.
  • 17% of revenue is walking out with EVA and Good Home; FY27 headline revenue will optically decline and may spook screens.
  • Protective Devices weakness was the FY26 problem and has not been confirmed as fixed.
  • Persistent margin weakness flagged even in the strong Q4 — the profit recovery is partly base effect.
  • Thin liquidity in a ~₹1,450 cr company; entering and exiting size is slow.
  • No analyst coverage to speak of — no consensus target to anchor against, which cuts both ways.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a concrete capital-allocation event — buyback, special dividend, or a sensibly-priced acquisition using the Wipro proceeds. That is the catalyst that re-rates this.
  • Downgrade to Watch: the cash sitting idle a year after the deal closes with no stated plan, or Protective Devices deteriorating further and dragging the post-divestment mix.
