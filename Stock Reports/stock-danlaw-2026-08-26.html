Verdict

The business deserves a look; this price does not. Danlaw compounds revenue at ~20% with 26–27% returns on equity, almost no debt and zero promoter pledge — but the stock has risen 189% in six months and closed at its upper circuit on six consecutive sessions to 26 August, on an average of ₹0.51 crore of daily turnover, against no company news whatsoever.

The underlying company is real and reasonably good. FY26 revenue grew 20% to ₹262 cr and profit 21% to ₹23.0 cr; Q1 FY27 was the strongest first quarter it has printed (revenue +34.6%, PAT +70.9%). Return on capital employed has been 27–40% for three years, borrowings are ₹30 cr against ₹100 cr of net worth, the promoter has pledged nothing, and the founder-chairman draws ₹27 lakh a year — modest discipline for a company at this valuation.

What has changed is not the business but the price. The stock round-tripped from ₹1,910 (Sep 2024) to ₹518 (Mar 2026) and is now back at ₹1,691, of which the move from ₹970 happened in the four weeks since 31 July. Over that stretch the company filed nothing but routine results, a cost-auditor appointment and an AGM notice. BSE has the scrip under Enhanced Surveillance Measure Stage 1, which is precisely why the daily move keeps stopping at exactly 5.00% — and it already asked the company to explain a price move once, on 6 April 2026, receiving the standard "purely market driven" reply.

The counter-argument is that 32.4x trailing is not obviously expensive for 20%+ growth at 26% ROE: KPIT trades at 36x and Tata Elxsi at 32x. But those are ₹16,000–23,000 cr businesses with 21–26% operating margins, dividends, deep coverage and real liquidity, and both have de-rated 40–56% from their highs over the same year Danlaw quadrupled. Paying a large-cap ER&D multiple for a 15%-margin hardware maker with ₹0.5 cr of daily turnover and no institutional holder at all is the trade being offered here. Hold: worth tracking, not worth chasing into a locked circuit.

This is not an offshore captive of its US parent — it is a domestic hardware company. Danlaw India is widely described as an offshore development centre for Danlaw Inc, and the associate relationship invites the assumption that group billing drives the P&L. The FY25 accounts say otherwise: total sales to Danlaw Inc were ₹7.94 cr and total foreign-exchange earnings ₹8.18 cr — 3.6% and 3.7% of revenue respectively. Roughly 96% of the business is products sold in India. The transfer-pricing risk usually attached to this name is therefore small; the concentration risk sits with undisclosed domestic customers instead.

Business

Nature of business

Incorporated in 1992 and headquartered in Hyderabad, with an electronics factory at Verna, Goa and a branch in Bengaluru. It designs and manufactures automotive electronics — AIS-140-certified 4G and 2G Telematics Control Units in IP67 housings, Body Control Units, data loggers and diagnostic tooling — and sells embedded software development, embedded testing and CAN/LIN conformance testing alongside. ISO 9001:2015 and IATF 16949 certified. Roughly 88% of revenue is product sales and 96% is domestic. It is part of the Danlaw group founded by Raju Dandu, whose US arm owns V2X specialist Cohda Wireless — but that IP sits at Danlaw Inc, not in the listed Indian entity, a distinction worth keeping straight when the group's connected-car credentials are used to frame the stock.

Moat

Narrow but real, and mostly regulatory and qualification-based. IATF 16949 certification plus AIS-140 type approval are genuine barriers to a new entrant selling telematics units to Indian vehicle OEMs, and automotive electronics carry multi-year programme qualification cycles that create switching costs once designed in. What is missing is proprietary IP of consequence — the group's V2X assets are held above the listed company — and pricing power, which 15% operating margins and 61.7% material cost argue it does not have. Treat it as a qualified contract manufacturer with an engineering arm, not as a technology licensor.

Risks

You cannot get out. Six-month average turnover is ₹0.51 crore a day and the median day trades 2,836 shares. On 20 August the price rose the full 5% on 1,544 shares — about ₹21 lakh. A position of any size cannot be exited without moving the price against itself, and with the scrip in BSE's ESM Stage 1 framework the 5% band cuts both ways: a reversal locks at the lower circuit with no exit at all. This risk is independent of whether the business does well.

  • The move has no disclosed cause. Six consecutive upper circuits to 26 August, +74% in four weeks, against a filing record containing only routine results. BSE already queried a price move on 6 April 2026 and was told it was "purely market driven". Whatever is setting this price, it is not published information.
  • Undisclosed customer concentration. ₹192 cr of domestic product sales, no segment reporting and no major-customer disclosure. A single OEM programme ending could take out a large share of revenue and shareholders would learn of it only in the results.
  • Hardware margin fragility. Materials are 62% of revenue and quarterly EBITDA margin has swung 12.8%–18.6%. FY25 already showed what a flat year looks like: revenue +3.5%, profit −14%.
  • Working-capital intensity. Cash conversion cycle 110 days, inventory 104 days, debtors 78. FY26 cash from operations was ₹21 cr against ₹23 cr of profit and free cash flow only ₹12 cr — growth consumes cash, and there is no dividend.
  • Governance is thin rather than bad. No pledge, modest promoter pay and disclosed related parties are real positives; but a family-weighted small board, a promoter that is also a lender and an associate, and near-zero voluntary disclosure leave a minority holder with little visibility.
  • Sector de-rating. Automotive ER&D multiples have compressed hard over the past year. Danlaw is priced as if that has not happened.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the price consolidates back toward the low-₹1,000s (≈20x FY27E) and the company starts disclosing what it sells and to whom — segment reporting, customer concentration, or an investor presentation. Sustained turnover above ₹5 cr a day, or exit from ESM, would make a position exitable.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a further leg up without disclosed cause (say above ₹2,000, ~40x FY27E); or promoter or family selling into the rally; or escalation to ESM Stage 2 / GSM; or a quarter that shows the FY26–Q1 FY27 acceleration was a single large order rather than a run-rate.
