Verdict

The growth numbers are big, and very little of the growth is organic. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹527 cr, up 143%, and profit ₹68 cr, up 127%. The group order book is ₹8,857 cr, over five years of the listed company's trailing revenue. On 17 August it took control of Steel Infra Solutions (SISCOL, 51%) for ₹626 cr: ₹127 cr cash, borrowed from Tata Capital, plus 7.07 cr new shares at ₹71.25. That share issue is why the promoter stake fell from 41.92% to 40.00% in August.

What still worries us. FY26 operating cash flow was −₹253 cr and free cash flow −₹494 cr, paid for with a ₹857 cr rights issue. Inventory went from ₹86 cr to ₹433 cr, from 59 to 209 days. The company's own "pro-forma" numbers combine the listed company with group entities still being merged in (Lloyds Infrastructure, Metalfab, Techno), which will mean more shares when the merger completes. So the P/E on the headline numbers depends on a share count nobody has published yet. All of this runs through a group where the same promoters are on both sides of most deals.

At ₹82 the stock is 8% below the July note's ₹89 and trades at 57x trailing earnings. Stays Hold, high risk. The business is growing fast, but we can't yet see how much of it produces cash or how much of the group each share will own once the merger is done.

Business

What it does

Formerly Lloyds Steels Industries. It designs and builds heavy equipment and delivers engineering and construction projects for oil and gas, ports, steel plants, power and infrastructure, from a large works at Murbad, Maharashtra. It is being turned into a broader engineering and construction group by merging in sister companies (Lloyds Infrastructure, Metalfab Hightech, Techno Industries) and buying SISCOL. It also has licensed technologies for shipbuilding (Fincantieri), low-emission steel (TMW) and drones. The stated target is ₹10,000 cr revenue by FY30.

Moat

Not proven yet. Heavy fabrication and construction projects are won on bids and delivery. The bet is on the licensed technologies and much larger capacity. None of them has a revenue track record yet.

Order book

₹8,857 cr, group-wide at 30 June 2026, company-stated. That is 5.5x the listed company's trailing revenue but covers the merged group, so it isn't directly comparable. The listed company's own order book was about ₹2,800 cr in coverage of the same results. The group figure grew mostly by adding entities, not by winning new work in the listed company.

Risks

Deals with related companies, and more dilution to come. The promoter group sits on both sides of the mergers and of SISCOL (Lloyds Enterprises is a co-buyer). The share-exchange terms for the pending group merger decide how much of the pro-forma profit each existing share gets, and those terms are set by related parties.
  • Cash doesn't match profit. Operating cash flow was −₹253 cr in FY26, with inventory days at 209.
  • Profit quality: unusually low tax, big other income, and associate profit included in net profit.
  • Heavy retail ownership: 58% public, 2.3% institutions, 4.2 lakh holders. The stock swings sharply.
  • Doing too much at once: three mergers, one acquisition, several technology licences and a 2x capacity build, all at the same time.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the merger completes with a published post-merger share count that puts the stock below about 35x merged earnings, and half-year operating cash flow turns positive.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a merger ratio that favours the promoter entities, more debt-funded acquisitions from within the group, or operating cash flow negative again for FY27.
