Verdict

Sell. Don't buy it. The FY26 earnings the price rests on have not been turned into cash, and almost half of them depend on one related-looking customer. Revenue from operations went from ₹22.1 cr in FY25 to ₹148.8 cr in FY26 (+573%). Profit went from ₹5 cr to ₹37.1 cr, the year the IPO was being prepared. The RHP explains how. Defence revenue jumped from ₹1.5 cr to ₹103.3 cr, and it was mainly "supplies of drone kits, which involve high-value electronic components". Quick Pay Private Limited alone was ₹69.9 cr (47.0%) of FY26 sales. Millworks had lent Quick Pay money and then turned that ₹5.75 cr loan into equity. The RHP also says the two companies "have some common shareholders". Some of the material went straight from the supplier (Big Bang Boom Solutions) to the customer on a bill-to-ship-to basis, and partial assembly took place at the partners' sites. That makes it look more like pass-through trading than precision machining.

The cash test fails. Year-end trade receivables were ₹138.7 cr against ₹148.8 cr of FY26 revenue (93%), up from ₹6.8 cr a year earlier. Operating cash flow was negative in three of the four years: −₹11 cr in FY26 on ₹52 cr of EBITDA. Free cash flow has been negative every year (−₹19 cr in FY26). Payables also rose to 349 days, so suppliers are financing the gap. ₹4.6 cr of FY26 other income, about 9% of PBTd, was a year-end foreign-exchange gain. The company blames slow collections on "war-like conditions" delaying payments from foreign customers. Until H1 FY27 shows those receivables being collected, the ₹37 cr profit is an accounting figure, not a proven one.

Price. At ₹987 you pay 46.9x FY26 EPS of ₹21.0d on the enlarged share count, and about 7.5x post-IPO bookd. That is cheaper than listed defence-precision peers (Azad 118x, Paras 105x, Unimech 96x). But those peers' earnings are audited through several cycles and backed by cash. Millworks has a single breakout year that has not yet converted to cash. Promoters' average cost is ₹0.05 a share and pre-IPO investors paid ₹94.58. The anchor lock-ins have expired (50% about 16 Aug, the rest about 15 Oct), and one anchor, LRSD, has already filed SAST disclosures for selling. A 193x-subscribed SME float that has tripled since the IPO has plenty of supply above it and no fund holder beneath it.

For a buy decision. Don't buy. The October news is positive: two developmental HAL orders (no value stated), a non-binding UAV-engine MoU with TridenTech, and a 3D-printing centre. None of it addresses the receivables or the concentration in Quick Pay. Revisit after the H1 FY27 (Sep-26) results, due by 14 Nov 2026. What would change the view is set out below. Related parties and governance flags. (1) Quick Pay Pvt Ltd was 47% of FY26 revenue. Millworks lent it money, then converted the ₹5.75 cr loan into 5,332 shares, roughly ₹10,785 a shared. The RHP says the two have common shareholders but are "not related" under the definitions. This is the main flag: the largest customer is partly funded by the supplier. (2) Big Bang Boom Solutions, a Chennai anti-drone company, is the supplier for these defence programmes. It did sub-assembly, gave access to its facility and shipped directly to customers. (3) V3 Technologies, the promoters' own partnership firm, transferred machinery to the company in exchange for shares. It is also party to an agreement to sell immovable property to the company. (4) Two partnership firms, Hindustan Spring Mfg and Universal Automobile & Dairy Products, were acquired by business transfer in Mar 2025. (5) The RHP admits delayed and non-compliances under the Companies Act, and notes that one promoter (Sowmya Madhu) has no prior industry experience. (6) There were 8 preferential allotments in 14 months before the IPO to about 30 investors, many of them individuals and family offices based in Mumbai, followed by a 200:1 bonus. That is the usual SME pre-IPO pattern. Lock-ins: anchors are now free. Under ICDR, pre-IPO non-promoter shares and the promoters' holdings above the minimum contribution unlock in tranches from about Jul 2027, and the promoters' 20% minimum contribution stays locked for 3 yearsd.

Business

Nature of business

Founded in Nov 2021 in Bengaluru. Its first orders were executed in Q2 FY23. The company does CNC machining of precision components and sheet-metal parts, and builds integrated assemblies to print and to spec for defence, railways, aerospace and semiconductor equipment. It runs four units with 3.83 lakh installed machine-hours (Unit 4 was commissioned in FY26), has 159 employees and 74 customers, and exports to 9 countries. Railway work (₹35 cr in FY26) is the organic core. The FY26 step-change came from drone kits for defence, sold mainly through Quick Pay. IPO proceeds go to plant and machinery (up to ₹61.0 cr), working capital (up to ₹68.8 cr of a ₹164 cr requirement) and general corporate purposes. No external agency appraised these uses.

Moat

Thin. Vendor approvals with railway OEMs and the new HAL developmental orders take time to win. The rest of the work is commodity job-shop machining. The drone business depends on two partner companies, and Millworks owns neither the design nor the end customer.

Order book

₹67.1 cr as of 5 Jun 2026 (RHP), made up of confirmed POs and annual rate contracts across railways, defence, aerospace and semiconductors. That is only 0.45x FY26 revenued, so the order book does not cover even half of last year's revenue. The HAL orders since then are developmental and carry no stated value, and the TridenTech MoU is non-binding. No updated order book has been filed since listing.

Risks

The one that matters most. ₹138.7 cr of receivables, 93% of FY26 revenue, concentrated in customers the RHP does not name, while 47% of revenue comes from a single investee. A slow collection or a write-down in H1 FY27 would cut the FY26 profit base. That profit is what the 46.9x multiple rests on.
  • Revenue quality. The pre-IPO year grew 6.7x, driven by drone kits with high electronic-component content, shipped bill-to-ship-to via a partner. It is unclear how much of the work is Millworks' own machining.
  • Cash burn. CFO was negative in 3 of 4 years and FCF negative in all four. The RHP projects 147 receivable days for FY27. IPO cash will go into working capital rather than growth.
  • Supply overhang. Anchors are free and already selling, and pre-IPO holders bought at ₹94.58 against ₹987 now. On a 2,094-holder SME float, more pre-IPO and promoter shares unlock from about Jul 2027.
  • Liquidity. The trading lot is 400 shares, about ₹3.95 lakh. Turnover since listing averages ₹14.6 cr a day, inflated by the listing weeks. SME circuits can trap exits.
  • Customer concentration. Top 10 customers are 92% of revenue and the single largest is 47%.
  • Disclosure. Reporting is half-yearly only, customers are anonymised in the RHP except Quick Pay, and there are admitted compliance lapses.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: H1 FY27 shows receivables at or below about ₹100 cr, positive operating cash flow for the half, and Quick Pay below 30% of revenue, with revenue run-rate holding near FY26.
  • Upgrade to Buy: all of the above for two consecutive halves (H1 and FY27), plus an order book of at least 1x revenue from named OEMs, DPSUs or railways, and a price at or below about 30x cash-backed EPS.
  • Stay at Sell / add conviction: receivables keep rising, a provision or write-off appears, Quick Pay grows as a share of revenue, further related-party investments are made, or H1 is filed late.
