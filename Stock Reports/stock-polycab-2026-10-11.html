Verdict

The new-entrant scare has de-rated the whole category, including the leader least exposed to it. At about 38xd FY27 consensus earnings, the best-capitalised cable franchise in India is a Buy. FY26 revenue grew 29% to ₹28,884 cr and profit 32% to ₹2,708 cr. Operating cash flow was 117% of operating profit, and free cash flow nearly tripled to ₹2,340 cr. Q1 FY27 was the highest first quarter on record: revenue ₹8,210 cr (+39%), PAT ₹797 cr (+33%), domestic W&C +43%.

The stock is ₹8,255, 18.5% below its ₹10,129 high, and most of the fall came after UltraTech launched Ultravolt on 3 Sep. Citi's read, which we share, is that the threat is mainly to wires, a retail and channel product where a cement distribution network helps. Cables need approvals, certifications and many SKUs. Polycab's mix is 70–75% cables, so it is less exposed than RR Kabel or Havells, yet it trades on a similar multiple (43x against their 50x and 39x).

Testing the tweet (Jefferies "India's New Industrial Revolution", via NDTV Profit, 10/14 Sep 2026: Polycab a Buy as a data-centre capex beneficiary).

Confirmed: the report exists (published 9 Sep). It sizes India's data-centre build-out at 2 GW today, rising to 10 GW in five years, a ~$45 bn opportunity across power, cooling, construction and networks. Jefferies separately called Polycab's post-Ultravolt fall a buying opportunity at ~35x one-year-forward earnings.

Not supported by the numbers: Polycab does not disclose data-centre revenue, and BOB Capital's Q4 FY26 review calls data centres "still at an early stage" for the company. The public coverage we found does not name Polycab within the report itself unreconciled. So data centres are a plausible extra source of growth, not something the reported numbers show yet. The Buy here rests on the core franchise, the cash generation and the de-rating, not on data centres.

Counter-argument: part of Q1's 39% growth is copper price pass-through rather than volume (JM Financial's point), and EBITDA margin slipped to 13.8% from 14.5% a year ago. International revenue fell 13% and EPC 11%. If Ultravolt prices aggressively to buy share, the 13–14% margin is where it shows first.

Business

Nature of business

Polycab makes wires and cables (about 85–90% of revenue) through 26 plants: building wires, LV/MV/HV power cables, and control, instrumentation and specialty cables. It sells through 3,900+ dealers and 1.9 lakh retailers, and exports to 94 countries. FMEG (fans, switches, lighting, switchgear, solar products) is a smaller, faster-growing second leg, now profitable at an 8% EBIT margin. A small EPC arm executes power-distribution projects. Organised W&C market share is about 30–31% per management.

Moat

Real, and it comes from scale and distribution. The largest dealer and electrician network in the category, procurement scale in copper and aluminium, and approvals and certifications across utility, industrial and infrastructure buyers. The weak point is building wire, which is closest to a commodity and where a well-funded entrant with a retail network (UltraTech's 5,000 building-solutions outlets) can compete. Cables, the larger share of Polycab, are protected by approvals and engineering.

Order book

Risks

A price war in wires. UltraTech (₹1,800 cr, aiming for top-two in five years) and Adani's Praneetha Ecocables are both well funded. Net margins in C&W are only 5–7% industry-wide (Jefferies), so a 1-point price cut is material. Polycab's quarterly EBITDA margin has already ranged from 12.7% to 15.8% in a year.
  • Copper. Revenue growth is partly inflation. A copper correction would flatter margin but cut reported growth and could prompt inventory losses.
  • Data-centre expectations. The theme helps the multiple but is not in the reported numbers, and disappointment would reverse that support.
  • International and EPC both fell double digits in Q1. Exports were a stated growth leg.
  • Working-capital reversal. FY26 free cash flow was helped by stretched payables (108 days), which will not repeat every year.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: below about ₹7,400 (roughly 34x FY27 consensus EPSd), or Q2/Q3 FY27 domestic W&C growth above 20% with consolidated EBITDA margin at or above 13.5% while Ultravolt ramps.
  • Downgrade to Hold: two consecutive quarters of consolidated EBITDA margin below 12.5%, or management conceding share in building wire.
  • Downgrade to Sell: an open price war, with margin below 11%, or a return to negative free cash flow from a working-capital build.
