Verdict

The "11x versus peers at 44x" pitch is the whole reason to look at this, and it is the one number I would not take at face value. Lalithaa is a genuine business: 61 stores across 51 cities, a discount-jeweller model with real scale, ₹25,024 cr of FY26 revenue, and a credible anchor book (₹508 cr from Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, ICICI Prudential and others at the top of the band). ROE of 41.6% against a peer average of 21.7% looks outstanding. On FY26 earnings the issue is priced at roughly 11x, against Kalyan and Titan on ~44x.

But look at how FY26 happened. Revenue grew +0.65% in FY25 and then +48% in FY26. Profit grew +1.4% in FY25 and then +177% in FY26. Net margin went 2.14% → 2.16% → 4.03% — it nearly doubled in a single year, in gold retail, where margins are structurally thin and famously stable. Now add three disclosed facts: the company holds ₹9,816 cr of inventory, it does no gold-price hedging, and despite ₹1,010 cr of reported profit its operating cash flow was negative ₹397.76 cr. This is not a guess: analysts covering the issue state that because the company runs no hedging policy, the entire benefit of the gold price rise flowed into margins — and that those margins should moderate once gold stabilises. FY26's profit is substantially a gold windfall, not an earnings run-rate. Do not apply at ₹201.

Normalise the margin and the "discount" mostly disappears. Put FY26's ₹25,024 cr of revenue on the FY24–FY25 net margin of ~2.15% and profit is roughly ₹538 cr, not ₹1,010 cr. Against the ~₹11,250 cr valuation that is about 21x — still below Kalyan and Titan, but nothing like a 75% discount, and a fair price rather than a bargain for a regional retailer. The 11x headline and the 41.6% ROE are computed on the same possibly-inflated numerator. This is the single calculation to run before applying.

Two flat years followed by an explosion. In a business selling a commodity at a making charge, a doubling of net margin does not come from selling better — it comes from the commodity. Gold jewellery was 92.33% of FY26 revenue, so there is nothing else in the mix that could have done this. EBITDA margin tells the same story: 6.5% in FY26, up 240 bps, implying roughly 4.1% the year before.

One funding detail worth knowing. Customer advances — the gold savings/instalment schemes — have grown from ₹1,943.23 cr (FY24) to ₹3,145.41 cr (FY25) to ₹5,042.75 cr (FY26). That float now funds roughly half the ₹9,816 cr inventory book, which is cheap capital and a genuine structural advantage in Indian jewellery retail. It is also a liability that must ultimately be settled in gold, so it does not insulate the company from a gold move — it scales with it.

Where the money goes

  • ₹998.68 cr — funding inventory for 10 new stores
  • ₹34.55 cr — capital expenditure on store fit-outs
  • Remainder — general corporate purposes
Roughly 83% of the fresh money buys more gold. Only ~₹35 cr of the ₹1,200 cr is actual store capex; the rest funds the inventory to fill them. That is normal for jewellery retail — but it means the IPO proceeds increase the size of the unhedged gold position that just produced the windfall. You are being asked to fund a larger bet on the same variable, in the same direction, at the top of a gold cycle.

The bull case

  • Real scale and a real format — 61 stores, 51 cities, ₹25,000 cr of revenue on a discount-jeweller model that has clearly worked in its home market.
  • Serious anchor book — ₹508.20 cr at ₹201 from 22 investors: Goldman Sachs Bank Europe (19.68%), Morgan Stanley India Investment Fund (4.27%), and domestic mutual funds at 45.26% via ICICI Prudential, Bandhan, Samco and Bank of India. This is not a papered book.
  • Genuinely cheaper than listed peers even after normalising — ~21x against Kalyan and Titan near 44x. Some discount is deserved for a regional player; not necessarily all of it.
  • High capital efficiency in the reported numbers, and low debt relative to revenue (₹1,238.1 cr of borrowings on ₹25,024 cr of sales).
  • Gold demand in South India is culturally entrenched and among the most resilient consumer categories in the country.

Risks

Profit without cash. FY26 delivered ₹1,009.8 cr of reported PAT and negative ₹397.76 cr of operating cash flow — a gap of about ₹1,408 cr. Some of that is deliberate inventory build for expansion, which is defensible. But a company whose record profit year produces no operating cash, on an unhedged commodity book, is the textbook setup for earnings that look better than they are. Read the cash-flow statement before the P&L.
  • No gold-price hedging. Stated plainly in the disclosures. It cuts both ways — and it has just cut favourably. A gold correction would reverse the margin expansion directly.
  • ₹1,066 cr GST dispute outstanding, against a ₹11,250 cr valuation — roughly 9% of market cap at stake. Other promoter-related matters also remain open.
  • 100% South India — 61 stores, all in one region. The expansion plan is national, which means executing in markets where the brand has no recognition and incumbents are entrenched.
  • 92.33% gold concentration — no studded/diamond mix to cushion margin, which is exactly what Titan and Kalyan use to stabilise theirs.
  • Promoter selling — the entire ₹500 cr OFS is Kiran Kumar Jain. None of it reaches the company.
  • Modest GMP (~11%) and a split street — Swastika says Subscribe with "moderate conviction", SBI Securities says Neutral. That is not the profile of a broadly-loved issue.

How I'd play it

  • Don't apply at ₹201. The valuation case rests on an earnings number I cannot verify as recurring, and the fresh proceeds scale up the same unhedged exposure that produced it.
  • Revisit post-listing once two quarters show whether the ~4% net margin holds without a rising gold price behind it. If it does, this is genuinely cheap and the rating changes.
  • If you do apply, treat it as a trade on the ~11% grey-market premium, sized accordingly — not as a position.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: two post-listing quarters with net margin holding above ~3.5% while gold is flat or falling, and positive operating cash flow; or the RHP confirming the FY26 margin jump came from operating leverage rather than inventory gains.
  • Downgrade to Sell: net margin reverting toward ~2% on a gold correction, the GST dispute resolving against the company, or the national expansion showing weak store-level economics outside the South.

Confirmed — this is no longer an inference

Analyst commentary states the mechanism explicitly: "The company's strong growth over the past two years can be attributed to a sharp rise in gold prices, especially since the company does not have any hedging policy, leading to the entire benefit of the gold price rise flowing into the margins. However, margins of the company may moderate once the gold price stabilises." Separately, the company itself attributes the negative operating cash flow to a sharp rise in gold prices requiring higher capital investment in stock. So the same variable — gold — is simultaneously inflating the reported margin and draining the cash. That is the whole story of FY26, and it is now sourced rather than deduced.

The RHP itself remains inaccessible from this environment (SEBI, BSE, NSE archives, the BRLM sites and the company's own PDF are all blocked by the network egress policy), so the restated cash-flow statement has not been read line by line. But the causal question this note hinged on is answered.
