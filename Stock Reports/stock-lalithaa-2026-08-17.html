Earlier drafts of this note inferred the earnings driver from secondary sources. The RHP settles it, and one disclosure decides the whole case.

Verdict

Lalithaa sold 0.74% more gold in FY26 and reported 177% more profit. That single pairing is the entire investment case, and it is not a good one. The RHP discloses volumes directly: gold sold rose from 21,531.77 kg in FY25 to 21,691.43 kg in FY26. Over the same year the average realised gold rate rose from ₹7.33 mn/kg to ₹10.63 mn/kg (+45%). Management's own explanation of the 48.09% revenue increase lists three causes: store count going from 60 to 61, the higher gold rate, and that 0.74% volume gain. There is no fourth reason, because there isn't one. The growth is the gold price.

The profit is the same story one layer down. Gross margin went 7.59% → 8.52% → 10.00%. The company states plainly that it does not hedge — "we do not employ hedging practices such as availing gold metal loans facilities unlike the practices most of our competitors employ" — so gold bought cheap and sold dear runs straight through the P&L. EPS went ₹7.20 → ₹7.29 → ₹20.20. At ₹201 that is ~10x reported earnings, and the "75% discount to Titan and Kalyan" headline is built on it. But Titan and Kalyan do hedge, via gold metal loans. Lalithaa's apparent cheapness is largely the absence of a hedge during a 45% gold move. Do not apply at ₹201.

What normalisation actually gives you. Revert gross margin to FY25's 8.52% on FY26 revenue and gross profit falls ~₹371 cr; revert to FY24's 7.59% and it falls ~₹604 cr. That maps to roughly ₹735 cr and ₹562 cr of PAT respectively, against the reported ₹1,009.8 cr — so the true multiple at ₹201 is somewhere around 14–18x, not 10x. And that is the optimistic framing, because it assumes revenue holds. With volume flat, revenue only holds if gold stays at these levels: if gold merely stops rising, FY27 gets a flat top line and a reverting margin at the same time.

Look at FY25 as the control experiment. The company opened seven new stores (53 → 60) and gold rose 23% — and revenue grew 0.65%. That combination can only mean volumes fell hard. Then in FY26 it opened one store, gold rose 45%, and revenue grew 48%. Two consecutive years in which the underlying business did not grow; one of them happened to coincide with a gold spike.

Gold nearly doubled across two years. The RHP also notes that Indian jewellery demand fell from 549 tonnes in FY25 to 425 tonnes in FY26 (−22.6%) as record prices hit affordability. Against that backdrop, Lalithaa holding volume flat is a genuine share gain — the strongest fact in the bull case, and the reason this is a Hold rather than something harsher.

A correction to my earlier note

I previously framed the negative operating cash flow as "profit without cash." The RHP shows that was too harsh. Operating profit before working-capital changes was ₹1,678.8 cr in FY26, up from ₹761.3 cr — strongly positive and more than doubled. The negative headline figure comes from a deliberate ₹3,943.5 cr increase in inventories, roughly half funded by a ₹1,925.6 cr rise in customer advances (the gold savings schemes). FY25 operating cash flow was positive ₹288.7 cr; FY24 was −₹18.0 cr. So the FY26 outflow is an inventory build for expansion, not evidence that the profit is fictitious. The profit is real cash margin — it simply isn't repeatable.

The hedging disclosure, verbatim

"Further, we do not employ hedging practices such as availing gold metal loans facilities unlike the practices most of our competitors employ to protect them from price fluctuations, including the use of gold metal loans, forward contracts and options etc. … Therefore, we are exposed to major gold price fluctuations. Although we have managed to avoid such situations in the past by way of effective inventory management and replenishment of inventory on daily basis, we cannot assure you that we will continue to manage such fluctuations in future."

The RHP also states directly that "an increase in the price of gold may result in an increase in our income from inventory held for sales" — the company acknowledges the mechanism. Note the symmetry it implies: what lifted FY26 will subtract in a falling market, and peers who hedge will not take that hit.

The bull case, fairly stated

  • Real share gain. Flat volume while the national jewellery market fell 22.6% is a genuine competitive result — the discount model wins customers when gold is expensive.
  • Scale. ₹25,024 cr of revenue, 61 stores across 51 cities, 21.7 tonnes of gold sold a year.
  • Customer float. Advances of ₹5,042.8 cr fund roughly half the ₹9,816 cr inventory book — cheap capital and a structural advantage.
  • Credible anchor book — ₹508.20 cr at ₹201 from Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, ICICI Prudential, Bandhan and others.
  • Gross margin is genuinely trending up — 7.59% → 8.52% → 10.00%. Some of that may be mix and scale rather than pure gold, and if any of it sticks the normalised multiple improves.
  • Even normalised, ~14–18x is below Titan and Kalyan.

Risks

  • No volume growth for two years. FY25 volumes fell despite seven new stores; FY26 was +0.74% off that lower base.
  • Unhedged, by choice, unlike peers. The margin is a directional bet on gold that shareholders inherit.
  • Double exposure in a flat-gold scenario — revenue stops growing and gross margin reverts, simultaneously.
  • ~83% of fresh proceeds buys inventory (₹998.68 cr of ₹1,200 cr; only ₹34.55 cr is store fit-out capex), scaling the same unhedged position.
  • ₹1,066 cr GST dispute — roughly 9% of the market cap.
  • 100% South India, with a national expansion into markets where the brand is unknown.
  • 92.33% gold concentration — no studded mix to stabilise margin the way peers do.
  • Entire OFS is the promoter, M. Kiran Kumar Jain. Insurance is partly held in the promoter's name.
  • Split street — Swastika Subscribe (moderate conviction), SBI Securities Neutral. GMP only ~11%.

How I'd play it

  • Don't apply at ₹201. The headline multiple is an artifact of an unhedged gold position, and the RHP's own volume disclosure removes any ambiguity about it.
  • If you hold allotment, treat the listing as the exit rather than the entry.
  • The number to watch post-listing: gold volume in kilograms, not revenue. Revenue will keep flattering or punishing this company for reasons that have nothing to do with how well it trades.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: two post-listing halves showing volume growth (kg sold) with gross margin holding near 10% while gold is flat or falling — that would prove the margin is structural, and at 14–18x it would be genuinely cheap.
  • Downgrade to Sell: gold correcting materially, gross margin reverting toward 8%, or the national expansion showing weak store economics outside the South.
