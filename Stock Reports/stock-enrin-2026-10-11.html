Verdict

Earnings are accelerating, the backlog covers two years of revenue, and it is the cheapest of India's listed T&D equipment makers. The multiple is high, but the growth earns it, so this is a Buy. Over three quarters, year-on-year PAT growth has gone from +35% to +52% to +68%. The June quarter delivered ₹441 cr on ₹2,486 cr of revenue and a 23.6% operating margin. The order backlog is ₹19,331 cr, about 2.05xd trailing revenue, and up 16% in a year. Transmission is 56% of revenue and grew 42%. Capacity is being doubled at Kalwa and switchgear expanded at Sambhajinagar (₹740 cr, FY26–28). A further ₹2,060 cr greenfield will take transformer capacity to 60,000 MVA by FY30–32, funded from internal accruals.

On valuation, 72x trailing is expensive in absolute terms. On Motilal Oswal's estimates it is 53.5xd FY27 (September 2027), against 79–115x trailing for GE Vernova T&D, Hitachi Energy, ABB and CG Power. Every broker that has published since August rates it Buy, and the 15-analyst consensus is 23% above spot. The stock is 22% below its 52-week high.

Testing the tweet (Jefferies "India Industrial Revolution" via NDTV Profit, 10/14 Sep 2026: Siemens Energy India a Buy as a data-centre beneficiary).

Confirmed: Jefferies made ENRIN one of its five preferred industrials on 2 Sep 2026 with a ₹4,800 target, the highest in our table. Motilal Oswal independently lists "data-centre requirements in the 220/400 kV range" as one of four new ordering streams, alongside STATCOM, TBCB transmission tenders and exports.

But: data centres are a supplementary driver, not the main one. The backlog is built on grid transmission and renewable integration. India's 900 GW renewable target by FY36 is what fills the order book, and the data-centre component is not disclosed. The Buy stands without it.

Counter-argument: the history is short. There is one full financial year (to September 2025), and before that a carve-out from Siemens Ltd. Cash-flow and net-cash figures are not yet consistent across sources unreconciled. Orders are lumpy: STATCOM ordering was weak until Q2 FY26. Motilal Oswal has already cut FY27 estimates 5% because new capacity commissions later than planned. At 54x forward, a single quarter of weak inflow can take 20% off the price, as the drop from ₹3,968 to ₹2,105 within the year showed.

Business

Nature of business

ENRIN has two segments. Power Transmission (56% of 9M FY26 revenue) covers power transformers (Kalwa), high-voltage switchgear (Sambhajinagar), substations, HVDC and STATCOM/FACTS, sold to utilities, transmission developers, renewable parks and increasingly data centres, plus exports via the parent. Power Generation (44%) covers industrial steam turbines up to 250 MW, refurbishment of large steam turbines, and gas-turbine services and long-term service agreements, supported by a ~1,500-engineer R&D centre in Gurgaon that serves global projects. It does not manufacture gas turbines in India.

ENRIN and GE Vernova T&D are the two high-margin, high-return transmission pure-plays, and ENRIN is the cheaper of the pair. Hitachi Energy trades at 115x with a 15% margin, so the market is paying more for order growth than for profitability there.

Moat

Strong. It rests on technology and qualification. HV transformers, GIS and HVDC need parent technology, long utility qualification and type-testing, and in India the market is effectively shared among Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy and GE Vernova, with transformer capacity sold out industry-wide. Pricing power is visible in the margin. The moat's main dependency is the parent, which owns the technology and decides which global projects come to India.

Order book

₹19,331 cr as at 30 Jun 26, ≈ 2.05xd trailing revenue, up 16.4% from ₹16,601 cr a year earlier. Q3 inflow was about ₹3,294 cr, led by transmission (420 kV GIS for a large solar park, ±300 MVAr STATCOMs). Motilal Oswal's 2 Sep note cites an "INR135b" backlog unreconciled, which is probably the transmission book alone, but this could not be confirmed.

Risks

Valuation against order lumpiness. At 72x trailing and 54x forwardd, the price assumes inflow keeps compounding. Transmission ordering depends on TBCB tender timing and large single orders, and a quarter with book-to-bill below one would test the multiple. The 52-week range of ₹2,105–₹3,968 shows how far it can swing.
  • Capacity, not demand, sets the pace. New Kalwa and switchgear capacity phases in over FY26–28 and the greenfield over FY30–32. Brokers are already trimming for commissioning delays.
  • Parent dependence and related-party flows. Technology, royalties and export allocations all run through Siemens Energy AG, so terms can change at the parent's discretion.
  • Short and messy reported history. Cash flows are not yet consistent across sources, and the September 2026 annual report is the first clean comparison.
  • Supply-side catch-up. Global and Indian transformer capacity additions (Hitachi, GE Vernova, CG Power, domestic players) will eventually ease today's pricing power.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: below about ₹2,700 (roughly 46x Motilal Oswal FY27E EPSd) with the backlog intact. Or a quarter with inflow above ₹4,000 cr and margin at or above 22%.
  • Downgrade to Hold: two consecutive quarters of book-to-bill below 1 (backlog falling below about ₹17,500 cr), or operating margin back below 19%.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a stall in the transmission ordering cycle (TBCB pause) with backlog shrinking while the multiple stays above 60x, or adverse changes to parent royalty or export terms.
