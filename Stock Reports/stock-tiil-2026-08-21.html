Verdict

Buy — unchanged, and this is the rare case where a 34% rally left the stock no dearer. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹2,479.20 on 3 Aug 2026, calling it "the value pick at ~23x". The stock is ₹3,319. It trades on 22.2x. Earnings rose faster than the price: trailing EPS went from roughly ₹108 to ₹149.78, up about 39%, against a 34% price move.

Q1 FY27, reported 13 August, is what did it. Consolidated sales ₹805 cr, up 27.2%. PAT ₹138 cr, up 68.3%. Operating margin 22% — the highest in the five-quarter series and up from 18%. The last note was written with these results still pending; they came in at the top of the ₹620–713 cr revenue and ₹85–108 cr PAT range it had sketched, and then beat it.

The franchise is unchanged and still the reason to own it. Technocraft is the world's second-largest maker of steel drum closures with roughly 37% share outside China — a dull, tiny-ticket product where failure is expensive and buyers do not switch on price. Around it sit scaffolding and formwork (₹240 cr and ₹165 cr of Q1 revenue, driven by US construction), steel tubes and cotton yarn.

The old criticism still stands. The fabric division continued to lose money in Q1 FY27. The rationalisation flagged in the last note has not yet shown up as an exit, so commodity yarn losses are still masking the quality of the closures franchise. A genuine exit remains the trigger to raise conviction further.

Business

Drum closures. The core franchise: the sealing fittings on steel drums. Technocraft is number two globally with roughly 37% share outside China. The product costs very little and fails expensively, so buyers qualify a supplier and stay. This is the segment that has paid for everything else for decades.

Scaffolding and formwork. The current growth engine — ₹240 cr of steel scaffolding and ₹165 cr of aluminium formwork in Q1 FY27 alone, against ₹2,759 cr of full-year group revenue. Demand is driven by US and European construction, so this is the segment that carries genuine cyclical risk, and it is the one currently expanding margin.

Steel tubes and cotton yarn. Tubes are a competent commodity business. Yarn is the problem: loss-making, cyclical, capital-absorbing, and it obscures the group's real return profile. Management has signalled rationalisation and set up new overseas subsidiaries, but Q1 FY27 still carried fabric losses.

What you are buying. A niche global near-monopoly plus a cyclical construction supplier plus a commodity drag, at 22x. The upside case is that the drag gets removed and the multiple re-rates towards what the closures business alone deserves.

Risks

  • Fabric losses persist. The stated rationalisation has not produced an exit. Until it does, group returns are understated and the story is unresolved.
  • Lumpy earnings. Quarterly PAT ranged from ₹54 cr to ₹138 cr in the last five quarters. One good quarter is not a trend, and the 22.2x multiple leans on a strong one.
  • US construction cycle. Scaffolding and formwork growth depends on overseas construction demand, which turns without warning.
  • Borrowings of ₹819 cr against ₹2,000 cr of reserves. Manageable, but it has roughly doubled since FY22 (₹506 cr) while profit was flat.
  • Five years of flat profit before FY26. PAT was ₹274 cr in FY22 and ₹263 cr in FY25. The Buy case assumes the recent inflection persists rather than reverting.
  • Promoter at the 75% ceiling. No headroom to signal confidence by buying, and any future fundraise means dilution or a sell-down.
  • ASM surveillance and a shrinking holder base. Both point to a tighter, more volatile float than the ₹7,530 cr market cap suggests.

What would change the rating

  • Stays a Buy — at 22x with margin expanding and the closures franchise intact, even allowing for the fabric drag.
  • Raise conviction — a confirmed exit or demerger of the yarn/fabric business. That is the single event that would let the market value the good businesses on their own.
  • Downgrade to Hold — margin falling back under 16%, the December quarter reverting to ₹50–60 cr of PAT with no seasonal explanation, or borrowings rising past ₹1,000 cr.
  • Downgrade to Sell — a US construction downturn hitting scaffolding volumes while fabric losses continue.
