Verdict

The business is getting better and the stock has got cheaper. At 24x trailing earnings, Buy, but keep the position small: it is high risk. Revenue growth has picked up for four quarters running: +20%, +13%, +28% and +35% year on year. Profit in the June 2026 quarter rose 71% to ₹5.9 cr. FY26 revenue was ₹262 cr (+20%), operating profit ₹39 cr (+24%) and profit ₹23 cr (+22%). ROCE is 28%. Leases aside, the balance sheet is close to net cash: ₹27 cr of cash against ₹19 cr of bank debt. The share count has not changed since 2022: no preferential issue, no warrants and no QIP.

The stock ran from ₹987 on 3 August to ₹1,805 on 4 September on the back of the Q4 and Q1 results. It has since fallen 32% to ₹1,235 with no negative filing. As a result, the trailing multiple is 23.6xd, close to the roughly 20x of a year ago, when growth was in single digits. That is below Pricol (28x) and far below the EMS names (Syrma 74x, Avalon 92x). If FY27 profit grows 25%, the stock is on 21xd FY27 earnings.

The counter-arguments are real, and they are why this is high risk. (1) Liquidity: the stock trades about ₹63 lakh a day, so getting in or out has to be done patiently with limit orders. (2) Opacity: the FY25 and FY26 annual reports are filed as image scans. The company has never disclosed its customer concentration in any filing this note could read. No broker covers the stock, and no institution holds it in size. (3) Margins are thin and volatile: operating margin ran 12.7–18.6% over eight quarters. The company imports much of its component cost: FY24 foreign-exchange outgo was ₹76 cr, 36% of revenue. That exposes margins to the rupee and to chip prices. (4) Capital allocation: it has paid no dividend since at least FY11 despite three years of ₹19–23 cr profit.

The parent link is smaller than the name suggests. Danlaw Inc (Michigan) holds 23.88% directly, and the Dandu group's Danlaw Systems India holds 30.01%. But on the last readable related-party note (FY24), sales to Danlaw Inc were only ₹3.5 cr, or 1.7% of ₹211 cr revenued. FY24 foreign-exchange earnings were ₹3.5 cr in total. This is now a domestic auto-electronics manufacturer, not a captive offshore centre for its US parent. The remaining related-party links are modest: a USD 2.5 m ECB from Danlaw Inc (long-term borrowings were down to ₹2.65 cr by March 2026), office rent paid to RRG Properties LLP (promoter-linked), software services bought from Darby Telematics, and hardware sales to Akteena Technologies. The FY25 and FY26 notes could not be read, so whether the 1.7% has changed is a gap.

What would change my view: I would add more aggressively if Q2 FY27 revenue is ₹80 cr or more (+22% year on year), with operating margin at least 15%. I would downgrade if growth falls back below 10%, if a pledge or a related-party transaction of real size appears, or if the price runs back toward ₹1,600 without earnings to match. The full list is under "What would change the rating".

Business

Nature of business

Danlaw Technologies India (DTIL) is an IATF 16949-certified developer and manufacturer of connected-vehicle and automotive electronics. Products include 2G and 4G telematics control units, data loggers, body control units, connected gateway modules with cybersecurity, diagnostic tools, and battery-swapping trackers. For EVs it makes battery management systems, inverters and motor controllers. It also does engineering and R&D work in automotive, medical and consumer electronics. Manufacturing (PCB assembly and sub-system integration) is at L-15 Verna Industrial Estate, Goa, which came with the 2022 merger of Danlaw Electronics Assembly. Engineering is in Hyderabad and Bengaluru. Customers are described only as "OEMs and Tier-1 suppliers in India, the US and Europe" plus fleet and insurance-telematics users. No customer is named and no concentration figure is disclosed in any filing this note could read.

Relationship with Danlaw Inc. DTIL began as Danlaw Inc's offshore development centre. Today Danlaw Inc is a 23.88% shareholder, which the FY24 accounts call an "associate company". It is not a holding company in the legal sense. The promoter group controls 61.87% through Danlaw Systems India (30.01%), Danlaw Inc and the Dandu family. The FY24 related-party note shows sales to Danlaw Inc of ₹3.10 lakh of goods plus ₹347 lakh of services, about 1.7% of revenue, and ₹185 lakh of services bought from it. Interest on the USD 2.5 m ECB (6-month SOFR + 2%, 5-year term) was ₹147 lakh. Most revenue is domestic. FY24 foreign-exchange outgo of ₹76 cr, against earnings of ₹3.5 cr, shows the business imports components and sells in India.

Moat

Narrow. Telematics and body-control units are designed into a vehicle programme and usually stay for its life, which makes revenue sticky once a programme is won. IATF certification and Danlaw Inc's IP in V2X and diagnostics help it win programmes. Against that, the company is small next to Tier-1 suppliers such as Pricol and Minda, its bill of materials is largely imported, and OEMs hold the pricing power. A 15% operating margin is good for electronics manufacturing but leaves little cushion.

Risks

Illiquidity. Average turnover is ₹63 lakh a day (about 5,000 shares). A ₹10 lakh order is about 16% of a normal day's trade. The 16–21 Oct slide shows how quickly the price moves on modest selling, so build and exit a position over several sessions with limit orders.
  • Undisclosed customer concentration. A few OEM programmes probably make up most of revenue, but this is unverified. Losing one could cut revenue sharply.
  • Import-heavy cost base. FX outgo was 36% of revenue in FY24. A weaker rupee or a chip shortage would hit margins directly.
  • Margin volatility. Operating margin swung between 12.7% and 18.6% within a year. Net margin is 6–12%.
  • Information risk. There is no broker coverage, no conference calls were found, and the annual reports are scanned. Problems may only show up in the quarterly numbers.
  • Related parties. Small today, but the channels (ECB, services, rent and associate companies) mean value could leak if their scale grows.
  • Auto cycle. Demand depends on Indian vehicle production and on EV and telematics adoption.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: Q2 FY27 revenue of at least ₹80 cr (+22% year on year) with operating margin at or above 15%, and H1 FY27 operating cash flow positive. Or any disclosure of customer mix showing no customer above about 30%.
  • Downgrade to Hold: Q2 FY27 revenue growth below 10% or operating margin below 12%. Or the price back above about ₹1,600 (around 27x FY27 earnings at 25% growth) without results to justify it.
  • Downgrade to Sell: any promoter pledge or promoter selling, a related-party transaction above about 10% of revenue, a preferential issue to promoters at a discount, or two consecutive quarters of falling revenue year on year.
