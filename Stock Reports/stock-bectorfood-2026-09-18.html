Verdict

A good quarter, but not enough yet to upgrade. In July, FY26 profit had gone nowhere for three years (₹140 cr, ₹143 cr, ₹141 cr) while the company spent heavily on new plants. The June quarter broke that pattern. Revenue was ₹549 cr, up 16%. EBITDA was up 24%, margin rose to 13.1%, and profit was up 26% to ₹38.8 cr. Biscuits (+15.7%) and bakery (+17.5%) both grew, and price increases covered higher raw material costs.

Our July upgrade test was two quarters of double-digit profit growth. This is the first. March was +3%. The stock is ₹222, up from ₹208 at the last note and 31% above the ₹169 at which Abakkus bought in July. At 46x trailing earnings, much of the recovery is in the price. The average broker target of ₹263 is about 19% above today.

The balance sheet is clean: net cash, and free cash flow positive in FY26 for the first time in three years. Management targets 14% margin by Q4 FY27 and 15–16% by FY30, on revenue doubling to ₹4,000 cr. If the September quarter confirms the trend, this becomes a Buy. For now it stays Hold, with a positive tilt.

Business

What it does

Two businesses. Biscuits under the Mrs. Bector's Cremica brand (₹325 cr in Q1, about 60% of sales): strong in North India, plus exports to more than 60 countries and private-label work. Bakery under English Oven (₹215 cr in Q1, about 40%): one of India's largest premium bread and bun makers, and a major supplier of buns to fast-food chains such as McDonald's and Burger King. The FY24–FY26 spending on new plants has built capacity for both. The plan now is to fill it.

Moat

Mixed. Biscuits are a tough market: Britannia, Parle and ITC compete hard on price, and Bectors' brand is regional. Bakery is better. Supplying fast-food chains takes strict quality approval, and there are long relationships and daily fresh delivery. Few competitors can do that at national scale.

Order book

Not relevant for a consumer company. Sales run through distributors, retail and fast-food supply agreements. The forward plan is management's target of ₹4,000 cr revenue by FY30, about double FY26.

Risks

Paying for a recovery that has lasted one quarter. At 46x trailing earnings, the price assumes margin reaches 14% and profit grows in double digits for years. If the September quarter comes in flat like March did, the multiple has a long way to fall.
  • Input costs: wheat, palm oil, sugar and packaging. Margin slipped in FY25–FY26 when these rose. Price increases worked in Q1, but that can't be repeated forever.
  • Foreign selling: five quarters in a row. If it continues, the price falls with no change in the business.
  • Fast-food customer concentration in bakery. Slower store openings by a few chains feed straight into bakery growth.
  • Low returns on new capacity until it fills up: ROCE is 14%, down from 25%.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the September quarter shows double-digit profit growth again, making two in a row, with margin at or above 13.5%. Or the price falls back toward ₹190 (about 39x) while the recovery continues.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin back below 12% for two quarters, or revenue growth under 10% as the new capacity sits idle.
