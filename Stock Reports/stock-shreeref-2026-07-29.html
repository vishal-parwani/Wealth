Update · 13 Aug 2026 — a new data-centre cooling vertical, but the orders behind it are small. Shree has entered a strategic association to bring magnetic-bearing chiller technology to mission-critical data-centre applications, with management expecting the vertical to contribute 10–15% of revenue over three years. The adjacency is genuinely logical — naval HVAC and refrigeration engineering transfers well to data-centre cooling, and the end market is growing quickly. Set against it, the concrete orders announced this month are minor: ₹2.54 cr from Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders and ₹9.75 cr from Kongsberg Maritime India, roughly ₹12 cr combined against a ₹1,128 cr market cap. FY26 annual report filed 8 Aug; 20th AGM on 31 Aug. Price ₹316 (from ₹338), on ~52x earnings against a 52-week range of ₹161–406. Rating held (Speculative · Defence Niche, Rich Valuation). The data-centre angle is a reason to keep watching, not yet a reason to pay 52x — it is a stated intention, not revenue. Wait for it to appear in the P&L.

Verdict

A real naval-qualification moat wrapped around a very small, lumpy, richly-valued business — and one accounting flag that deserves your attention. Shree Refrigerations makes chillers, marine HVAC and refrigeration plants, and has become a qualified supplier into Indian naval shipbuilding: systems aboard INS Dunagiri and INS Agray, and orders across the yard ecosystem — Hindustan Shipyard, Mazagon Dock, Cochin Shipyard and Kongsberg Maritime. Naval qualification takes years and tends to stick, which is the genuine argument for owning this. FY26 delivered: revenue ₹153.5 cr (+55%), PAT ₹21.4 cr (+65%), with the new Karad plant doubling capacity.

Three things temper it. First, valuation: ~56–66x on a ₹21.4 cr profit after a ~98% six-month run. Second, concentration: the ₹195 cr backlog appears to lean heavily on a single ₹107 cr Hindustan Shipyard award (five fleet support ships, won Aug 2025) — so "order book" here is closer to one big programme than a diversified pipeline, and the remaining disclosed wins are ₹5–10 cr each. Third, and most important, the company resubmitted its FY26 results with a cash-flow revision. Even a technical restatement is a governance signal worth weighting in a company with one year of listed history. Own it small, as a speculative sleeve on naval indigenisation — and read the FY26 annual report's cash-flow statement before adding.

Business & order book

Chillers, refrigeration and air-conditioning equipment and HVAC components, with the valuable mix being marine/defence: warship air-conditioning and refrigeration plants, and cooling systems for shipbuilding programmes. Disclosed awards:

  • Hindustan Shipyard — ~₹107 cr (HVAC supply & installation, five fleet support ships; awarded Aug 2025). The anchor order, and likely the majority of the backlog.
  • Mazagon Dock — two orders (AC plant and FCU repair; timelines ~12 months and to Jun 2027) — repair/AMC-type work, valuable because it is recurring.
  • Kongsberg Maritime — ₹9.75 cr (fleet support ship cooling) — an international partner qualifying the company is a genuine credibility marker.
  • Cochin Shipyard — ₹4.83 cr (8 kW refrigeration plant, NGMV) — note the delivery timeline stretches to Feb 2030, illustrating how slowly defence revenue converts.

Capacity roughly doubled with the new Karad plant. Jul 2026 housekeeping: ESOP 2026 approved (up to 10,00,000 shares) and Ms. Rucha Shende regularised as Whole-Time Director.

Bull case

  • Qualification is the moat. Naval HVAC requires shock- and vibration-qualified equipment and multi-year approval; once on a platform class, incumbency and repeat/refit orders follow.
  • Indigenisation tailwind: India's warship and support-vessel programme is long-dated and explicitly favours domestic suppliers. Clearing Kongsberg's vendor bar shows the quality travels.
  • Recurring service revenue: the Mazagon repair/FCU work is the most under-appreciated line — refit and AMC income is stickier and less lumpy than new-build.
  • Small base, big lever: at ₹153 cr revenue, a single fleet programme moves the P&L materially — capacity is already in place to serve it.

Risks

The restatement is the headline risk. FY26 results were resubmitted with a cash-flow revision. In a company one year listed, with no analyst scrutiny and a 56–66x multiple, any restatement — technical or not — raises the bar on trust. Verify the audited cash-flow statement and the auditor's remarks before increasing exposure.
  • Order concentration: a ₹195 cr book anchored by one ₹107 cr award is programme risk, not diversification. A slip on that contract hits both revenue and the narrative.
  • Glacial conversion: a Cochin order delivering to 2030 shows how long defence revenue takes to land; quarters will be violently uneven.
  • Valuation: ~56–66x demands sustained 40%+ growth just to hold the multiple; the stock is already ~17% below its ₹406 all-time high, which is what the first pause looks like.
  • Dilution: EPS grew 28% against 64% PAT growth on IPO-year dilution; the new ESOP pool adds a further path.
  • Working capital: defence/PSU receivable cycles are long and can consume the cash that growth generates — the exact area the restatement touched.
  • No coverage: no broker publishes a target; price discovery is retail-driven.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: a clean audited FY26 cash-flow statement with no auditor qualification, plus either a pullback toward ₹250–280 (~40x) or order inflows taking the book beyond 2x revenue with less single-programme reliance.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: any further restatement or auditor qualification, growth decelerating below ~25% at this multiple, or receivables stretching visibly.
