Verdict

A real defence-indigenisation niche that has already been re-rated to reflect it. Shree Refrigerations makes chillers, marine HVAC and refrigeration systems, and has quietly become a qualified supplier into Indian naval shipbuilding — equipping INS Dunagiri and INS Agray, and winning orders from Kongsberg Maritime (₹9.75 cr, Fleet Support Ship cooling) and Cochin Shipyard (₹4.83 cr, NGMV refrigeration plant). Naval qualification is the moat: it takes years, and once you're on a platform you tend to stay. FY26 delivered on it — revenue ₹155 cr (+56%), TTM PAT ₹21.5 cr versus ₹12.6 cr in FY25 — and the Karad plant has doubled capacity. But the market has done the maths already: the stock is up ~98% in six months and trades near 56–66x earnings on a ₹155 cr revenue base with a ₹195 cr order backlog (~1.3x revenue — respectable, not commanding). At ₹338 it sits ~17% below its ₹406 high, so this is a pause, not a breakout. Worth owning as a small speculative sleeve on India's naval build-out; not at a size that assumes the multiple holds.

Business

Manufactures chillers, refrigeration and air-conditioning equipment, and HVAC components — with the valuable part of the mix being marine and defence applications: naval vessel air-conditioning and refrigeration plants, and cooling systems for shipbuilding programmes. Customers/programmes disclosed recently include Kongsberg Maritime (Fleet Support Ships), Cochin Shipyard (NGMV), and installed systems aboard INS Dunagiri and INS Agray. Capacity roughly doubled with the new Karad plant. Governance/comp housekeeping in Jul 2026: ESOP 2026 approved (up to 10,00,000 shares) and Ms. Rucha Shende regularised as Whole-Time Director (18 Jul).

Bull case

  • Naval qualification is a genuine barrier. Defence marine HVAC requires shock/vibration-qualified equipment and long approval cycles — incumbency on a platform class is sticky and repeat-ordering.
  • Indigenisation tailwind: India's warship and support-vessel programme is multi-year and explicitly favours domestic suppliers; Kongsberg and Cochin Shipyard orders show it clears international-partner scrutiny too.
  • Growth is real, not narrative: revenue +56% and PAT roughly +64% in FY26, with capacity already doubled to serve the next leg.
  • Small base (₹155 cr revenue) means a single large naval programme can move the numbers materially.

Risks

~56–66x for a ₹21.5 cr profit. The re-rating has done most of the work already (+98% in six months). At this multiple the stock needs sustained 40%+ growth just to stand still — and it is already ~17% off its ₹406 high, which is what a first pause in a momentum name looks like.
  • Lumpy, tender-driven orders: the disclosed wins are ₹5–10 cr each against a ₹195 cr book; defence procurement timelines slip routinely, so quarters will be uneven.
  • Customer concentration in a handful of shipyards/PSUs; a programme deferral hits disproportionately.
  • EPS lags PAT (+28% vs +64%) on IPO-year dilution — and the new ESOP pool adds a further, if modest, dilution path.
  • Working capital: defence/PSU receivable cycles are long and can consume the cash that growth generates.
  • Short listed history (IPO Jul 2025) — one year of public reporting, thin analyst coverage, and figures here come from aggregators rather than audited filings I have reviewed.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: a pullback toward ₹250–280 (~40x) with the order book intact, or order inflows stepping up decisively (book >2x revenue) confirming the naval franchise compounds rather than trickles.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: growth decelerating below ~25% while the multiple stays 50x+, receivables stretching, or the order book flat-lining for two or more quarters.
