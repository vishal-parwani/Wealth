Verdict

Hold — unchanged, and like the last note the problem is price, not the business. The prior note rated this Hold at ₹371.25 on 3 Aug 2026. The stock is ₹540.85 — up 45.7% in 18 days, and it closed at its 52-week high. Nothing in the reported numbers changed in those 18 days. FY26 results were already public when the last note was written. What has happened since is one new manufacturing unit and two small orders.

The business is real and the balance sheet is clean. FY26 revenue ₹30.65 cr, up 49.6%. Revenue has roughly tripled in three years, from ₹10.37 cr in FY23. ROCE is 31.8%, borrowings are ₹4.18 cr against ₹36.85 cr of reserves — effectively net cash after the IPO. For a job shop this is a good set of numbers.

What stops it being a Buy is the gap between profit growth and price. FY26 profit rose just 13.1%, to ₹7.51 cr, while revenue rose 49.6%. Margin fell from 32.4% to 24.5%. So the company is buying growth with margin — and the market is paying 86.8x trailing for it, up from about 57x at the last note. The entire move since 3 August is multiple expansion, not delivered earnings.

What stops it being a Sell is that nothing is deteriorating. Revenue is accelerating, profit is still growing, returns are high and there is no debt. That is a very different picture from a business in decline. The rating stays Hold because an excellent small company at a stretched price is a Hold, not a short.

Business

Nature. Build-to-print precision machining. Apsis makes components and sub-assemblies to designs supplied by its customers, for aerospace, defence and healthcare. Work runs from CAD/CAM through process development to CNC machining, handling parts up to about 1,200 mm. The business began as the partnership firm Apsis Latitude in 2012, became a company in 2022 and listed on the NSE SME board on 18 March 2026 at ₹110, opening at ₹153.

Moat. Thin, and process-based rather than structural. What protects it is qualification: getting approved to supply an aerospace or defence prime takes time, and once a part is qualified into a supply chain it is awkward to move. There is no scale, brand or IP advantage. This is a small job shop in a fragmented market where the customer holds pricing power — which is visible in FY26's margin drop from 32.4% to 24.5%.

Capacity. Originally two sheds in Peenya Industrial Area, Bangalore. Unit II opened 14 August 2026. Capacity was the binding constraint on a company this size, so the second unit matters more than any single order — but no capex or output figure has been put to it.

Peers. MTAR Technologies, Azad Engineering and Dynamatic Technologies are the listed comparisons, and all are 7–15x Apsis's size. None is like-for-like. The useful takeaway is that Apsis trades at a premium multiple to companies with far longer records.

Risks

  • Valuation against profit growth. 86.8x trailing while FY26 profit grew 13.1%. The multiple needs earnings to roughly double to look ordinary.
  • Margin compression is already visible. PAT margin fell from 32.4% to 24.5% in FY26. If revenue growth is being bought with price, the next leg costs more margin.
  • No institutional floor. Zero mutual fund ownership, 307 shareholders, 18.72% float, an SME board. Price moves are violent in both directions and exits can be hard.
  • Stale information. Last earnings are H2 FY26 to March, last shareholding is March, and both are about five months old. The company reports half-yearly, so this is normal for an SME — but it means you are buying with less current information than a main-board stock.
  • Institutions already sold once. DII holding fell 3.65 points within two weeks of listing. Whether that continued through the run-up cannot be checked until the September filing.
  • Concentration. Single-location until this month, roughly ₹31 cr of revenue, and no disclosed customer split. Losing one relationship would be material, and the split is not published.
  • Lock-in expiry. All promoter shares are locked today. Any release date is a supply event on a stock with almost no float.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy — H1 FY27 showing profit growth back above 40% with margin holding near 25%, or a price fall bringing trailing P/E under about 45x with the growth intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell — H1 FY27 profit flat or falling, margin below 20%, or the September shareholding showing institutions continuing to sell. Any of those, at this multiple, turns a stretched Hold into a Sell.
  • Stays a Hold — while the business keeps growing, carries no debt and earns 30%+ returns, but the price has already run ahead of the earnings.
