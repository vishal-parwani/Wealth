Verdict

Exceptional growth with fragile foundations, at a full price. Hold, high risk. Netweb designs and assembles high-performance computers, AI GPU servers, storage and private-cloud systems in India. It is the main domestic beneficiary of the government's sovereign-AI compute build-out. Revenue went from ₹445 cr in FY23 to ₹2,184 cr in FY26, a 70% compound rate, and Q1 FY27 revenue of ₹820 cr was up 172%. AI systems were 62% of it. The confirmed order book is ₹2,507 cr, with another ₹848 cr where Netweb is the lowest bidder (L1).

Three things keep this from a Buy. First, concentration: the top five customers went from 62% to 81% of revenue in one quarter. Big sovereign-AI contracts are lumpy, and one delayed tender shows up directly in a quarter. Second, economics: operating margin of 12–15% and net margin of about 10% are those of a systems integrator passing through expensive GPUs, not a technology owner. Inventory days went from 91 to 170 in FY26 and borrowings from ₹8 cr to ₹282 cr. Third, price: 108x trailing and 71x FY27 consensus.

Where the name came from. It was on @TheAlpha10X's 20 September 2026 list of 63 "high growth + high quality" stocks for buying the dip. High growth: confirmed, emphatically: a 70% three-year compound rate, accelerating. High quality: half-confirmed. ROCE of 37% and RoE of 33% are excellent, and FY26 operating cash flow was ₹790 cr. But that cash came from a ₹894 cr jump in other liabilities, customer advances and supplier credit, not from margin. The 81% customer concentration is the opposite of quality in the usual sense. The dip: confirmed. At ₹4,723 the stock is 19% below its ₹5,813 high and 1.4% below the ₹4,790 at which institutions bought ₹1,200 cr of new shares in August.

The counter-argument is real. Consensus expects FY27 revenue of ₹4,168 cr, 91% growth and far above the 35–40% management guided in May, and FY28 EPS of ₹85.65. On that, the stock is 55x FY28, and the order book plus L1, about ₹3,355 cr, roughly equals the ₹3,348 cr of revenue that consensus still needs for FY27 after Q1.d If Q2, due about 30 October, holds an ₹800 cr+ run-rate, the case strengthens quickly.

Business

Nature of business

The company designs and assembles supercomputers (HPC clusters), AI GPU servers and "AI factories", private-cloud and hyper-converged infrastructure, enterprise workstations, storage and data-centre servers. Manufacturing is in India. Customers are government and research institutions, defence, the India AI Mission's sovereign-compute build-out, hyperscale and enterprise data centres, and universities. AI systems were 62% of Q1 FY27 revenue. The company partners with the major GPU and CPU makers rather than designing chips itself. Its value-add is system design, integration, software stack and local manufacturing.

Moat

Narrow, built on position more than technology. Netweb is the incumbent domestic supplier for government HPC and sovereign-AI work, where local manufacture, security clearance and a track record on national supercomputers matter. That is a genuine barrier against foreign OEMs in public tenders. It is weaker in enterprise and hyperscale, where global server makers compete directly. The core components, GPUs and CPUs, are bought from partners who hold the pricing power.

Order book

₹2,507 cr confirmed at 30 Jun 2026, ≈ 0.93x trailing revenue, plus ₹848 cr L1, which is lowest bidder and not yet contracted. Reports describe the wider pipeline as above ₹10,000 cr, but the figure is quoted as both ₹1,040 cr and ₹10,410 cr unreconciled, so it is not relied on here. Management guides about 60% conversion over 18–24 months.

Risks

Customer concentration at 81%. Five customers, likely including large sovereign-AI and government programmes, account for four-fifths of revenue. A single tender delayed by a quarter could take revenue back toward the ₹300 cr base of a year ago. At 108x, the stock would not survive that intact.
  • GPU supply and pass-through economics. Lead times have doubled to 16–20 weeks. Margin depends on component pricing Netweb does not control, and export-control changes on advanced GPUs are a tail risk.
  • Working capital. Inventory at 170 days and borrowings up 35x in a year. Rising interest is already visible in the P&L.
  • Promoter selling. Two sales in 18 months at successively higher prices.
  • Policy dependence. India AI Mission funding and procurement timing drive a large share of the book.
  • Thematic ownership. New fund holders are innovation and digital funds that rotate quickly.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q2 and Q3 FY27 both above ₹800 cr revenue with top-five concentration falling back below 70% and the order book growing; or the price reaching about ₹3,850, 45x FY28 consensus.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a quarter below ₹500 cr revenue, the confirmed order book falling below ₹1,500 cr, operating cash flow turning negative as advances unwind, or a further promoter sale.
