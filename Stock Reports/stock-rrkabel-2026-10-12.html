Verdict

What changed since 24 Sep 2026 — downgraded from Buy to Hold. The price is up 7.6%, from ₹2,530 to ₹2,722, and has recovered all of its post-Ultravolt fall. The 24 September note missed the biggest event of the quarter. On 3–4 September, UltraTech Cement launched Ultravolt: an ₹1,800 cr wires venture aiming for a top-two position within five years, starting with house wires and light-duty cables and offering richer dealer and electrician incentives than incumbents. RR Kabel fell 5–9% that day and about 9% over the following three weeks. The stock has since rallied back, consensus now gives only 6% upside (₹2,885, 15 analysts), and mutual funds were net sellers of 9.8 lakh shares in August. The operating story is intact; the risk-reward is now balanced rather than attractive.

The business is executing well, but at ₹2,722 the price already reflects that, while a well-funded new competitor is entering RR Kabel's home market. Q1 FY27 was the best quarter in the company's history. Revenue rose 54% to ₹3,168 cr, EBITDA nearly doubled, and the long loss-making FMEG arm reached operational breakeven. Wires-and-cables segment margin rose 230 bps to 9.9%, and management guides to 10.5% by FY28. Consensus expects FY27 profit up 48%.

The price is the problem. At 42.4xd FY27 consensus EPS and 34.2xd FY28, the stock is priced for that guidance to be met in full. The multiple is higher than Polycab's (44x trailing on a 14% margin and 33% ROCE) for a business with a 9% margin. The new risk lands where RR Kabel is exposed. Wires are its slower-growing half (+12% volume in Q1 against +25% for cables) and the retail channel is where Ultravolt competes. Analysts estimate the incumbent response, through richer incentives and sharper pricing, could cost 15–75 bps of margin. That is roughly the expansion the FY28 guidance depends on.

Two other things argue against adding here. Cash generation has not kept up with profit: FY26 operating cash flow was ₹295 cr against ₹786 cr of EBITDA, and free cash flow was ₹7 cr, as inventory days rose from 59 to 81. And Q1's ₹205 cr PAT included ₹49 cr of other income, against ₹13 cr a year earlier. Neither is a red flag, but both mean the headline growth flatters the cash. This is a Hold: keep it while the margin trajectory holds, and add on weakness rather than at the top of the range.

Deep dive · added 13 Oct 2026, price ₹2,632

Keep holding; don't add before the Q2 print, and don't sell into it either. The business is in its best shape since listing. Q1 was a record, FMEG broke even, India Ratings upgraded the company to AA on 16 Sep, and the balance sheet can fund the ₹1,200 cr capex plan. The problem is price and position. At 41xd FY27 consensus the stock is the most expensive cable maker on Screener. The new entrant, Ultravolt, is attacking domestic house wire, which is RR Kabel's largest and highest-margin product. Earnings-based scenarios give about +9% in the base case and −18% to −23% in a bear case where Ultravolt takes 75 bps of margin. That balance justifies holding, not adding. The stock is down 3.3% since the 12 Oct note, at ₹2,632 against ₹2,721.50.

2 · Competitive threat and business quality

  • Ultravolt is aimed at RR Kabel's profit pool. On the Q1 call, management put domestic wire margins at 11–12%, against 6–7% for domestic cables, 11–12% for export cables and about 5% for export wires. Domestic house wire is therefore RR Kabel's best business, and it is exactly what UltraTech launched first. Ultravolt brings ₹1,800 cr of investment at Jhagadia, 10.98 lakh km a year of house-wire and light-duty-cable capacity (it claims #2 in wires by capacity), more than 1 lakh retailers, 5,000+ UltraTech Building Solutions outlets and 500 districts at launch. It also offers richer dealer, retailer and electrician incentives than incumbents. Commercial production started 1 Sep, three months ahead of plan.
  • Estimates of the damage differ widely. One estimate is 15–75 bps of operating margin, depending on each company's wire exposure. Nuvama raised its gross-margin hit for incumbents to 1.5–2.5%, from 1.0–1.5%. Kotak had already built in 200–250 bps of wire-margin compression from ad spend and trade terms, aggressive pricing or not. India Ratings expects the impact "could be limited" given demand, but lists it as a monitorable. These figures measure different margins and are unreconciled. RR Kabel is near the top of any wire-exposure range. Wires are roughly 70–73% of W&C sales if the 27% cable share is right (one secondary source, not a filing).
  • Other entrants. Adani Enterprises formed a 50:50 JV with Kutch Copper, Praneetha Ecocables, in March 2025 for wires and cables, but no product launch was found. No JSW wires entry was found. Aditya Birla's entry is Ultravolt itself, under UltraTech. Industry capacity is rising in cables too, a point raised on the Q4 call; management said it sees no excess.
  • Channel. RR Kabel has more than 1.5 lakh retail touch points and adds 20,000–25,000 a year. The dealer and distributor count is being consolidated, and management expects it may fall. North and West make up about 65% of domestic sales, East is weak and South is improving. Ultravolt's 1 lakh retailers at launch is already about two-thirds of RR Kabel's reachd. B2C vs B2B is not disclosed. Management says most cable growth still goes through the distribution channel and that B2B (project, industrial, power and HV cable) is the stated growth priority.
  • Pricing since September. No RR Kabel price-list action after Ultravolt's launch was found. Retail prices move with copper (2–3% triggers), and price hikes were last reported in Dec 2025 and Jan 2026. Management said on 27 Jul that it had seen no special pricing pressure from new entrants. That predates Ultravolt.
  • FMEG drag is shrinking but not gone. FY26 revenue was ₹959 cr (+3%). Losses narrowed from about ₹60 cr (FY24) to ₹46 cr (FY25) and ₹33 cr (FY26, India Ratings). Q1 FY27 broke even, but management said Q2 breakeven "may not be possible" because of seasonality. Targets are annual breakeven in FY27, 20% growth and sustained profit within 2–3 years. About a third is made in-house (fans, switchgear) and premium products are about 25% of revenue. The range includes the Luminous home-electricals business bought in 2022.
  • Capex and returns. Project RRise is ₹1,200 cr over FY26–28, about 80% for cables (more aluminium cable, HV, specialty). Management said about ₹300 cr was spent in FY26 and ₹600–650 cr is planned for FY27. Screener shows ₹263 cr of FY26 investing outflow, while India Ratings says only "around 5%" of the plan was incurred in FY26. The sources disagree, and management's figure fits the cash flow better. The near-term addition at Silvassa is wire capacity, with wire utilisation at only 65–70%: this is not the moment to be adding house-wire tonnes. Sources also differ on Silvassa's size (18,000 t a year in the 12 Oct note, against 12,000 t operational in one report). Consensus FY28 EBITDA of ₹1,379 cr is ₹593 crd above FY26, already beyond management's "2.5x cumulative EBITDA" RRise goal. A 25 Sep ₹77 cr cash purchase of U M Cables' optical-fibre business (a Usha Martin subsidiary) is a small adjacency (0.3% of market capd). It is not the debt-funded deal in the Sell trigger.
  • Balance sheet: comfortable, but tighter than the headline. Screener net debt is ₹246 crd. India Ratings' adjusted net leverage, which includes letter-of-credit acceptances for copper, was 1.78x FY26 EBITDA (1.02x Q1 annualised). Its downgrade trigger is above 1.5x sustained, and it notes management "may take higher long-term debt" for working capital. Another ₹3,000 cr of bank limits was rated on 16 Sep. Income-tax orders of about ₹88 cr are under appeal. The auditor is B S R & Co. (limited review, Oct 2025 outcome filing).
  • Holders: no sponsor overhang. Promoters (Kabra family) held 61.38% at June 2026 with no pledge. TPG exited fully through May 2024 block deals at about ₹1,701. The overhang now is institutional: DIIs fell from 13.62% to 11.87% over the year to June, and funds sold a net 9.8 lakh shares in August. The partial September disclosure (51 schemes reported so far, ICICI Prudential not yet in) shows a further net −2.3 lakh. The September shareholding pattern was not yet on Screener.

Add on either route. Route 1, after the Q2 print: W&C margin ≥ 9.5% and wire volume still growing ≥ 10% and the FY28 10.5% guidance reaffirmed, ideally repeated in Q3. Route 2, on price: a pullback to ₹2,250 or below (35x FY27 EPS, about 28x FY28d) with the margin trend intact.
Trim or exit: Trim by a third to a half if wire volumes fall year on year, if W&C margin drops below 8.5%, or if H1 operating cash flow is negative with term debt drawn. Exit if management cuts the FY28 margin guidance in response to Ultravolt, if FMEG slips back into sustained losses in the same quarter as a margin cut, if India Ratings' adjusted leverage moves above 1.5x through a debt-funded acquisition, or on a close below ₹2,000 (the bear-case floor) after a weak print.

Business

Nature of business

The company has two segments. Wires & cables (house wire, industrial and specialty cable) is about 91% of revenue and almost all of the profit, and 29% of revenue is exported, much of it to the Middle East. FMEG (fans, lighting, switches and appliances, including the home-electricals range bought from Luminous in 2022) is about 9% of revenue, was loss-making for years, and reached operational breakeven in Q1 FY27. Growth is now led by cables: cable volume grew 25% and wire volume 12% in Q1, and cable capacity is running at about 90% utilisation against 65–70% for wires.

Moat

Distribution. In Indian house wire, the electrician and dealer decide the brand at the point of installation, so the network is the barrier and the product is close to a commodity. RR Kabel has built that network over decades. Ultravolt is a direct test of this moat: UltraTech brings a cement-dealer network that already reaches every building site, a balance sheet that can afford years of incentive spending, and a stated top-two ambition. The moat is real but narrow in wires. It is stronger in cables (specification-driven, institutional and export customers), which is where RR Kabel is putting 80% of its capex.

Order book

Not a meaningful concept for this business. Wires and cables are sold through distribution and short-cycle institutional orders, not a disclosed backlog.

Risks

A well-funded entrant in house wires, against a valuation that assumes margin expansion. The FY28 guidance of a 10.5% W&C margin is about 60 bps above Q1's 9.9%. The analysts' estimated cost of defending share against Ultravolt is 15–75 bps. If both play out, the margin expansion that consensus EPS assumes is largely spent defending share, and 42x forward earnings has little room for that. At Prabhudas Lilladher's 35x on FY28 EPS of ₹79.63, the stock would be worth about ₹2,790d, roughly today's price. At 30x, it would be worth ₹2,390d.
  • Copper. Copper is the main input and prices have risen sharply. Cable pricing passes it through with a lag, so a sharp move squeezes margin for a quarter or two. Copper also inflates reported revenue growth: Q1 volume grew 17% against 57% segment revenue growth.
  • Cash conversion. FY26 free cash flow was ₹7 cr on ₹492 cr of profit, with inventory days at 81 against 59. If growth slows while inventory stays high, the working-capital build becomes visible.
  • FMEG durability. One breakeven quarter is a milestone, not proof. Fans are seasonal, and management's FY27 full-year breakeven still has to be delivered.
  • Institutional rotation. Domestic funds were sellers in the June quarter and again in August. FIIs and momentum funds, the current marginal buyers, are the quickest holders to leave.
  • Export concentration. 29% of revenue is exported, much of it to the Middle East; the Q1 recovery there could reverse.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q2 and Q3 FY27 showing W&C EBIT margin at or above 10% and wire volumes still growing after Ultravolt's launch, so that the new entrant is visibly not costing share or margin. Alternatively, a pullback to about ₹2,250 (35x FY27 EPS) with the margin trend intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: W&C margin falling back below 8.5% or EBITDA margin below 7%, management cutting the FY28 margin guidance in response to Ultravolt, FMEG returning to losses, or a debt-funded acquisition.
