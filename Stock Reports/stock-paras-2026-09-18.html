Verdict

A better quarter than the July note assumed, at a price that already expects a lot more. A correction first. The 3 August update gave Q1 FY27 profit as ₹14.3 cr. That was the June 2025 quarter. The real June 2026 quarter was clearly better: revenue ₹128 cr (+38%), operating margin 25%, profit ₹21 cr (+50%). FY26 grew 31% in revenue and 46% in profit. Margins have held at 25–28% for six quarters. The business is doing well.

The worry is cash. Customers took 278 days to pay in FY26 and inventory sat for 244 days. The full cash cycle is 435 days. That has improved from 555 days, but it's still extreme. FY26 operating cash flow was ₹25 cr against ₹122 cr of operating profit, and free cash flow has been about zero or negative for three years. This is normal for Indian defence suppliers, which get paid slowly by government buyers. But it means the reported profit arrives as cash a year or more later.

At 117x trailing earnings and 15x book, the stock is at the top of an expensive sector (Apollo 114x, Zentec 84x, Astra 82x). The only aggregated broker target is ₹820, 40% below today. Institutions did buy in June (FIIs +3.2 points, DIIs +1.8), and the business deserves a premium. Stays Hold, now with the high-risk tag. The stock more than doubled from its low in six months, and a price this high leaves no room for a slip.

Business

What it does

Two arms. Defence & space optics: sights, submarine periscopes (a ₹72 cr DRDO order for two optronic periscopes is due for delivery around now), and space-grade optics for ISRO and satellite makers. This is India's only listed private player in the niche. Defence engineering: electromagnetic-pulse (EMP) protection, defence electronics, heavy engineering, and increasingly counter-drone systems through group companies. Almost all customers are the defence ministry, DRDO labs and public-sector defence companies.

Moat

The strongest in this group. Space-grade optics and EMP protection take years of qualification, are designed into long-life programmes, and have almost no Indian private competition. Periscopes and satellite optics used to be imported. The moat protects margin (25%+). It doesn't protect against slow payment by government customers.

Risks

The price. 117x trailing earnings and 15x book, after a rise from ₹585 to ₹1,527 within a year. Defence small-caps are bought and sold as a group. A sector pullback, or a quarter that disappoints, could cut the price by a third without any change in the business.
  • 435-day cash cycle. Profit turns into cash slowly and unevenly, because government payments come late.
  • Lumpy orders. Revenue depends on defence procurement timing and a few large programmes.
  • Related parties: counter-drone and optics work runs partly through family group companies.
  • Thin, disputed coverage: the only consensus (₹820) sits 40% below the price.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a pullback to about ₹950–1,000 (80–85x trailing, in line with peers) while growth stays above 25%. Or receivable days below 200 with operating cash flow at or above 75% of operating profit.
  • Downgrade to Sell: growth below 20% for two quarters at this multiple, receivable days rising again, or promoters resuming sales.
