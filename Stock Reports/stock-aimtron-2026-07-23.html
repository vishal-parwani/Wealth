Aimtron Electronics Limited

NSE SME: AIMTRON · ~₹2,759–2,890 cr mkt cap · Q1 FY27 revenue +94% · order book ₹604 cr
Hold · High risk

Verdict

The best top-line execution in your SME cluster — held back from an upgrade by one specific gap in the disclosure. Aimtron is an electronics manufacturing services (EMS) player riding India's electronics-localisation wave, and Q1 FY27 was emphatic: revenue ₹84.0 cr, up 94% YoY, against an unexecuted order book of ₹604 cr as at 30 June — roughly 1.8x the annualised Q1 run-rate, which is real forward visibility. Demand is coming from telecom, power and IoT, with domestic customers at ~78% of orders. FY26 revenue had already nearly doubled. On growth alone this would be the pick of the small-caps here.

The reason it stays Speculative rather than moving up: the Q1 release put out revenue and order book but not profit or margin in the sources available to me. A 94% revenue increase tells you nothing about whether EMS margins — historically thin, and vulnerable to component costs and customer mix — came with it. At ~56–60x trailing earnings you are paying a growth multiple, and I'm not willing to upgrade on half the income statement. Get the PAT and margin line, and this becomes a genuine upgrade candidate. The second constraint is structural and unchanged: this is an NSE SME line, with lot-size trading and thin liquidity that punishes exits.

Business

Contract electronics manufacturer — PCB assembly, box-build and related design/engineering services — serving OEMs across telecom infrastructure, power electronics and IoT devices, with a domestic-heavy customer base (~78% of orders). The structural case is India's electronics-manufacturing push: import substitution, PLI-driven localisation and global supply chains diversifying away from China. EMS is a volume business where scale, component sourcing and design-win stickiness determine whether growth converts to profit. AGM held 30 Jul 2026; a promoter investor-meet was held in Surat on 18 Jul.

Bull case

  • Growth is exceptional and order-backed — +94% revenue with a ₹604 cr book behind it is not a one-quarter fluke; it is contracted work.
  • Structural theme: Indian EMS is one of the clearest multi-year manufacturing stories, with policy support and genuine import-substitution economics.
  • Diversified end-markets — telecom, power and IoT reduce reliance on any single vertical (better than the single-programme concentration seen in some defence small-caps in this library).
  • Design-win stickiness: once an EMS partner is qualified into a product's bill of materials, revenue tends to persist for that product's life.

Risks

Growth without a disclosed margin. The single most important missing number is Q1 PAT. EMS margins are structurally thin (often mid-single-digit) and highly sensitive to component prices and customer mix. Revenue +94% at a compressing margin is a very different investment from revenue +94% at a stable one — and at ~56–60x you are paying as though it is the latter.
  • SME liquidity: lot-size trading and thin volumes; exits in a drawdown are genuinely hard, and circuit filters can trap positions.
  • Customer concentration typical at this scale — an OEM programme pause hits disproportionately.
  • Working-capital intensity: EMS ties up cash in inventory and receivables; rapid growth can consume more cash than it generates.
  • Component-cycle exposure: semiconductor and passive-component pricing swings pass straight through to margin.
  • Thin scrutiny: negligible analyst coverage; figures come from company releases and aggregators, not filings I have reviewed.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: disclosure of Q1/H1 FY27 PAT showing margins held or expanded alongside the 94% revenue growth, with operating cash flow tracking profit. On those numbers, ~56x for this growth rate would be defensible.
  • Downgrade to Sell: revenue growth converting into flat or falling profit (the classic EMS trap), receivables/inventory ballooning, or order-book growth stalling.
