Verdict

Sell, down from Hold. The downgrade trigger set on 11 Oct has fired: promoter holding is 55.22% in the September 2026 pattern, below the 59% line. The cause is not creeping dilution. Three promoters sold 7 lakh shares, about ₹84 cr at roughly ₹1,203, in bulk deals on 30 June 2026. That is 7.8%d of their own stake, sold at a price 4.5% above today's, while the stock trades at 40x trailing earnings on revenue that fell in the latest quarter. The previous note described the promoter stake as "stable for a year". That was wrong: the deal settled into the September quarter, and the June-quarter figure of 59.90% did not yet show it.

What changed since 11 Oct 2026. The price fell 6.4%, from ₹1,230.30 to ₹1,151.20 (29 Oct, intraday). Most of the 5–6 October jump on Antique's initiation has gone: the stock slid to ₹1,094 by 22 Oct with no company filing to explain it, then rose 5.2% on 27 Oct, the session after the Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) approvals. The rating moves from Hold to Sell, still high risk. New facts: (1) the September shareholding shows promoters at 55.22%, with DIIs up to 3.62% and FIIs down to 0.78%; (2) Q2 FY27 results are due today. The board meets on 29 Oct for the quarterly results and an interim dividend, and the earnings call is on 30 Oct, so the figures were not available when this note was written; (3) no new defence order has been filed since the ₹20 cr MoD order on 1 Aug; (4) the DAC's ₹2.12 lakh cr Acceptance of Necessity (26 Oct) touches naval platforms only in general terms; (5) still no mutual fund holds it, and Antique is still the only broker covering it.

For a buy/hold decision: don't buy; holders should trim or exit and re-enter on evidence. The bull case is unchanged and real. ROCE is 31%, the balance sheet holds net cash, operating cash flow was ₹86 cr in FY26, and EBITDA margin has risen four quarters in a row to 26.3%. Against that, the promoters, who know the order pipeline best, sold a block at ₹1,203. Meanwhile the filed backlog of ₹117 cr cannot fund the 31% revenue CAGR in Antique's model. At 39.7x TTM, the price already assumes growth resumes. The orderly exit counts for something: a 360 ONE AIF took 4.41 lakh of the 7 lakh shares. It is not a distress sale, but it is a sale, at a higher price than today's.

Today's Q2 print could reverse this quickly. The base is soft. Q2 FY26 revenue was ₹48 cr at a 19% margin, the weakest quarter of FY26, so positive YoY growth is a low bar. What would matter is revenue above about ₹55 cr (+15%), margin held at 24% or more, and a disclosed order book above ₹200 cr. That combination moves the rating back to Hold (see triggers). A second quarter of falling revenue would confirm the Sell.

Business

Nature of business

The core business is special-alloy steel sections used as hull stiffeners in naval warships, along with ballast bricks, welding consumables, armoured-steel profiles and high-altitude heaters. Customers include the Indian Navy, Army, DRDO and the shipyards: Mazagon, Garden Reach, Cochin, Goa, Hindustan Shipyard and L&T. A second division makes dairy equipment at Kalol. RF-over-fibre, composite doors and hatches, AUVs and smart ammunition are early, pre-revenue bets.

Moat

Narrow but real. Naval special steels need metallurgical qualification and long trial cycles. Once a section is qualified on a ship class, the yard rarely switches suppliers. The limit is scale. The addressable market is the Navy's hull programme, and orders arrive in ₹20–50 cr lumps.

₹117 cr unexecuted as at 30 Jun 26, about 0.5x TTM revenued, against ₹103.4 cr at 31 Mar 26 according to the FY26 annual report. The tender pipeline is ₹289 cr, which is bids, not orders. Risks

Insiders sold at ₹1,203 while the multiple prices in growth that the order book does not support. At 40x, the market expects years of 25%+ earnings growth. The filed numbers show revenue down 15% in the latest quarter, a backlog of half a year, and margin already at a record, so there is little room left for margin to keep driving earnings. If Q2 revenue disappoints, nothing in the price cushions it.
  • Further promoter selling. Having sold once at about ₹1,200, the promoters may sell again into any rally.
  • Customer concentration. The MoD and a handful of shipyards account for most of the defence revenue, and award timing is lumpy.
  • Margin reversion. 26.3% is a record. Product mix or one order can move it several points.
  • Retail-driven price. Shareholders went from 4,656 to 28,320 in a year. The stock rose 30% in two sessions on one broker note and then gave most of it back.
  • Upside risk to this call. A strong Q2 on a soft ₹48 cr base, or a large shipbuilding-steel order tied to new naval platforms, could re-rate the stock quickly. Antique's ₹1,725 target is 50% above spot.
