Verdict

Buy — reaffirmed. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹1,777 on 6 Aug 2026; the price has moved 1.6% since and nothing in Q1 FY27 argues against the call. Revenue of ₹5,527.7 cr was up 49% year on year (33% in dollars), PAT up 110% to ₹518.6 cr, EBITDA up 74% with margin at 20.3%. Order intake was $691 mn TCV and the twelve-month executable order book reached $2.23 bn, up 44%.

Read the growth correctly. Encora closed 1 May 2026, so roughly two months of an acquired business sits in this quarter. Organic growth is a fraction of the headline 49%, and the year-on-year comparison stays flattered until Q1 FY28. That is not a reason to discount the result — the order book is up 44% and that is a forward measure — but anyone underwriting 49% as a run-rate is misreading it.

At 41.5x trailing, Coforge is not cheap against Indian large-cap IT. It is growing several times faster than any of them, with AI-led services at 86% of revenue and a book that gives twelve months of visibility. Borrowings fell from ₹1,070 cr to ₹728 cr even while funding an acquisition. The multiple is defensible; it is not a bargain.

Business

Nature. Mid-cap IT services concentrated in three verticals — banking and financial services, insurance, and travel and transportation. The model is offshore-led delivery against multi-year contracts, so the executable order book is a genuine forward indicator rather than a pipeline estimate.

Encora. Closed 1 May 2026 and is the single largest change to the business in years. It adds scale and engineering capability, and it is the reason FY27 growth optics are what they are. Integration risk is the live question: acquisitions of this relative size in IT services have a mixed record on margin retention and key-person attrition.

Peers. Persistent Systems, LTIMindtree, Mphasis in the Indian mid-cap tier; EPAM and Globant internationally. Coforge's growth rate currently leads the domestic set, which is what the premium multiple is paying for.

Moat. Moderate. Vertical depth in insurance and travel creates switching cost and domain pricing power, but IT services is a business where scale and delivery consistency, not proprietary technology, do the defending.

Risks

  • Encora integration. The dominant near-term risk. Margin dilution or attrition would undo the case quickly, and two months of contribution is too little to judge.
  • Comparison inflation. Headline growth stays acquisition-flattered until Q1 FY28. Organic deceleration could be masked for three more quarters.
  • Multiple. 41.5x trailing against large-cap IT at roughly half that. The premium requires growth to persist; the stock fell 6% on a quarter that beat on both lines, which shows how little tolerance is priced in.
  • Client concentration by vertical. BFSI and travel are both cyclical and both sensitive to US discretionary technology budgets.
  • Currency. Rupee revenue growth of 49% against 33% in dollars — a meaningful part of the reported number is translation, and it reverses if the rupee firms.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively — Encora margins holding above 18% through two full quarters, with organic constant-currency growth disclosed separately and above 15%.
  • Downgrade to Hold — order intake falling below $500 mn TCV in a quarter, EBITDA margin slipping under 17%, or evidence of integration attrition.
  • Downgrade to Sell — organic growth turning negative once the acquisition laps, or the multiple exceeding 55x without a matching step in the order book.
