Verdict

The margin recovery is real, but at ₹60.5 you pay 32.5x trailing and about 23x a guided FY27 for a 12% ROCE business. Hold, high risk. FY26 was broken by US tariffs. Operating margin fell from 7.8% to 3.5%, and nearly half of pre-tax profit came from other income. The recovery since then is clean: operating margin went 1.4% → 3.3% → 5.2% → 6.6% over the last four quarters. June-quarter operating profit was ₹34.7 cr, up 129% YoY, and exports recovered to ₹155 cr (29% of sales) after the US Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs. Management targets FY25-level margins (about 8%) by Q4 FY27 and 15–20% revenue growth. A ₹300 cr greenfield EHV plant at Narmadapuram, Madhya Pradesh, is due to start partial production in Q1 FY28.

The counter-argument is the record. The company had a negative net worth in FY15–FY17. Operating cash flow was negative in FY24 (−₹101 cr) and FY26 (−₹38 cr) as receivables doubled to ₹413 cr. The share count has risen about 2.6x in eleven yearsd, through repeated preferential issues (2022 warrants, 2024 and 2026 placements). Another 72 lakh promoter warrants are still to convert. Peers with 20–30% ROCE trade at 40–45x, so Paramount is not expensive for the sector, but it is not a sector leader.

The tweet, tested. A 17 Oct 2026 tweet (@TheAlpha10X) said Sunil Singhania "invested ₹50 cr in Paramount Communications through a preferential allotment at ₹42/share". Confirmed, but it is not new. The board approved the issue on 13 May 2026, shareholders approved it at the 6 Jun EGM (relevant date 7 May, SEBI floor price ₹41.68), and the allotment committee allotted the shares on 1 Jul 2026. The Abakkus vehicles were Abakkus Diversified Alpha Fund (64,28,571 shares, ₹27.00 cr) and Abakkus Diversified Alpha Fund-2 (54,76,190 shares, ₹23.00 cr). That is 1,19,04,761 shares for ₹50.0 cr, 3.64%d of the enlarged equity. The September 2026 shareholding confirms 1.96% + 1.67%. At ₹60.52 the block is worth about ₹72 cr, +44% on entry. Under SEBI ICDR rules (Reg. 167), these non-promoter shares are locked in for six months from the date of exchange trading approval. This note did not find that date, so the lock-in most likely runs to around January 2027. The tweet probably reflects the September shareholding filing, not a fresh purchase. Abakkus is engaged: its analyst asked about export margins on the August call. A ₹50 cr bet that is already up 44% says the entry price was right. It does not by itself make ₹60 a good price.

Business

Nature of business

Paramount makes power (LT/HT, control, instrumentation, UL-certified), telecom (optical fibre, jelly-filled), railway signalling and axle-counter cables, plus house wires, solar and fire-survival cables. It also does some EPC work (HTLS, OPGW, submarine cable repair). Plants at Dharuhera (Haryana) and Khushkhera (Rajasthan) run near full utilisation, with 29,664 t of metal throughput in FY26. FY26 revenue split: US exports ₹550 cr, Indian institutional and government ₹1,183 cr, and retail ₹179 cr. The company says it was the largest Indian exporter of LV cable to the US in CY2025. It pivots between exports and domestic sales as US demand swings: exports were 50% of revenue in FY23, 26% in FY24, 43% in H1 FY26 and 16% in H2 FY26.

Moat

Narrow. It has real assets: UL and other approvals plus eight US distributors with "zero rejections over six years" (company claim), railway and BSNL approvals built over decades, and 950+ institutional customers. But cables are largely a copper and aluminium pass-through business with many competitors, and the much larger players are expanding capacity fast.

Order book

₹615 cr at 30 Jun 2026 (₹583 cr at 31 Mar): ₹518 cr domestic, ₹97 cr export, and ₹455 cr of it power cables. That is under four months of revenued. The company does not take fixed-price orders beyond three months because of metal-price risk, so a small order book is by design and gives little long-term visibility. Narmadapuram (31 acres, ₹300 cr, EHV cables up to 132 kV) targets ₹500 cr of revenue in FY28 and ₹1,200 cr in FY29. 132 kV approvals come only after production starts.

Risks

  • US trade policy. FY26 showed what a tariff shock does: export EBITDA turned negative. The IEEPA ruling helped, but Section 232 or other duties could return. Customers now bear tariff changes, which shifts the risk to demand instead of margin.
  • Working capital and cash. Receivables of ₹413 cr are about half of net worth. Operating cash flow was negative in two of the last three years, and Narmadapuram capex plus a planned term loan will raise debt to as much as 0.3x equity.
  • Equity dilution habit. There have been three preferential rounds since 2022, and 72 lakh warrants are still to convert. Equity is the company's usual source of growth capital.
  • History. Losses and negative net worth in FY15–FY17. The franchise has survived a near-failure, but it is not a long, clean record.
  • Placement supply. 2.2 cr preferential shares (6.7% of equity), with several trading-oriented holders among them, become free to sell when the six-month lock-in ends, probably around January 2027.
  • Execution on Narmadapuram. Cable machinery lead times are long. A slip past Q1 FY28 would push the ₹500 cr FY28 revenue target.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q2 and Q3 FY27 operating margins of 7% or more with other income under ₹5 cr a quarter, H1 operating cash flow positive and receivables falling. Or a price near ₹45–48, about 18x FY27 earnings at the guided midpoint.
  • Downgrade to Sell: operating margin falling back below 4% (new US duties, or copper squeezing fixed-price orders), another preferential issue below market, or Abakkus and the other ₹42 allottees selling heavily once the lock-in ends.
