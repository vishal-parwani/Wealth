Sudarshan Chemical Industries Limited

NSE: SUDARSCHEM · ~₹8,836 cr mkt cap · World's largest pigment producer post-Heubach
Buy

Verdict

The right conclusion for entirely the wrong reason. The pitch circulating on this stock is "market cap / sales below 1, cheapest in its history, below COVID lows." That is arithmetically true — ₹8,836 cr against ₹9,787 cr of FY26 revenue is 0.90x — and it is analytically worthless. The ratio collapsed because the denominator tripled: Sudarshan bought Heubach's global pigment business out of insolvency in March 2025 and consolidated ₹6,400 cr of low-margin revenue. The multiple didn't de-rate; the sales base exploded. Buying a stock because it consolidated a distressed acquisition is not value investing.

But there is a real thesis here, and it is better than the one being marketed. Sudarshan is now the world's largest pigment producer, and FY26 EBITDA margin was 5.9% against the ~11–13% the standalone Indian business used to earn. On a ₹9,787 cr revenue base, every 100 bps of margin recovery is ~₹98 cr of EBITDA. That is the actual option you are buying. And Q1 FY27 (reported 12 Aug) is the first hard evidence it is being exercised: consolidated PAT ₹103.4 cr, +42% YoY, against a company that was loss-making through most of FY26. Net debt is falling (₹934 cr → ₹755 cr). Accumulate in tranches — but size it as a turnaround, because that is what it is.

Do not use market-cap/sales on this company. It is the single most misleading ratio you could pick here. A business that triples revenue by absorbing an insolvent competitor at ~6% margin will always show a collapsing MCap/Sales, and it tells you nothing about whether the equity is cheap. The honest lens: annualise Q1 FY27 PAT (₹103.4 cr × 4 ≈ ₹414 cr) and you get ~21x — reasonable for a global #1 mid-cycle, not "cheapest in history."

What actually happened

Sudarshan acquired the global pigment operations of Germany's Heubach Group through subsidiary Sudarshan Europe B.V. for €139.6 m (~₹1,277 cr), completing March 2025. Heubach was in insolvency at the time. The acquisition made Sudarshan the largest pigment producer in the world and roughly tripled revenue — but it imported an unrestructured cost base, high overheads and operational inefficiency. The integration programme unifies three legacy businesses (Sudarshan, Heubach, and Clariant's former pigment unit) into a single entity branded "ONE Sudarshan."

FY26 was the cost of that decision. Q2 FY26 profit fell 61% with operating margin at 5.54% (from 13.57%). Q3 FY26 posted a net loss of ₹115.3 cr despite sales up 216%. Heubach's FY26 EBITDA guidance was cut from €25 m to ~€16 m. The nine months to Dec 2025 produced a net loss of ~₹42 cr.

The inflection

  • Q4 FY26: Business EBITDA ₹118 cr, helped by "value capture" initiatives and the end of customer destocking. Net debt down to ₹755 cr from ₹934 cr in December.
  • Q1 FY27 (12 Aug 2026): revenue ₹2,642.1 cr (+5.4%), consolidated PAT ₹103.4 cr (+42%); profit attributable to owners ₹97.3 cr against ₹47.2 cr. Loss-making to solidly profitable in two quarters.
  • Structure simplification: board gave in-principle approval (12 Aug) to move the 70.26% stake in Sudarshan Colorants India from indirect subsidiary holding to direct parent ownership — a tidy-up that usually precedes cleaner consolidated reporting.
  • Street: consensus target ₹1,112–1,257 with 15–20% FY27 PAT growth modelled.

Bull case

  • Margin optionality is the whole trade. 5.9% today vs 11–13% historically on a base three times larger. Getting even halfway back is transformative in absolute rupees.
  • Global #1 scale in a consolidated industry — pigments is a business where scale drives purchasing power and customer qualification, and there is now no larger player.
  • Bought distressed. €139.6 m for a global franchise is a low entry price; the value was always going to come from restructuring, not from day-one earnings.
  • Deleveraging underway and a dividend maintained (₹5) through a loss-making year.

Risks

One good quarter is not a turnaround. Q1 FY27 is a single print following a year that included a ₹115 cr quarterly loss and a guidance cut. Integrations of insolvent European assets routinely take three to five years and rarely proceed in a straight line. Management has already missed its own Heubach EBITDA number once (€25m → €16m), which is the most important single fact in this report about how much to trust the guidance.
  • Revenue growth is anaemic ex-acquisition — Q1 FY27 was +5.4%. Strip the acquisition out and this is a low-growth business; the entire equity story rests on margin, not volume.
  • European restructuring risk — labour costs, plant rationalisation and works-council negotiations in Germany are slow and expensive.
  • Little upside to consensus — at ₹1,110 the stock is already at the bottom of the ₹1,112–1,257 target band. The street is not underwriting a big re-rating.
  • Cyclical end-markets — pigments feed coatings, plastics and inks; the FY26 destocking cycle showed how quickly demand pauses.
  • Debt still meaningful at ₹755 cr net against depressed earnings, though the trend is right.

What would change the rating

  • Add more aggressively: two more quarters at or above Q1 FY27's run-rate with consolidated EBITDA margin through 8%, or Heubach hitting a raised (not cut) EBITDA guidance.
  • Downgrade to Hold: any further guidance cut, a return to quarterly losses, or margin stalling around 6% into FY28 — at which point the "recovery to 11%" premise needs rewriting.
