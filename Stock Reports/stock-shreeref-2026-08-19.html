Business

Nature. Designs and manufactures HVAC and refrigeration systems for defence and marine applications — naval vessel air-conditioning, chilled water plants, cooling for shipboard systems — plus industrial refrigeration. Customers are Mazagon Dock, the Indian Navy directly, and international primes such as Kongsberg.

Moat. Stronger than the size suggests. A reported 64% share of Indian defence HVAC reflects qualification cycles measured in years and vessel-class-specific design approval. Once specified into a ship class, the supplier tends to stay for the life of the programme and the associated spares. The ₹2.41 cr Navy spares order is an example of that annuity.

Peers. No close listed comparable in the defence HVAC niche. Broader marine and defence engineering names — Bharat Electronics, Mazagon Dock as a customer rather than a peer, and unlisted HVAC specialists — set the competitive backdrop.

Capacity. The new Karad facility adds 50,000 sq ft, which is a meaningful addition for a company with ₹153 cr of revenue and signals confidence in order conversion.

Risks

  • Half-yearly reporting. The structural problem with this name. Financial data is stale for months at a stretch, and at 55.4x that is a lot of trust to extend.
  • Stale ownership data. Shareholding 4½ months old, mutual fund data 12 months old. Institutional positioning is effectively unobservable.
  • Extreme H1/H2 skew. H1 FY26 produced ₹2 cr of PAT against ₹20 cr in H2. If H2 FY27 disappoints there is no interim signal to see it coming.
  • Single customer group. Mazagon Dock and the Navy dominate. Defence procurement timing is outside the company's control.
  • Size and float. ₹1,193 cr market cap with 4,461 shareholders — thin liquidity and wide moves on modest volume.
  • ROCE 13.8% is modest for a business with 64% niche share, suggesting the share does not yet translate into pricing power.
