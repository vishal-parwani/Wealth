Verdict

A very good business that has doubled in five weeks. The business deserves the attention; the price has taken all of it and more. Milky Mist makes paneer, curd, cheese, yoghurt and ice cream, mostly under its own brand, from a large plant at Perundurai in Tamil Nadu. Unlike most Indian dairies it sells value-added products rather than liquid milk, which is why it earns a 15% operating margin against 5–11% for Dodla, Heritage and Hatsun. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹973 cr, up 44%, with EBITDA up 75% and margin up 2.6 points to 14.9%. Paneer revenue grew 34%, cheese 38%, curd 27%.

The share price is the problem. The IPO was priced at ₹140 on 11–13 August, listed at ₹165 on 18 August, and the stock is ₹309 — 121% above the issue price in five weeks. On reported trailing earnings that's 129x. Those earnings carry an unusually low tax charge (20% in FY26, 12% in Q1, and a negative rate in the March quarter from a deferred-tax credit). At a normal 25% rate, trailing profit is about ₹166 cr and the stock is on roughly 143xd. Even on a generous FY27 estimate — 40% revenue growth, 15% margin, lower interest after debt repayment — it's about 83xd, against Hatsun, the closest listed comparison, at 73x with far slower growth.

The balance sheet also needs watching. Before the IPO, debt was ₹1,672 cr against ₹463 cr of equity, 3.6x, built up by years of capex. Free cash flow has been negative every year (−₹152 cr, −₹233 cr, −₹163 cr). The IPO raised ₹1,428 cr of new money, of which ₹497 cr repays debt and ₹469 cr goes into more capacity at Perundurai. That fixes the leverage but not the pattern: this business consumes cash to grow. Hold, high risk. Worth owning at a sensible price; ₹220 would be about 59x FY27, which for 40% growth is defensible.

Business

What it does

Founded as a dairy in Tamil Nadu and incorporated in its present form in 2014, Milky Mist buys milk from farmers and turns it into value-added products: paneer (its biggest line and the one it is best known for), curd, cheese, yoghurt, ice cream, butter, ghee and dairy whiteners. Most Indian dairies sell mainly liquid milk, which earns thin margins; Milky Mist's mix is why it earns 15%. Distribution runs through general trade, modern retail and quick commerce, supported by company-owned coolers placed in shops — the reason ₹155 cr of the IPO goes into more of them. Manufacturing is concentrated at Perundurai, Erode district.

Milky Mist has the best margin in Indian dairy and is priced accordingly — nearly twice Hatsun's multiple and five times Dodla's. Its 31.9% return on equity looks spectacular, but that is partly leverage: it earned that return on a small equity base with ₹1,677 cr of debt. Return on capital (13.7%) is the fairer measure, and on that it is merely in line with peers.

Moat

A real consumer brand in a category that is hard to build in. Paneer and curd are fresh products: they need chilled distribution, daily replenishment and a name shoppers trust. The coolers in shops are physical lock-in that a new competitor has to match store by store. Against that, Amul is the dominant national brand with far bigger scale, and private-label dairy in modern retail and quick commerce keeps prices honest.

Order book

Not applicable to a consumer company. The forward commitments are the Perundurai expansion and the cooler rollout funded by the IPO.

Risks

The price doubled in five weeks on one quarter of listed results. At 129x reported earnings — or about 143x after normal tax — there is no room for a soft quarter. Milk procurement costs swing with the season, and a bad summer or a jump in milk prices hits margin directly.
  • Tax normalising: the last two quarters were taxed at −40% and 12%. At 25%, reported profit falls about a quarter.
  • Cash-hungry growth: free cash flow negative for three years. The IPO fixes the balance sheet once; the next expansion needs the business to fund itself.
  • Small float and lock-in expiries: 20% free float, anchor lock-ins passing now and in November.
  • Concentration: manufacturing largely at one site, and the south is the core market.
  • Amul and private label cap pricing in paneer and curd over time.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a price near ₹220 (about 59x our FY27 estimate). Or two quarters at 15%+ margin with revenue growth above 35% and positive free cash flow, which would show growth no longer needs new money.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin back below 12% on milk-cost inflation, revenue growth under 25%, or fresh borrowing for capex within a year of the IPO.
