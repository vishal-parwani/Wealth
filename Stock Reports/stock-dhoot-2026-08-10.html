Verdict

A genuinely good franchise being sold at a price that already pays for the next three years — by a private-equity owner who is keeping 43% of it. Dhoot is the dominant Indian 2W/3W wiring-harness maker: 41% share by value of the domestic two- and three-wheeler harness market in FY26, and roughly 70% of the electric 2W/3W harness market. That is a real moat, and the EV mix shift is a genuine content-per-vehicle tailwind. But three things stop this from being a buy-and-hold at ₹871. First, the growth is being bought, not generated — FY26 revenue rose 31% while PAT rose only 12%, with EBITDA margin down 17.15%→15.71% and PAT margin 10.19%→8.70%. Second, 44.9x trailing earnings leaves no room for that margin trend to continue. Third, Bain Capital sells down 55%→42.8% and stays — a ~₹7,600 cr overhang that has to clear the market eventually. The ~29% grey-market premium and a 61%-mutual-fund anchor book say the listing pop is likely. The business at this price is a different question, and the answer is: wait.

The business

Founded in Aurangabad in 1999 by Rahul Radhavallabh Dhoot, now headquartered in Pune. It designs and manufactures automotive wiring harnesses and adjacent electrical & electronics content — junction boxes, cables, cord sets, charging connectors, off-board chargers, sensors and lithium battery assemblies. The Bajaj Auto relationship started in 2003 and took the company from ₹1 cr to ₹100 cr of turnover in three years; Bajaj remains the anchor customer and the anchor risk.

Footprint is ~20–22 manufacturing and engineering facilities across India, the UK, Slovakia, Thailand, South Korea and Japan, serving OEMs in 8 countries. The international layer was built by acquisition — TFC Slovakia (as a step-down subsidiary of Dhoot UK) and PHTL in the UK for £1.9 m. Management has stated a ₹5,000 cr revenue target over five years and ~₹500 cr of investment over three years (₹250 cr earmarked for acquisitions, the rest greenfield).

Why "first-generation" matters here. Rahul Dhoot is not part of the Videocon Dhoot family. Same Aurangabad lineage, different branch — he spent a month at his father's auto-dealership business in 1994 and then left to start this. The Videocon/ICICI association that surfaces in searches is not this company. Worth stating plainly because the surname will otherwise cost the stock a governance discount it does not deserve.

The single most important line in this report. Revenue two-year CAGR is ~27%. PAT two-year CAGR is ~15%. Profit is compounding at barely half the rate of sales, and the gap widened in FY26 rather than narrowing. Some commentary circulating on this IPO quotes a "PAT CAGR of 32.84%" — that figure is the cumulative two-year increase (298.8 → 396.8) mislabelled as a CAGR. Do not let it into your thinking. At 44.9x you are paying a growth multiple for a company whose earnings growth is decelerating.

The likely mechanics: acquisition-led revenue (Slovakia, UK) consolidating at structurally lower margin than the Indian 2W business, plus finance costs on the debt used to fund it. The fresh issue fixes the second half of that — ₹766 cr of borrowings retired should add roughly ₹50 cr of post-tax profit, taking pro-forma EPS to ~₹22 and the multiple to ~40x. That is a one-time step-up, not growth, and it still is not cheap.

Where the money goes

  • ₹493.9 cr — repay/prepay the company's own borrowings
  • ₹272.6 cr — invested into subsidiaries to repay their borrowings
  • ₹150 cr — two new harness plants at Jhajjar (Haryana) and Hosur (Tamil Nadu), adding ~23% capacity
  • Up to ~₹350 cr — unidentified acquisitions plus general corporate purposes (capped at 25% individually, 35% combined)

So roughly 55% of the fresh money is deleveraging, only ~11% is growth capex, and a further quarter is a blank cheque for M&A that has not been named. For a company whose margin decline is plausibly acquisition-driven, handing it another ₹350 cr of undisclosed acquisition budget is not an obviously shareholder-friendly use of proceeds.

The bull case

  • Genuine category leadership — 41% value share in domestic 2W/3W harnesses is not a contestable position over any short horizon; harness design is embedded per-platform and switching costs are high.
  • EV is content-accretive, and Dhoot already owns it — ~70% share of the electric 2W/3W harness market. An EV needs more, higher-spec electrical content (HV cabling, charging connectors, battery assemblies) than an ICE two-wheeler. This is the one part of the story worth paying up for.
  • Growth well ahead of the sector — ~27% revenue CAGR against 12–15% for the legacy auto-ancillary market.
  • Balance sheet repaired — D/E 0.78x → 0.35x, and the fresh issue takes it lower still.
  • Serious anchor book — ₹918 cr from 72 funds, 61.3% domestic mutual funds across 8 houses / 46 schemes. ICICI Pru Smallcap (7.81%), SBI Multicap (5.99%), Mirae Focused (4.90%), ADIA (3.81%), BlackRock India Fund (3.48%). This is real institutional money, not a papered-over book.

Risks

Customer concentration is severe, and it is one customer. Bajaj Auto alone was 32.3% of revenue in 9M to Dec 2025. Top five ≈ 72%. Top ten >80%. There are no long-term volume commitments from any OEM. A Bajaj market-share loss, an in-sourcing decision, or a single pricing renegotiation transmits straight to the P&L — and at 45x, a guidance miss re-rates violently.
  • Single-site capacity risk — over half of installed wiring-harness capacity sits at one location. Fire, flood or labour action there is a company-level event, not a plant-level one.
  • Margin trend is the wrong direction and has been for two years running. Nothing in the disclosure guarantees it inflects.
  • Bain's residual 42.8% is a multi-year supply overhang.
  • Undisclosed M&A — up to ~₹350 cr of proceeds for acquisitions not yet identified, from a management team whose last acquisitions coincided with margin dilution.
  • Fully priced — even the brokers recommending it concede the issue leaves little on the table. The commonly-quoted "44.9x vs peer average 56.8x" comparison flatters: I could not verify the peer set's composition, and Indian listed auto-ancillary comparables span a very wide multiple range depending on which names are included.
  • 2W cyclicality — this is a leveraged bet on Indian two-wheeler volumes, a notoriously cyclical end-market.

How I'd play it

  • Apply at ₹871 (cut-off) for the listing. 29% GMP, a 61%-MF anchor book and a well-known franchise make a positive listing the base case. Size it as a lottery ticket — retail allotment in an oversubscribed book will likely be one lot anyway.
  • Do not treat allotment as a position. If it lists near ₹1,120 that is ~58x trailing FY26 earnings for a business growing profits at 12%. My default there is to sell into the listing.
  • Where it becomes interesting as a hold: ₹700–780 (~36–40x, or ~32–36x on post-deleveraging EPS) and at least two quarters of evidence the EBITDA margin has stopped falling. Post-listing corrections in high-GMP IPOs frequently deliver exactly that entry.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: EBITDA margin stabilising at/above ~16% for two consecutive quarters with PAT growth reconverging toward revenue growth; or Bajaj dependence falling below ~25% via genuine customer diversification.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: a large unidentified acquisition announced at a dilutive multiple; any Bajaj volume/share loss; or margin dropping below ~15%.
