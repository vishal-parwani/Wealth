Verdict

Still a Hold. Q2 settled neither question the 30 October report asked. The board approved the September-quarter results on 12 November. The meeting ran from 15:21 to 15:52 IST, after the market had closed. Revenue was ₹2,997 cr, up 63.5% year on year and 20% on Q1, and 10% above the ₹2,724 cr consensus. Orders rose 86% to ₹4,121 cr, and the backlog reached a record ₹33,335 cr. Earnings did not keep up. Reported EBITDA was ₹406 crd, a 13.6% margin, against the 16.4% consensus and 14.6% in Q1. Profit was ₹318.5 cr, up 20.5% but 11% below the ₹357 cr estimate.

The margin depends on which number you use. The company reports an "operational EBITDA" of ₹494 cr, a 16.5% margin, up from 16.0% in Q1 and 15.9% a year ago. That measure leaves out an ₹88.1 cr notional loss on foreign-exchange and commodity derivatives and the revaluation of open orders. Trendlyne's consensus is on the reported basis: its Q1 "actual" is ₹363.5 cr, matching the filed accounts. On a like-for-like basis Q2 is a miss. The underlying cost pattern is also mixed. Material costs rose to 61.7% of revenue, from 57.5% a year ago and 59.4% in Q1, which the CFO put down to product mix. Operating leverage on staff and overheads offset most of that, but not all.

Valuation is unchanged and still demanding. At ₹28,545 the stock trades at 106x trailing earnings, 82x consensus FY27 and 55x FY28. Those estimates are dated before the result and have not yet been revised. The H1 miss makes FY27 harder to reach. To hit the ₹346.94 consensus EPS, the second half has to earn ₹209.5 a shared, 58% more than H2 FY26 and 52% more than this H1. A cut to FY27 would push the forward multiple higher at the same price.

Trigger test (from the 30 October report). Upgrade to Buy required a Q2 operating margin of at least 17% and a backlog of at least ₹32,000 cr, or a price of about ₹25,800. The backlog leg passes at ₹33,335 cr. The margin leg fails on every measure: 13.6% reported and 16.5% on the company's own adjusted figure. The price leg also fails, with the stock 10.6% above ₹25,806 (50x FY28). No upgrade. Downgrade to Sell required a multiple above 80x FY27 together with a backlog that falls for two quarters running, or an operating margin of 12% or below. The multiple leg is met at 82.3x. The backlog rose for a second quarter, from ₹32,222 to ₹33,335 cr, and the margin is 13.6% reported. No downgrade. The rating stays at Hold on the evidence, not by default.

Counter-argument. On a cash and order view, this was a good quarter. Ex-HVDC orders were up 86% year on year and 22% on Q1. Exports rose 41%, service orders 77%, and data-centre orders were the second-largest segment after transmission. The HVDC megaprojects, Bhadla–Fatehpur and Khavda, have only begun to be recognised as revenue, at about 10% and 4–5% complete according to the call. Management calls the MTM loss notional: it comes from revaluing open orders and hedges at the period end. If it unwinds, H2 margins look like Q2's 16.5% rather than 13.6%. Against that, the loss has now appeared in two consecutive quarters, ₹60 cr and ₹56 cr through the P&L, so it is hard to treat as one-off. Operating cash flow for the half was only ₹91 cr, against ₹874 cr a year ago, as HVDC working capital built up.

Reported EBITDA is PBT plus depreciation and finance cost, less other income, taken from the filed statement. For Q2 that is 430.40 + 30.16 + 2.59 − 56.83 = ₹406.3 cr, which matches Screener's ₹406 cr. Adding back the ₹88.1 cr notional derivative and foreign-exchange loss that the company excludes gives its ₹494.4 cr. Consensus figures are Trendlyne's pre-result means: revenue from 12 analysts, EBITDA from about 10, profit from 9, and EPS from 4. Business Upturn quoted a slightly different "market estimate" of ₹2,740 cr revenue, ₹448 cr EBITDA and ₹359 cr profit, which points to the same result. What moved the margin: materials rose to 61.7% of revenue from 57.5%, the line for exchange and commodity swung from a ₹12.8 cr gain to a ₹56.1 cr loss, and other income fell to ₹56.8 cr from ₹82.6 cr. Offsetting those, staff costs fell to 6.2% of revenue from 8.5%, and other expenses to 16.6% from 17.7%. PBT margin fell from 19.3% to 14.4%. The tax rate was 26%.

Business

Nature of business

The company makes and installs transmission equipment for utilities, industry and railways. Products include power and traction transformers, HVDC converter stations, switchgear and GIS substations, and power-quality equipment such as STATCOMs, including India's first grid-forming STATCOM, at Fatehgarh III. It also sells grid automation and services, and from this year battery storage. Customers include Power Grid and the state transmission companies, TBCB developers and their EPC contractors, renewable developers, railways and metros, and data-centre operators. Export customers are reached through the parent group. HVDC links bring the largest and lumpiest orders and are recognised as revenue over several years. Data centres were the second-largest order segment in Q2. The company does not report data-centre revenue separately.

Moat

Real, and among the strongest in Indian capital goods. Only a handful of companies worldwide hold HVDC converter technology, and Hitachi Energy is the largest. Q2 showed the moat beyond HVDC as well. Non-HVDC orders rose 86% year on year, and the company won cooling-system and GIS scope on the Khavda South–Olpad HVDC project, which shows its position in the supply chain. Qualification with Power Grid and the transmission companies is slow and depends on track record, and the installed base drives service work, up 77% in Q2. The limits showed in the quarter too. Pricing power is weak in tendered utility contracts, and material costs moved against the company faster than it could pass them on.

Risks

Margin is now the main risk, and Q2 confirmed it. Reported operating margin fell for a third straight quarter, to 13.6%. Consensus FY27 needs about 18.8%d in H2 on the same basis. If brokers cut FY27 to reflect H1, the forward multiple rises above today's 82x. At 55x FY28 the price still assumes the group keeps its premium. At 50x FY28 the stock would be about ₹25,800, 10% below today; at 40x it would be ₹20,600.
  • Material costs and fixed-price contracts. Materials were 61.7% of revenue, up 420 bps year on year. Brokers had warned about commodity costs on fixed-price HVDC and transformer contracts, and Q2 is the first quarter where that showed in the numbers.
  • Hedge and MTM volatility. The exchange and commodity line cost ₹60 cr in Q1 and ₹56 cr in Q2, after a gain a year ago. The company's "operational EBITDA" excludes the notional part, but reported profit carries it.
  • Working capital and cash. H1 operating cash flow fell to ₹91 cr as HVDC inventories and contract assets built up. Net cash is still ₹4,389 cr, but free cash flow was negative in H1, at −₹278 crd.
  • HVDC lumpiness. H1 orders fell 32% because last year included a large HVDC award. The next awards depend on tender timing.
  • Treasury income. Other income fell to ₹57 cr from ₹83 cr a year ago, and it shrinks further as capex uses up the QIP cash.
  • Parent-subsidiary flows and index flows. Related-party contracts were approved on 12 October. Next 50 inclusion brings passive demand, and forced selling if the stock drops out.

One more quarter of falling margin would bring the second condition close. A cut to FY27 consensus that pushes the multiple above 90x without a margin recovery would also count.
