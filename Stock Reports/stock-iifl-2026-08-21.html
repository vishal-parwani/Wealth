Verdict

Buy — unchanged, but the margin of safety is thinner than at the last note. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹627.50 on 13 Aug 2026, describing it as "a regulatory-crisis recovery that is now most of the way done, still priced like it isn't". The stock is ₹679.80, up 8.4%, and it rose 6.77% today alone. It now sits ₹7 below its 52-week high and above the ~₹625 average analyst target the last note cited.

The recovery is no longer in doubt. Quarterly PAT has risen five times in a row: ₹274 cr, ₹418 cr, ₹501 cr, ₹623 cr, ₹713 cr. Q1 FY27 profit was up 160% year on year and 14% sequentially, AUM reached ₹1,15,523 cr (+38%), ROE hit 19.5% against 13.1% for FY26 as a whole, and ROA was 3.10%. FY25's ₹578 cr profit, gutted by the RBI's gold-loan embargo, is now two years behind.

The valuation still works. 13.8x trailing, about 10.7x on annualised Q1 earnings, and 2.1x book. A lender earning 19.5% ROE and targeting ~25% for FY27 at 2.1x book is not expensive. That is the whole case.

Three things temper it. Management guides FY27 AUM growth of 16–20%, roughly half the 38% just delivered — the base effect is ending, by their own account. Asset quality ticked the wrong way, GNPA from 1.46% to 1.55% and NNPA from 0.73% to 0.82%. And foreign holders cut from 28.16% to 23.86% in the June quarter. The easy re-rating has happened; from here it is an earnings story, not a recovery story.

The FII cut is the one thing to weigh against the results. Foreign holding had been climbing steadily — 26.62% to 28.16% over three quarters — and then dropped 4.30 points in the June quarter, the same quarter the company reported 19.5% ROE. Retail absorbed it: public holding rose from 39.92% to 43.80%. Foreign investors remain the largest non-promoter block at 23.86%, so this is a trim rather than an exit, but it is a change of direction worth watching in the September filing.

Promoter holding is 24.84% and has not moved — unusually low for an Indian NBFC, and unchanged to two decimal places across five quarters. With no controlling shareholder, this company is governed more like a widely-held institution than a promoter business, which cuts both ways.

Business

Nature. IIFL Finance is a diversified retail NBFC lending against gold, and into home loans, microfinance, loans against property and digital/unsecured products, with ₹1,15,523 cr of AUM.

The gold-loan concentration is now the defining fact. Gold loans grew 114% year on year to ₹58,406 cr — roughly 51% of total AUM. That is the same product the RBI banned the company from writing in March 2024, an embargo lifted in September 2024. The recovery in earnings is largely the recovery in this one business, and the company is now more concentrated in it than before the ban.

Why gold lending is attractive and why regulators watch it. It is short-tenure, fully collateralised and high-yield, so it produces the ROA that drives the 19.5% ROE. It is also where conduct and valuation practices attract scrutiny — which is precisely what happened in 2024.

Asset quality. GNPA 1.55% and NNPA 0.82% are good absolute numbers for a retail NBFC. Both moved the wrong way this quarter, and doing so while growing AUM 38% is worth monitoring — fast growth can flatter ratios by expanding the denominator, so a rise despite that is a genuine, if small, signal.

Risks

  • Regulatory concentration. Half the loan book is gold loans — the exact business the RBI shut down in 2024. A repeat would be more damaging now than it was then.
  • Growth is normalising by design. FY27 AUM guidance of 16–20% against 38% delivered. The base effect that produced the headline numbers is ending.
  • Asset quality turned. GNPA 1.46% to 1.55%, NNPA 0.73% to 0.82%, while growing fast. Small, but the first deterioration of the recovery.
  • FII selling. 4.30 points in one quarter, reversing three quarters of buying.
  • Price near the high. ₹679.80 against a ₹687 high, and above the ~₹625 average target the previous note cited. The valuation gap that made this obvious has largely closed.
  • Leverage is inherent. ₹69,176 cr of borrowings against ₹13,835 cr of reserves. That is normal for a lender and it is also why credit costs of 1.5–1.7% matter so much.
  • Peak profit not yet regained. FY24 PAT was ₹1,974 cr; FY26 was ₹1,817 cr.

What would change the rating

  • Stays a Buy — 2.1x book for a lender running 19.5% ROE and targeting 25%, with five quarters of consistent improvement behind it.
  • Raise conviction — FY27 ROE tracking towards the 25% target with GNPA back under 1.5%, and FII holding stabilising.
  • Downgrade to Hold — GNPA above 2%, ROE falling back under 15%, or AUM growth undershooting the 16–20% guidance.
  • Downgrade to Sell — any fresh regulatory action on the gold-loan book, or a credit-cost spike beyond the guided 1.7%.
