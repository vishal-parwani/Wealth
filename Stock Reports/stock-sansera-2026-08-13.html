Sansera Engineering Limited

NSE: SANSERA · ~₹24,111 cr mkt cap · +204% in one year · 2.2% off its all-time high
Hold

Verdict

A genuinely good business undergoing a genuinely real transformation, at a price that has already paid for both — twice over. Sansera makes precision forged and machined components: connecting rods, rocker arms, crankshafts, gear shifter forks. Dull, capital-intensive, cyclical work it does well. What re-rated the stock is the ADS division — Aerospace, Defence and Semiconductor — which grew revenue 155% to ₹315 cr in FY26, carries roughly ₹3,870 cr of cumulative lifetime orders, and is guided to ₹550–600 cr in FY27. Sansera is a Tier-1 supplier to Boeing and Tier-2 to Airbus, the ADS facility is nearing full utilisation, and management expects those margins to reach 25–30% against a blended 19%. None of that is hype. It is a real, high-barrier business being built inside an auto-component company.

The problem is arithmetic. The stock is up 204% in a year to ₹3,851.50, sits 2.2% below its all-time high, and trades at 74x trailing earnings. ADS is about 9% of revenue. You are being asked to pay a 74x multiple on the entire company for a division that is one-eleventh of it. And the sharpest fact in this report: Antique's target price is ₹2,520 — roughly 35% below the current price — built on 35x FY28 estimated earnings. That is not a bear taking a swipe; it is a valuation two years forward, and it still lands a third lower than where the stock trades today. Hold if you own it and let the ADS ramp play out. Buying here requires believing the market is right and the analyst modelling FY28 is wrong by 35%.

Do the FY28 arithmetic yourself. Antique's ₹2,520 at 35x FY28E implies FY28 EPS of roughly ₹72, or about ₹449 cr of net profit — up from a trailing base near ₹324 cr. That is a credible, healthy growth path. But at today's ₹3,851.50 you are paying ~53x those same FY28 earnings, two years early. For that to work out, ADS has to beat its own guidance materially and the market has to keep awarding an aerospace multiple to a company that still earns ~73% of revenue from internal-combustion parts.

The quarter, read carefully

Q1 FY27 (approved 12 Aug) was a record: revenue ₹1,021.3 cr, up 33.3%, with EBITDA margin improving to 19.2%. That is a strong top line and genuine operating improvement.

But profit went backwards sequentially, and it matters for how you value this. Q4 FY26 delivered PAT of ₹121.4 cr on ₹998.7 cr — a ~12.2% net margin. Q1 FY27 delivered ₹87.4 cr on ₹1,021.3 cr — 8.6%. More revenue, less profit. Note also that EBITDA margin rose while PAT margin fell, which points below the operating line: depreciation and interest from the ₹350–400 cr annual capex programme. Annualise Q4 and you get ~₹486 cr of earnings (~50x). Annualise Q1 and you get ~₹350 cr (~69x). Those are wildly different answers, and anyone quoting a single forward multiple on this stock has quietly picked one. Until the gap is explained — one-offs in Q4, seasonality, or a genuine step-up in depreciation — treat the cheaper number with suspicion.

The ADS business — the actual story

  • Revenue ₹315 cr in FY26, up 155%, with ₹109 cr in Q4 alone. Guided to ₹550–600 cr in FY27, which would nearly double it again.
  • ~₹3,870 cr of cumulative lifetime orders — multi-year visibility, though note this is a lifetime figure, not an annual one, and should not be compared against a single year's revenue.
  • Tier-1 to Boeing, Tier-2 to Airbus. Aerospace qualification takes years and is extremely sticky once won; this is a real barrier to entry, not a marketing line.
  • Margins of 25–30% expected as the facility reaches peak utilisation, against a blended 19.2%. Mix shift alone lifts group margin.
  • Sansera is not alone in this pivot — NRB Bearings and Craftsman Automation are running the same play, which is worth remembering when the story is told as though it were unique.

The rest of the business

Revenue mix at H1 FY26: auto ICE 72.9%, auto tech-agnostic 14.4%, xEV plus non-automotive 12.7%. The pivot away from ICE is real and deliberate — tech-agnostic and xEV revenue has climbed from 6.1% in FY22 — and capex is being steered toward xEV, ADS and agnostic parts. But nearly three-quarters of revenue still comes from internal-combustion components, and that is the base the 74x multiple is mostly attached to.

Capex runs ₹350–400 cr a year, funded from internal accruals rather than dilution or debt — a genuine positive. Net debt to OPBITDA is expected to stay at 1.3–1.6x: moderate, manageable, but not a fortress balance sheet, and it does mean the depreciation and interest drag visible in Q1 continues.

What's genuinely good here

  • Revenue growth accelerating: FY26 +16%, Q4 +28%, Q1 FY27 +33%.
  • EBITDA margin expanding through the capex cycle — 17.1% → 18.1% → 19.2%.
  • A high-barrier aerospace franchise being built with real customers and real orders, not an announced intention.
  • Capex self-funded; leverage moderate.
  • Deliberate, measurable reduction in ICE dependence over four years.
  • Quest PMS has publicly stayed bullish through the rally — informed money on the other side of the valuation argument.

Risks

The valuation is the risk, and it is a large one. At 74x trailing, a de-rating to even 40x — still a premium multiple for an auto ancillary with an aerospace kicker — costs about 45% even if earnings keep growing. The stock has tripled in twelve months and sits 2.2% from its high, so there is no cushion in the price and no margin for a disappointing quarter.
  • The one named target is 35% below spot. Antique at ₹2,520 on 35x FY28E. I could not find a broad consensus panel, so treat this as one considered view rather than the street's verdict — but it is a considered view built two years forward, and it still says the stock is a third too expensive.
  • ADS is ~9% of revenue carrying the valuation of the whole. If the ramp slips a year, the multiple has nothing to stand on.
  • 73% ICE exposure remains a structural headwind on a 10-year view, whatever the near-term order book says.
  • Aerospace concentration — Boeing and Airbus build-rate cuts, or a single programme deferral, hit the highest-multiple part of the business hardest.
  • The Q4-to-Q1 profit reversal is unexplained in the sources available and needs resolving before anyone underwrites a forward number.
  • Cyclicality — auto components remain tied to vehicle volumes across two-wheelers, passenger vehicles and off-highway.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a de-rating toward ₹2,500–2,800 (roughly 35–40x FY28E) without deterioration in the ADS ramp; or ADS revenue beating the ₹550–600 cr guide with group EBITDA margin sustained above 20% and the Q1 PAT-margin dip proven to be a one-off.
  • Downgrade to Sell: ADS revenue undershooting guidance, group EBITDA margin rolling back below ~18%, or a second consecutive quarter of falling net margin — at 74x, any of those re-rates the stock hard.
