Verdict

A very good business at a price that still assumes nothing goes wrong. Hold for now, and decide after the 29 October results. Milky Mist turns milk into paneer, curd, cheese, yoghurt and ice cream from one large plant at Perundurai, near Erode in Tamil Nadu. It sells almost no liquid milk, the thin-margin product most Indian dairies rely on. That is why it earns a 15% operating margin, against 5–11% at Hatsun, Heritage, Dodla and Parag. The June quarter was its first as a listed company. Revenue was ₹973 cr, up 44%. Operating margin rose to 14.9%. Gross margin rose 2.7 points to 34.2%, helped by price increases of about 10.5%. Paneer (₹248 cr, about a quarter of sales) grew 34%, cheese 38%, curd 27%, ice cream 60%, and yoghurt more than doubled on high-protein variants.

At ₹272 the stock is 94% above the ₹140 issue price. That's 114x reported trailing earnings, or about 126xd if those earnings were taxed at a normal 25%. Recent quarters carried unusually low tax. Even on our generous FY27 estimate it is about 73xd. Hatsun, the closest listed comparison, trades at 62x, though it grows about half as fast. The only post-listing broker target is DAM Capital's ₹175 Buy from listing day, 36% below today's price. For someone deciding whether to buy, the business case is strong, but the price has already priced in several years of it. ₹220 (about 59x FY27d) would be a reasonable entry for 35–40% growth. Hold, high risk.

What changed since 24 Sep. The price fell 12%, from ₹309 to ₹272, after peaking at about ₹335. The rating stays Hold. The fall leaves the stock less expensive, but not cheap. Four things are new:

  • Results date: the board meets on 29 October to approve the September-quarter results. That will be the first look at the half-year balance sheet, which shows whether the IPO money actually reduced debt.
  • Food-safety reports: in early October, media reported that the central food regulator had asked Tamil Nadu to sample Milky Mist paneer. On 10 October the company said it had received no notice. The stock fell 5% on 12 October anyway. Separately, in late September FSSAI flagged labelling claims on three of its products sold through quick commerce.
  • Funds trimmed: in the September disclosure, 15 schemes sold and Motilal Oswal Small Cap sold its whole stake. Mirae ELSS was the one large new buyer.
  • Cost pressure at peers: Hatsun and Heritage both reported the September quarter with lower margins than a year ago, a sign that milk costs are rising across southern dairy.

The stock is also under the exchanges' additional-surveillance framework. It was placed on the short-term list in September and now sits at long-term Stage 4, according to Trendlyne. That means tighter margin requirements for traders, and it usually dampens speculative volume.

Take off about ₹200 cr of depreciation and about ₹80 cr of interest (lower after the debt repayment), then tax at 25%. That leaves roughly ₹285 cr of profitd, or ₹3.7 a share. Milk. Milk is 50–55% of input costs. Milky Mist pays farmers about ₹41–42 a litre, roughly ₹45 landed. It collects 13.2 lakh litres a day, 28% more than a year ago, and buys 10–12% from third parties. Aavin, Tamil Nadu's state dairy and the price-setter for the region, has raised its procurement price by ₹3 a litre twice. Management said it was "still assessing" the effect and usually passes cost increases on after 2–3 months. That lag is the margin risk for the September and December quarters. Peers show it already: in the latest quarter, Hatsun's margin fell from 13% to 11%, Dodla's from 8% to 5% and Heritage's from 7% to 6%. Sales split 69% South and 31% elsewhere; management expects 60/40 within five years.

The IPO fixes the balance sheet once. It doesn't change the habit of spending more than it earns. From FY24 to FY26, debt rose from ₹1,042 cr to ₹1,677 cr while cash never went above ₹21 cr. Free cash flow was negative every year. Of the IPO's ₹1,428 cr of new money:

  • ₹497 cr repays debt.
  • ₹469 cr goes into more capacity at Perundurai.
  • ₹155 cr buys coolers for shops.

On the call, management described more than ₹700 cr of capex across these projects, a whey-protein plant (12–18 months away) and over 50,000 new coolers over three years, funded by "IPO proceeds and bank financing". That means some new borrowing. It also gave no figure for debt actually repaid so far. Two things work in its favour. Working capital is negative, because suppliers are paid more slowly than customers pay in. And the Perundurai paneer line runs at only about 50% utilisation, so management says the plant could support 3–3.5x FY26 revenue. A new cheddar line (120 tonnes a day) was commissioned in Q1, and a Maharashtra plant is about three years out.

Business

What it does

Milky Mist buys about 13 lakh litres of milk a day, mostly direct from farmers around Erode, and turns all of it into value-added products: paneer, curd, cheese, yoghurt, ice cream, butter, ghee and cream. It is India's largest private packaged-paneer brand (about 19% share), the largest private packaged-cheese brand in South India, and one of the top two private packaged-yoghurt brands nationally. It sells through general trade, modern retail and quick commerce, with e-commerce growing fastest. Company-owned coolers in shops keep its fresh products visible and cold. Manufacturing is concentrated at Perundurai.

Moat

Milky Mist has a real brand in a category where brands are hard to build. Paneer, curd and yoghurt are fresh products. They need chilled distribution, daily restocking and a name shoppers trust. Tighter enforcement against "analogue" paneer made from non-milk fats helps branded players. Coolers in shops are physical lock-in that a newcomer must match store by store. Against that, Amul and Hatsun have far greater scale. Private labels in modern retail and quick commerce keep prices in check. And as the October reports showed, a single food-safety headline can damage a fresh-dairy brand quickly.

Order book

Not applicable to a consumer company. The forward commitments are the Perundurai expansion, the whey-protein plant and the cooler rollout funded by the IPO.

Risks

Valuation leaves no room for a soft quarter. The stock trades at 114x reported earnings, about 126x at normal tax. Southern milk procurement prices are rising, and Hatsun, Dodla and Heritage have all lost margin. Milky Mist passes on cost increases 2–3 months late. A September quarter with margin back near 13% would probably re-rate the stock sharply lower.
  • Food safety and brand: the paneer sampling reports have been denied but not closed. A failed sample or a regulatory order would hit the core product, about a quarter of revenue.
  • Tax normalising: the last two quarters were taxed at −40% and 12%. At 25%, reported profit is about 10–15% lower.
  • Cash-hungry growth: free cash flow has been negative for four years. New capex is partly bank-funded.
  • Supply: the anchor unlock on 11 November, the pre-IPO lock-in ending around February 2027, a 20% float and ASM surveillance.
  • Concentration: manufacturing is largely at one site, and 69% of revenue comes from the South.
  • Competition: Amul, Hatsun and private labels cap pricing in paneer and curd over time.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a price near ₹220, about 59x our FY27 estimated. Or a September quarter with revenue growth above 35% and margin of at least 14.5% despite the milk-price increases. That should come with a half-year balance sheet showing debt down to about ₹1,200 cr or less, which would put the multiple on a firmer footing.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin below 12%, or revenue growth under 25%, in the September or December quarter. A confirmed adverse FSSAI finding on paneer. Or debt in the half-year balance sheet not materially below ₹1,677 cr, meaning the IPO money went to capex rather than repayment.
