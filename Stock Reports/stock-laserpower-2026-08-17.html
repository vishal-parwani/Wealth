Verdict

Hold — unchanged, but for a different reason than before. The prior note rated this Hold at ₹291.80 on 13 Aug 2026; the price is up 11% since. The rating stays, and the honest basis for it is that the June quarter cannot currently be read with confidence.

The conflict. Business Standard reports Q1 FY27 consolidated net profit down 58.1% to ₹20.73 cr from ₹49.47 cr. Two other outlets report the same quarter as PAT up 28.8% to ₹21.1 cr. The current-quarter figure agrees across all three at roughly ₹21 cr — it is the year-ago base that differs, ₹49.47 cr versus an implied ₹16.4 cr. The aggregated data shows ₹49 cr for Jun 2025, alongside an EPS of ₹774.06 for that quarter, which is a pre-IPO share count. The likeliest explanation is that the year-ago quarter carried an exceptional item or a pre-listing capital structure that makes the comparison meaningless. That could not be confirmed against the filing itself.

What is not in dispute: revenue rose 14.8% year on year to ₹521.5 cr, EBITDA rose 25.7% to ₹65.9 cr, and margin improved to 12.6% from 11.5%. On the operating line the quarter was fine. Sequentially revenue fell from ₹673.9 cr in March, which is normal for EPC-linked recognition.

Borrowings are the standing concern: up from ₹504 cr to ₹871 cr in FY26, with debtor days rising from 159 to 216 and the cash conversion cycle from 111 to 168 days. An EPC business funding a growing receivable with debt is the classic way these stories go wrong, and it is why the high-risk tag stays on.

Business

Nature. Two linked businesses. Manufacturing makes power cables and conductors for transmission and distribution. EPC executes substation and transmission line projects, largely for state utilities and private developers. The order book splits almost evenly between them, ₹1,433 cr and ₹1,356 cr.

Why the mix shift matters. FY26 revenue fell 10% while EBITDA rose 20% and margin went from 10% to 13%. That is management declining low-margin EPC turnover in favour of manufacturing. It is the right decision for returns and the wrong one for headline growth, and it explains a revenue decline that would otherwise look alarming.

Peers. Apar Industries, KEI Industries and Universal Cables in conductors and cables; Kalpataru and KEC in transmission EPC. Laser Power is materially smaller than all of them.

Moat. Limited. Utility approval and qualification take time and create some incumbency, but conductors are close to commodity and EPC is competitively bid. The defence is execution and relationships, not product.

Risks

  • Receivables funded by debt. Borrowings ₹504 cr → ₹871 cr while debtor days went 159 → 216. This is the single biggest risk on the name — state-utility receivables are slow and can stay slow.
  • Reporting opacity. Two credible sources disagree on whether June-quarter profit rose or fell. Until a continuous quarterly series is published, earnings quality cannot be independently checked.
  • Thin float. 75.3% promoter holding leaves 12.35% public float. Price is easily moved and a promoter sell-down to meet listing norms would add supply.
  • No history. One quarter of shareholding data and a partial quarterly P&L. There is not yet enough of a listed record to judge consistency.
  • EPC concentration. Roughly half the order book is project execution, where cost overruns and delays land directly in margin.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy — a clean four-quarter disclosed series showing margin held above 12%, with borrowings flat or lower and debtor days back under 180.
  • Downgrade to Sell — borrowings rising again alongside debtor days, order book shrinking below ₹2,000 cr, or the profit-reporting conflict resolving to the downside.
  • Stays a Hold — while the June-quarter conflict is unresolved and only one quarter of shareholding history exists.
