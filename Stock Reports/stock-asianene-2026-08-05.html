Verdict

Genuine operating momentum, wrapped inside a related-party merger you cannot yet value. Asian Energy (formerly Asian Oilfield Services) has transformed itself: FY26 revenue ₹791.1 cr, up 70.1%, adjusted PAT ₹60.6 cr (+43.6%), an order book of roughly ₹1,750 cr at March-end (~2.2x revenue, and that excludes Kuiper), major wins from Vedanta and MCL, and a net zero-debt balance sheet helped by ₹92 cr of warrant conversion. It is also climbing the value chain in two directions at once — internationally through the Kuiper Group acquisition (UAE offshore drilling, targeting US$60–65 mn revenue in FY27), and upstream into actual production at the Indrora block in Gujarat, where it holds a 50% participating interest.

The reason this is Speculative rather than Accumulate is the thing dominating the story: shareholders approved the merger of Oilmax Energy Private Limited into AESL at a court-convened meeting on 12 June 2026, at a swap ratio of 117 AESL shares for every 10 Oilmax shares, with completion targeted in Q3 FY27. Oilmax is a promoter-side entity — so this is a related-party transaction that will materially change the share count and the earnings base, and I could not establish Oilmax's financials or the resulting dilution from public sources. Until you can see what minority holders are paying for and how many new shares get issued, the ~29x adjusted (or ~34x reported) multiple is being applied to an entity that is about to stop existing in its current form. Two further blemishes: EBITDA grew only 36.6% against 70.1% revenue growth (margins compressed), and adjusted PAT of ₹60.6 cr sits ~₹9 cr above the reported ₹51.84 cr.

Business

Services to the energy, mineral, coal and power sectors, reported across two segments — Oil & Gas and Mineral & Other Energy. Historically a seismic-survey and oilfield-services contractor; the shape of the company is now changing on three fronts:

  • Kuiper Group (UAE) — 100% acquired, consolidated from FY26. An offshore drilling services platform giving Middle East exposure; guided to US$60–65 mn revenue in FY27, which would be a meaningful share of group revenue.
  • Indrora block, Gujarat — 50% participating interest, i.e. the company now owns production rather than only servicing it. The NM-01 well is producing ~100 BOPD, with management targeting ~1,000 BOPD by FY27 via further drilling and field development.
  • Oilmax merger — see below. Management frames the combined entity as "end-to-end energy solutions," with an ambition of ~₹3,000 cr consolidated revenue by 2029.

FY27 guidance for the standalone India services business is 30–40% growth with improved margins.

The Oilmax merger — the single most important thing to diligence. Shareholders approved the scheme at a court-convened meeting on 12 June 2026. Swap ratio: 117 AESL shares for every 10 Oilmax Energy Private Limited shares (i.e. 11.7 AESL shares per Oilmax share). Completion is targeted for Q3 FY27. Oilmax is a promoter-side private company, which makes this a related-party transaction: the fairness of the ratio to minority shareholders is the whole question, and it turns on Oilmax's reserves, contracts and earnings — none of which I could verify from public sources. Before acting, read the scheme document and the independent valuation/fairness opinion, and work out the post-merger share count. Everything in the snapshot above is pre-merger and will be restated.

Bull case

  • Growth is real and order-backed: +70% revenue with a ~₹1,750 cr book at ~2.2x revenue — and that figure excludes Kuiper, so group visibility is better than it looks.
  • Moving up the value chain: owning production (Indrora) earns far better economics than day-rate services, and a 100 → 1,000 BOPD ramp would change the margin profile materially.
  • International diversification via Kuiper reduces reliance on Indian PSU tendering and adds Middle East offshore exposure.
  • Clean balance sheet: net zero debt with ₹92 cr received from warrant conversion — unusual for a growing oilfield-services company, and it funds expansion without leverage.
  • Blue-chip counterparties — Vedanta and MCL wins in FY26 validate the execution credentials.
  • Relative strength: +22% over a year while the Nifty fell ~8%.

Risks

You are valuing an entity that is about to be restated. ~29x adjusted earnings pre-merger tells you little about what you own post-merger. Unquantified dilution is the core risk here, not operations.
  • Margin compression: EBITDA +36.6% on revenue +70.1% means margin fell — growth is being bought with lower-quality revenue mix, or acquisition consolidation is diluting it.
  • Adjusted vs reported gap: ₹60.6 cr adjusted against ₹51.84 cr reported. Prefer the reported number until you can see what the ~₹9 cr of adjustments are.
  • Related-party governance: a promoter entity merging into the listed company is exactly the structure where minority interests can be diluted at an unfavourable ratio. Shareholder approval is not the same as a fair price.
  • Ambitious production target: 100 → 1,000 BOPD is a 10x ramp inside roughly a year, dependent on drilling success. Treat as option value, not a forecast.
  • Cyclicality and oil-price exposure: now that it owns production, crude prices hit revenue directly rather than only through client capex.
  • Customer concentration in PSU/large-corporate tenders; award timing is lumpy and politically sensitive.
  • Thin coverage — no meaningful analyst following; figures here come from company releases and financial media, not audited filings I have reviewed.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Accumulate: the merger completing on terms that are demonstrably fair — a published independent valuation, a clear post-merger share count, and pro-forma earnings that leave the multiple at or below the pre-merger ~29x. Add to that: Indrora production tracking toward the 1,000 BOPD goal, and EBITDA margin recovering above ~14%.
  • Downgrade to Avoid: a swap ratio that proves dilutive on scrutiny, margins continuing to compress as revenue grows, or the Indrora ramp stalling near current output.
