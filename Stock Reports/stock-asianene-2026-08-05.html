Verdict

An excellent operating year attached to a related-party merger that dilutes existing holders by ~46% — and the stock is sitting near its 52-week high going into it. The business is genuinely performing: FY26 revenue ₹791.1 cr (+70.1%), adjusted PAT ₹60.6 cr (+43.6%), a Q4 exit run-rate of ₹338.2 cr (+57% YoY), an order book of ~₹1,750 cr (~2.2x revenue, excluding Kuiper), wins from Vedanta and MCL, and a net zero-debt balance sheet topped up by ₹131 cr of warrant money since Nov 2024. It is also moving up the value chain — internationally via Kuiper (UAE offshore drilling, guided US$60–65 mn in FY27) and upstream into owned production at the Indrora block.

Now the part that decides the investment. Oilmax Energy Private Limited is Asian Energy's holding company, and it is being merged into the listed entity at 117 AESL shares per 10 Oilmax shares. Oilmax has been valued at roughly ₹2,582 cr — more than AESL's own ₹1,891 cr market capitalisation — on multiples of about 53x FY25 EPS and 36x EV/EBITDA, which analysts have flagged as rich. Promoter holding drops from 65% to 47% and existing shareholders face roughly 46% equity dilution. When the board approved the scheme on 6 Sep 2025, the shares fell 13–14% in a day. They have since recovered to ₹392, within 3% of the 52-week high.

So you would be buying near the highs, at ~31x adjusted (or ~37x reported) FY26 earnings, immediately before a 46%-dilutive transaction that hands the promoter's own vehicle a full valuation. The structural logic is defensible — collapsing a holding company removes a layer and simplifies the story — but the price paid for it is the issue, and the market's first instinct was to mark the stock down sharply. Wait for completion (targeted Sep–Oct 2026 / Q3 FY27) and re-underwrite the combined entity on actual post-merger share count and earnings.

The Oilmax merger — the whole investment case

Key facts, now verified.
  • Oilmax is the parent, not a third party. Oilmax Energy Private Limited (CIN U40101MH2008PTC185357, incorporated 2008, Mumbai; crude petroleum and natural gas extraction) is Asian Energy's holding company. This is a holdco collapse — a related-party transaction by definition.
  • Swap ratio: 117 AESL shares per 10 Oilmax shares. Board approved the draft scheme 6 Sep 2025; shareholders approved at a court-convened meeting 12 Jun 2026; completion targeted Sep–Oct 2026 (Q3 FY27).
  • Dilution: ~46% for existing shareholders. Promoter holding falls from 65% to 47% — because Oilmax's own non-promoter shareholders (~26% of Oilmax) arrive directly on AESL's register as the parent's AESL stake is cancelled.
  • Price paid: Oilmax valued at approximately ₹2,582 cr — versus AESL's entire market cap of ~₹1,891 cr — at roughly 53x FY25 EPS and 36x EV/EBITDA. Analyst commentary has called this high.
  • Market's verdict on announcement: the stock fell 13–14% the day the board approved it.

How to think about it. Because Oilmax's principal asset is its ~65% stake in AESL, the merger is partly AESL paying for shares in itself. At ₹1,891 cr market cap, that 65% stake is worth roughly ₹1,229 cr — which implies the rest of Oilmax (its own E&P assets) is being valued at around ₹1,350 cr. Whether that is fair depends entirely on Oilmax's standalone reserves, contracts and cash flows. Public data is thin: FY25 revenue sat in a ₹500–1,000 cr band with a reported 42% revenue CAGR and 108% EBITDA CAGR, but FY24 was weak — revenue down 7.5% and profit down 44%, so the growth is not linear. Scheme-stage projections for the combined entity (revenue ₹593 cr, EBITDA ₹143 cr, PAT ₹91 cr) appear to be on an FY25 basis and are already below AESL's standalone FY26 revenue of ₹791 cr — treat them as stale rather than as guidance.

The one genuinely positive reading: holding-company structures usually trade at a discount, and collapsing one removes a layer of leakage, simplifies governance and can unlock value for minorities. That is the bull argument, and it is not trivial. But it only works if the entry ratio is fair — and 53x for the promoter's vehicle is not obviously fair.

Business

Services to the energy, mineral, coal and power sectors across two segments — Oil & Gas and Mineral & Other Energy. Historically a seismic-survey and oilfield-services contractor; now changing shape on three fronts:

  • Kuiper Group (UAE) — 100% acquired Aug 2025, consolidating from FY26. Offshore drilling services giving Middle East exposure; guided to US$60–65 mn revenue in FY27.
  • Indrora block, Gujarat — 50% participating interest, so the company now owns production. NM-01 well at ~100 BOPD, management targeting ~1,000 BOPD by FY27.
  • Oilmax merger — above. Management's stated ambition for the combined group is ~₹3,000 cr revenue by 2029.

FY27 guidance for standalone India services: 30–40% growth with improved margins.

Bull case

  • Operating momentum is real and order-backed: +70% FY26 revenue, Q4 +57%, and a ~₹1,750 cr book at ~2.2x revenue that excludes Kuiper.
  • Moving up the value chain: owned production (Indrora) earns far better economics than day-rate services; a 100 → 1,000 BOPD ramp would reshape margins.
  • International diversification via Kuiper reduces dependence on Indian PSU tendering.
  • Clean balance sheet: net zero debt with ₹131 cr of warrant proceeds — growth funded without leverage.
  • Holdco collapse has structural merit — removing the parent layer simplifies the entity and can close a structural discount.
  • Blue-chip counterparties (Vedanta, MCL) and relative strength: +70% off the 52-week low.

Risks

~46% dilution, at a rich price, to the promoter's own vehicle — with the stock 3% off its high. That is the risk-reward problem in one line. The market fell 13–14% when this was announced; it has since recovered, which means the dilution is arguably less discounted now than it was a year ago.
  • Valuation of the acquired entity: ~₹2,582 cr at ~53x FY25 EPS / 36x EV/EBITDA for Oilmax, versus AESL's own ~31x. Minorities are buying the parent at a premium multiple to themselves.
  • Oilmax's own record is uneven: FY24 revenue −7.5% and profit −44%, before the FY25 recovery. This is not a steadily compounding asset.
  • Margin compression: FY26 EBITDA +36.6% on revenue +70.1% — growth is coming with weaker mix or dilutive consolidation.
  • Adjusted vs reported gap: ₹60.6 cr adjusted against ₹51.84 cr reported; anchor on the reported figure until the ~₹9 cr of adjustments is explained.
  • Ambitious production target: 100 → 1,000 BOPD is a 10x ramp inside about a year, dependent on drilling success. Option value, not forecast.
  • Oil-price and cycle exposure now hits revenue directly through owned production, not just client capex.
  • Thin scrutiny: negligible analyst coverage, no published price targets; figures here come from company releases, earnings-call coverage and aggregators, not audited filings I have reviewed.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: merger completion with pro-forma numbers that stand up — i.e. combined earnings that leave the post-dilution multiple at or below today's ~31x — plus Indrora production tracking toward 1,000 BOPD and EBITDA margin recovering above ~14%. A pullback toward the low-₹300s ahead of completion would also improve the entry materially.
  • Downgrade to Sell: post-merger pro-formas showing the combined entity is more expensive than AESL standalone, margins continuing to compress, or the Indrora ramp stalling near current output.
