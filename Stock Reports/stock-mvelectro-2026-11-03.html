Verdict

Q2 FY27 results have not been declared as of 3 Nov 2026. No board-meeting date for them could be found in the exchange filings or aggregators checked. The trading window has been closed since 1 Oct "until 48 hours after" the results. Under SEBI's listing rules the results are due by 14 Nov 2026. The latest declared quarter is Q1 FY27, approved on 25 Aug 2026. Revenue was ₹12.77 cr, down 5.1% YoY, with an operating loss of ₹5.38 cr (−42% margin) and a net loss of ₹6.89 cr. That quarter ended before the IPO money arrived.

Hold, high risk. The franchise is real but the price already assumes it gets built. In September 2025 MV became the first Indian private company to win CLW/RDSO approval for an indigenously designed IGBT propulsion system. In FY26 it then won about 27% of the net procurable quantity in Indian Railways' locomotive propulsion tenders. At 30 Jun 2026 that left 564 sets worth ₹922 cr (ex-GST and AMC) to supply to CLW, BLW and PLW, plus an ₹86.5 cr MEMU development letter of acceptance. That book is about 19x trailing revenued, and it carries no technology royalty. The IPO raised ₹290 cr, of which ₹180 cr is earmarked for the working capital that was the stated bottleneck. Management guides to 10 sets in September, 25 in November and 40–45 a month from January. On that plan it expects ₹400 cr of FY27 revenue and a 10%+ PAT margin at run-rate.

Why not Buy. (1) Nothing has been delivered at scale yet. FY26 propulsion revenue was ₹4.7 cr. Installed capacity was 114 sets a year at 2.6% utilisation. Q1 FY27 revenue fell YoY. Reaching the FY27 target needs about ₹387 cr in nine monthsd. (2) Valuation. Assume 480 sets a year at ₹1.63 cr each and a 10% margin. That gives roughly ₹78 cr of PAT, so ₹2,005 cr is about 26x management's own FY28 run-rated, before anything slips. (3) Supply overhang. The 90-day anchor lock (15.35 L shares, 5.6% of equity) ended on 1 Nov. About 47 L pre-IPO public shares and about 1.03 cr promoter shares above the 20% minimum contribution unlock around 4 Feb 2027. Those include Madhuri Kela's 11.5 L shares, bought at an effective ₹273.50. (4) Concentration and governance. Indian Railways was 77% of FY26 revenue. The promoter, Mohit Vohra, is also MD of listed group company Quadrant Future Tek, which has been both a supplier and a customer.

Why not Sell. Only a handful of suppliers own an approved design: Medha, BHEL and the MNCs. Indian Railways' 5-year locomotive procurement plan on IREPS covers about 10,000 sets worth about ₹19,800 cr. A fresh CLW tender for about 748 locomotive sets is in progress. If the ramp shows up in Q3 FY27 deliveries, earnings could catch up with the price within 12–18 months. The stock is already 25% below its 14 Oct closing high. Q2 and Q3 dispatches are the test.

Business

Nature of business

MV designs, assembles and makes railway electrical and power-electronics equipment in Faridabad, Haryana, with Unit 1 for manufacturing and a DSIR-recognised R&D centre. It had 206 staff at May 2026. There are three lines. (1) IGBT 3-phase propulsion equipment for 6,000 hp locomotives (WAG-9/WAP-7 class): the traction converter-inverter, auxiliary converter, vehicle control unit and driver display. The design is the company's own, so it pays no royalty. (2) Switchgear and panels for coaches and EMUs. (3) Cable protection: conduits, end fittings and cable jackets. Customers: Indian Railways directly (CLW, BLW, PLW, zonal railways) was 76.7% of FY26 revenue, up from 73.0% in FY25 and 67.8% in FY24. The top 3 were 82.7%, the top 5 86.7% and the top 10 93.0%. The rest goes to OEM suppliers of Indian Railways, several of them related parties. Effectively 100% of the business is government or utility. Imported content is heavy: IGBTs, capacitors and processors from China, the UK, Hong Kong and Singapore. The top supplier is 39% of purchases. In development: composite and hotel-load converters, 12-car MEMU propulsion (24-month delivery window), and an automatic fault locator for overhead lines under a tie-up with South Korea's PNC Technologies. Traction motors are planned with Hansung Motors.

Capacity, capex and working capital

  • Capacity. Installed propulsion capacity was 114 sets a year in FY26, at 2.6% utilisation. Management says the lines can now do about 55 sets a month on two shifts and targets a stable 40–45 a month from January 2027. Cable products are moving to a new Unit 2 on company-owned land in Haryana to free Unit 1. More test set-ups are planned for both units.
  • Capex. There is no capex object in the IPO; equipment is funded by internal accruals and a ₹7 cr Bajaj Finance term loan. Investing outflow was ₹17.9 cr in FY26. Fixed assets rose from ₹23 cr to ₹37 cr.
  • Working capital is the business model. Cash conversion went to 571 days in FY26, with inventory at 731 days. The RHP plans for 105 inventory days, 60 receivable days and 45 payable days. Its estimate of net working capital is ₹138 cr at FY27 and ₹286 cr at FY28, met from ₹180 cr of IPO money, ₹48 cr of bank limits and internal accruals. Management says payments on its first three dispatches arrived within 15 days. Bank lines: cash credit of ₹18 cr plus a ₹5.4 cr insta-fund.

Moat

A real regulatory and engineering barrier, but a narrow one. Propulsion is safety-critical. Each sub-system is type-tested, followed by RDSO field trials (about 4 months or 30,000 km) and production-unit vendor approval. MV took roughly five years and a 50,000 km trial to get there. Make-in-India rules favour indigenous design ownership, so MV avoids the royalty cost that licensees carry. But tenders are L1, priced competitively. Management says it was L1 in one tender and L2 in two, with thin gaps across the six or seven bidders. Each new platform (MEMU, Vande Bharat, higher-hp locomotives) needs separate approval. The barrier protects against new entrants, not against Medha or the MNCs on price.

Order book

₹922 cr executable at 30 Jun 2026: 564 locomotive propulsion sets for CLW, BLW and PLW, ex-GST and ex-AMC, about ₹1.63 cr a setd. That is ≈18.9x TTM revenued and 0.46x the market cap. On top of that are ₹67.7 cr of 3-year AMC on those sets and an ₹86.5 cr MEMU letter of acceptance (MCF Raebareli, 27 Jun 2026, plus ₹4.7 cr AMC). The MEMU work is development-category and supplies only after RDSO approval. Two small development orders (composite converter ₹2.5 cr, hotel-load converter ₹0.8 cr) bring the product book to about ₹1,011 cr. On 27 Aug management quoted "₹989 cr locomotive plus ₹86 cr EMU", including AMC. Some sets fall due by March 2027 and some in FY28. LDs run at 0.5% a week up to a 10% cap, and extensions are discretionary. Pipeline, not firm: the CLW tender for about 748 locomotive sets, with BLW and PLW tenders expected to follow, and overhead-line fault-locator tenders.

Risks

Execution of the ramp is the whole thesis. The company has to go from a few small batches to about 40 sets a month, roughly 50x FY26's propulsion output, inside two quarters, on imported semiconductors with lead times of up to six months. A slip of one or two quarters costs LDs (up to 10% of contract value) and pushes the first profitable quarter into FY28. At 26x even management's run-rate earnings, the stock has no buffer for that.
  • Lock-in supply. Anchors were fully free from 1 Nov. Around 4 Feb 2027 about 47 L pre-IPO public shares (17% of equity) become sellable, some bought at ₹273.50, along with about 1.03 cr promoter shares above the minimum contribution.
  • Single customer, single product. 77% of revenue is Indian Railways, and the order book is 100% locomotive propulsion from three production units. Tender timing or a cut in locomotive procurement would hit directly.
  • Margin squeeze. The contracts are fixed-price, while global electronics prices have risen. Management is relying on bulk buying for 200–300 sets. Gross margin was already down to 29% in FY26.
  • Working capital and cash burn. FY26 operating cash flow was −₹57.5 cr. The RHP's own estimate needs ₹286 cr of working capital by FY28. If collections slow, another equity or debt raise follows.
  • Governance. The promoter is MD of another listed company. There is a pattern of related-party trade with group firms (36.8% of COGS in FY25), a group-level RoC penalty under appeal, and a disclosed FEMA lapse.
  • Liquidity and volatility. Since listing the closing range has been ₹560–978, with +17% and −25% moves inside a month. Retail now owns 33%.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q3 FY27 (the December quarter) shows revenue of ₹100 cr or more, meaning roughly 60 sets dispatched, with positive EBITDA and inventory days falling. Also needed: an award in the current CLW, BLW or PLW tender round that replaces the volume being delivered. A pull-back toward ₹550–600 before February's lock-in expiry would make the same evidence cheaper.
  • Downgrade to Sell: Q2 or Q3 FY27 revenue under ₹50 cr, or management cutting the ₹400 cr target. Or any of: LDs or short-closure notices on the CLW, BLW or PLW orders; losing the 748-set CLW tender outright; a fresh equity raise within a year of listing; or promoter selling once the February 2027 lock-in ends.
