Verdict

Buy — unchanged, and the margin recovery that is the thesis is now three quarters old. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹1,110 on 13 Aug 2026, arguing the widely circulated "market cap below sales, cheapest ever" pitch was worthless and that the real option was margin recovery on a tripled revenue base. The stock is ₹1,215, up 9.5%. That argument still stands, and the evidence has strengthened.

Look at operating margin, not the P/E. It ran 8%, 6%, 1.8%, 8%, 10% across the last five quarters. The December 2025 quarter lost ₹116 cr. The June 2026 quarter made ₹103 cr with margin at 10% — the best since the Heubach business was consolidated. FY26 as a whole ran 5.9%; the standalone Indian business used to earn 11–13%. On a ₹9,787 cr revenue base every 100 bps is roughly ₹98 cr of EBITDA, so the four-point recovery already delivered is worth about ₹400 cr annualised.

Ignore the 95x headline. It divides today's price by trailing earnings that include a loss-making quarter. Annualise Q1 FY27's ₹103 cr and you get roughly ₹412 cr, putting the stock on about 23.5x — a fair price for the world's largest pigment producer mid-turnaround, and neither the bargain nor the bubble the two competing narratives claim.

The unusual fact that frames everything. Promoters hold 8.19%. Mutual funds hold about 26%. This is now an institution-owned company where the founding family is a minority holder — and the funds were still buying in July.

Promoter holding went from 35.82% to 8.19% in under two years, and the reason is mixed. The step from 16.41% to 8.20% in September 2025 was an 8.21% reclassification of promoter-group holders into the public category — an administrative change, not stock sold. But the slide from 35.82% to 16.41% before that was real selling, in tranches: Rathi family members disposed of a 3% block for about ₹197 cr and further parcels in open-market transactions.

How to read it. The founding family is now a minority holder in a company that mutual funds own three times over. That removes promoter-alignment as a support for the investment case and makes this a professionally-owned business whose fate rests on management execution and institutional patience. Within the residual promoter group, Rajesh Balkrishna Rathi holds the largest single stake at 5.15%, and the company has confirmed no encumbrance on promoter shares in FY26.

The institutions went the other way, decisively. DII holding rose from 13.67% to 26.41% and FII from 4.65% to 8.48% over the same period. Whatever the family was doing, professional money was accumulating throughout.

Business

Nature. Sudarshan makes colour pigments — for coatings, plastics, inks and cosmetics. Following the Heubach acquisition it is the largest pigment producer in the world, with roughly ₹9,900 cr of revenue against ₹2,539 cr as recently as FY24.

What the acquisition actually bought. Heubach's global pigment operations were purchased out of insolvency in March 2025 for about €127.5 million. That price bought scale, global customer relationships and manufacturing footprint very cheaply — and also a business that was losing money, which is why the group margin fell from 11–13% to 5.9%.

The entire investment case in one sentence. Can Sudarshan run Heubach's assets at Indian-business margins? Three quarters of evidence say it is moving that way — 1.8% to 10% — but the work is maybe half done, and turnarounds of insolvent European manufacturing are not usually linear.

Balance sheet. ₹2,473 cr of gross borrowings against ₹3,434 cr of reserves, with net debt reported falling. This is a leveraged turnaround, which is why the risk tag is on despite the size and the institutional ownership.

Risks

  • The turnaround can stall. Two quarters ago this company lost ₹116 cr with margin at 1.8%. That is how quickly it can go wrong.
  • Fund concentration. Tata Small Cap owns about 6.3% of the company; 19 funds own about 26%. Crowded ownership cuts both ways, and there is no promoter block to absorb an exit.
  • No promoter alignment. The founding family holds 8.19% after selling down from 35.82%. Whatever the reasons, the usual skin-in-the-game argument is gone.
  • Leverage. ₹2,473 cr of borrowings on a business earning 10% operating margin and 5.46% ROCE. Fine while margin recovers; painful if it does not.
  • Reported ROE is 0.54%. The recovery has to be believed from the quarterly margin line, because the annual return ratios still look terrible.
  • European manufacturing exposure. Heubach's assets carry European energy and labour costs and European demand.
  • Beware the marketed pitch. "Market cap below sales, cheapest in history" is arithmetically true and analytically meaningless here — the denominator tripled by absorbing a low-margin business.

What would change the rating

  • Stays a Buy — while operating margin keeps climbing towards the historic 11–13% and the quarterly PAT trend holds.
  • Raise conviction — two more quarters at or above 10% margin, or net debt falling below ₹500 cr.
  • Downgrade to Hold — margin slipping back under 7%, or a quarter of losses.
  • Downgrade to Sell — Tata Small Cap materially reducing, margin returning to the FY26 average of 5.9%, or borrowings rising to fund operating losses.
