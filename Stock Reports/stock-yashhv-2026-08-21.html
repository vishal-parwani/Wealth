Verdict

Hold — unchanged, but for better reasons than last time. The prior note rated this Hold at ₹932.00 on 3 Aug 2026. The stock is ₹912.20, down 2.1%. Unlike most names in this library, the price has not run away since the last note — what has changed is that the evidence underneath it got stronger.

FY26 was a very good year. Revenue ₹235 cr, up 56.7%. Profit ₹37 cr, up 76.2% — profit growing faster than sales, which is the opposite of what most fast-growing small manufacturers manage. Operating margin went from 23% to 26% in the second half. ROCE is 28.6% and borrowings are ₹38 cr against ₹170 cr of reserves.

The forward case is unusually concrete. The unexecuted order book was ₹400 cr at 31 March 2026, against ₹150 cr a year earlier — a 2.7x increase and about 1.7x FY26 revenue. The greenfield bushing plant at Vadodara is expected to start trial production in H1 FY27 and commercial production in H2. So there is both demand on the books and capacity arriving to serve it. The company also paid its first dividend, ₹1.40 a share.

What holds it at Hold is the price and the promoter register. 70x trailing already assumes the order book converts. And promoters have gone from 57.94% in March to 54.79% in August — 3.15 points sold while the stock tripled off its low. That is the single fact that keeps this from being an upgrade.

The promoter sell-down is the thing to weigh. Holding was flat at 57.9% for a year, then fell to 57.24% in June and 54.79% in August — 3.15 points, or roughly ₹86 cr at today's price, sold into a rising market. Sell-downs are often benign: promoters diversify, or meet minimum public shareholding norms. But this one is recent, accelerating, and happening at 70x earnings while the company tells the market about a ₹400 cr order book. Both facts are true at once and the note does not resolve them.

Against that, the buyers are getting better. FIIs went from 0.05% in March to 2.17% in August. The shareholder count has gone from 2,501 to 6,341 in a year. Fund houses are building. So the float being released is being absorbed by more, and more institutional, holders — which is a healthier distribution than the reverse.

DII holding has drifted down from 11.06% in March to 8.99% in August, even as mutual fund holding specifically rose. The two are consistent: the Negen and YR funds sold 6,49,000 shares over the same window, and those are non-mutual-fund vehicles inside the DII bucket.

Business

Nature. Yash Highvoltage makes transformer bushings — the insulated fittings that carry a conductor through a transformer casing. The range covers oil-impregnated paper (OIP) condenser bushings, resin-impregnated paper and synthetic (RIP/RIS) bushings, high-voltage and high-current bushings, wall bushings and oil-to-oil bushings, plus retrofit work on existing transformers.

Why it is growing. Bushings are a consumable-adjacent component in transmission and distribution: every new transformer needs them and every ageing one eventually needs them replaced. India's grid build-out and transformer replacement cycle is the demand driver, and the shift from OIP to dry RIP/RIS technology is a product-mix upgrade the company is positioned for. That mix shift is the most credible explanation for margin rising to 26%.

Moat. Moderate, and better than a general machining shop. Bushings are a safety-critical, type-tested component; qualification with a transformer OEM or a utility is slow and specifications are conservative, which keeps incumbents in place. The counterweight is that the customer base is concentrated among a few large transformer makers and state utilities, and they hold the pricing power.

Capacity. The Vadodara greenfield plant is the near-term swing factor. Trial production is guided for H1 FY27 and commercial output for H2 FY27. If it slips, FY27 growth depends on the existing lines and the order book converts more slowly than the multiple assumes.

Risks

  • Promoter selling. 3.15 points in five months, accelerating, at a high multiple. The most important open question in this note.
  • Valuation. 70x trailing already assumes the ₹400 cr order book converts and the new plant lands on time. Little room for either to disappoint.
  • Execution risk on the new plant. Greenfield commissioning slips often. A delay pushes revenue into FY28 while the multiple is paid today.
  • Half-yearly reporting. Last earnings are to March 2026 — about five months old, and the next are not due until roughly November. Problems surface late in a stock like this.
  • Customer concentration. Transformer OEMs and state utilities are a narrow buyer base, and utility ordering is lumpy and politically timed.
  • Short public record. Only two full years of published annuals. FY26's margin step-up has not yet been shown to be durable.
  • Rising borrowings. Up from ₹24 cr to ₹38 cr to fund the expansion. Modest against ₹170 cr of reserves, but the direction is worth tracking through the capex.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy — promoter holding stabilising for two consecutive disclosures, plus the Vadodara plant confirmed in commercial production on schedule. That combination would remove both of the reasons this is not already a Buy.
  • Downgrade to Sell — further promoter selling below about 52%, the plant slipping past FY27, or H1 FY27 margin falling back under 20%.
  • Stays a Hold — while the growth, order book and fund buying are genuinely strong but the promoters are heading the other way at 70x.
