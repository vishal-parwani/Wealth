Verdict

Why it jumped (26–27 Oct). 26 Oct: confirmed — the Q2 FY27 results. The board approved them on Saturday 24 October and filed them at 15:54 that day, so Monday was the first session to trade on them. Consolidated revenue was ₹388 cr (+72% YoY, +55% QoQ), EBITDA ₹111 cr (+88%) at a 28.6% margin against 21.4% in Q1, and PAT ₹46 cr (+54%). Every line was a quarterly record. The stock gapped up to ₹389.80 from ₹374.80 and reached ₹409.80 in the first 15 minutes. It then faded to ₹395. A second leg came during the earnings call, which the company had scheduled for 11:11 IST: in the 11:45–12:15 bars it ran from ₹411 to ₹431.70 on 1.24 crore shares. It closed at ₹410.90 (+9.6%) on 5.26 crore shares, about 8x the three-month average. Peers moved far less that day: BEL +0.3%, HAL +1.1%, Data Patterns −0.5%, Nifty +0.7%. Paras (+6.5%) and Zen (+3.4%) bounced off a weak 23 October, so a small part of the move was the sector rebounding. What management said on the call isn't verified: only the recording link has been filed, with no transcript yet.

27 Oct: likely a sector move; I couldn't confirm its cause. Apollo opened at ₹415.50, touched ₹446.40 by 09:45 and closed at ₹425.40 (+3.5%) on 3.9 crore shares. Defence small caps moved more on the same day: Data Patterns +9.2% on six times its normal volume, ideaForge +10.0% and Astra Microwave +4.7%, while BEL rose 1.4% and the Nifty was flat. Apollo filed nothing price-sensitive that day: only TV-interview notices (CNBC Awaaz at 13:25, while the stock was drifting lower) and the results newspaper advertisement. I found no news item that explains the sector move.

Not the cause: the promoter money. The "₹1,100 cr at ₹416" is the promoter group's share of a preferential issue announced on 6 July, not new news. The MD's sons, Chanakya and Kanishka Reddy Baddam, are to take 1.305 crore warrants each at ₹416.60, ₹1,087 crd in all. Only 25% (₹272 crd) is payable on allotment, and the rest on conversion within 12 months. The issue has not been allotted: the last filing (22 Sep) answered NSE's observations on the EGM notice, and the share count hadn't moved at the September quarter-end.

What changed since 24 Sep 2026. The price is up 6.6%, from ₹399.05 to ₹425.40. It had swung between ₹375 and ₹429 in October before the results. The rating stays Hold, and the high-risk flag stays on. The quarter answered the August worry about margins: consolidated EBITDA margin recovered from 21.4% to 28.6%, and standalone margin hit a record 35%. The company also reports positive operating cash flow of ₹22 cr for the half, and working-capital days of 353 against 359 in FY26. The order book edged up to ₹1,765 cr from ₹1,704 cr; QRSAM is still not a filed order. Against that, three things weigh. The consolidated tax charge was 50% of profit before tax (₹46 cr on ₹92.5 cr), and the filings I could read don't explain it. Borrowings rose to ₹663 cr from ₹543 cr in six months. And the funding plan is now on the page. The company has agreed to buy 41.33% of Premier Explosives for ₹1,550 cr, plus an open offer for another 26% at ₹698 a share (up to ₹976 crd). That is a combined ₹2,526 crd for a business with ₹388 cr of FY26 turnover and ₹290 cr of net worth. It is paid for by a ₹3,322 cr preferential issue of equity and warrants at ₹416.60, which adds about 21.5%d to the share count. The cash-flow worry from August is better but not solved: after ₹156 crd of half-year capex, free cash flow was still about −₹134 crd.

For a buy/hold decision: hold if you own it; don't buy at ₹425. This is a genuinely strong quarter. Revenue for the half is up 78%, which puts the year well ahead of the 40–45% growth guidance. But the price already carries it. At ₹425 the stock is on 114x trailing earnings and roughly 100x the FY27 consensus profit (₹158 crd, +47%). Once the preferential shares and warrants convert, that becomes about 122xd. The consensus target of ₹469 from 4 analysts leaves 10% upside. The 55 outside investors taking the equity are paying ₹416.60 and accepting a lock-in, so today's buyer gets no discount to them. The Premier deal turns a defence-electronics company into an electronics-plus-energetics group at a price of about 9.7x Premier's salesd. Premier's own shares have fallen to ₹588, 16% below the ₹698 open-offer price, after dropping 8% and 7% on 26–27 Oct. I found no filing explaining that fall. A better entry is one of two things: a pullback toward the ₹380s, or a filed QRSAM or MIGM production order large enough to double the book.

Business

What it does

Apollo Micro Systems designs, builds and tests electronic and electro-mechanical sub-systems for Indian defence programmes. Its products are missile electronics, fuzes, naval mines and underwater systems, avionics and ground support, and its customers are DRDO laboratories, defence PSUs and the services. It is now building an energetics arm. IDL Explosives, its step-down subsidiary for commercial and bulk explosives, is already consolidated. Premier Explosives, which makes solid propellants for missiles and space, is being acquired: 41.33% under purchase agreement plus a 26% open offer. The stated goal is to move from Tier-1 supplier to "complete weapon systems". The company holds a Government of India licence to make missiles, torpedoes, mines, aerial bombs and rockets. Two Make-II prototype orders (a guided bomb kit and an autonomous anti-submarine vessel) are its first prime-contractor bids.

Moat

The moat is programme incumbency. Once a sub-system is qualified on a DRDO missile or naval platform, it stays there for that programme's production life. Apollo cites participation in 60+ DRDO programmes as a sub-system partner. The limits haven't changed: the customer is a monopsony that sets price and payment terms, and award timing is outside the company's control. The 15 September court case is a reminder that low bids invite challenges.

Order book

₹1,765 cr consolidated as at 30 Sep 2026 (company-disclosed), up from ₹1,704 cr at 8 Aug. That is about 1.5xd trailing revenue. Since the Q2 run-rate is converting the book faster than it refills, cover is falling, not rising. In August management talked of a ₹3,500–4,000 cr book by year-end, carried by large awards (QRSAM, MIGM) that are expected but not awarded. That comes from a secondary report of the call and should be read as pipeline, not order book. MIGM has Defence Acquisition Council approval and is "entering production"; QRSAM has nothing filed.

Risks

A ₹2,500 cr acquisition and a ₹3,322 cr share issue, at 114x earnings. The Premier purchase is larger than Apollo's own ₹1,519 crd net worth, and it is in a different business (propellants). Funding it adds about 21.5%d to the share count at ₹416.60. If the issue were delayed or under-subscribed, the gap would have to be filled with debt: borrowing limits were raised at the same EGM, and CARE's rating sits on watch.
  • Q2 tax rate of 50%, unexplained in the filings I could read. If it is structural (subsidiary losses that can't be offset), consolidated earnings will keep lagging standalone.
  • Cash conversion is still negative after capex. H1 operating cash was +₹22 cr against about ₹156 crd of capex; inventory is ₹909 cr and receivables ₹640 cr.
  • The order book is thin relative to the run-rate. ₹1,765 cr against an annualised ₹1,550 crd from Q2. Growth from here depends on big awards that haven't been filed.
  • Litigation on the underwater-mine tender. The award is subject to the DRDO's pre-award verification after the High Court ruling.
  • Almost no active institutional ownership. Mutual-fund holding is about 1.2%, 13 of 14 schemes are passive, and DIIs hold 1.5%. The holder base is retail-heavy and volatile, as the ±8% days in September and October showed.
  • Value leakage to ShauryAstra, which is 34% owned, if the "complete weapon systems" business develops there.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: a QRSAM or MIGM production order filed with the exchanges that takes the book above ₹3,000 cr; or full-year FY27 free cash flow at or above zero with consolidated margin held above 25%. A price back near the ₹380s with the H1 numbers intact would also do it.
  • Downgrade to Sell: the preferential issue stalling or the promoter-group warrants lapsing unconverted; the Premier deal funded with debt instead; consolidated EBITDA margin back below 20%; or the tax rate staying near 50% for another quarter without an explanation.
