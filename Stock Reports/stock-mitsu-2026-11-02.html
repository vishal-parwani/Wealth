Verdict

Q2 FY27 results are declared (board meeting 31 Oct 2026, unaudited standalone, limited review). Revenue was ₹95.8 cr, up 3.7% YoY and up 0.7% QoQ. EBITDA was ₹11.9 cr at a 12.4% margin. That is double the 6.4% of Q2 FY26 but down from 16.3% in Q1 FY27. PAT was ₹6.1 cr, up 224% YoY and down 30% QoQ, and EPS was ₹4.49. For H1, revenue was ₹191.0 cr (+7.5%), EBITDA ₹27.4 cr (14.3%) and PAT ₹14.8 cr (+365%). Raw materials rose to 63.3% of sales from 59.3% in Q1d, which explains almost all of the sequential margin drop. The stock fell 6% on 30 Oct, before the results, and was trading about 6% lower again on the morning of 2 Nov.

Cheap on the trailing numbers, but those numbers flatter the business. Hold, high risk. Ten times trailing earnings and 2.2x book look low next to Time Technoplast (16.6x) and Pyramid Technoplast (17.3x). Profit has tripled in a year. Still, three things argue against buying at ₹206, after a 150% rise from the March low:

(1) The margin step-up is partly a one-off. Management said in May that HDPE, the main input, rose about 40% after the war began in March 2026. Customers re-price about a month later, so Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 earned inventory gains on stock bought at the old price. Asked in May whether that inventory gain would reverse, MD Manish Dedhia answered "yes, yes, obviously". Q2's gross margin fell back to 36.7%, just below Q2 FY26's 37.4%d. Most of the YoY EBITDA gain in Q2 came from other expenses, which fell from 24.0% to 18.0% of salesd, and the filings don't explain that drop. Management's own sustainable range is 10–13%. (2) Growth is not showing up. In May the company guided about 30% revenue growth for FY27. H1 delivered 7.5%. The ₹1,000 cr revenue target for FY28 would need about 2.9x FY26 revenue in two years. Treat it as aspiration, not guidance. (3) The balance sheet is working harder. Debt rose from ₹59 cr to ₹96 cr in six months to fund an IBC plant whose cost has never been disclosed. H1 operating cash flow was ₹9 cr on ₹27 cr of EBITDA, because payables were run down by ₹15 cr.

The counter-case is real. The operating improvement began in Q3 FY26, before the HDPE spike, as management pruned low-margin SKUs. Hospital-furniture parts are up 30% and now 16% of sales. The IBC line is due in Q3 FY27, and promoters are putting in fresh money through warrants at ₹151. If Q3 holds a margin of 12% or more while IBC starts, normalised EPS is about ₹20 and the stock looks cheap. If the margin drops to 10%, EPS is about ₹17 and the multiple about 12xd: fair for a working-capital-heavy micro-cap with three-quarters of sales from ten customers.

Business

Nature of business

Mitsu makes blow-moulded and injection-moulded plastics, mainly from HDPE, at four plants in Maharashtra: three at Boisar/Tarapur and one at Khalapur. Capacity is 36,600+ MTPA, on 58 blow-moulding and 22 injection-moulding machines. About 84% of revenue is industrial packaging (H1 FY27: ₹154.8 cr, +4%): drums, jerry cans, bottles, jars, pails (100 ml to 250 L), caps and closures, sold to chemical, agrochemical, pharma, lube, edible-oil, flavour and FMCG makers. Hospital furniture parts are 16% of H1 sales (₹29.8 cr, +30%), sold under the Furnastra brand: bed boards, side rails, mattress platforms and a patented in-built CPR profile, sold to hospital-bed OEMs; delegates from Arjo (Poland) and Malvestio (Italy) visited the plant this year. Management says it added 150+ customers last year and 30+ in Q1 FY27. Infra furniture, automotive and others make up the remainder (stadium chair shells, fuel tanks). Exports are only about 2% of sales. The company has 700+ customers, but the top ten took 74.6% of FY26 revenue. Polymer pass-through: most contracts re-price monthly with a one-month lag, and some spot pricing was used during the 2026 price spike. Mark-up is a percentage of raw-material cost, so a rising HDPE price carries margin up with a lag and a falling one compresses it.

Moat

Narrow. Rigid HDPE packaging is a regional, freight-bound and fragmented business with about 30,000 processors. Raw material is about 60% of the selling price, and pricing is cost-plus with a one-month lag. What Mitsu has is UN/PESO-certified packaging for dangerous goods, long relationships (top-ten customers buy about ₹26 cr a year each on average), an unusually wide size range from one shop, and in hospital furniture, design registrations, a patent and OEM approvals that take time to win. None of this gives pricing power. Margins of 7–10% before 2026 show what the packaging core earns in a normal year.

Order book & capex

No order book is disclosed; this is a run-rate supply business, not a project business. Confirmed capex: (1) a 3,550 MTPA blow/injection expansion, online from Aug 2026, at about ₹2 cr per management. That figure looks low for that much capacity, and no breakdown was given. (2) A fully automated IBC (intermediate bulk container) plant at Khalapur, due in Q3 FY27. Its cost has never been disclosed. Asked directly in May and August, management said it would "announce very soon". CWIP of ₹32.7 cr and H1 investing outflow of ₹43 cr are the only measure, so the true project cost could be higher. IBCs are a higher-margin product, but Time Technoplast and Pyramid are entrenched there. The ₹1,000 cr FY28 revenue target has no supporting capacity plan: at FY26's roughly ₹17 cr of revenue per 1,000 td, it implies about 60,000 t of output, 1.6x current capacity, against 21,000 t sold in FY26.

Risks

Margin mean-reversion. Two quarters at 16% were helped by inventory gains on an HDPE price spike. Q2's gross margin is already back below last year's. If other expenses drift back toward 20–22% of sales, their FY24–FY26 level (24% in Q2 FY26), EBITDA margins return to 7–10% and trailing EPS of ₹20 falls to roughly ₹7–12d.
  • Customer concentration. The top ten customers are 75–78% of revenue in every year shown. Losing one large chemical or agro customer would take a high-single-digit share of sales.
  • Guidance credibility. FY27 guidance was about 30% growth and H1 delivered 7.5%. The ₹1,000 cr FY28 target is about 2.9x FY26 revenue. The capex figures quoted on calls are thin or missing.
  • Leverage and cash conversion. Net debt went from ₹59 cr to ₹96 cr in six months, with almost no cash on hand. H1 operating cash flow was 33% of EBITDA. There is no current public credit rating.
  • IBC execution. The plant is new, its cost undisclosed, and it enters a market led by much larger rivals. Commissioning slipping past Q3, or a slow ramp, adds depreciation and interest without revenue.
  • Governance signals. The MD is also CFO. The secretarial auditor resigned and the CS changed in Q2. Preferential warrants went to a non-promoter broker-dealer, Rikhav Securities. None of this is a red flag alone, but together they are worth watching.
  • Liquidity. BSE-only, ₹0.8 cr a day of turnover, no institutional holders. The stock moved from ₹80 to ₹247 in six and a half months and can fall as fast.
  • Dilution. 10 lakh warrants at ₹151 add 7.4% to the share count within 18 months.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: Q3 FY27, around Feb 2027, with an EBITDA margin of 12% or more and revenue growth above 10% YoY, with the IBC plant commissioned and its cost disclosed. Or the price falling to about ₹170, roughly 10x a ₹17 normalised EPS, with Q3 margins at 11% or better.
  • Downgrade to Sell: Q3 EBITDA margin below 10%, below management's own floor; or net debt above ₹120 cr without IBC revenue; or a large-customer loss showing up as packaging revenue falling YoY; or promoters failing to convert their warrants.
