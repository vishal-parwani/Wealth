A four-month-old NSE-SME listing: a Bangalore precision-machining shop making aerospace/defence/healthcare components, growing fast off a tiny base (PAT ₹1.0 Cr FY23 → ₹7.5 Cr FY26*) and priced like it will keep doing so — ~57x earnings on a ₹430 Cr cap. The central tension is genuine, high-return operational momentum against a valuation, a float and a public track record that leave almost no room for a stumble.

02Business & Qualitative

Model. Apsis is a build-to-print precision machining vendor: it makes components and sub-assemblies to client-supplied designs and specifications for the aerospace, defence and healthcare industries. Work runs CAD/CAM → process development → CNC machining out of a single facility in Peenya Industrial Area, Bangalore (Shed 1 + Shed 2), with machines able to handle parts up to ~1,200 mm. Roots go back to the partnership firm M/s Apsis Latitude (2012), corporatised in 2022 and taken public in 2024–26.

Moat. Thin and process-based rather than structural. Advantages, where they exist, come from qualifications/approvals to supply aerospace & defence primes, tolerances and repeatability, and switching friction once a part is qualified into a customer's supply chain. There is no scale, brand or IP moat — this is a small job-shop competing in a fragmented precision-machining market where capacity is the barrier and customer concentration is a live risk.

05Valuation

At ₹357 the stock trades on ~57x trailing earnings and ~8.8x book, with a 0% yield — a growth-and-momentum multiple, not a value one. That prices in continued 25–40% earnings growth with sustained >25% ROE. For context, the IPO priced at ₹110 (Mar 2026) and the stock has ~3.2x'd from issue and ~2.3x'd from its ₹153 listing print in under four months — most of the return is re-rating, not yet fresh delivered fundamentals. The margin of safety is effectively nil: any growth wobble, margin normalisation toward FY26's ~24%*, or SME-liquidity unwind can compress the multiple hard.
