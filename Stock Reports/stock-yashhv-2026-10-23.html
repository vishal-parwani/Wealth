Verdict

H1 FY27 results (21 Oct 2026). Revenue was ₹194.5 cr, up 95% from ₹99.6 cr a year earlier and up 43% on the ₹135.6 cr of H2 FY26d. EBITDA before other income was ₹53.6 crd, a 27.6% margin, against 21.3% a year ago and 26.1% in H2 FY26. The company's own figure of ₹54.7 cr at 28.1% includes ₹1.1 cr of other income. PAT was ₹36.4 cr, up 166% (+54% on H2 FY26), and basic EPS was ₹12.55, against ₹4.77 and ₹8.31d. Gross margin rose from 45% to 52%d. The order book reached ₹760 cr at 30 September, up from ₹400 cr in March, which implies about ₹555 cr of orders won in six monthsd. Operating cash flow was ₹45 cr, against ₹9 cr for all of FY26. ₹101 cr of preferential and warrant money arrived and short-term debt was repaid, so the company now holds ₹85 cr of net cashd. The auditor's limited review is unmodified. Exports and replacement & services rose from about 9% to about 14% of sales. The new plant is not yet making its own cores: the first invoice was for RIP bushings assembled from imported cores, and full core manufacturing is due "during Q3". The stock jumped 14% intraday the next day, then gave most of it back. It closed today 1.3% above its pre-results level.

The business is better than the price assumed, but the promoter family is selling. Hold, high risk. On the business alone, this half-year clears the upgrade bar the 12 October note set, which was 40% growth at a 24% margin. Growth was 95% at 27.6%. The ₹760 cr order book is 2.3 times trailing revenued. With the price down 6% since that note, the trailing P/E has fallen from 95x to 58x. Doubling H1 gives about 48x FY27 earnings, and if the second half again beats the first by 30% the multiple is about 42xd. That is expensive but no longer extreme for a company growing this fast with net cash.

What stops an upgrade is the shareholding. On 7 September Twinkle Shah sold 2.48 lakh shares and Keyur Shah 52,000, all at about ₹947: 3.0 lakh shares, ₹28 cr, roughly a full point of the company. It was disclosed on 8 September, and the September pattern confirms promoters fell from 54.79% to 53.79% with no dilution in between. That was a market sale six weeks before a 166% profit print, and eight weeks after the company sold shares to investors at ₹721. The 12 October note's Sell trigger was "promoter share counts falling again (an actual sale, not dilution)". That condition is met. I am not mechanically downgrading, because the sale is small (1.8% of the family's stake, after 0.7 points in May) and the result that followed was strong, not weak. But two rounds of selling in four months, at a third of today's price for the May lot, are a reason not to add.

New facts: the H1 FY27 print above; the order book up 1.9x in six months; net debt turned into ₹85 cr of net cash; and September MF data showing WhiteOak Flexi Cap buying 1.6 lakh shares. Business

Nature of business

Yash makes transformer bushings, the insulated fittings that carry a conductor through a transformer casing. The range covers oil-impregnated paper (OIP) condenser bushings, resin-impregnated paper and synthetic (RIP/RIS) bushings, high-current bushings to 25,000 A, wall bushings and retrofit work. It sells to utilities, EPCs and transformer OEMs. The company calls itself the only independent Indian maker, and one of fewer than 12 worldwide. In H1 FY27 it remained a single segment. Domestic sales fell from about 91% to about 86% of revenue, while exports and replacement & services together rose from about 9% to about 14%. The split comes from presentation pie charts, so treat it as approximate. Product mix was essentially unchanged, with one line still about 85–86% of sales.

Moat

Moderate and improving. Bushings are safety-critical, and the company says utilities take 8–10 years to qualify a new supplier. Yash claims approvals at every Indian utility, including nuclear, and a 96% repeat-order rate. The seismic IEEE qualification is the kind of slow, costly credential that keeps rivals out of US utility tenders. The weakness hasn't changed: a concentrated buyer base of transformer makers and utilities, and RIP/RIS cores still imported until the new plant's winding line runs.

Risks

Promoter selling into strength. The family sold at ₹748 in May and ₹947 in September, both times shortly before strong results and the second time just after the company raised equity at ₹721. The amounts are small next to a 53.8% stake, but the direction is consistent. A third round would turn a pattern into a policy.
  • Valuation. 58x trailing and 10.9x book. The multiple has halved through earnings rather than price, but a merely good H2 would still leave little room.
  • Working capital. Receivables more than doubled to ₹69.5 cr in six months, and other current assets nearly quadrupled. The half's strong operating cash flow leaned on ₹63 cr of payables and other liabilities, probably advances, which reverse as orders are delivered.
  • Plant ramp. End-to-end RIP/RIS core manufacturing is still to be commissioned in Q3 FY27. Yields, utility re-approvals for cores made in-house, and depreciation on a ₹220 cr facility will weigh on margins until volumes arrive.
  • Margin durability. Gross margin jumped 7 points with no explanation in the filings. If mix or one-off pricing drove it, H2 could give some of it back.
  • Further dilution. 8.32 lakh warrants at ₹721 (about 2.8%), plus ongoing ESOP allotments, with ₹1.9 cr of ESOP cost in H1.
  • Infrequent reporting. The company reports half-yearly. The next numbers are H2 FY27, due around May 2027.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: the December 2026 shareholding showing no further promoter sales, plus confirmation that in-house RIP/RIS core manufacturing is running in Q3. Or a price near ₹1,000 (about 36x FY27 EPS of ₹28 if H2 beats H1 by 30%), even with the promoter overhang.
  • Downgrade to Sell: another promoter market sale; or H2 FY27 operating margin below 22% or revenue below H1's ₹194.5 cr with the order book flat; or receivable days stretching past 90 without order-book growth to explain it.
