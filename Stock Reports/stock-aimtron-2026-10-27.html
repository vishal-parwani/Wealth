Verdict

Why it jumped · 27 Oct 2026. The stock closed at ₹1,662.30, up ₹51.75 or 3.2% on 27 October, after ₹1,610.55 on the 26th. It is up 3.7% from the 23 October close of ₹1,602.55. Volume was 24,800 shares, about half the six-month average. No company-specific cause can be confirmed. The latest exchange filings are from 23–24 October: the EGM proceedings, and the voting results approving the preferential issue. Neither is new information on the 27th. The likely driver is a sector move. Over the same two sessions (26–27 Oct), EMS and defence-electronics names bounced harder than Aimtron did: Avalon +7.8% and +8.3%, Apollo Micro +9.6% and +3.5%, Syrma +4.3% and +1.6%, Kaynes +3.7% and +1.3%. Aimtron rose 0.5% and then 3.2%. A 3% move on half-normal volume in a 26%-float SME stock is noise-sized. I found no news item tied to it.

The real jump was in September, and its cause is confirmed. On 21 September the company gave notice of a board meeting to consider a fund-raise, and the stock rose 7.3% (₹1,662 → ₹1,784) on 1.33 lakh shares. On 25 September the board approved a ₹205 cr preferential issue at ₹1,674.47 a share. The allottees are Clarus Capital, 360 ONE, Niveshaay, AAA Apex and two Alchemy funds, all non-promoter AIFs. The stock rose 5.2% that day and reached its ₹2,010 high on 30 September. It has since given all of that back. The 22 October EGM approved the issue (+3.7% that day, intraday +6.5%), and the stock fell 6.0% the next session.

What changed since 24 Sep 2026. The price is down 7.5% from ₹1,798 to ₹1,662, and 17% below the 30 September peak. The rating stays Hold, high risk. Three new facts arrived. First, the ₹205 cr equity raise (above), which is shareholder-approved but not yet allotted. Second, the US subsidiary AIC booked US$11.8 mn of orders in the quarter to 17 September, about three times ICS's historical run-rate, from five new customers. Third, the Sep-2026 shareholding shows promoters down to 68.07% from 70.90%. About a third of that drop is the promoter's 4 August bulk sale of 2.1 lakh shares at ₹1,480 to an Alchemy fund. A pilot order from Curtiss-Wright (16 Sep) has no disclosed value. No new financials have been filed. H1 FY27 results are still pending (below).

For a buy/hold decision: hold if you own it, don't initiate here. The equity raise matters. Pro forma, it turns ₹41 cr of net debt into roughly ₹164 cr of net cashd. It also takes book value to about ₹440 cr, which brings P/BV down to about 8.5xd on the enlarged 2.24 cr shares. A 5.5% dilutiond is a modest price for that. Six AIFs committing ₹205 cr at ₹1,674 is also the first meaningful outside check on these accounts. But the issue exists because the business consumes cash. Operating cash flow was −₹19 cr in FY25 and −₹40 cr in FY26 against ₹72 cr of reported profit, and receivables rose ₹118 cr. Today's ₹1,662 is just below the price those investors are paying, and they get a lock-in, not a discount. At 77x trailing earnings, a buyer here pays for growth that has not yet turned into cash.

The catalyst is H1 FY27, likely in the first half of November. Last year's half-year results were approved on 4 November 2025. Watch three numbers: (1) H1 revenue against the ₹604 cr order book and the ₹84 cr Q1 business update; (2) half-year operating cash flow; (3) receivables against ₹135 cr in March. If operating cash flow turns positive with receivables flat, the upgrade case is live. If not, the raise was funding the working-capital hole.

Business

Nature of business

Aimtron is a Vadodara-based ESDM/ODM company, incorporated in 2011. It started in PCB design and assembly and is moving into box-build, system integration and product design. End markets are industrial electronics, power and energy, ruggedised/defence-adjacent electronics, IoT, battery storage and medical. The February 2026 acquisition of ICS (Decatur, US; about US$17 mn revenue; customers include Caterpillar and John Deere) operates as Aimtron International Controls (AIC). It is the base for the "India–USA platform" pitch: US-facing customer work with Indian manufacturing behind it.

Moat

None yet. Contract electronics is a scale-and-execution business, and customers dual-source. If the design/ODM capability and the US customer relationships work, they would create stickiness. Today that is a plan supported by bookings.

Order book

₹604 cr consolidated as of Q1 FY27 (27 Jul 2026), about 2.0x FY26 revenue. This is a company disclosure, unaudited, with no conversion schedule. AIC's US$11.8 mn (~₹100 crd) Q2 bookings to 17 Sep are incremental, but no updated group order book had been filed by 27 Oct.

Risks

Profit without cash, now funded by equity. Two years of negative operating cash flow against ₹72 cr of profit. The ₹205 cr raise buys time; it does not prove the receivables are good. If H1 FY27 shows another cash outflow, the raise will look like funding the gap rather than growth.
  • No MF holder, no broker coverage. The only outside validation is AIFs buying in at roughly today's price.
  • Promoter selling. One disclosed ~1% sale at ₹1,480 in August, and a further unexplained ~1-point decline. Small, but it is a change of direction for a promoter who was buying in 2025.
  • Valuation. 77x trailing earnings and 14.6x book (about 8.5x post-issued) for a business that has not converted profit to cash.
  • Half-yearly reporting. A deterioration can run six months before it is visible.
  • Liquidity. About 0.5 lakh shares a day; today's move happened on 24,800 shares. Exiting a position of size takes days.
  • Acquisition-led growth. The stated use of proceeds includes "mergers and JVs across globe". More US deals add integration and goodwill risk to a ₹300 cr-revenue company.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 (due by mid-November) shows positive operating cash flow with receivables at or below ₹135 cr while revenue keeps growing above 50%. Or a price pullback well below the ₹1,674 preferential price with the business case intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: a third successive period of operating cash outflow with debtor days rising past 200, a receivables write-off, further promoter selling, or the preferential issue failing to complete.
