Verdict

Hold. The order book and the latest quarter justify the growth story, but at 36x trailing earnings the stock already prices it in, while the cash goes into battery and electrolyser capex that has yet to earn anything.

The counter-argument is cash and ambition. FY26 operating cash flow was −₹10 cr against ₹55 cr of profit. Capex took free cash flow to −₹119 cr. The margin profile is also drifting down as EPC grows: 17% in FY24, 12% in FY26.

Business

Nature of business

The established business, "Power Transmission Solutions" and 71% of the order book, has three parts. It manufactures and supplies stringing tools, ACS wire, optical-ground-wire (OPGW) cables, joint boxes and Emergency Restoration Systems (temporary towers utilities keep for line failures). It runs EPC for OPGW installation, live-line work and underground cabling. It has the first Make-in-India ERS. The new-energy side, 29% of the book, does solar and BESS EPC through Advait Greenergy, and is building BESS assembly, electrolyser and fuel-cell manufacturing at Dholera, Gujarat. The customers are state transmission and distribution utilities, POWERGRID (OPGW approval received in Q1) and renewable developers such as KPI Green.

Moat

Narrow, and limited to the product lines. ERS and OPGW need utility type-approvals and installation know-how, and Advait was first to make ERS in India. Those niches carry the higher margins. The EPC work now driving revenue is tendered and has no moat. In BESS and electrolysers Advait is a new entrant against much larger players, and the HiTHIUM deal secures inputs, not customers.

Order book

₹1,330 cr as at 30 Jun 26, about 1.7x TTM revenued. It includes L1 positions and is split 71% transmission and 29% new energy. Management targets ₹1,600–1,650 cr by March 2027 and a ₹2,000 cr pipeline. The July–August wins of about ₹500 crd came after the 30 June figure, so part of them is likely already counted as L1.

Risks

Capex before cash. FY26 free cash flow was −₹119 cr while the Dholera BESS and electrolyser plant is still being built. More capex lands in FY27. If EPC receivables stretch at the same time, the company will need either more debt (now ₹128 cr) or another equity issue.
  • Execution against the projection. ₹1,251 cr for FY27 requires twice the current quarterly run-rate. A miss would land on a stock priced at 36x trailing.
  • Margin dilution. EPC and solar work carry lower margins than ERS and OPGW products. EBITDA margin has gone from 17% (FY24) to 12% (FY26).
  • New-energy technology risk. Electrolysers and fuel cells are early-stage with uncertain demand. Green-hydrogen EPC revenue fell to ₹0.2 cr in Q1.
  • Utility concentration. Gujarat discoms (PGVCL, GETCO) and other state utilities dominate the book, so payment timing depends on state finances.
  • Holding structure. Value is increasingly spread across subsidiaries and step-down subsidiaries.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 revenue above ₹450 cr with EBITDA margin at or above 13% and positive operating cash flow, or a pullback to about 25x TTM (about ₹1,320).
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin back below 11%, the Dholera start slipping past FY27, an equity raise at a discount, or promoter holding falling below 65%.
