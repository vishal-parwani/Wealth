Verdict

Buy — unchanged, but the cheap-stub arithmetic needs correcting. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹1,059 on 13 Aug 2026. The stock is ₹1,102.50, up 4.1%. The cash story and the Wipro sale are both intact; what this note changes is how the operating business should be valued.

The cash is real. Roughly ₹790 cr of cash and short-term investments against ₹24 cr of borrowings, plus ₹256 cr arriving from Wipro for the EVA and Good Home brands, agreements signed 6 Aug 2026 and closing by 30 Sep 2026. Call it ₹1,015 cr of net cash inside a ₹1,561 cr company — about 65% of the market cap, leaving a stub of roughly ₹546 cr for the operating business.

The correction. The previous note set that stub against FY26 PAT of ₹65.7 cr, implying about 8x. That double-counts: most of that ₹66 cr is interest earned on the very cash just subtracted. FY26 operating profit was only about ₹26 cr on ₹857 cr of revenue — a 3% margin. Post-tax that is nearer ₹19 cr, so the stub was on roughly 29x, not 8x. You cannot both strip out the cash and keep the income it generates.

Why it is still a Buy. Because the June quarter changed the operating number, which is the thing that matters. Operating profit was ₹15 cr against ₹3 cr a year earlier, margin 6.0% against 1.2%, and PAT ₹21 cr, up 64%. Annualise that and operating profit is ~₹60 cr, post-tax ~₹45 cr, putting the stub on about 12x. That is genuinely cheap — conditional entirely on the margin holding. One quarter is not proof, which is why this is an accumulate rather than a table-thump.

Capital allocation is the entire debate, and the register explains why. Promoters hold 74.57% — effectively at the 75% ceiling — so minority holders have no practical ability to force a decision on the cash. The TTK promoter group has not historically been in a hurry to deploy or return it. A cash pile is worth what management does with it: a special dividend or buyback would crystallise the value immediately; sitting on it for another five years would not.

The ₹256 cr from Wipro is the near-term test. It will land by 30 September, and what is announced alongside it — reinvestment, buyback, dividend, or silence — tells you more about this investment than any quarter of results will.

Only 22,809 shareholders and a 21.39% public float. This is a thinly held, thinly traded stock despite a ₹1,561 cr market cap, and price moves can be abrupt in both directions.

Business

Nature. TTK Healthcare operates in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, protective devices (condoms, under the TTK-Protective banner) and foods. Following the Wipro sale it exits the home and personal care brands EVA and Good Home.

What is actually improving. Operating margin has risen every quarter for a year — 1.2%, 2.6%, 3.4%, 5.0%, 6.0% — while revenue grew modestly. FY26's profit decline was attributed to Protective Devices weakness, so the recovery in margin suggests that segment has stabilised. This is the operating evidence the investment case needs, and it is only four quarters old.

What the Wipro sale does. It converts a ₹148 cr revenue line into ₹256 cr of cash — a sale at about 1.7x revenue for brands in a category TTK was sub-scale in. Strategically sensible; it makes the company smaller, more focused and even more cash-heavy.

The structural issue. A 3–6% operating margin business is not intrinsically high quality. The investment case is not "great business at a fair price" — it is "ordinary business, improving, available for less than its cash-adjusted worth, with a catalyst".

Risks

  • Capital allocation. The single biggest risk. ₹1,015 cr of net cash under a 74.57% promoter with no history of urgency. Cash that is never deployed or returned is worth less than its face value to a minority holder.
  • The margin turn is four quarters old. The stub is cheap on annualised Q1 and expensive on FY26. Everything depends on which persists.
  • Working capital blew out from 48 to 161 days in FY26, without explanation in the sources reviewed here.
  • No institutional ownership. Zero mutual funds. Abakkus's 2.07% is a single PMS position, not broad institutional support.
  • Thin float. 21.39% public, 22,809 holders. Illiquid for the size.
  • Low returns on the underlying business. 6.30% ROE and 8.04% ROCE, flattered or depressed depending on how you treat the cash — either way, not a compounder's profile.
  • Revenue shrinks after the sale. ₹148 cr of FY26 revenue departs with EVA and Good Home when the deal closes.

What would change the rating

  • Stays a Buy — while operating margin holds at or above 5% and the stock trades below a sensible cash-adjusted value.
  • Raise conviction — a buyback or special dividend announced alongside the Wipro proceeds, or two more quarters at 6% margin. Either resolves the central question.
  • Downgrade to Hold — operating margin falling back under 3%, or the Wipro proceeds landing with no stated plan.
  • Downgrade to Sell — working capital deteriorating further, or the cash being spent on an unrelated acquisition at a full price.
