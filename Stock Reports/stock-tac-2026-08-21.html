Verdict

Buy — unchanged, and the June quarter strengthened the case. The prior note rated this Buy at ₹437.20 on 3 Aug 2026. The stock is ₹417.85, down 4.4%. It sits 53.6% below its ₹900 high, so this is not a name that has run away from you.

Q1 FY27 was the best quarter the company has reported. Consolidated sales ₹19.78 cr against ₹9.76 cr, up 102.7%. PAT ₹8.05 cr against ₹3.40 cr, up 136.8%. Operating margin went up to 48.2% while doubling revenue, which is rare. FY26 itself was revenue ₹57 cr, up 90%, with PAT ₹26 cr, up 73%.

The valuation is the unusual part. 31.9x trailing is not cheap in absolute terms, but management has guided to roughly ₹100 cr of FY27 revenue against ₹57 cr in FY26. Hold the current 40% PAT margin and that is about ₹40 cr of profit, or roughly 22x forward earnings for a business growing over 100% with no debt and 38% ROCE. That combination is why this stays a Buy rather than a Hold.

What keeps the risk tag on. Zero institutional ownership, an SME board, and — see the financials below — most of the profit sits in subsidiaries rather than the parent. None of that is a reason to avoid it, but all of it is a reason to size it as a speculative position rather than a core one.

Read the consolidated column, and know why. Standalone and consolidated tell opposite stories. On a standalone basis FY26 revenue fell from ₹23.49 cr to ₹20.01 cr and PAT fell from ₹13.09 cr to ₹4.86 cr. On a consolidated basis revenue rose to ₹57 cr and PAT to ₹26 cr. So roughly two-thirds of revenue and about 81% of profit sit in subsidiaries, not the listed parent, and the parent entity shrank last year.

That is not necessarily a problem — the group runs international operations and the growth is genuinely there at group level. But it means the quality of these numbers depends on subsidiary accounting you cannot see from the aggregator, and it is the single thing worth checking in the annual report before adding to a position. The standalone P/E is 175x; the consolidated is 31.9x. Anyone quoting one without the other is telling you half the story.

The institutional exit is the awkward fact in this note. FIIs held 9.81% at listing and 0.04% now; DIIs went from 2.23% to zero. Institutions sold all the way through a period when revenue went from ₹12 cr to ₹57 cr. There is no public explanation. The benign reading is that SME lot sizes and liquidity make the stock unworkable for a fund regardless of the fundamentals, which is a real constraint and would resolve on a main-board migration. The unfriendly reading is that they saw something in the accounts. This note cannot settle which, and says so rather than picking the comfortable one.

Promoters are steady at 56.66%, essentially unchanged in two years, and the shareholder count has gone from 1,193 to 5,143. Retail has absorbed the entire institutional exit.

Business

Nature. TAC Infosec, which trades as TAC Security, sells cybersecurity software rather than services. Its ESOF platform does vulnerability management and risk quantification — scoring an organisation's security posture and prioritising what to fix — with compliance automation alongside it. Revenue is subscription-led, which is what produces the margin profile below.

Why the margins look the way they do. A 48% operating margin and a 40% PAT margin are top-tier for cybersecurity globally, where most peers at this stage burn cash. That is either a genuine product advantage or a sign of a narrow, high-value customer base being served cheaply. Two years of holding those margins through 90%+ growth argues for the former, but the concentration question below remains open.

Safehouse. Acquired July 2026 for about ₹1 cr — small enough to be a product and team purchase rather than a financial event. It takes the company from enterprise into consumer security via the BodyGuard product. Consumer is a different motion from enterprise SaaS, with different economics, so treat it as an option rather than a pillar until it shows revenue.

Anchor holder. The Kedia family stake of roughly 14.6% is the closest thing this register has to an institution, and the name is widely traded on that basis.

Risks

  • Everything sits in subsidiaries. ~81% of consolidated profit is outside the listed parent, and the parent shrank in FY26. This is the first thing to verify in the annual report.
  • No institutional ownership at all. Zero mutual funds, zero DII, FII at 0.04%. There is no institutional bid to arrest a fall, and no institutional diligence to lean on.
  • SME board. Thin liquidity, large lot sizes, wide spreads. The stock has already fallen 54% from its high once.
  • Key-man and anchor concentration. A 14.6% family stake is the register's backbone; an exit would hit price and sentiment together.
  • Client concentration. Not disclosed. A ₹57 cr software business with these margins likely depends on a modest number of large contracts.
  • Guidance risk. The Buy case leans on ₹100 cr FY27 revenue. Company guidance is not an estimate from an independent analyst, and there is no broker to check it against.
  • Margin, not revenue, is the swing factor. At a 30% PAT margin the forward multiple is 29x rather than 22x, and much of the attraction goes.

What would change the rating

  • Stays a Buy — while quarterly growth holds above 50% with PAT margin near 40% and the company tracks its ₹100 cr guidance.
  • Upgrade in conviction — a main-board migration, or the first institutional positions appearing. Either would remove the liquidity discount that is arguably why it trades at 22x forward while growing over 100%.
  • Downgrade to Hold — growth slowing below about 30%, PAT margin under 30%, or receivables rising faster than revenue.
  • Downgrade to Sell — the Kedia family reducing materially, or the FY27 guidance being cut.
