Business

Nature of business

Yash makes transformer bushings, the insulated fittings that carry a conductor through a transformer casing. The range covers oil-impregnated paper (OIP) condenser bushings, resin-impregnated paper and synthetic (RIP/RIS) bushings, high-current bushings to 25,000 A, wall bushings and retrofit work. The demand comes from India's grid build-out and transformer replacement cycle, plus a new push into the US through representative agents. The ₹151 cr preferential raise funds an upgrade of the RIP line from 245 kV to 550 kV.

Moat

Moderate. Bushings are safety-critical and type-tested, and qualifying with a transformer maker or utility is slow, which keeps suppliers in place once approved. Utility approval and product homologation are exactly what the US representatives are hired to do, so US revenue will take time to build. The weakness is customer concentration among a few transformer makers and state utilities, who hold the pricing power.

Order book

₹400 cr unexecuted at 31 Mar 2026, about 1.7x FY26 revenue, against ₹150 cr a year earlier. The figure comes from trade press and the May 2026 call rather than a filing this note could open. No later figure has been disclosed. H1 FY27 results should bring one.

Risks

Valuation at a record high. 95x trailing and 19x book for a ₹235 cr-revenue company that reports twice a year. A merely good H1 FY27, say 30% growth, could take a quarter off the price with nothing going wrong in the business.
  • Ramp-up of the new plant. Commercial production has started, but yields, approvals and utilisation on a new RIP/RIS line usually take several quarters to settle.
  • Working capital. Inventory days went from 138 to 201 and the cash conversion cycle from 112 to 187 days in FY26. Operating cash flow covered only 16% of EBITDAd.
  • Further dilution. 8.32 lakh warrants at ₹721 convert within 18 months of allotment, about 2.8% more shares.
  • Infrequent reporting. Half-yearly results mean problems surface late.
  • Customer concentration. Transformer makers and state utilities are a narrow buyer base, and utility ordering is lumpy.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: H1 FY27 revenue growth of 40% or more with operating margin at or above 24%, and the September 2026 shareholding showing no further promoter sales. Or, on the current numbers, a price near ₹950 (about 48x FY27 earnings if profit grows 50%).
  • Downgrade to Sell: promoter share counts falling again (an actual sale, not dilution), H1 FY27 operating margin below 20%, or no Vadodara revenue visible by H2 FY27.
