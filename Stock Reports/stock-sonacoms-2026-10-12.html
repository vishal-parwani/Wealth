Verdict

What changed since 24 Sep 2026 — Hold kept, but for a different reason. The price is down 2.2%, from ₹816 to ₹798. The bigger change is the consensus target. The 24 September note carried a stale ₹619 from 14 analysts. Trendlyne's consensus now stands at ₹783 from 18 analysts; it was ₹632 ninety days ago and ₹765 sixty days ago. Most of that move followed the July results. The stock is therefore no longer "32% above every target": it sits 2% above the mean and 3% below the median of ₹821. The 24 September note also missed two July facts. Sona signed two electric-powertrain joint ventures with Denso, and the June-quarter growth includes an acquired railway business. Neither changes the margin problem. Q2 FY27 results, likely in late October, are the next input. Your Buy override on this name still stands against the report's Hold.

The valuation gap to analysts has closed, but the margin problem behind the Hold has not. At ₹798 the stock trades on 59.5xd consensus FY27 earnings of ₹13.41 and 46.6xd FY28. Consensus expects profit to grow 29% this year. That multiple leaves no room for a miss, and the one line that has to improve, operating margin, is still falling: 28% in FY24, 24% in FY26 and 23% in the June quarter.

The operating case is strong. Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹1,301 cr on Screener's consolidated basis (₹1,310 cr in the company release). Battery-EV revenue more than doubled to ₹436 cr, or 44% of automotive sales. The net order book is ₹24,000 cr, 5.4x FY26 revenue, and 64% of it is electric. The Denso joint ventures give the company Japanese IP and an OEM partner for high-voltage traction motors and inverters. They also bring in roughly ₹800 cr of cash when the two- and three-wheeler unit is sold down to Denso at a ₹1,750 cr enterprise value.

The headline growth is flattered by the railway acquisition. Sona bought Escorts Kubota's railway equipment division (₹913 cr FY25 revenue) for ₹1,600 cr, and it first appears in the September 2025 quarter. That is why revenue stepped from ₹854 cr to ₹1,138 cr in one quarter. Strip out about ₹230 cr a quarter of railway revenue and June-quarter organic growth is roughly 26%d, not 52%. From the September 2026 quarter the acquisition is in the base, so reported growth should slow sharply even if nothing goes wrong.

The balance sheet is clean (₹94 cr net debt on ₹5,983 cr equity), and the promoter block has not sold a share. This is a good business at a full price. Buying more makes sense only after margin stabilises or the price falls towards the bearish end of the target range. Selling makes sense only if the order book or margin breaks.

Business

Nature of business

Sona makes differential assemblies and gears, conventional and micro-hybrid starter motors, BSG systems, and traction motors and controllers for battery-electric vehicles. It sells to global OEMs in North America, Europe, India and China. Since late 2025 it has also made railway equipment (brakes, couplers, suspension) through the former Escorts Kubota division. Electrification works in its favour: 44% of automotive revenue is already battery-EV, as is 64% of the order book. Europe and North America were about two-thirds of revenue at last disclosure; management wants eastern markets to supply more than half within five years.

Moat

Genuine. Driveline and traction-motor programmes are designed into a vehicle platform years before production and validated by the OEM. Once nominated, the supplier usually keeps the programme for its life. The ₹24,000 cr order book is that moat in rupees. The weakness is pricing power: OEMs know they hold a captive supplier for a multi-year programme, and the margin line shows it. The Denso tie-up deepens the technology moat in high-voltage systems but shares the economics.

Order book

₹24,000 cr net order book at the end of Q1 FY27 (July 2026), about 5.4x FY26 revenue. ₹15,400 cr of it (64%) is EV programmes. A separate ₹800 cr sits in the Robotics & Physical-AI vertical. These are nominated lifetime programme values, not firm purchase orders, so timing depends on OEM volumes. Western EV programmes have been deferred before.

Risks

The multiple assumes a margin recovery that has not started. At 59.5x FY27 consensus earnings, the price needs 29% profit growth and stable margins. EBITDA margin has fallen for four straight quarters to 22.5% on Screener's basis, and operating profit has been flat at about ₹290 cr a quarter for a year. Reported growth will also slow from the September 2026 quarter, when the railway acquisition enters the base. A de-rating to the 35–45x paid for slower auto-component names would take the stock to ₹470–600d on FY27 EPS.
  • Growth is not reaching operating profit. Revenue was up 52% year on year in Q1 (about 26%d organic) while EBITDA was up 42% and flat quarter on quarter. The EV mix and the railway business appear to carry lower margins than the legacy driveline business.
  • Promoter-level dispute. Control of Aureus, the 28% promoter, is the subject of a Supreme Court-mediated family dispute worth a reported ₹30,000 cr. An outcome that forces a stake sale or contests board nominations would weigh on the stock.
  • Capital allocation. Management has signalled acquisitions, and cash will reach roughly ₹2,200 cr with the Denso proceeds. A full-priced deal in robotics or a new geography could dilute returns on equity, already only 11%.
  • Western EV and tariff exposure. North America and Europe drive most revenue and the EV order book. US tariff changes or further EV programme deferrals hit directly.
  • Crowded register. Domestic institutions own 42% and mutual funds alone about 37%. Widely held mid caps fall hard when flows reverse.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Buy: EBITDA margin holding at 23% or better through Q2 and Q3 FY27 with double-digit organic growth after the railway acquisition laps, or a pullback to roughly ₹650 (about 48x FY27 EPS, near Motilal's ₹652) with the order book intact.
  • Downgrade to Sell: margin below 22% on the company's own basis, a cut to the ₹24,000 cr order book, an expensive acquisition, or a court outcome that forces the promoter to sell a large block.
