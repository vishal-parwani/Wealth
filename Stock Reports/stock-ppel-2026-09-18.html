Verdict

Downgrade to Sell. The price has gone far past the level we said not to chase, and nothing new about the business has come out. The IPO note liked the business: 32% revenue growth, 33% return on capital, 58 customers including overseas ones, a first step into aerospace. It called ₹301 cheap. It also set limits: buy at ₹350–420, don't chase above ₹520. The stock listed at ₹470 on 4 August and is now ₹706. That's 135% above the issue price and 45x FY26 earnings, all in 33 trading sessions.

Nothing new supports that. As an SME listing it reports only twice a year, so the first results since listing (H1 FY27) come in November. The rise is driven by the trading, not by new information. Meanwhile the FY26 filings show a few things that need watching. Inventory days rose from 76 to 148 in two years and the cash cycle from 74 to 134 days. The top five customers are 74.7% of revenue. Free cash flow was −₹30 cr in FY26, even before the ₹106 cr of IPO-funded expansion.

At 45x, Poojaa costs nearly as much as listed die-casters many times its size (Craftsman 59x, Sansera 65x), with far less liquidity. Sell, high risk. For anyone who got IPO shares, that means take the gain.

Business

What it does

Founded in 1992 as Pooja Castings. It makes aluminium castings (gravity, low-pressure and high-pressure die casting) and precision-machined parts, over 600 of them, for engines, drivetrains, EV powertrains and electrical systems. About 72% of revenue is automotive. Customers include OEMs and tier-1 suppliers in Germany, the US, Italy and Switzerland. The customer count went from 39 to 58 in two years. It has recently qualified as a supplier to aerospace customers.

Moat

Moderate. Getting approved as a supplier by OEMs and tier-1s, especially overseas and in aerospace, takes time, and 25 repeat customers show relationships that last. Die casting as a craft is competitive, and aluminium prices pass through to customers with a delay.

Order book

No order book is disclosed. Sales follow customer production schedules. The ₹106 cr capex from IPO money is the forward commitment. Returns on it will show from FY28.

Risks · to the Sell call

Momentum and a strong first half-year. SME stocks with little stock available and a well-known anchor investor can keep rising. If H1 FY27 shows 35–40% growth with margin intact, 45x trailing could look like 30x forward.
  • For holders: the price has run with no new results, anchor lock-ins are ending, and SME liquidity means gains can reverse quickly.
  • Customer concentration: the top five customers are 74.7% of revenue.
  • Working capital: inventory days doubled in two years.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: price back to ₹450–520 (29–33x FY26), or H1 FY27 profit growth above 35% that brings the multiple into that range.
  • Upgrade to Buy: back in the ₹350–420 range from the IPO note, with the business on track.
