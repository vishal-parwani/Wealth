Verdict

Downgrade to Sell. What we warned about is happening, and the price has gone the other way. The prospectus showed that FY26's 177% profit jump came almost entirely from gold prices. The company sold only 0.74% more gold, but it doesn't hedge, so the 45% rise in gold went straight into margin. The first quarter as a listed company shows that reversing. June-quarter revenue was ₹6,031 cr, up 26%, but material costs rose from 87.0% to 90.8% of revenue. EBITDA margin fell to 6.2% from 9.1%, interest rose, and profit fell 22% to ₹208 cr.

The IPO note said don't apply at ₹201, but rated it Hold because the stock looked about 14–18x on normal margins. The stock listed at ₹265, reached ₹338 before results, hit a 5% lower circuit on 15 September, and is now ₹305. At this price, the same normalised-profit range (₹562–735 cr) puts it at 23–30xd. That is for a business whose gold volumes barely grew in two years, whose margin depends on gold prices, and which carried ₹2,000 cr of net debt before the IPO.

Listed regional peers trade at 10–20x (Senco, PN Gadgil). Kalyan's 41x comes with hedging and national scale. The one real strength is holding volume flat while India's jewellery demand fell 23%. That was enough for Hold at ₹201. It isn't enough at ₹305. Sell, high risk. If gold rises sharply again, profit will jump again, and that is also the risk to this call.

Business

What it does

A family-run South Indian jewellery chain, founded 1985, with 61 large-format stores (53 two years ago). Gold jewellery is 92% of revenue and tier-2/3 towns are 62%. It sells on thin margins, promising low making charges, and funds a large share of its stock through customer gold-savings schemes. It doesn't take gold metal loans or otherwise hedge. The prospectus says most competitors do.

Lalithaa's 39% ROCE and 42% ROE are the FY26 gold windfall passing through an unhedged balance sheet, not normal returns. On the same margin it looks cheap. Normalise the margin and it costs more than PN Gadgil or Senco, with less volume growth than either.

Moat

A local one. Big stores, low making charges and gold-savings schemes build loyal customers in Tamil Nadu, Andhra and Karnataka. Holding volume flat through a 23% national demand drop shows that loyalty is real. But it doesn't give the company pricing power over the gold itself, which is what drives profit.

Order book

Not relevant for a retailer. The forward commitments are the store rollout funded by the IPO and the customer-advance balance (₹1,926 cr added in FY26), which is jewellery owed to scheme customers.

Risks · to the Sell call

Another sharp rise in gold. Without a hedge, Lalithaa's profit rises with gold. If gold resumes its 2025–26 climb, margin will widen again and the P/E will look low. The Sell is a view that the FY26 windfall won't repeat on schedule, not a view on where gold goes.
  • For holders: gold staying flat means flat revenue and falling margin together. Q1 was a first taste.
  • A fall in gold would mean losses on ₹9,800 cr of unhedged stock, which Kalyan and Titan are largely protected from.
  • Interest cost is rising (₹62 cr in Q1 vs ₹50 cr a year ago) as inventory grows.
  • Small float and lock-in expiries in late September and November.

What would change the rating

  • Upgrade to Hold: price back near ₹220–240 (about 17–18x on the FY25-margin profit). Or gold volumes, disclosed in the annual report, growing more than 5% a year, showing new stores are adding business.
  • Upgrade to Buy: the company adopts a gold-metal-loan hedge (removing the windfall-and-loss swing) and volumes grow, making profit less dependent on gold prices.
