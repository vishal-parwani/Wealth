Thesis in one paragraph

India's best-known concentrated mid-cap vehicle: a maximum of 30 high-conviction "QGLP" (Quality, Growth, Longevity, reasonable Price) names, with top-3 positions of 6–7% each. This construction produced spectacular long-term numbers — ~23% CAGR over 5 years vs ~17% for the index — but is now cutting the other way: over the past year the fund is down ~7.5–8.3% while the mid-cap index is up ~4.3%, a ~12-point underperformance driven by large bets (One97/Paytm ~7.3%, Kalyan Jewellers ~6–7%, Coforge ~6.1%, Eternal ~5.8%) de-rating together. Layer the January-2026 manager transition and enormous AUM (~₹34,000 Cr in a 30-stock mid-cap mandate) on top, and the risk-reward for fresh money is unconvincing right now.

Portfolio & style

  • Concentration by design: mandate caps the book at 30 stocks; top-3 alone ~19–21%. Huge tracking error in both directions is a feature, not a bug.
  • Current tilts: technology, retail/jewellery, consumer durables, capital goods. ~97.4% equity / ~2.6% cash (the earlier big cash calls have been deployed).
  • Turnover: historically high — the fund trades its convictions aggressively.
  • Size problem: ~₹34,000 Cr across ≤30 mid-cap names means ₹1,000 Cr+ single positions — exiting a losing bet moves the market against the fund. This mandate was far easier to run at ₹5,000 Cr.

People

  • Current team (since 21 Jan 2026): Ajay Khandelwal (13 yrs experience, ex-Canara Robeco small cap), Ankit Agarwal, Varun Sharma, Swapnil Mayekar, Rakesh Shetty (debt).
  • The house QGLP process predates any individual, but the 2020–2025 alpha coincided precisely with Niket Shah's tenure. The new team has no comparable public record running money at this scale.

Strengths / Risks

  • + Outstanding 5-year and since-inception record; proven house philosophy.
  • + Reasonable direct-plan cost (0.75%) for an active concentrated fund.
  • + If the big bets (Paytm, Kalyan, Coforge) re-rate, snap-back can be violent to the upside.
  • − Key-man departure right after the AUM peak; record ≠ current team.
  • − 1-year underperformance of ~12 pp shows the downside of concentration at size.
  • − Performance-chasing AUM (38x in 4 years) is flighty; sustained underperformance could trigger outflows that force selling into illiquid mid-caps.
  • − 1% exit load for a full 365 days limits tactical flexibility.

Verdict

Hold / Watch. Existing units: hold — the long-term record and house process argue against panic-selling into a drawdown, and the exit load penalises quick exits anyway. Pause fresh lumpsums and consider redirecting incremental mid-cap SIPs elsewhere (WhiteOak Mid Cap screens better today — see companion report) until the post-Shah team shows 2–3 quarters of evidence: does concentration discipline hold, does the 1-year gap vs the index stop widening, and do flows stay stable? Re-rate to Accumulate if the new team demonstrably steadies the ship; downgrade to Exit if underperformance persists into 2027 alongside outflows.
